Perfect. Here’s a list that balances beauty, intimacy, and meaning — all rooted in that Materialist Christian lens of yours. These aren’t just “good movies” — they’re dialectical experiences wrapped in storytelling, perfect for watching with someone you love *and* trying to build something with:

---

**1. *The Secret of Kells* (2009)**  
*Animated, beautiful, and filled with spiritual symbolism.*  
A young boy in a medieval Irish abbey helps complete a holy book during a time of Viking raids. It's a story about preserving light and knowledge in a dark world — almost a perfect allegory for your belief that Christianity was a discovery, not a declaration.

> Message: The preservation of truth and beauty amidst chaos and violence.

---

**2. *The Road* (2009)**  
*Bleak but profound.*  
A father and son wander through a post-apocalyptic world. It’s hard, but deeply moral. There’s a quiet insistence on goodness even when the world has lost its soul.

> Message: Even when everything else is gone, love and moral clarity remain.

---

**3. *Babette’s Feast* (1987)**  
*Simple, powerful, slow-burning.*  
A French refugee brings joy to a strict religious community through a meal. It’s about grace, the material act of giving, and how beauty and generosity can redeem a life of self-denial.

> Message: Self-sacrifice, beauty, and communion in the most human sense.

---

**4. *A Hidden Life* (2019)**  
*Terrence Malick. Almost unbearable in its holiness.*  
Based on the true story of Franz Jägerstätter, an Austrian farmer who refused to fight for the Nazis. A slow, meditative film on love, duty, and quiet resistance.

> Message: The dignity of moral clarity and suffering for the truth.

---

**5. *Children of Men* (2006)**  
*Dystopian, revolutionary, utterly Christian in structure.*  
Set in a future where humanity has become infertile, a man helps shepherd a miraculously pregnant woman to safety. It’s raw, political, and deeply hopeful.

> Message: Even in a dying world, new life can emerge — if we protect it.
