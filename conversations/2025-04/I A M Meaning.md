The letters **I**, **A**, and **M** on some versions of the **Orthodox Cross**—especially in Slavic traditions—are **not standardized** across all depictions, but here’s one common interpretation found in Eastern Orthodox iconography:

- **I**: *Iisus* (Іисус / Иисус) – Jesus  
- **A**: *Alef* or *Adam* (some interpretations vary)  
- **M**: *Maria* (Mary), or alternatively, *Matka* (Mother)

However, in some modern symbolic representations, particularly those stylized for more contemporary use, **"I A M"** may be placed deliberately to echo the phrase **"I AM"**, referencing:

> **Exodus 3:14** – *"I AM WHO I AM."*  
> (In Greek: *Ego eimi ho ōn*, or in Slavonic: *Аз есмь Сый*)

This usage ties into the divine name of God, understood as eternal existence or self-being. It’s a theologically rich phrase found in both Orthodox liturgical tradition and icons of Christ, particularly **Pantocrator** icons where the Greek **Ὁ ὬΝ** ("The One Who Is") appears in Christ's halo.

So if you’re seeing **"I A M"** on an Orthodox cross, it may be a Westernized symbolic addition rather than a traditional Orthodox convention—but it resonates strongly with **God's declaration of His being** and Christ’s identity as **"I AM"** in the Gospel of John (e.g., John 8:58).
