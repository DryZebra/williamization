## Assistant

You're referring to a passage from Lenin's 1913 article **"The Historical Destiny of the Doctrine of Karl Marx"**, where he says:

> *"The theoretical victory of Marxism compelled its enemies to disguise themselves as Marxists. Liberalism, rotten and decaying, sought to rejuvenate itself in the form of socialist opportunism. This opportunism, in its turn, engendered a whole trend of 'revisionism' in the socialist movement."*

You're absolutely right that Lenin isn't just speaking abstractly here—he's responding to **real ideological enemies** within the socialist movement of his time. Here's the context:

---

### **Who was Lenin talking about?**

1. **Eduard Bernstein** – The primary target of “revisionism”:
   - Bernstein was a German Marxist who argued that capitalism was becoming more stable and less exploitative.
   - He denied the necessity of revolution and instead advocated for gradual reform and parliamentary democracy.
   - Lenin saw this as a betrayal—Bernstein *called himself a Marxist* but hollowed out Marxism's revolutionary core.

2. **The Second International and the German SPD**:
   - While formally Marxist, many parties within the Second International had become opportunistic and reformist.
   - They embraced the **language** of Marxism, but in practice abandoned revolutionary struggle.
   - Lenin viewed them as bourgeois liberals in disguise.

3. **Russian "Legal Marxists"**:
   - Intellectuals in Russia who accepted some parts of Marxist economic theory but rejected revolutionary conclusions.
   - They used Marxism to critique czarist Russia, but wanted to blend it with liberalism, not proletarian revolution.
   - Lenin thought they were “wolves in sheep’s clothing”—helping capitalism by neutering Marxism.

---

### **So what did Lenin mean, exactly?**

He meant that:
- Marxism had become so clearly *scientifically correct* and powerful that even its opponents had to *pretend* to be Marxists.
- But they did so to **undermine** it from the inside.
- These enemies weren't monarchists or capitalists—they were **opportunists**, **reformists**, and **liberal socialists**.

This line from Lenin is still used today to describe those who use Marxist rhetoric to advance fundamentally non-Marxist goals—typically to support the status quo rather than abolish it.
