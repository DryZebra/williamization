Great questions—and you're already on the right track. Let’s clear up the real relationship between the **KJV**, **Critical Text**, and **NKJV**, especially from a **Materialist Christian** lens.

---

## **1. The KJV and Its Source Text (Textus Receptus)**
- The KJV (1611) is based on the **Textus Receptus**, a Greek manuscript compiled by Erasmus in the 1500s.
- At the time, this was **the best text available**, but it relied on **a limited set of late Byzantine manuscripts** (mostly from the 12th century or later).
- Some verses and phrases in the KJV are **missing from the oldest manuscripts** we’ve since discovered (like Codex Sinaiticus and Vaticanus, 4th century).

**Examples of questionable additions in KJV:**
- **1 John 5:7 (“the Comma Johanneum”)** – A Trinitarian formula likely inserted later, not found in early manuscripts.
- **Mark 16:9–20** – The longer ending of Mark is absent from the earliest Greek texts.
- **John 7:53–8:11 (the woman caught in adultery)** – Also missing from earliest sources.

But—and this is crucial—**none of these undermine the moral dialectic** of Scripture. These additions are:
- **Doctrinally interesting**, but
- Not **morally transformative** in the way Romans 12:2 or James 2:17 are.

So for someone studying the evolving truth of Scripture, the **KJV is still very solid**, especially since its **language structure preserves tension** and doesn't flatten into modern spiritualism.

---

## **2. The Critical Text (Nestle-Aland / UBS)**
- Modern translations like **ESV, NASB, NIV, NRSVUE** are based on the **Critical Text**, which:
  - Uses **older manuscripts** (some 4th–5th century).
  - Compares thousands of fragments to reconstruct the most accurate reading.
- It removes likely later additions and **leans more toward literal accuracy**.

**So are they in conflict?**  
Not really. They **complement** each other:
- **KJV** preserves **the historical English Protestant voice** and moral structure.
- **Critical Text-based Bibles** offer **clarity, correction, and comparison**—great for study.

---

## **3. What About the NKJV (New King James Version)?**

**Pros:**
- Keeps the **KJV structure and rhythm**.
- Updates archaic grammar and spelling.
- Translates from the **same Textus Receptus base**, but compares with **Critical Text and Majority Text** in footnotes.

**Cons:**
- Still includes verses that the Critical Text sees as additions (like KJV).
- It’s *cleaned up* but **slightly more interpretive** in some places.

**For you?**
- **NKJV is great** as a bridge if you're easing into KJV but want a little more clarity.
- But if you’re already thinking dialectically and are okay with the language, **KJV remains the most ideologically secure**—less touched by modern metaphysical assumptions.
