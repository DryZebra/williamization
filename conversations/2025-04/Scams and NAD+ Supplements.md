There *are* more scams lately—and you’re not imagining it. Let’s break it down.

---

### **Why are there so many scams now?**

1. **Low Barrier to Entry:**
   - Social media, AI-generated websites, and drop-shipping let people spin up scammy products fast with minimal cost.
   - Anyone with Photoshop and ChatGPT can fake testimonials, studies, or “news articles.”

2. **Algorithmic Manipulation:**
   - Platforms like TikTok, Instagram, and even YouTube reward engagement, not truth.
   - Fake health miracles, emotional testimonials, and conspiracy-adjacent health advice spread fast.

3. **Desperation & Burnout:**
   - People are anxious, sick, tired, and looking for control. Supplements offer a sense of hope, especially in a system where healthcare is expensive and confusing.

4. **Science-Washed Marketing:**
   - Marketers are borrowing the language of neuroscience, biochemistry, and longevity science to give fake legitimacy. Phrases like “cellular regeneration,” “bioavailability,” “energy metabolism,” and “mitochondrial health” are used even if there's no real backing.

---

### **What is NAD+? Why is it trending?**

**NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide)** is a coenzyme found in all living cells. It's important in energy production, especially in mitochondria. Here’s the story:

1. **It’s Real… But Mostly in Lab Rats:**
   - NAD+ levels decline with age, and boosting them has shown some promise in extending lifespan in mice.
   - Supplements like *nicotinamide riboside (NR)* or *nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN)* are precursors that supposedly raise NAD+.

2. **The Hype Explosion:**
   - Harvard scientist David Sinclair and others made NAD+ famous in the longevity community.
   - Supplement companies jumped on it. Now it’s sold as a miracle anti-aging pill, even though the science is *very* early.

3. **Regulation is a Joke:**
   - Supplements in the U.S. aren’t regulated like drugs. You can sell sugar pills as long as you don’t make *explicit* medical claims.
   - Many NAD+ supplements are underdosed, overpriced, or totally ineffective.

---

### **So what’s going on with all the supplements?**

It’s a **perfect storm**:
- More people are losing faith in traditional healthcare.
- More people are online, looking for solutions.
- AI and social media have made it easy to *fake legitimacy*.
- Supplement laws in the U.S. are basically: “Just don’t poison people.”
- Capitalism rewards quick cash over long-term trust.

---

If you're seeing new miracle supplements *every week*, you're right—this is a bubble. We’re in a “gold rush” phase for longevity, biohacking, and anti-aging scams.

### **What’s the deal with methylene blue?**

Originally? It’s a **synthetic dye** developed in the late 1800s. Later used:
- As an **antidote** for certain poisonings (like cyanide).
- As a **urinary antiseptic**.
- As a **stain** in biology labs.
- And more recently, as a **nootropic and mitochondrial enhancer** in biohacking circles.

---

### **Why are people hyping it now?**

- **Longevity and Nootropic Claims:**
  - It may improve mitochondrial respiration in cells.
  - It *might* cross the blood-brain barrier and help with cognitive performance.
  - Animal studies show promise, but human evidence is minimal.

- **Visual Appeal:**
  - It literally turns your tongue or urine blue. That’s “viral” content bait—people show it off online, making it look edgy or “secret.”

- **Low Dose = High Hype:**
  - At tiny doses, some studies suggest benefits.
  - But higher doses? *Neurotoxicity* is a real risk.
  - And nobody online knows what they’re doing with dosing.

---

- **Overcomplexity**: “Only smart people will understand this biochemistry chart.”
- **Parasocial influencers**: Trust is shifting from doctors to podcasters and TikTokers.
