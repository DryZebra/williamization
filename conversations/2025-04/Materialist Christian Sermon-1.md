**Sermon: Bread Upon the Hillside**  
**John 6:1 (KJV)** – *After these things Jesus went over the sea of Galilee, which is the sea of Tiberias.*

---

Brothers and sisters, let us pause and open our hearts to the Word.

*After these things…* The Scripture speaks not in isolation but as part of a living story. "These things" are not forgotten. They are carried forward. What Jesus has done, what the people have witnessed, and what history has already shaped—they all remain, flowing like the waters across the sea of Galilee.

Here we see Christ moving—crossing to another shore. Not fleeing, not retreating, but continuing. Always continuing. The sea is not merely a boundary—it is a passage. A change in setting, yes, but also a signal of transformation. Galilee, a place of the people. Tiberias, a Roman name, imposed on the land like so many empires before and after. Even in the names, the tension is clear—Galilee and Tiberias. Hebrew life and imperial force. The people's sea and Caesar’s sea.

Yet Christ does not reject the sea. He uses it. He passes over it. He acknowledges the material reality of empire, geography, and history—but does not halt in the face of them. There is work to be done on the other side.

So the story moves, as all history moves—not in stasis, but in dialectical motion. The people move, the teacher moves, the sea itself moves.

And as Jesus crosses over, so too do we—leaving behind not the lessons of the past, but bringing them with us, transformed, as we follow Him into the next moment.
