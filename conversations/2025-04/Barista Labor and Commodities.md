---

### **1. Is the Barista a Proletarian?**

**Yes**, by Marxist definition. The proletariat is defined not by *what* they produce, but by *how* they survive—**by selling their labor-power for a wage** in a capitalist economy.

A barista works for a wage, does not own the means of production (coffee machine, beans, building, etc.), and is exploited by their employer (the surplus value from their labor is appropriated by the capitalist). Therefore, they are proletarians.

---

### **2. Is the Coffee a Commodity?**

This is where it gets more nuanced.

According to Marx:
> "A commodity is an object outside us, a thing that by its properties satisfies human wants... and is exchanged for something else."

The barista **does not produce** the coffee *as a commodity* in the classical factory sense (i.e., it is not made *ahead of time* for the general market and stored as a tradable good). But that **does not disqualify it as a commodity**.

Instead:
- The **service** of making and handing over that drink, **at the moment of exchange**, is commodified labor.
- The **coffee** handed over *is a commodity*—but a *customized, perishable, real-time one*.

So your instinct is half-right: it's *not* a traditional commodity in the same way as a prepackaged item on a store shelf. But under capitalism, **services can be commodified too**, and barista labor is packaged and sold just like HVAC repair or a haircut.

---

### **3. What About Surplus Value?**

Both the **service labor** (barista, HVAC repair, cleaning) and **manufacturing labor** (factory workers, welders, miners) can produce surplus value, depending on how their labor is organized.

- If a barista makes $100 worth of drinks in a shift, but is paid $50, then $50 is **surplus value** extracted by the capitalist.
- Same with a miner who digs $1,000 worth of coal but is paid $200.

The form differs, but the exploitation relation is the same.

---

The ACP is using a **visual stereotype** of the "real worker"—hard hat, wrench, grit—to appeal to *working-class authenticity*. This tactic is **culturally powerful**, but it risks:

This is what the critic is pointing out: **you don’t have to swing a hammer to be working-class**.
