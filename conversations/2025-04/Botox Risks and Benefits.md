Great questions. Your skepticism is healthy—any drug that paralyzes nerves *should* be treated with caution. Here's a breakdown of the potential **drawbacks**, the **reasons for its growing preventative use**, and the **long-term concerns**:

---

### **Drawbacks of Botox**
1. **Temporary paralysis of muscles**: Botox works by blocking acetylcholine, a neurotransmitter that causes muscles to contract. This can lead to unintended muscle weakness near the injection site (e.g., droopy eyelids, uneven eyebrows, or difficulty swallowing).

2. **Potential for resistance**: Over time, some people develop antibodies to Botox, making it less effective.

3. **Short-term side effects**: These can include bruising, headaches, flu-like symptoms, or pain at the injection site.

4. **Expression loss**: Repeated Botox use in facial muscles can lead to a "frozen" or unnatural look due to reduced movement.

5. **Cost**: It's expensive and needs to be repeated every 3–6 months to maintain effects.

---

### **Why It's Becoming Popular as a Preventative**
- **Prevention of dynamic wrinkles**: These are wrinkles caused by repeated facial expressions (smiling, frowning, etc.). By reducing muscle movement early—often in the late 20s or early 30s—people try to prevent deeper static wrinkles from forming.
- **Social media & beauty trends**: There's growing pressure to maintain a youthful appearance, especially in industries tied to public image.
- **Normalizing of cosmetic procedures**: Botox is increasingly seen as routine maintenance, like getting your hair or nails done.

---

### **Long-Term Concerns**
1. **Muscle atrophy**: Over time, the unused facial muscles can shrink or weaken, potentially changing facial structure or expression.

2. **Dependence**: Once you start Botox, stopping can make aging feel more dramatic, since the treated muscles haven't developed normal creases over time. This can lead to a perceived “rebound aging.”

3. **Changes in surrounding muscles**: Other facial muscles may overcompensate, potentially leading to new lines or imbalances.

4. **Unknowns**: Because Botox became popular for cosmetic use only in the last 20 years or so, we don't have extensive data on 30–40+ years of regular use.

---

### Final Thought
You're absolutely right to be wary of claims that there's *no* long-term impact. While Botox is generally considered safe when administered properly, it does affect the body’s natural function—and doing that repeatedly over decades isn’t risk-free.
