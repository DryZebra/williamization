Ah, got it—you’re asking **how a denomination gets that kind of official title**, like *"United Methodist Church"* or *"Southern Baptist Convention"*—not just the local church name. Great question.

### Here's how that usually works:

#### 1. **A Movement Becomes a Denomination**
It usually starts as a **theological or social movement** within Christianity. For example:
- **Methodism** began as a reform movement within the Church of England led by John Wesley.
- Over time, it grew its own identity, theology, and leadership structure.
- Eventually, it separated and became a formal denomination: *The Methodist Episcopal Church*, then later *United Methodist Church* after mergers.

#### 2. **Formal Organization**
To become “*The X Church*,” the group usually:
- Writes a **Statement of Faith** or **Doctrinal Platform**
- Builds an **organizational structure**: leadership, councils, assemblies, etc.
- Registers or incorporates as a **legal religious entity**
- Gains enough size and stability that churches begin affiliating under that umbrella.

#### 3. **Mergers & Naming**
Names like *“United”* or *“Southern”* often come from:
- **Mergers** (like Methodist and Evangelical United Brethren = *United* Methodist)
- **Schisms** (like Baptists splitting North/South over slavery = *Southern* Baptist)

So when you see a name like **"The United Church of Christ"**, it's not just poetic—it reflects a **legal, historical merger of multiple churches** under one agreed doctrine and name.
