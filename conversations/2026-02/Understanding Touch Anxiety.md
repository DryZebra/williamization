## 1. Touch is not one thing to the brain

We talk about “touch” like it’s a single sense. It’s not.

Your nervous system splits touch into **categories** based on *meaning*, not skin contact:

- **Attachment touch** (bonding, chosen, safe)
- **Role-based touch** (family, authority, obligation)
- **Social-ritual touch** (handshakes, hugs, politeness)
- **Threat touch** (unpredictable, invasive, unwanted)

Your body reacts *very differently* to each.

For you:
- **Attachment touch** with your girlfriend → regulated, safe, meaningful, grounding
- Almost everything else → *category mismatch* → alarm

So when you imagine holding a stranger’s hand, or even a family member’s, your system isn’t thinking “hand.”
It’s thinking:

> *Why is someone entering my body-space without my choosing it?*

That’s not disgust.  
That’s **boundary defense**.

## 2. Why family touch can feel worse than stranger touch

This part surprises people, but it’s important.

Touch from:
- a stranger = *clearly optional, clearly external*
- family = *loaded with expectation, history, obligation*

Your nervous system doesn’t ask:
> “Is this person safe?”

It asks:
> “Am I allowed to say no without consequences?”

With your girlfriend, the answer is yes.  
With family, especially parents and siblings, the answer is often *historically unclear*.

Even if nothing bad happened, **ambiguity alone** is enough to wire avoidance.

So your body says: *don’t risk it*.

That “😬 YIKES” reaction is your nervous system enforcing **autonomy**, not rejecting love.
