# Materialist Christianity Research Prompt

## User

If I was going to write a scientific research paper about materialist Christianity, what would the research prompt be?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Ezra — if you’re writing this as a *scientific research paper*, the prompt cannot be “Is Christianity true?”  

That’s theology.

It also can’t be “Is God real?”  

That’s metaphysics.

A scientific research prompt has to ask:

- What observable phenomenon are we measuring?
- What hypothesis are we testing?
- What counts as evidence?
- What would falsify it?

Materialist Christianity, as you define it, is not about metaphysical belief. It’s about **whether biblical moral structures function as historically emergent survival constraints in human societies**.

So the prompt must test that.

Here are several research-grade versions, depending on discipline.

---

## 1. Sociological / Historical Framing (Most Direct)

**Research Prompt:**

> Do the core moral teachings of early Christianity correspond to empirically observable social survival constraints in complex societies?

**Operationalization:**
- Identify key teachings (enemy-love, humility, voluntary redistribution, sexual restraint, forgiveness, etc.)
- Identify measurable societal variables (trust density, violence rates, institutional stability, economic cooperation, long-term demographic persistence)
- Compare societies with and without those moral constraints
- Examine collapse vs. continuity

**Hypothesis:**
Christian moral teachings reflect descriptive constraints required for durable cooperative systems rather than arbitrary theological decrees.

**Falsifier:**
If societies lacking these constraints demonstrate equal or superior long-term stability under comparable material conditions.

---

## 2. Evolutionary Anthropology Framing

**Research Prompt:**

> Are the ethical prescriptions of early Christianity adaptive mechanisms for stabilizing large-scale, low-kin cooperation?

This situates Christianity inside:
- Cultural group selection
- Costly signaling theory
- Moral norm internalization
- Reciprocal altruism

You’d test whether:
- “Turn the other cheek”
- “Love your enemies”
- “Sell what you have”
- “The meek inherit”

Function to reduce retaliation cycles, signal trustworthiness, and prevent elite capture.

---

## 3. Cognitive Science / Moral Psychology Framing

**Research Prompt:**

> Does internalization of Christian moral narratives increase pro-social behavior and cooperative stability independent of metaphysical belief?

This is important for your system.

You are claiming:

The mechanism works whether or not God exists.

So you would measure:
- Believers
- Cultural Christians
- Secular individuals who internalize similar moral structures

If the structure functions without metaphysical belief, that supports your claim of moral discovery rather than divine imposition.

---

## 4. Political Economy Framing (Most Radical)

**Research Prompt:**

> Did early Christian ethical norms emerge as structural responses to imperial economic stratification in the Roman world?

This connects directly to:
- Class relations
- Debt structures
- Patron-client systems
- Resource extraction

You test whether:
- Enemy-love
- Jubilee-like redistribution
- Anti-hoarding rhetoric
- Radical forgiveness

Were material responses to instability created by Roman imperial extraction.

That would align deeply with your dialectical model.

---

## 5. Your Specific Position (Most Precise to You)

Here is the one that captures your actual thesis:

> Are biblical moral laws better understood as descriptive models of social collapse thresholds rather than prescriptive divine commands?

That is your cleanest scientific framing.

It removes:
- Supernatural claims
- Faith claims
- Metaphysical assertions

And asks:

Are these moral “laws” descriptions of reality like gravity is?

# If I Had to Write the Abstract for You

Your working hypothesis would read something like:

> This study investigates whether core New Testament moral teachings function as historically emergent descriptions of social survival constraints rather than arbitrary theological mandates. By analyzing early Christian texts alongside socio-economic data from the Roman imperial period and cross-cultural cooperative models, the paper tests whether these teachings correlate with empirically observable stability mechanisms in large-scale human societies.

That’s scientific.
That’s falsifiable.
That’s not theology.
That’s not preaching.

That’s research.

Now let me ask you something carefully:

Do you want to prove Christianity is materially true?

Or do you want to prove that Christianity is the most complete moral compression discovered so far in human history?

Those are two very different research designs.

And which one you choose determines everything.
