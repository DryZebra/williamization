The phrase **"you will know them by their fruits"** comes directly from **Jesus** and is found **only in the New Testament**:

---

### 📖 **Matthew 7:15–20 (KJV)**  
> **15** Beware of false prophets, which come to you in sheep's clothing, but inwardly they are ravening wolves.  
> **16** **Ye shall know them by their fruits.** Do men gather grapes of thorns, or figs of thistles?  
> **17** Even so every good tree bringeth forth good fruit; but a corrupt tree bringeth forth evil fruit.  
> **18** A good tree cannot bring forth evil fruit, neither can a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit.  
> **19** Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.  
> **20** **Wherefore by their fruits ye shall know them.**

---

### 📖 **Luke 6:43–45 (KJV)** — similar teaching  
> **43** For a good tree bringeth not forth corrupt fruit; neither doth a corrupt tree bring forth good fruit.  
> **44** **For every tree is known by his own fruit.** For of thorns men do not gather figs, nor of a bramble bush gather they grapes.  
> **45** A good man out of the good treasure of his heart bringeth forth that which is good; and an evil man... that which is evil: for of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaketh.

---

### ❌ Old Testament?  
The exact phrase **"you will know them by their fruits"** does **not appear** in the Old Testament.  
However, the idea of **judging a person or nation by their outcomes, actions, or “fruit”** is present in metaphorical form:

#### 📖 **Isaiah 5:1–7** — Israel as God’s vineyard bearing wild grapes  
> God expected justice but saw bloodshed; righteousness but heard cries of distress.

#### 📖 **Jeremiah 17:10 (KJV)**  
> I the LORD search the heart, I try the reins, even **to give every man according to his ways, and according to the fruit of his doings.**

---

What happens to those who produce bad fruit?

## Assistant

In both the **Old** and **New Testament**, those who produce **bad fruit** (or no fruit) are warned of judgment, destruction, or rejection. “Fruit” symbolizes the **results** of one's life—actions, character, morality, or obedience. Here’s how it's treated:

---

## 🌿 **New Testament – Teachings of Jesus**
### 🔥 Matthew 7:19  
> "**Every tree that bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.**"

- This is part of Jesus’ warning about false prophets.
- The “tree” is the person, and “fire” often symbolizes divine judgment.

---

### 🌿 Matthew 3:10 (John the Baptist)  
> "**And now also the axe is laid unto the root of the trees: therefore every tree which bringeth not forth good fruit is hewn down, and cast into the fire.**"

- A direct warning to the Pharisees.
- Echoes the same judgment: if your life shows no true righteousness, you are cut off.

---

### 🌿 John 15:2,6 (Jesus on the True Vine)  
> "**Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away... If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch... and they are burned.**"

- Being in proximity to Christ isn’t enough—fruit must come.
- Lack of fruit = separation and burning.

---

## 🌾 **Old Testament Parallels**
### 🍇 Isaiah 5:1–7 — *Song of the Vineyard*  
> "He looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth **wild grapes**... I will lay it waste..."

- Israel was God’s vineyard.
- Because it yielded bad fruit (corruption, injustice), God promises to destroy it.

---

### 🍂 Jeremiah 17:10  
> "**I the LORD... give every man... according to the fruit of his doings.**"

- Your fruit determines your reward or judgment.

---

## ✝️ Summary:  
| **Produces Good Fruit** | **Produces Bad Fruit / No Fruit** |
|--------------------------|------------------------------------|
| Blessed (Psalm 1:3)       | Cut down and burned (Matt 7:19)     |
| Abides in Christ (John 15)| Removed from the vine (John 15)    |
| Righteous tree of life    | Rejected, judged, laid waste       |
| Receives reward (Jer 17)  | Receives just punishment (Jer 17)  |

---

This teaching is central to biblical moral logic: **Fruit reveals truth**. Not words, not appearances—**outcomes**. In Materialist Christianity, this maps directly to **material consequences** of belief and action.
