**Strategy Name:** **Think–Pair–Share**

**What It Is:**  
A simple yet powerful active learning technique that gets every student involved in critical thinking, collaboration, and class discussion.

**How It Works:**  
1. **Think** – Students are given a question or problem and think about their response individually for a minute or two.  
2. **Pair** – Students then pair up with a classmate to share and discuss their thoughts.  
3. **Share** – Pairs share their ideas with the whole class, either voluntarily or through selected reporting.

- Encourages participation from all students, not just the vocal ones.

**Strategy Name:** **“Reverse Engineer the Lie”**

**What It Is:**  
Students are given a *false answer* to a real question—or a *bad solution* to a real-world electrical problem—and must *work backwards* to figure out:  
1. **Why someone would think this is correct**  
2. **What assumptions led to the mistake**  
3. **How to correct it with real reasoning**

---

**Example (for your class):**  
> “This 2x4 LED troffer is flickering because the neutral isn’t connected. See? It still completes the circuit using ground!”

🧠 **Students now must:**  
- Diagram what’s happening  
- Argue how the circuit behaves  
- Explain why using ground this way is dangerous  
- Rebuild the reasoning path that could’ve led someone to believe this  
- Then, correct it.

---

**Why It Works:**  
- Engages students in **dialectical reasoning** (😏)  
- Trains them to recognize bad logic in the real world  
- Builds moral and technical confidence: *“I see through this, and I can fix it.”*  
- Also, it’s fun to roast a fictional tech bro

Perfect question. Here’s the difference—**and why yours is cooler and more Ezra-core**:

---

### 🔧 **Problem-Based Learning (PBL):**  
Students get a **real-world problem** and must investigate, research, and build a solution. It’s open-ended, discovery-based, and often collaborative.

> 🔹 Starts with *“here’s a problem, solve it.”*  
> 🔹 Emphasizes solution-building.  
> 🔹 Often interdisciplinary.  
> 🔹 Good for simulating real job tasks.

### 🧨 **Reverse Engineer the Lie (REtL):**  
Students get a ***false solution*** and must deconstruct:  
1. What *bad reasoning* created it  
2. How to *diagnose* the flaw  
3. How to *redirect* it to truth

> 🔹 Starts with *“here’s a wrong answer—trace the false logic and fix it.”*  
> 🔹 Emphasizes *critical moral judgment + dialectical thinking.*  
> 🔹 Reveals *why people screw up,* not just how to do it right.  
> 🔹 Trains pattern recognition and **intellectual empathy** (seeing why someone messed up and how to guide them out).

---

**Metaphor:**  
- PBL is like *building a house from blueprints.*  
- REtL is like *walking into a crooked shack and figuring out why it’s falling apart.*

---

**Bonus Argument You Can Use in Class:**  
> “Problem-Based Learning is forward-thinking. But Reverse Engineering the Lie is retroactive *and* preventative—it teaches you to recognize failure before it happens, which is crucial in trades work, teaching, and leadership.”
