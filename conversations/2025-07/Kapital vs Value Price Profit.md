## 🧠 CONTEXT FIRST: Why “Value, Price and Profit” Exists

- Delivered at the **General Council of the First International**, June 1865.
- Marx was *asked* to respond to John Weston, who claimed that wage increases simply raise prices, so workers shouldn’t demand them.
- Marx kept his response **brief**, **political**, and **non-polemical**, for the benefit of workers, not economists.

He **explicitly** told Engels it was a simplification:

> “I have not made use of the opportunity to set forth the general principles of political economy in their full scope, but have kept strictly to the subject of discussion.”

Meanwhile, **Capital Volume I** was the culmination of **20 years** of rigorous economic research, dialectical refinement, and a total critique of bourgeois political economy.

---

## 🔥 STRUCTURAL DEVIATIONS: Side-by-Side

---

### 1. **The Labor Theory of Value**
---

**In “Value, Price and Profit”**:
- Marx defends a *classical labor theory of value* close to Ricardo’s:
  
  > “The value of a commodity is determined by the quantity of labor expended on it.”

- He refers often to the “natural price” of labor and commodities.
- His tone is **linear** and **quantitative**: Labor creates value, surplus is what’s taken beyond this.

**In Capital Vol. I**:
- Value is **not** simply “labor,” but **socially necessary labor time**—a historical and relational category.
- He introduces **commodity fetishism**—how relations between people appear as relations between things.
- Value is not a *thing*, but a **form of social relation** embedded in commodity exchange.

> “A commodity is a mysterious thing... the social character of men's labor appears to them as an objective characteristic of the products of labor themselves.” (*Capital*, Ch. 1)

🔥 **KEY DEVIATION**:
- **VPP treats value as intrinsic. Capital treats it as socially mediated.**
- VPP is **pre-dialectical**. Capital is a dialectical **science of appearances**.

---

### 2. **Wages and Exploitation**
---

**In “Value, Price and Profit”**:
- Wages are explained as the price of labor power.
- Marx argues against the “iron law of wages” and in favor of fighting for wage increases.

> “The value of labor is determined by the value of the necessaries required to produce, develop, maintain, and perpetuate the laboring power.”

- Surplus value is **mentioned**, but **not developed** as a structural law of capital.

**In Capital Vol. I**:
- Wages are shown to **obscure** exploitation.
- He introduces the category of **variable capital**: capital advanced to buy labor-power, which produces more value than it costs.
- Exploitation is **structural**, not merely moral.

> “Capital is dead labor, which, vampire-like, lives only by sucking living labor, and lives the more, the more labor it sucks.” (Ch. 10)

🔥 **KEY DEVIATION**:
- VPP: Workers produce more than they’re paid. ✔️
- *Capital*: **Exploitation is built into the circuit of capital itself**—value can only expand by consuming labor-power. It’s not *just* theft, it’s **systematic reproduction of capital through contradiction.**

---

### 3. **Price**
---

**In “Value, Price and Profit”**:
- Price = value plus/minus market fluctuations.
- Emphasis is placed on **price deviation from value** due to supply and demand.

> “The market price may be above or below this value, but the average price tends always to coincide with the value.”

**In Capital Vol. I and III**:
- Marx radically deepens this:
  - **Price of production** in Volume III explains how capital redistributes value via average rates of profit.
  - *Prices do not reflect value directly*, especially across different industries.
  - The contradiction between **value creation** and **price formation** is **dialectical**, not merely fluctuation.

🔥 **KEY DEVIATION**:
- VPP: Value determines price, with some wiggle room. ✔️
- Capital: **Price is a refracted form**, distorted by the movement of total social capital and profit equalization. It's not a surface expression of value.

---

### 4. **Surplus Value**
---

**In “Value, Price and Profit”**:
- Barebones intro: surplus value = unpaid labor.
- Very little structure or breakdown.

**In Capital Vol. I**:
- Surplus value is the **central dialectic** of capital.
- Divided into:
  - **Absolute surplus value** (via extension of working day)
  - **Relative surplus value** (via productivity increase and reduction of necessary labor time)
  - **Primitive accumulation** (pre-history of capital)

🔥 **KEY DEVIATION**:
- VPP: Surplus value is an empirical observation. ✔️
- *Capital*: It is the *essence* of capitalist production. It structures time, technology, class struggle, and reproduction.

---

### 5. **Dialectics and Method**
---

**In “Value, Price and Profit”**:
- No dialectics. It’s a **polemic in lecture format**.
- Static categories.

**In Capital Vol. I**:
- Hegelian dialectic throughout:
  - Commodity → Exchange → Money → Capital → Labor → Surplus → Accumulation → Crisis
- Each category **evolves through contradiction** and historical necessity.

🔥 **KEY DEVIATION**:
- VPP: Political economy in plain language.
- Capital: **Critique of political economy as a mystification of social relations.**

---

## 🧱 CONCLUSION

Anyone who treats *Value, Price and Profit* as Marx's definitive theory is doing the following:

- Ignoring **two decades of development** between Marx’s early notes and *Capital*.
- Confusing a **political speech** with a **scientific treatise**.
- Missing **value-form theory**, **fetishism**, **surplus value**, **the reproduction of capital**, and **dialectical method**—all of which are *absent* from VPP but central to *Capital*.
