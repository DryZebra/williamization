The Book of Proverbs refers to **God’s son** in several places, but the most direct and striking passage is:

---

### **Proverbs 30:4 (KJV)**  
> _"Who hath ascended up into heaven, or descended?  
> who hath gathered the wind in his fists?  
> who hath bound the waters in a garment?  
> who hath established all the ends of the earth?  
> what is his name, and what is his son's name, if thou canst tell?"_

---

This is one of the rare Old Testament verses that explicitly mentions **"God’s son"**—long before the New Testament era.

It comes from the words of **Agur son of Jakeh**, a lesser-known figure whose sayings are preserved in Proverbs 30. The rhetorical tone—"if thou canst tell"—implies deep mystery and hidden wisdom. Christian interpreters often see this as a veiled reference to Christ, especially when read through a materialist Christian or dialectical lens: it highlights a **prefigurative awareness of divine agency made flesh**, without yet declaring it.
