## Assistant

Here is a direct reference from *Capital, Volume I* by Karl Marx where he distinguishes **productive** and **unproductive** labor:

---

### 📘 **Marx, *Capital* Vol I, Chapter 16: "Absolute and Relative Surplus-Value"**

> “**Productive labour**, in its meaning for capitalist production, is **labour which produces surplus-value** for the capitalist, or which serves the self-expansion of capital. Hence **it is not the labour which is performed for the satisfaction of the personal needs of the worker**, but **that which is exchanged against capital** and **reproduces capital**.”

> “The same labour may be productive or unproductive depending on whether it is **exchanged against money as money, or money as capital**. Labour which is directly exchanged with revenue is **unproductive**.”

---

### ✅ Summary:
- **Productive labor** = labor that creates **surplus value** for capital (i.e. exchanged for capital and results in profit).
- **Unproductive labor** = labor that is exchanged directly for **revenue or consumption**, not for the sake of capital expansion (e.g. a servant, teacher hired by a private household, or an actor hired for a show funded by ticket sales).

---

If you're using *Capital Volume I* in the **Penguin** or **MECW** edition, this appears in:

- **Penguin edition (trans. Ben Fowkes)**: p. 644–645
- **MECW Volume 35**: near the end of Chapter 16
