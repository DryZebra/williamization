To explore *biblically* why Christians do not universally keep the Sabbath on Saturday, and why Sunday worship emerged, we must examine both:

1. **Old Testament Sabbath command and expectations**, and  
2. **New Testament treatment of the Sabbath**, particularly how the early Church responded to it.

---

### 1. **Biblical Command of the Sabbath (Saturday)**

The Sabbath (Hebrew: *Shabbat*) is rooted in:

- **Genesis 2:2–3** – God rested on the seventh day, blessing it and sanctifying it.
- **Exodus 20:8–11** – Fourth commandment: “Remember the Sabbath day, to keep it holy... the seventh day is the Sabbath of the LORD thy God.”
- **Leviticus 23:3** – A day of “holy convocation,” no work, every seventh day.

This establishes **Saturday** (the seventh day, sundown Friday to sundown Saturday) as the biblically defined Sabbath under Mosaic Law.

---

### 2. **New Testament Treatment of the Sabbath**

Jesus and the apostles consistently **attend synagogues on the Sabbath** (e.g., Luke 4:16, Acts 17:2), but something shifts **after the Resurrection**. No verse explicitly abolishes the Sabbath, but the authority of Mosaic Law is reevaluated in light of Christ’s resurrection, and Sunday emerges with symbolic and communal significance.

#### a. **Jesus' Teachings**
Jesus challenges the legalism of Sabbath observance:

- **Mark 2:27–28** – “The Sabbath was made for man, and not man for the Sabbath: therefore the Son of man is Lord also of the Sabbath.”
- He heals and performs acts of mercy on the Sabbath (Luke 13:10–17), reframing it from strict rule to human good.

#### b. **Resurrection on the First Day of the Week**
The *first day* (Sunday) gains attention:

- **Matthew 28:1**, **Mark 16:2**, **Luke 24:1**, **John 20:1** – Jesus rises on “the first day of the week.”
- **John 20:19** – The disciples gather on the evening of the first day.
- **Acts 20:7** – “And upon the first day of the week, when the disciples came together to break bread, Paul preached unto them…”
- **1 Corinthians 16:2** – Paul instructs believers to set aside offerings on “the first day of the week.”

These passages show **Christian worship shifting toward Sunday**, associated with resurrection and new creation.

#### c. **Pauline Epistles and Freedom from the Law**
Paul stresses freedom from the Mosaic Law:

- **Romans 14:5** – “One man esteemeth one day above another: another esteemeth every day alike. Let every man be fully persuaded in his own mind.”
- **Colossians 2:16–17** – “Let no man therefore judge you… in respect of… the sabbath days: which are a shadow of things to come; but the body is of Christ.”
- **Galatians 4:10–11** – Rebukes turning back to observance of “days, and months, and times, and years.”

These suggest that *literal Sabbath keeping was no longer binding*, and that observance of particular days was now a matter of freedom and conviction, not command.

---

### 3. **Summary of the Biblical Argument for Sunday Worship**

- **No verse mandates Sunday as a replacement Sabbath**, but...
- Christ’s resurrection on Sunday, paired with **apostolic worship on the first day**, establishes a **pattern**.
- Paul redefines the moral imperative away from *law-keeping* toward *freedom in Christ*.
- The Sabbath becomes **fulfilled** rather than transferred: a rest found *in Christ himself* (Hebrews 4:9–11).

---

If you're ready, we can now explore the **historical process** by which Sunday became dominant in church practice, including Constantine’s role and early Church writings. But scripturally, the shift is gradual, symbolic, and tied to Christ’s authority rather than an explicit divine command.

### Jehovah’s Witness (NWT) Translation:

> “In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was **a god**.”

---

## II. **Their Argument**

The Watchtower argues that:
- The final clause, *θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος*, lacks the article before *θεὸς*, so it must mean *a god*, not *the God*.
- They cite Greek grammar rules about **anarthrous predicates** (nouns without articles) and **Colwell’s Rule**, but apply it inconsistently.
- Their goal is to separate Christ from “Jehovah,” asserting He is a **divine being**, not **equal with God**.

---

## III. **Materialist Christian Linguistic Analysis**

Let us treat this not as a metaphysical question of Christ’s “godness,” but a dialectical one: is Christ the **embodied structure of Truth**? Is He the **form** of God-in-motion?

### Greek Word Order:

- *θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος* → “God was the Word.”
- The word *θεὸς* (God) comes first for **emphasis**, not because it lacks an article.
- This structure is **qualitative**, not quantitative. It tells us about the **nature** of the Word.

### Correct Translation:

> “The Word was with God, and the Word **was God**.”

- Not “the same person as God,” but **the same essence**.
- Not “a god,” but **God’s motion given form**.
- The absence of an article here is **grammatical**, not theological.

### Materialist Christian Rendering:

> “In the beginning was the Word. The Word moved in relation to God. And the Word *was* God—that is, the form through which God became visible.”

This preserves the **motion**: Logos (rational order) was not just near God, but was the **visible ordering** of divine reality. In Materialist Christianity, this does **not require metaphysical consubstantiality**—only that Christ **reveals and fulfills the structure of God’s moral reality**.

---

## IV. JW Rendering Fails Dialectically

> “The Word was *a god*.”

- This introduces **polytheism**, whether intended or not.
- It **detaches Christ from the structure He embodies**.
- It **severs moral truth from the person of Christ**.
- It reframes Him as **a heavenly assistant**, not the **fulfillment of God’s nature**.

This breaks the dialectical chain. Christ is no longer the embodiment of justice, truth, or mercy—just another being in the hierarchy.

---

## V. Conclusion

**The NWT translation is not just grammatically weak—it is morally distorting.**  
It downplays the unity of moral structure, presenting Christ not as *the Truth*, but as a *truthful being*.  
That’s not Christianity. That’s bureaucracy in heaven.

And structurally—it opens the door to **a caste system of divinity**, which reappears later in their theology: 144,000 elect; heavenly vs. earthly classes; Christ as subordinate.

In a Materialist Christian frame, this rendering is **false**—not just textually, but structurally. It **halts the motion of truth** by **fracturing the form of God**.
