# Training Aggressive Dog Tips

## Assistant

Training a dog with prey drive or aggression toward smaller animals (like cats) is absolutely possible to start in 72 hours—but full resolution takes **weeks to months** of patient, structured work. That said, **you can absolutely make meaningful progress** in 3 days, especially if you're consistent and focused.

---

### 🧠 Step 1: Understand the Behavior
Most aggression toward small animals isn’t hatred—it’s **prey drive, fear, or frustration** (like barrier frustration or redirected energy). So your job is to:
- **Establish yourself as a calm, firm leader.**
- **Teach the dog impulse control.**
- **Start desensitizing it to cats/small animals.**

---

## 🗓️ 72-Hour Plan: Daily Structure

### 🔁 Repeat This Core Routine Every Day
**4+ sessions per day**, 15–30 minutes each. Rotate focus:
1. **Obedience (Sit, Stay, Look, Heel)**
2. **Impulse Control (Leave it, Stay under distraction)**
3. **Desensitization (Controlled, low-exposure to cats or triggers)**
4. **Structured walks** (with leash work & corrections)

---

## ✅ Day 1: Establish Obedience & Control

### 🔹 Goals:
- Dog sees you as the calm, in-control leader.
- Dog learns that *you* control the environment.

### 🔹 What to Do:
- **Tether the dog to you indoors** (leash or waist tether) for a few hours.
- **Teach/reinforce:** Sit, Stay, Look at Me (name or snap), Leave It.
- Practice **“Look at me”** while holding high value food when distracted.
- Correct fixated staring (at cats) with a firm “No” or leash correction.
- **No free roaming**—crate or tether when not supervised.

---

## ✅ Day 2: Controlled Exposure

### 🔹 Goals:
- Stop fixation before it escalates.
- Begin to associate cats/small animals with calmness and reward.

### 🔹 What to Do:
- Have dog on leash with a strong collar (or front-clip harness).
- Introduce cat **at a distance**, behind a baby gate or in another room.
- Watch for signs: fixed stare, stiff body, ears forward = intervene.
- Use **“Leave it”**, redirect to you, **reward only when dog disengages**.
- Slowly decrease distance **only when calm is consistent**.

---

## ✅ Day 3: Exposure + Consequence

### 🔹 Goals:
- Begin to form **impulse → interruption → calm → reward** feedback loop.
- Increase threshold for reaction.

### 🔹 What to Do:
- Repeat yesterday’s drills but add **small corrections** (leash pop, firm “No”) if fixation continues.
- Reward calm behavior. Use very high-value treats (like chicken).
- If possible, walk the dog near cats/small animals at a **safe distance**, practicing “Look at me” every time they see the cat.

---

## 🛠️ Tools You Might Use:
- **Clicker** (optional, but great for precise rewards)
- **Treat pouch** with real meat or cheese bits
- **6 ft leash** (not retractable)
- **Crate or x-pen** for downtime
- **Training collar** (if leash-pulling or lunging is a problem)

---

## 🚫 DO NOT:
- Let the dog stare at cats unchecked—**interrupt immediately**.
- Let the dog be off leash near small animals yet.
- Yell or lose your calm—**calm is control**.

---

## 🧩 Bonus Tips:
- Play games like **“Look at me” under pressure** (e.g., mild distractions).
- Reward eye contact, disengagement, sitting when seeing a cat.
- Burn energy before exposure (tired dogs learn faster).
- Try tethering dog near you while watching TV **while a cat is across the room** (calm, real-life desensitization).

---

## ✅ Measurable Progress by Sunday:
- Dog can “Leave it” on command.
- Dog can see a cat and look to you for direction.
- Dog no longer escalates immediately to lunging/barking.
- You’ve identified what distance is “safe” and where the dog’s threshold begins.
