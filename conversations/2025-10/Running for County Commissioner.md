## Big picture: what it means to run for county commission

To run effectively, you need to treat this not just as a personality contest but as a contest over the state’s role, the burdens and privileges of local government, and how money, capital, and class power flow at the county level.

Key things you’ll have to reckon with:

- **Budget control and revenue sources**. The county commission is not just symbolic — it often sets property tax rates, approves budgets, decides infrastructure spending, and influences which areas are prioritized (roads, social services, public safety, land use).  
- **Interlocking interests**. Developers, real estate capital, state agencies, growth coalitions, corporate actors will push hard. You’ll need to understand their levers — permitting, zoning, subsidies, procurement.  
- **Administrative structure**. The commission doesn’t run everything directly — there is usually a county manager, department heads, bureaucracies. The commission’s power is in oversight, appointments, authorization, and political direction.  
- **Legal and procedural constraints**. Counties are creatures of state law. Your powers are bounded by what the state and constitution allow. You’ll need to know what you *can’t* do as much as what you can.  
- **Political optics and ground power**. You need to mobilize people, align with social movements, manage opposition, and run a campaign that speaks to working people.  

The struggle will often be: getting enough resources to do the things you want (roads, social services, environmental protection, equitable development) while resisting pressure from capital interests who want to steer development in their direction.

So your campaign must *not* be vague. You need to show not just “I’ll fix stuff” but *which stuff, by what means, with whose money, and whose interests served*.

## From a class-perspective: pitfalls & strategy

Because your worldview is class analysis, here are extra lessons:

- **Don’t replicate bourgeois politics**. Don’t just talk “goody programs” that center on charity or spectacle. Show how your county office will shift power — e.g. how to tax land better, limit extraction by private capital, democratize planning, reallocate revenue to underfunded neighborhoods.  
- **Stay connected to mass base**. Many local officeholders lose touch with real working people. Keep listening sessions, meet in neighborhoods, bring working class voices into your campaign.  
- **Avoid corruption traps**. Building a campaign infrastructure with money from real estate/developers is dangerous: you’ll be beholden. Aim for small-donor funding if possible, or safeguards.  
- **Link your county platform to state and national issues**. Because some of your leverage is via cooperation or clash with state government (e.g. transportation, mandates, grants).  
- **Plan for governance, not just campaigning**. You’ll need to choose your department heads, understand proposals deeply, and resist capture by bureaucrats or contractors.  
- **Be ready to defend, not just pitch**. Opponents will misrepresent, accuse, litigate. You’ll need a rapid response team, clarity on your record, iron discipline.
