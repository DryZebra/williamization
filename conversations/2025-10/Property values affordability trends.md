# Property values affordability trends

## User

Is there a single year in American history where property values exclusively trended downwards and became more affordable?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Good question. The short answer is: **no single year in American history has seen property values fall everywhere and become more affordable across the board**.  

Here’s why:

---

### 1. Property values move unevenly
Housing markets are local. Even during national downturns, some regions still go up. For example:
- **The Great Depression (1930s):** National home prices fell sharply, but not every county or city saw continuous decline.
- **Savings & Loan Crisis (late 1980s–early 1990s):** Certain metro areas like Texas and New England were hit, while others kept rising.
- **Great Recession (2007–2012):** This is the closest to what you’re asking. U.S. median home prices fell year after year from about 2007–2011. But even then, not all areas went down—some places (like parts of North Dakota due to the oil boom) saw prices increase.

---

### 2. Affordability isn’t the same as lower prices
Even in years when home values fell nationally:
- **Unemployment rose** (1930s, 2008–2009), so fewer people could afford homes.
- **Credit tightened**, so even cheaper houses weren’t more “affordable” in practice.
- Inflation also complicates the picture—prices might fall in dollar terms, but interest rates or wages change affordability.

---

### 3. The closest example
The **Great Recession (2008–2011)** is the clearest nationwide sustained downturn:
- **Case-Shiller U.S. National Home Price Index** shows consistent year-over-year declines for several years.  
- Median home prices nationally dropped about **30%** from peak (2006) to trough (2011).  
- But not every state trended down each year—e.g., much of Texas remained relatively stable.

---

✅ **Conclusion:**  
There has never been a year where *everywhere* in America property values fell and homes became more affordable in practice. The **closest period was 2008–2011**, but even then, local exceptions existed, and affordability wasn’t guaranteed because of job losses and credit tightening.
