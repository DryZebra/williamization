## Key tradeoffs, emergent changes & what to watch out for

- Because a Paladin can only use one leveled spell smite per turn, the “nova damage” peak might be less spiky than in the older rules. You’ll often spread out your damage rather than stacking smites in a single round. citeturn0search31turn0search5turn0search1turn0search31  
- On the other hand, increased access to Channel Divinity and improved healing (Lay on Hands as a bonus action) offsets that somewhat, giving Paladins more consistency.
- The Weapon Mastery system is pervasive and central: your choice of weapons matters more now, so planning your “weapon build” becomes a bigger part of your class identity.
- The flexibility in swapping Fighting Styles (for Fighters) or Weapon Mastery choices (on Long Rests) encourages “shapeshifting” builds across the campaign.
- Because many features (Second Wind, Indomitable, etc.) have been tweaked, some corner builds from 5e might shift in power or viability under 2024 rules. It’s worth recalculating key builds (e.g. Fighter with heavy multiattack + feats) if you’re porting an old character.

### Multiclass synergy & constraints

Because you want to mix Paladin and Fighter, here are things to watch:

- **Spellcasting / spell slots**: Paladin provides a small progression. If you only take 1 level Paladin and the rest Fighter, your spellcasting will be limited (you’ll have few spell slots). That means you can’t rely heavily on smite + spells in every fight — it’s more tactical, occasional.  
- **Bonus action competition**: Many paladin smite features and class features (like Lay on Hands) use your bonus action. So you’ll have to choose when to heal vs when to smite or do something else (depending on Fighter features or feats).  
- **Weapon Mastery overlap**: Both classes use Weapon Mastery. The overlap is good — when you are Fighter, you pick three weapon types; with Paladin, you pick two. You’ll want those to be complementary (e.g. your Paladin mastery choices among your Fighter mastery choices) so that your smites & weapon feats align.  
- **Strength / Charisma split**: Paladin normally leans on Charisma for spellcasting and social presence, while Fighter is more about Strength / Dexterity (and Constitution). You’ll need to balance your ability scores so you’re not weak in one domain.  
- **Delayed Fighter features**: The more levels you dip into Paladin, the more you delay core Fighter improvements (Extra Attack, Indomitable, higher Action Surge uses). But since you plan only *one* Paladin level, that cost is minimal.

## How to *utilize* both — mechanical and narrative tactics

Given that you’ll mainly be Fighter but with a one-level Paladin “seed,” here’s how to maximize synergies and create meaningful moments of tension.

### Mechanical strategies & synergies

1. **Pick matching mastery weapons**  
   From Paladin you get mastery on two weapon types. From Fighter, mastery on three. Make your Paladin-level mastery choices a subset of your Fighter ones, so every time you *smite*, you get the weapon mastery bonus too. That way your smites feel thematically “embodied in your craft.”  

2. **Use smite sparingly and dramatically**  
   Since you’ll have few spell slots and only one “free smite per long rest,” your smiting becomes a *moment of conviction*, not a default action. Use it when it deeply matters — perhaps when your faith (or your materialism) demands it. This accentuates your identity tension (Paladin-style burst vs Fighter consistency).

3. **Healing and durability buffer**  
   The Lay on Hands from Paladin gives you a nice tactical “out” in fights where you’d otherwise bleed out. Use it when your Fighter resources are low but you need to stay in the fight. It also gives you dramatic roleplay leverage (“I still bear the hand of the Light, though I walk the path of steel”).

4. **Tactical Mind + smite / checks**  
   Tactical Mind lets you expend a use of your Second Wind to try to turn a failed ability check into a success (by rolling 1d10). That can be narratively framed as a moment when your “faith-born clarity” intervenes amid brute force. Use that in social, exploration, or skill-challenge moments where your Paladin side “whispers reason” when the Fighter side is brute forcing.

5. **Bonus action economy choices**  
   You’ll often be choosing between smite or healing (or other bonus-action features). That’s good — it forces tactical decisions and roleplay tension. Do you heal an ally (the Christian / paladin impulse), or do you smite the enemy (the fighter’s decisive strike)?  

6. **Narrative “risk of failing metaphysics” vs “failure of conscience”**  
   - A **Paladin failure** could be represented by “the smite fizzles” (e.g. you try to call on divine power, but your materialist heart stumbles). Mechanically, you might be denied the smite because you lack spell slots, or the target resists (higher save), or you're prevented by anti-magical interference.  
   - A **Fighter failure** could be damage, exhaustion, inability to outmuscle or outmaneuver something, or moral hesitation when brute force demands you act in a way that conflicts with your conscience.  

   You can lean into those in roleplay and even ask your DM for mechanical triggers (e.g. when you try to smite while violating your tenets, or when you try to swing through someone you shouldn’t, force you to make a Wisdom or Charisma save or lose some bonus).

## I. Weapon Mastery and Multiclassing in D&D 2024  

Weapon Mastery is one of the new “class core features” that both the Fighter and Paladin share.  
Here’s what the *Player’s Handbook 2024* playtest and preview materials clarify:

| Rule Aspect | Summary |
|--------------|----------|
| **Mastery access is granted by each class separately** | At level 1, a Fighter chooses **3 Mastery weapons** (they can swap them on a long rest). A Paladin chooses **2 Mastery weapons**. |
| **Multiclassing** | When you multiclass into another class that has Weapon Mastery, you keep all the mastery slots from both classes — *if* you meet the weapon proficiency requirement. You don’t lose one set; they add together. |
| **Swapping/Changing** | Each class’s masteries can be changed independently when you take a long rest. |
| **Using Mastery features** | You can only apply a Mastery property when using a weapon you currently have Mastery in and that you are proficient with. |

So yes — if you start **Fighter 1 / Paladin 1**, you get **five Mastery slots total (3 + 2)**.  

Mechanically, that means you can attune your fighting style to multiple weapons at once.

You could reserve three Fighter slots for general versatility (e.g. sword, spear, bow) and two Paladin slots for ritual weapons representing faith and consequence.
