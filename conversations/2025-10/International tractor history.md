A large portion of those 1950s-era tractors were built under IH’s “row-crop” or general-purpose category (for farms with row crops like corn, cotton, etc). The idea was to build a tractor that could **cultivate**, **plough**, and perform other tasks across different crops rather than just one narrow purpose. ### “Culti-Vision” and the Offset Design  
One of the distinctive design features of many IH row-crop tractors was their emphasis on crop-row visibility and cultivator implement mounting, for which IH used the term **“Culti-Vision.”** citeturn1search4turn1search1turn1search6  
Here’s how that was realized:  
- On smaller models (for example the Farmall A), the engine and drive train were **offset to the left** (from the operator’s perspective) so that the driver could have better forward-and-downward visibility of the ground, especially when cultivating between rows. citeturn1search2  
- The seat/steering wheel might be placed more to the right side so the operator has a clearer view of the row and mounted implements. citeturn1search1  
- Wide-track adjustable rear wheels (and sometimes front track options) to accommodate different row spacings. citeturn1search4

### Adaptable Implements / Built for Versatility  
Some other design features built into the tractors of that era to support multi-task farm use:  
- PTO (power take-off) and belt pulleys for stationary/auxiliary power.  
- Mounting points for cultivators, ploughs, mowers.  
- Adjustable wheel width / track to match row spacing or specific crops.  
- Stable, rugged construction to last for decades (many of these tractors still exist today).
