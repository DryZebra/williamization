## Assistant

The form you’re describing is generally called **“Holocaust trivialization”** or **“Holocaust relativization.”**  

Here’s the distinction:  

- **Holocaust trivialization** – when someone compares the Holocaust to something else (e.g. abortion, COVID mandates, animal rights, etc.) in a way that *diminishes the Holocaust itself.* Instead of respecting the comparison partner, the Holocaust is made to look less unique, less horrific, or exaggerated.  
- **Holocaust relativization** – a broader category where people put the Holocaust “in context” with other atrocities, often suggesting it was just one event among many, thereby weakening its moral or historical weight.  
- **Holocaust inversion** – a related denial form where perpetrators are recast as victims (e.g., claiming Israelis are the “new Nazis”), which both diminishes the Holocaust and distorts history.  

So the exact form you’re asking about—where the comparison **reduces the gravity of the Holocaust rather than elevating the other event**—falls under **Holocaust trivialization** (sometimes described as a subtype of relativization).
