## **Independent Study: Practical Geometry of Conduit Bending**

### **Objective**
To apply trigonometric principles and field measurement techniques to the fabrication of a precision 3-bend saddle and offset bend in ¾″ EMT, demonstrating understanding of bend calculations, centerline radius, and developed length.

---

### **Learning Outcomes**
By completing this assignment, the student will:
- Calculate developed length using trigonometric relationships.  
- Identify critical bend reference points (stub, back-to-back, and center mark).  
- Produce accurate 10°–22.5°–45° compound offsets suitable for practical field installation.  
- Evaluate the relationship between theoretical and measured results.

---

### **Assignment Tasks**
1. **Research Phase**  
   - Read NEC Chapter 9, Table 4 (conduit dimensions) and Annex C (conduit fill).  
   - Review bending formulas:  
     - *Shrink = Offset × Multiplier × (1 – cosθ)*  
     - *Developed Length = Radius × Angle × π / 180*

2. **Planning Phase**  
   - Choose a *mock installation scenario* (e.g., conduit from wall box to overhead junction box offsetting around a 6″ obstruction).  
   - Draw your intended layout to scale with all dimensions labeled.

3. **Fabrication Phase**  
   - Bend a **10°–22.5°–45° saddle** in ¾″ EMT using hand bender or mechanical bender.  
   - Document:
     - Angle settings used  
     - Measured take-up and shrink  
     - Any correction factors observed

4. **Analysis Phase**  
   - Compare theoretical bend marks vs. measured results (±⅛″ tolerance goal).  
   - Explain in writing why any difference occurred (tool calibration, material spring-back, or measuring error).

5. **Submission Requirements**
   - Diagram of intended layout (hand-drawn or digital).  
   - Calculations sheet (offset, take-up, shrink).  
   - Two photos: layout before bending and finished conduit against a straightedge.  
   - One-page reflection titled *“The Geometry of Precision”* describing what was learned.
