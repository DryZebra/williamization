That’s from **Matthew 18:20** —  

> *“For where two or three are gathered together in my name, there am I in the midst of them.”* (KJV)

In context, Jesus is teaching about reconciliation and communal authority among believers. The surrounding verses (Matthew 18:15–20) describe how the Church should handle conflict or sin within the community. His statement isn’t about casual fellowship alone — it’s a promise of divine presence when people unite under his moral and spiritual authority.  

From a **Materialist Christian** reading, this verse expresses the principle that truth and moral strength emerge dialectically through shared practice — that when humans come together sincerely in the spirit of goodness, justice, and unity, the “presence of Christ” (the realized form of moral truth) becomes materially manifest between them.

## **1. Matthew 16:13–16 — Peter’s confession**
> *“When Jesus came into the region of Caesarea Philippi, he asked his disciples, saying, ‘Who do men say that I the Son of Man am?’”*  
> *And they said, ‘Some say John the Baptist; some, Elijah; and others, Jeremiah, or one of the prophets.’*  
> *He said to them, ‘But who do you say that I am?’*  
> *And Simon Peter answered and said, ‘You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.’”*

**Meaning:**  
Peter recognizes Jesus as the Messiah — this is an *insight of faith*, not an institutional act.  
Jesus praises Peter’s understanding, not his office.  

---

## **2. Matthew 16:17–19 — The “rock” and the “keys”**
> *Jesus replied, “Blessed are you, Simon son of Jonah, for this was not revealed to you by flesh and blood, but by my Father in heaven.”*  
> *“And I tell you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it.”*  
> *“I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven; whatever you bind on earth will be bound in heaven, and whatever you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven.”*

### What Catholics read:
They see this as **Jesus installing Peter as the first pope** — the foundation of the one true institutional Church.

### What the text actually says:
- Jesus blesses **Peter’s revelation**, not his bloodline or future position.  
- The “rock” can grammatically mean either Peter *or* his confession of faith — both are valid in Greek, but the context favors the **confession** because Jesus immediately praises divine revelation, not Peter himself.  
- The “keys” represent **understanding and moral responsibility**, not inheritance. The same “binding and loosing” language appears later in **Matthew 18:18**, applied to *all disciples collectively.*

So at this point, Jesus praises a moment of insight — not the birth of an empire.

---

## **3. Matthew 16:20–23 — The instant correction**
> *Then he strictly charged the disciples to tell no one that he was the Christ.*  
> *From that time on Jesus began to show his disciples that he must go to Jerusalem and suffer many things... and be killed, and be raised again the third day.*  
> *Then Peter took him aside and began to rebuke him, saying, ‘Far be it from you, Lord! This shall never happen to you.’*  
> *But he turned and said to Peter, ‘Get behind me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to me; for you are not setting your mind on the things of God, but on the things of men.’”*

**Here’s the critical moment:**
Within *three verses* of being told he’s the “rock,” Peter becomes the **stumbling block.**
Jesus identifies Peter’s human reasoning — the desire to preserve worldly power and glory — as *satanic*.  

That rebuke isn’t symbolic. It’s the moment Christ divides **spiritual revelation** (the real rock) from **human ambition** (the false foundation).

- The *Church* is therefore built on revelation, not hierarchy; on Spirit, not office.
