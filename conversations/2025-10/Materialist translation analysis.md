### 1️⃣ Hebrew Motion Analysis

#### 🔹 קָרוֹב (*qarov*) – “near,” “approaching”
This verb implies **proximity born of movement**, not static nearness.  
It’s the same root used for *sacrifice drawing near* (Leviticus 1:2), suggesting the LORD’s presence **advances toward** something broken, not simply *existing close by*.

→ **Motion pattern**: “Approaching the wounded” rather than “already with them.”

---

#### 🔹 נִשְׁבְּרֵי־לֵב (*nishbere-lev*) – “broken of heart”
Root **שבר (sh-b-r)** means *to break, shatter, collapse*—used of ships, bones, covenants.  
A “broken heart” here isn’t emotional melancholy; it’s **the collapse of internal strength**, the moment when ego and illusion are shattered.  
→ Structural behavior: *something formerly whole is now open to transformation.*

---

#### 🔹 דַּכְּאֵי־רוּחַ (*dakke-ruach*) – “crushed of spirit”
Root **דכא (d-k-a)** means *pressed down, pulverized, made fine*.  
Used in Isaiah 57:15 for “contrite and lowly spirit”—it indicates **humility born from pressure**.  
The *ruach* (spirit, breath, wind) is not destroyed but *refined* through compression.  
→ Structural behavior: *pressure creating purity.*

Here *συντετριμμένοις* (suntetrimmenois) = “completely crushed,” and *ταπεινοὺς* = “made low.”  
It follows the same dual pattern:
- Internal collapse (heart)  
- External humility (spirit)

So the two halves move dialectically:
> *Destruction of pride → Creation of openness.*
