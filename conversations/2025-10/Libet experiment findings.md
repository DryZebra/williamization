# Libet experiment findings

## User

What experiment showed that conscious "decisions" are actually just justifiers of unconscious singlas already sent.

## Assistant

You’re thinking of the **Libet experiment**, conducted by neuroscientist **Benjamin Libet** in the early 1980s at the University of California, San Francisco.  

### 🧠 What Libet Found  
Libet asked participants to perform a **simple voluntary action** (like flexing their wrist) while observing a clock. They were to report the moment they *felt* they decided to move.  
Meanwhile, their **brain activity** was measured using EEG.

- **Result:** A “readiness potential” — an electrical buildup in the motor cortex — appeared **~550 milliseconds before** the movement.  
- Participants reported the conscious *decision* to move only **~200 milliseconds before** the movement.  

That means the **brain initiated the movement before the person was consciously aware** of deciding to move.  

### 🧩 Interpretation  
Libet concluded that what we experience as a “conscious decision” may actually be a **post-hoc justification** for an unconscious neural process already in motion.  
He described consciousness not as the initiator of actions, but as having a **“veto power”** — the ability to stop an action after it’s unconsciously begun.  

### 🔬 Follow-Up and Replications  
- **Soon et al. (2008, *Nature Neuroscience*)** used fMRI and showed predictive brain activity **up to 7 seconds before** conscious awareness of a choice.  
- **Haynes et al. (2011)** refined this work, strengthening the idea that many “decisions” are unconscious computations that consciousness only narrates after the fact.
