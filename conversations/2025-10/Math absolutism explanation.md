## Assistant

That comment is basically a kind of **math-absolutism**. They’re treating mathematics not as a human practice (a tool we invented, refined, and apply in the world), but as an **independent eternal truth**—almost like a religion with axioms instead of gods.  

Here’s what’s going on:

---

### Why People Say This
- **Platonist view of math**: They’re assuming mathematics exists outside human minds, as an eternal “realm of truth.” In this view, 2+2=4 whether or not humans exist. So when they say “math is never wrong,” they mean the abstract structures of math are perfect and untouchable.
- **Defensive posture**: Often this comes from frustration with people who misuse statistics, probability, or logic. So they retreat to a comforting claim: “math itself can’t be wrong, only people can.”

---

### The Problem With That
- **Category error**: Math doesn’t “exist” in nature like a tree does. It’s a symbolic language humans created to describe patterns we see. A different civilization might have developed very different foundations (base-12, geometric reasoning instead of algebra, etc.) but still model the world.
- **Smuggling in metaphysics**: Saying “to deny math is to deny truth itself” elevates math into a quasi-divine role. It’s not just a tool anymore; it’s treated as the guarantor of reality. That’s the “pseudo religion” vibe you picked up.
- **Applications matter**: No one lives in “pure math.” Engineering, science, economics—these use math, but they rely on measurement, assumptions, and approximations. That’s where truth gets slippery.

---

### How You Could Respond
If you want to push back without sounding dismissive, you can point out:
- Math is **always a human abstraction**, not the thing itself. A circle in math is “perfect,” but no circle exists in the real world.  
- If math were truly “always right,” there wouldn’t be competing mathematical frameworks (Euclidean vs. non-Euclidean geometry, classical vs. intuitionistic logic, etc.). The fact that multiple systems can exist shows math isn’t a single eternal truth but a set of tools.
- Saying “denying math is denying truth” is like saying “denying grammar is denying truth.” Grammar structures our speech, but it’s not truth itself—it’s how we frame it.
