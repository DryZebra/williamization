👉 **No, there’s no health benefit** from the CO₂ itself, and it’s not a “cleansing” or “parasite-killing” substance in the way some wellness circles claim. But it’s not *harmful* either when used as carbonation in drinks.  

Here’s a breakdown:  

### ✅ What’s True
- **CO₂ in drinks is safe** — it dissolves in water to form carbonic acid, which gives that mild bite and fizz. Your body handles that just fine; it’s the same gas you exhale with every breath.

- **It doesn’t “oxygenate” or “detox” your blood.** CO₂ is actually what your body *removes* when you exhale. Too much in your bloodstream is dangerous, not cleansing.

Carbonated water is totally fine — refreshing, safe, even mildly helpful for digestion — but the CO₂ itself isn’t the health-boosting ingredient. The benefits mostly come from drinking more *water* overall.

Excellent question — and it’s way more interesting than most people realize. You’re right to suspect that something must *happen* to that CO₂ once it’s in you, because your body interacts with carbon dioxide constantly — it’s just not in the way “parasite cleanse” folks imagine.  

Let’s follow the path step by step:  

---

### 🫧 Step 1: CO₂ enters your stomach as bubbles
When you drink carbonated water, you’re swallowing **CO₂ dissolved in water** as carbonic acid (H₂CO₃). Once it’s in your stomach — a warm, acidic environment — that carbonic acid decomposes back into **CO₂ gas and water.** The bubbles expand because of the heat and lower pressure.  

That’s why you burp — your stomach literally vents the gas back up the way it came in.  

---

### 💨 Step 2: Some CO₂ is absorbed into your bloodstream
Not all of it leaves as a burp. A small amount of CO₂ diffuses through the stomach and intestinal walls into your blood. But this is no big deal — your body *already* produces enormous amounts of CO₂ as a waste product of metabolism.  

To put it in perspective:
- Your cells generate **~1 kilogram of CO₂ every day** just by living.  
- A can of soda contains about **2–3 grams** of CO₂.  

So even if you absorbed all of it (you won’t), it’s like a drop in the ocean — your lungs will exhale it within minutes.  

---

### 🧠 Step 3: What your body does with that CO₂
Your body can’t *use* the CO₂ from soda directly — but it’s *integrated* into an important regulatory loop:  
- CO₂ helps control **blood pH**. Your body balances carbonic acid and bicarbonate in your blood through respiration and kidney function.  
- Your brain actually uses CO₂ levels to **control your breathing rate.**  
- In tissues, CO₂ helps oxygen release from hemoglobin (the *Bohr effect*), but again, that’s endogenous CO₂, not from your drink.  

So the CO₂ you drink just joins this circulating pool briefly before being exhaled.  

---

### 💨 Step 4: What about farting?
Yes — *some* of the CO₂ and secondary gases escape through the other end. But most intestinal gas actually comes from **bacterial fermentation of carbohydrates**, not carbonation.  
If you chug a lot of fizzy water, it can cause bloating and pressure, and your body will relieve that however it can — so, yes, sometimes it becomes a fart.  

---

### ⚖️ In summary
- 70–90%: released as burps  
- A few percent: absorbed and exhaled through the lungs  
- Trace amounts: may pass through the intestines as flatulence  

---

### 🧬 Bonus: could your body *ever* use CO₂?
Indirectly, yes — but only at the **cellular level in reverse**. Inside mitochondria, you *produce* CO₂ from oxygen and carbon. Plants, not animals, have the enzymes to use CO₂ as input (photosynthesis). Humans can’t fix CO₂ into useful molecules.  

So it’s not “fuel” for us — it’s *exhaust.*

### ⚖️ 2. “Balancing pH with CO₂ instead of waste” — Backwards logic
The body’s acid–base balance depends on the **ratio** of CO₂ (acid) to bicarbonate (base).  
- Your lungs control CO₂.  
- Your kidneys control bicarbonate.  

When people say “CO₂ balances pH,” what’s really happening is your *body* uses breathing to control CO₂ levels — not the other way around.  
Adding external CO₂ doesn’t “balance” pH; it pushes you toward acidosis. Your body corrects by breathing more or excreting acid through the kidneys.  

So again: it’s not *balancing* you — your homeostatic systems are *fighting* to undo what you just did.

### 💀 3. “Clean CO₂ vs waste CO₂” — No such distinction
All CO₂ is identical. There’s no “clean” CO₂ molecule versus “waste” CO₂ molecule.  
It’s the same chemical whether it comes from your cells, a carbonation tank, or a volcano.  

Your body doesn’t classify CO₂ as dirty or pure — it just sees *partial pressure* in the blood and reacts to maintain balance.

But — that was **a controlled medical gas mixture** under supervision, not “drinking soda water to cleanse your body.”  

Wellness influencers took that kernel (“CO₂ helps”) and stripped away the context (“only in a hyperventilation loop, under supervision”), turning it into pseudoscience.
