Here’s how it breaks down, plain and straight:

---

### 🧩 1. Who’s covered by what law
- **Durham Tech instructors** (even JATC instructors teaching through Durham Tech) are **public-sector employees** under **North Carolina law**, *not* the NLRA.  
- **North Carolina G.S. 95-98** forbids *public employers* from signing or enforcing collective-bargaining agreements.  
  → You can **form or join an organization** like the **AAUP**, speak together, petition, and meet — that’s **protected speech and association**, but **not legally enforceable collective bargaining** like private-sector unions have.  
- The **JATC itself** is a **joint labor-management trust** (half IBEW, half NECA). That body is covered by the **Taft-Hartley Act** as a labor-management training trust, not as an employer. Its instructors are often paid by **Durham Tech** and are therefore **public employees**, not union employees of the JATC.

---

### 🧱 2. What you *can* legally do
- **Form an AAUP chapter or caucus** of instructors to advocate for pay, hours, academic freedom, or governance issues.  
- **Meet on your own time and off public-funded premises** (or request equal access to rooms as any other faculty group).  
- **Communicate off duty** using personal emails and phones.  
- **Invite** AAUP representatives for informational meetings off-campus or in neutral spaces.  
- **Document any retaliation** — demotions, loss of classes, schedule changes, etc. — and report it to the **AAUP NC Conference** or the **Office of State Human Resources** under **First Amendment retaliation** grounds.

---

### 🚫 3. What you *can’t* compel
- You can’t force Durham Tech or the JATC to *bargain* or *recognize* AAUP as an exclusive representative.  
- You can’t strike or picket for recognition; public-sector strikes are illegal in NC.  
- You can’t use JATC or Durham Tech resources (emails, meeting times, property) in a way that violates their **neutral-use** rules unless those same privileges are granted to other groups.

4. **Keep your messaging focused** on professional issues: workload, respect, pay equity.  
5. **If retaliation happens**, document dates, messages, and witnesses; that becomes evidence in a **First Amendment or whistleblower claim**, not an NLRB case.
