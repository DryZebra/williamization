### 1. **Direct Sun vs. Shade**
- When you’re standing in direct sunlight, your skin absorbs radiant heat.  
- This can make 54°F in the sun feel closer to the mid-60s (sometimes even warmer if there’s no wind).  
- In the shade, though, it will feel closer to the actual number.

### 2. **Wind and Humidity**
- If there’s a breeze, the windchill effect strips away warmth, making 54°F feel more like the 40s.  
- Low humidity (dry air) also makes cold feel sharper because your body loses heat faster.

### 3. **Night vs. Day**
- At night, there’s no sun warming you, so 58°F can feel *colder* than 54°F in sunlight.  
- Your body also cools faster in the dark without radiant heat around.

### 4. **Clothing and Activity**
- If you were sitting or standing still last night, you’d feel much colder at 58°F.  
- If you’re moving around in 54°F sunshine, you’ll feel noticeably warmer.
