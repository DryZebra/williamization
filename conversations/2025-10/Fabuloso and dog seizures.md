## What can affect whether it *might* lead to a seizure

Even though there's no direct proof that Fabuloso by itself reliably causes seizures, several factors could increase that risk in a specific scenario:

1. **Amount ingested** — A large dose is more hazardous than a tiny lick.
2. **Concentration / strength of the solution** — Stronger (undiluted or concentrated) solutions carry more risk.
3. **Dog’s size, age, and health** — Puppies, elderly dogs, or dogs with preexisting neurological, liver, or kidney issues are more vulnerable.
4. **Type of chemicals in the formula** — If the product contains ingredients that are neurotoxic, or substances that can lower the seizure threshold (e.g. alcohols, certain solvents), there could be risk.
5. **Route of exposure** — Ingestion is more dangerous, but inhalation of fumes, or absorption through the mouth/skin, might also contribute under high exposures.
6. **Time to treatment** — Delayed treatment can worsen signs.

So while Fabuloso doesn’t appear to be a known cause of seizures in standard, incidental exposure, under extreme or unusual circumstances, the risk of neurological symptoms (including seizures) cannot be absolutely ruled out.

## What to do if you suspect exposure

If you think your dog has ingested or been exposed to Fabuloso (or any household cleaner), do the following:

- Remove your dog from further exposure (move them to fresh air).
- Rinse their mouth with water (if safe / possible).
- Do **not** induce vomiting unless instructed by a veterinarian or poison control, because it could cause further damage depending on what was ingested.
- Call your veterinarian or a pet poison control hotline immediately (provide information about the product, amount, your pet’s weight, and symptoms).
- Monitor closely for signs such as salivation, vomiting, diarrhea, tremors, uncoordination, seizures, lethargy.
