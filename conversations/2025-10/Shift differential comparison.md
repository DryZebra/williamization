## Assistant

The **shift differential** language appears in both the CBA and the Recovery Agreement:

- In the **CBA (Inside Agreement 2025–2027)**, the shift premium is laid out in **Article III, Section 3.09 – SHIFT WORK**.  
  - **Second shift (swing shift):** +15% pay for all hours worked.  
  - **Third shift (graveyard shift):** +20% pay for all hours worked.  
  - The first (day) shift is paid at the regular hourly rate【17:2†CBA_553_2025_2027_copy.pdf†L15-L31】.

- In the **Recovery Agreement (Current Recovery 2025)**, shift differential is in the **Hours – Working Conditions** section.  
  - **Second shift:** +10% differential.  
  - **Third shift:** +15% differential.  
  - This applies only to projects longer than five days in duration【17:3†current recovery 2025.pdf†L1-L3】.

In short:
- **CBA (full-scale work):** +15% 2nd shift / +20% 3rd shift.  
- **Recovery (market recovery work):** +10% 2nd shift / +15% 3rd shift.

## User

But if it's a single night they don't have to pay?

## Assistant

Correct — under both agreements, they **only owe the shift differential if it’s an established shift** (usually at least **five consecutive days**).  

Here’s how it breaks down:  

- **CBA (Article III §3.09):**  
  It says *“multiple shifts of eight (8) hours for at least five (5) days’ duration may be worked.”*  
  → That means the 15% or 20% premium applies **only** when the contractor sets up a true 2nd or 3rd shift schedule for **five days or more**.  
  → If you just work a **one-off night** or **a few late hours**, it’s **regular rate + overtime** if applicable — **no shift premium**.  

- **Recovery Agreement:**  
  It says projects **not exceeding five days** can vary the schedule “without incurring shift premiums.”  
  → Same deal — **no 10% / 15% bump** for a short-term or one-night job.  

So yeah — a **single night** of night work doesn’t trigger shift differential pay under either agreement.
