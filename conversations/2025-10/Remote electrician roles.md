## What “remote electrician” means in practice

Because physical electrical work (wiring, installing, repair) requires you to be onsite, “remote electrician” usually refers to roles with some mix of remote, desk, or support duties tied to electrical work. Some examples:

| Role | What “remote” / virtual part looks like | On-site / travel expectations |
|---|---|---|
| Electrical Designer / CAD / Drafting | You design wiring diagrams, lighting layouts, electrical plans using software (AutoCAD, Revit, etc.) | Minimal — maybe occasional site visits for measurements, inspections, or field verification |
| Electrical Estimator / Cost Estimator | You take project plans, calculate quantities, cost out labor and materials, prepare bids | Rarely on site, but might review site docs, meet clients in person |
| Remote Technical Support / Troubleshooting | You help field techs or customers via video call, interpret data, guide repairs, review schematics | Some travel to site may be required |
| Project Manager / Electrical Project Coordinator | Oversee multiple jobs, manage budgets, schedules, subcontractors; coordinate remotely | You’ll still need to visit jobs periodically |
| Quality Assurance / Compliance / Inspector | Review as-built drawings, check compliance with codes, review documentation remotely | Occasional field audits or inspections may be required |
| Training / Instruction / Consulting | You conduct webinars, write training materials, consult on electrical systems, code compliance remotely | No or minimal travel |
| Remote Field Service with Travel | The base work is remote, but you might dispatch to sites as needed | Expect days or weeks in the field |

So in most cases, “remote electrician” doesn’t mean you never leave your home — but rather that a portion of the work is done remotely (design, planning, support, estimation), with site visits when necessary.

## Pros, cons & things to consider

**Pros:**

- More flexibility in schedule and location  
- Less physical strain (especially if you're older or have physical limits)  
- Opportunity to leverage design, engineering, or documentation skills  
- Combines field experience with desk/office work  

**Cons / Challenges:**

- Less hands-on satisfaction  
- Need good software skills (CAD, electrical design tools)  
- Might still require travel to sites — sometimes unpredictably  
- Some employers expect you to maintain licensure and field capability  
- Remote roles are more competitive (fewer of them)  

**Things to emphasize if applying:**

- Your hands-on electrical experience — that gives credibility  
- Ability to read and interpret schematics, blueprints, wiring diagrams  
- Software / CAD / design / drafting skills  
- Communication skills (especially remote troubleshooting)  
- Willingness to travel occasionally  
- Certifications, licensure, code knowledge
