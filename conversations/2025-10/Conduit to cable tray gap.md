# Conduit to cable tray gap

Good question — this comes up a lot when transitioning from raceways into a cable tray system.  

Per the **NEC**, the requirement isn’t stated as a fixed “maximum gap in inches.” Instead, it’s governed by **NEC 392.46** and **392.30(A)** in conjunction with the general support rules of Article 300:  

- **NEC 392.46 (Cable Entrances and Exits):**  
  Cables must be securely fastened within **12 inches** of the point where they enter or leave the cable tray, unless otherwise specifically allowed.  

- **NEC 300.11 / 300.12 (Support and Continuity):**  
  Conduits and raceways must be mechanically secured and supported, and the electrical continuity of the raceway system must be maintained.  

So in your case:  
- The rigid conduit must be **securely fastened** and **terminated properly** (with a bushing or connector where it meets the tray).  
- The cable must be **supported within 12 inches of exiting the conduit and entering the tray**.  

⚠️ That means:  
- The *gap* from the end of the conduit to the tray isn’t directly limited by inches in the NEC.  
- What matters is that once the cable leaves the conduit, it can’t be hanging unsupported for more than **12 inches** before being fastened in the tray.  
- Practically, most installations butt the conduit right up to the tray (with a bonding fitting if required), but if you do leave a space, you need to ensure the cable is secured within 12 inches.  

👉 Bottom line: You can leave a gap, but your cable must be supported within 12" of exiting the conduit into the tray.

Perfect — thanks for sharing that section of the NEC.  

Here’s the key point from **NEC 392.46** (what you highlighted):  

- You are allowed to run **individual conductors** or **multiconductor cable** from a cable tray into conduit/tubing *without a box*, as long as the conduit/tubing is **bushed** (to protect the insulation) and is used for **support or protection against damage**.  
- The conduit has to be **secured at the point of transition** (where it meets the tray), and it has to be **sealed** at the tray end if needed for environmental reasons.  

👉 **What this means for your installation (photo):**  
- There is no explicit “maximum gap in inches” between the end of the conduit and the tray in 392.46.  
- Instead, the conduit must either terminate *at* the tray or be close enough that the conductors are not exposed to damage while spanning that gap.  
- In practice, inspectors will expect the conduit to be either butted to the tray edge or within just a few inches, since the code language requires the conduit to be *installed for protection* and *secured at the transition point*.  

⚡ **Rule of thumb:** Don’t think in terms of “12 inches” here (that’s the cable securing rule once it exits conduit/tray). For conduit into tray, the intent is **mechanical protection right up to the entry point.** So the gap should be negligible — essentially touching or no more than an inch or two, depending on how rigid your install is.
