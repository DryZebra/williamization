# Soaking black beans tips

## User

Do you have to soak black beans for 24 hours before cooking?

## Assistant



## Assistant

No — you **don’t have to** soak black beans for 24 hours.  

Here’s the practical breakdown:  

- **Best practice:** Soak for **8–12 hours** (overnight). It softens them, reduces cook time, and makes them easier to digest.  
- **Shortcut method:** Boil them hard for **2 minutes**, then let them **sit covered for 1 hour** before draining and cooking normally. This “quick soak” works fine for next-day prep.  
- **No soak at all:** Totally possible — just rinse, cover with fresh water, and simmer low for **1.5–2.5 hours** until tender. You’ll need a little more water and patience.  

If you’re a working man cooking once a day, I’d **throw them in water before bed**, let them sit while you sleep, then cook them the next evening. That’s the most efficient rhythm.  

You want the beans to be soft but still hold shape — like work boots after a year: broken in, not falling apart.

## User

What about an instant pot with the rice at the same time?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Good question — that’s where timing matters.  

If you’re using an **Instant Pot**, black beans and rice *can* cook together, but only if you do it right:  

- **Unsoaked beans** take **25–30 minutes** at high pressure.  
- **Soaked beans** take **8–10 minutes**.  
- **White rice** takes **4–8 minutes**, depending on type.  
- **Brown rice** takes **20–22 minutes**.  

So:  
- If you use **brown rice** + **soaked black beans**, they cook perfectly together: **20 minutes, high pressure**, 10-minute natural release.  
- If you use **white rice**, you’ll overcook it to mush before the beans are done. The trick then is to **partially cook the beans first**, release pressure, **add rice**, and finish another 5–8 minutes.  

If you want *simplicity*, soak the beans overnight, use **brown rice**, and do it all in one go. One pot, one cleanup, no waste. That’s how you feed yourself without turning dinner into a science experiment.
