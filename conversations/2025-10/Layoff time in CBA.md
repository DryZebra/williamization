## Assistant

Yes — the current **Inside Agreement for IBEW Local 553 (2025–2027)** does mention layoff-related timing, but it doesn’t specify an advance *notice period* before a layoff.  

Here’s what it does say:  

- **Section 3.19a–b** covers recall and manpower requests.  
  - A former employee can be **recalled within 120 days** of being laid off.  
  - After a layoff, the **employer must wait 72 hours** before submitting new manpower requests, unless the replacement is for someone fired for cause.  
  - The employer must also **provide a reason for termination**【19:1†CBA_553_2025_2027_copy.pdf†L1-L4】.  

Aside from that, the CBA doesn’t require any minimum notice period before laying someone off — only that wages must be paid in full on the normal payday and that the Business Manager must receive a separation notice indicating the reason for termination【19:12†CBA_553_2025_2027_copy.pdf†L34-L36】.  

So, in short:  
- There’s **no required advance layoff notice**,  
- But the **Business Manager must be informed**,  
- Pay must be made promptly, and  
- The contractor can’t rehire until **72 hours after** a layoff unless it’s for a cause replacement.

## User

So there's no reason we can't lay them off at 330pm and pay until 530pm?

## Assistant

Correct — there’s **nothing in the CBA stopping that**.  

Here’s how it breaks down under the Inside Agreement:  
- **Layoff timing:** The employer has the right to lay off employees “at any time” (Section 2.02).  
- **Pay:** Section 3.04 says employees laid off “shall be paid their wages in full by electronic deposit on the normal payday.” There’s no rule about working out the rest of the shift.  
- **Notice:** No advance notice or minimum hours after notice are required.  

So if you lay them off at 3:30 PM but pay them through 5:30 PM, that’s actually **better than required** — you’re covering the full day and avoiding any claim of shorting pay.  

Only thing to watch: make sure the steward and the Business Manager are notified of the termination reason (Section 3.04 & 2.10). That keeps it clean.

## User

Should I lay off individually or as a group?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Do it **individually** — here’s why:  

- **Professionalism & respect:** One-on-one talks prevent embarrassment and show basic respect for each worker.  
- **Documentation:** You can note who was told, when, and what reason was given — that protects you later.  
- **Grievances:** Group layoffs can cause confusion and “he said, she said” issues. Individual meetings make the record clear and match Section 2.10, which says the steward must be notified of any men being terminated.  

**Best practice:**  
1. Inform the steward right before you start.  
2. Pull each worker aside privately, short and direct — “we’re reducing manpower, today’s your last day, paid through end of shift.”  
3. Give a written or verbal reason consistent with the CBA (“reduction in force,” “project completion,” etc.).  

That keeps it clean, legal, and professional.

## User

Two of them are working together. How do I handle it?

## Assistant

Handle it like this:  

1. **Talk to them separately.** Even if they work side by side, pull one aside first. Keeps emotions down and avoids the “pack reaction.”  
2. **Keep it short and even-toned.** Say something like:  
   > “We’re reducing manpower today. This isn’t about performance — it’s just the cut. You’re paid through 5:30. I appreciate your work.”  
3. **Let the second one know right after.** Don’t let rumors or confusion spread.  
4. **Tell the steward right after both conversations.** That’s required under Section 2.10.  

If they ask why both were picked, keep it factual — **“crew reduction / job progress / hours available”** — and never make it sound personal.  

That keeps it professional, compliant, and minimizes drama on the floor.
