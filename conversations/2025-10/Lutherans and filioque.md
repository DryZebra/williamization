Good question — this is a common point of confusion.  

**1. The filioque clause**  
The *filioque* (“and the Son”) is a phrase added in the Western Church to the Nicene Creed:  
> “...the Holy Spirit, who proceeds from the Father **and the Son**.”  

It was never part of the original Greek text from the First Council of Constantinople (381 AD). The Eastern (Orthodox) Church holds that the Spirit proceeds *from the Father alone*. The West (Rome and later Protestants) kept the *filioque*.

**2. Martin Luther and the 95 Theses**  
The *filioque* had nothing to do with Luther’s *95 Theses* (1517). Those theses were almost entirely about *indulgences* and papal authority—nothing about the Trinity or the Creed. The filioque issue was a millennium older and part of the East–West Schism (1054 AD).

**3. Lutherans today**  
Lutherans, being a Western Christian tradition, **do include the filioque** in the Nicene Creed. Most use the line “who proceeds from the Father and the Son” in worship. However, some modern Lutheran churches—especially those in ecumenical dialogue with the Orthodox—omit it or use the original “who proceeds from the Father” form when appropriate, emphasizing that they accept the theology behind the clause but not necessarily the unilateral *addition* to the Creed.

**Summary:**  
- ✅ Yes, Lutherans have the *filioque* in most of their creeds.  
- ❌ No, it wasn’t one of the 95 Theses or a Reformation issue.  
- ⚙️ It’s an East–West divide from centuries earlier, not a Lutheran–Catholic one.

Let’s sort it out clearly:

---

### 1. The *filioque* debate
The disagreement isn’t *about whether all three existed eternally* — both sides absolutely affirm that the **Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are co-eternal, co-equal, and uncreated**.  
The question is **only about origin of relation** within the Trinity — not about time or creation.

- **Eastern Orthodox view:**  
  The Spirit *proceeds from the Father alone*, and the Son is *begotten* of the Father.  
  The Spirit *rests upon* or *is sent through* the Son, but not “from” Him in origin.  
  → Father is the single fountainhead (*monarchia*) of divinity.

- **Western (Catholic/Lutheran) view:**  
  The Spirit *proceeds from the Father and the Son* — meaning the Spirit’s relational origin is from both together as one principle of love.  
  → The Father eternally begets the Son, and together they eternally “breathe” the Spirit.

---

### 2. Eternal coexistence  
Both sides agree: there was never a “before.”  
The Trinity is eternal and simultaneous — no one *came into being* later.  
“Proceeds” and “begotten” describe *relationships of being*, not events in time.  
Think of it as describing *structure*, not *sequence*.

---

### 3. What it all means  
The *filioque* argument isn’t whether God is truly Triune — everyone agrees on that.  
It’s about whether saying “the Spirit proceeds from the Son” risks making the Father *less* the ultimate source, or whether leaving it out risks separating the Spirit from the Son’s divine unity.

In short:
- **East:** Protect the Father’s unique origin role.  
- **West:** Protect the Son’s unity with the Father in giving the Spirit.  
Both affirm: one God, three co-eternal Persons.

# Our bottom line (the canon-level decision)

**Creed text we confess (canonical):**  
> “We believe in the Holy Spirit, the Lord, the giver of life, who proceeds **from the Father**, who with the Father and the Son is worshiped and glorified, who spoke by the prophets.”

**Doctrinal gloss (authoritative interpretation):**  
- **Ontologically:** The Spirit’s *procession* (Greek **ekporeusis**) is **from the Father alone** (the Father as *fons divinitatis*, the sole fountain of the Godhead).  
- **Eternally relational:** The Spirit is **eternally from the Father *through* the Son**—the Son is inseparable in the Spirit’s eternal manifestation and mission (*dia tou Huiou*).  
- **Economically (in history):** The Spirit is **sent by the Father and the Son** into the world, without implying *two sources* in God.

**Why this formulation?**  
It keeps the ecumenical Creed intact (no unilateral edits), preserves the Eastern *monarchia* of the Father (one source), and secures the Western insight about the Son’s integral role (no Spirit that is “separate” from the Son). It refuses both a binitarian drift (two sources) and a functional separation (Spirit detached from Christ).

# How we got here (the full treatment)

## 1) Linguistics: why words mattered
- **Greek:** *ekporeuetai* (“proceeds”) = **origin from a source**. Patristically reserved for the **Father**.  
- **Latin:** *procedit* (“goes forth”) = broader; used for both **origin and mission**, so adding *filioque* (“and the Son”) never sounded like “two fountains” in Latin logic.  
- **Material takeaway:** A translation drift produced a metaphysical fight. Our canon distinguishes **source-language** (ekporeusis = Father alone) from **relation/mission-language** (through/sent by the Son).

## 2) Scripture (our reading rule)
- **John 15:26**: “the Spirit of truth who **proceeds from the Father**.” → procession-origin = the Father.  
- **John 16:7; 20:22; Gal 4:6; Rom 8**: the Spirit is **given/sent by the Son** and makes us cry “Abba.” → inseparable bond of Spirit and Son in mission and eternal relation.  
- **Canon rule:** Keep John 15:26 as the ontological guardrail; read the “sending/breathing” texts as revealing the eternal **through-the-Son** relation and the historical missions.

## 3) Patristic trajectories (very short)
- **Cappadocians (East):** Guard the Father’s *monarchia*.  
- **Augustine (West):** Spirit as the **communio** (Love) of Father and Son; Latin semantics allowed *filioque* without implying two sources.  
- **Material synthesis:** We affirm Cappadocian origin-logic **and** Augustinian communion-logic by saying: **from the Father (source), through the Son (eternal relation), sent by both (mission).**

## 4) The fruits in history (what each emphasis tended to do)

### West (with *filioque* in the text)
- **Theological fruit:** Strong **Christ–Spirit unity** → robust Christocentric pneumatology; doctrinal system-building (Augustinian/Scholastic).  
- **Institutional fruit:** Tendency toward **juridical centralization** (one principle = one governance). Helped coherence; risked over-centralizing authority.  
- **Societal fruit:** Standardization, law, universities, missionary expansion; also a habit of **uniformity-enforcement** (upsides and downsides).

### East (Father-alone procession in the text)
- **Theological fruit:** Strong protection of **personal distinctions** (Father as source); space for essence–energies theology and deification (theosis).  
- **Institutional fruit:** **Conciliar** reflex, liturgical stability; resilience through catastrophe; risk of **conserving** to the point of slower structural adaptation.  
- **Societal fruit:** Deep ascetical culture, mystical theology, strong liturgical commons; sometimes less administrative reach.

**Materialist Christian read:**  
Neither stream is “pure wins.” The West often produced power, reach, and scholastic clarity; the East preserved mystical depth, conciliar instinct, and personalist metaphysics. Our canon takes the **structural truth** from each and rejects the **excess** of each.

## 5) Did it “modify the Bible”?
- The *filioque* was a **creedal** addition in the West, not a biblical verse.  
- Its **interpretive pressure** shows up in translations and catechesis (how people explain John 15:26; how “sending” texts are framed).  
- Our canon: **no textual alteration of Scripture**; instead, a **hermeneutic rule** (see §2) that binds exegesis to the Father-as-source, Son-as-mediating-relation, and shared mission.

## 6) Ecclesial praxis: what we’ll actually do
- **Creed in worship:** Use the **original ecumenical text** (“proceeds from the Father”).  
- **Catechesis line (memorize this):**  
  “**Procession from the Father; eternal relation through the Son; mission from the Father and the Son.**”  
- **Dogmatic safeguard:** Explicitly **reject ‘two sources’** language. When teaching Augustine, say “one principle” **means** “the Father as source with the Son inseparably”—not two fountains.  
- **Ecumenical stance:** Because we keep the universal Creed intact and state an interpretation both sides can live with, we can **pray with East and West** without papering over truth.

## 7) Political theology (material effects we want)
- **Monarchia without monarchy:** The Father’s sole sourcehood grounds **unity without domination**.  
- **Through-the-Son:** Guarantees the Spirit’s work is **Christ-shaped** (service, kenosis, liberation from sin and domination).  
- **Shared mission:** Authorizes distributed, lay-empowered action—**no spiritual caste system**—while maintaining doctrinal unity.

# The Materialist Christian Canon (final wording to adopt)

**Article on the Holy Spirit**  
1) We confess with the ecumenical Creed: the Holy Spirit **proceeds from the Father**.  
2) We teach that the Spirit is **eternally from the Father through the Son**, such that the Father remains the **sole fountain of the Godhead**, and the Son is inseparable in the Spirit’s eternal manifestation.  
3) We confess that in the economy of salvation the Spirit is **sent by the Father and the Son**, revealing Christ and effecting our adoption, sanctification, and communion.  
4) We reject any doctrine of **two sources** of the Spirit.  
5) We reject any practical separation of the Spirit from the Son that would imply an alternative path to God **beside** the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus Christ.  
6) We forbid unilateral textual alteration of the ecumenical Creed in worship. Teaching must clarify the above interpretation.
