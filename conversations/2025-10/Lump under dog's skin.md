# Lump under dog's skin

## User

My golden doodle (roo) has a lump in her armpit. It's not attached to the skin, it kinda "floats" beneath the skin. Don't freak out and tell me to call dog 911. What are my possibilities here?

## Assistant

Good — that’s exactly the kind of calm, clear observation that actually helps narrow things down.  
Since it’s **not attached to the skin** and feels like it “floats” under it, that already leans toward some of the *less dangerous* possibilities. Here’s the main range, roughly ordered from most common/benign to more concerning:  

1. **Lipoma (fatty lump)** – soft, smooth, movable, under the skin but not attached to muscle; very common in doodles and older dogs. Totally benign, though they can get large.  
2. **Reactive lymph node** – the armpit (axilla) has lymph nodes that can enlarge when the immune system is fighting something (infection, inflammation, even allergies). Usually small, oval, somewhat firmer than a lipoma, and sometimes come/go.  
3. **Sebaceous cyst** – usually more superficial, but if deeper, can feel like a floating pea. Might rupture or change over time.  
4. **Abscess or localized infection** – tends to be warmer, tender, sometimes with redness or swelling around it.  
5. **Soft-tissue tumor (e.g. fibroma, mast cell tumor, sarcoma)** – firmer, sometimes irregular shape, sometimes attached deeper later, may grow faster or feel different over time.  

---

### How to get a better sense at home:
- **Texture:** Soft and squishy = likely lipoma. Firm or irregular = worth checking.  
- **Pain:** If she flinches when you press it, infection or inflammation possible.  
- **Mobility:** Still freely movable = good sign.  
- **Growth:** Check weekly; use fingers or take a picture next to a coin for scale.  
- **Systemic signs:** Eating, energy, coat, and behavior normal = reassuring.  

---

If she’s otherwise healthy, you can absolutely **monitor** it for 2–4 weeks unless it grows quickly, gets firm/hot/painful, or she starts acting sick. If it stays the same, you can mention it at her next regular vet visit—they can confirm with a simple **fine needle aspirate** (quick poke, microscope look, often same-day answer).

Got it — that makes things even less alarming. If it’s **on the side of her chest, near but not inside the armpit**, that region doesn’t have major lymph nodes, so we’re mostly talking about **fat, skin, or muscle** origin.  

Here’s what that changes:  

1. **Lipoma (still #1 candidate)** – This is classic: soft, smooth, and movable under the skin on the chest or ribcage. That’s *exactly* where lipomas love to show up. They kind of glide under your fingers and don’t bother the dog.  
2. **Subcutaneous cyst or fatty granuloma** – Less common but similar feel. Often caused by mild irritation or minor trauma.  
3. **Muscle knot or small hematoma** – If she bumped herself hard (jumping, rough play, etc.), you can get a small pocket of fluid or scar tissue that feels like a lump for a while.  
4. **Less likely but possible** – benign fibroma, or in rare cases, a soft tissue sarcoma. Those tend to be **firmer**, not easily movable, and grow steadily.  

---

### What you can do now:
- **Gently roll it between your fingers.** Lipomas will move easily under the skin but feel smooth and doughy.  
- **Mark or photograph** the area so you can see if it grows over a couple weeks.  
- **Check symmetry:** sometimes the opposite side has a smaller version (especially in doodles with fat pads).  
- **Note her behavior:** if she’s eating, running, and letting you handle it with no pain, you’ve likely got a benign fatty lump or fluid pocket.
