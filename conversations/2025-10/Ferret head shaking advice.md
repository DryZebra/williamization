Ferrets have **very light bones** and a **long, flexible spine**, which helps them slip through tight spaces but makes them prone to **head, neck, and internal injuries** from falls. They don’t have much body mass to absorb impact, and their equilibrium system (in their inner ear) is extremely sensitive. So when they fall or bonk their head, they can get temporary dizziness, shaking, or even mild concussions — the “weird” reactions you’ve noticed before are very likely that.  

Here’s the real picture:  
- **Short falls (sofa, low bed)** usually just startle them.  
- **Medium to tall drops (like from hangers, counters, closets)** can cause head trauma, bruised ribs, or inner ear issues.  
- **Frequent smaller falls** can add up — repeated concussions or jarring hits can cause chronic balance or neurological problems later.  

The good news is ferrets are *resilient if they’re protected from repeated hits.* If you start treating his climbing like a toddler-proofing job — no high shelves, soft landings, blocked vertical routes — he’ll almost certainly be fine long-term.
