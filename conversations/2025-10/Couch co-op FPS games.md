## ⚙️ What’s Actually Going On

When you start Fortnite, Epic Games tries to:
1. **Check for an existing Epic Games Account** tied to your PlayStation Network ID.  
2. If none is found, it offers to **link** or **proceed without linking**.  
3. If you choose *“Proceed without linking,”* the game still has to:
   - Create a **temporary, backend Epic account** linked to your PSN.  
   - Sync your **cosmetics, XP, and cross-play permissions**.  
   - Verify anti-cheat and entitlement servers (especially since PS5 connects through Epic’s servers, not just Sony’s).  

This process touches **multiple Epic authentication servers** — and if any of them are slow (which happens during big updates or cross-platform sync issues), it can hang for 30–120 seconds or more.  

---

## ⏱️ Why It Sometimes Takes a While
- **Server load:** Fortnite servers spike during new seasons or weekend evenings.  
- **Account verification:** Epic must verify your PlayStation ID token before granting lobby access.  
- **Temporary Epic account creation:** Even without “linking,” one still gets made in the background.  
- **Network hiccups:** NAT type “Strict” or poor connection to Epic’s region servers can delay this step.  

---

## 💡 What You Can Do

- **Wait it out once.** The first time can take a few minutes — later launches are faster.  
- **Restart Fortnite** if it hangs longer than ~5 minutes; it often resolves on the second attempt.  
- **Optionally link your Epic account** later (Settings → Accounts → Connections → Epic Games). This usually speeds up startup by skipping that temporary-account handshake.  
- Make sure your **PS5 internet is open NAT type 2** — restrictive firewalls slow Epic’s authentication.
