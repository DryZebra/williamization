If the worker wants to work but the employer denies them, what comes next?

## Assistant

Good question — here’s how that plays out under your **Local 553 Inside Agreement** and general practice in our trade:  

1. **Management Rights Rule:**  
   The employer has full authority to decide who works and when, as long as it’s not discriminatory or retaliatory. The CBA gives them the right to *plan, direct, and control operations,* including **layoffs, transfers, or refusal to assign work** for safety or operational reasons. So legally, they can tell someone to stay home if they think it’s unsafe or a liability.  

2. **Employee’s Option – Documentation:**  
   The worker should get the denial **in writing or text** from the foreman or project manager. If the worker wants to protect their income or job status, they should also get a **doctor’s note** saying they’re fit for light duty or able to return. That note can matter later if there’s a dispute.

3. **If the Employer Refuses Anyway:**  
   The worker can contact the **union steward or business agent** to file a **grievance** under Article I, Section 1.06. The process is:  
   - Try to resolve it informally with the foreman or project manager.  
   - If that fails, elevate it to the **Labor-Management Committee** within 48 hours.  
   - If no resolution there, it can go up to the **Council on Industrial Relations (CIR)**, whose decision is final and binding.  

4. **Important Distinction:**  
   If the injury happened **off the job**, this usually isn’t a workers’ comp case, so the employer isn’t required to create a light-duty position. They *can* accommodate it, but don’t have to. The CBA doesn’t force them to.  

5. **Best Course for the Worker:**  
   - Keep the doctor’s note.  
   - Notify the steward or BA if the denial seems unfair or inconsistent with how others are treated.  
   - Don’t just show up without clearance — that could be written up as insubordination.  

In short: the **employer has the right to deny work**, but the **worker has the right to document, question, and grieve** it if it appears arbitrary or inconsistent. Would you like me to write out a short sample report or text the steward could use to log this situation properly?

What is 1.06 specifically and how does it apply to his grievance?

## Assistant

Here’s the exact language from **Article I, Section 1.06** of your Inside Agreement (Local 553 Inside Construction CBA 2025–2027):  

> **Section 1.06.** All grievances or questions in dispute shall be adjusted by the duly authorized representative of each of the parties to this Agreement. In the event that these two are unable to adjust any matter within 48 hours, they shall refer the same to the Labor-Management Committee.  

---

### What that means in plain English:
- **Step 1:** A grievance starts when there’s a disagreement or “question in dispute” between a worker (or the union) and the employer about rights under the contract.  
- **Step 2:** The **first attempt** must be between the **union representative (steward or business agent)** and the **employer’s representative (foreman, superintendent, or project manager)**.  
- **Step 3:** If they **can’t settle it within 48 hours**, it goes to the **Labor-Management Committee**, which is a six-person panel (three from the union, three from NECA). That committee makes the next decision.  
- **Step 4:** If that committee deadlocks, it can be escalated to the **Council on Industrial Relations (CIR)** for a final, binding ruling.  

---

### How this applies to your coworker’s case:
If the worker believes he’s **ready and willing to work** but was **unfairly denied the opportunity**, this becomes a *“question in dispute”* — specifically whether the employer is violating his right to work under the CBA or applying safety/medical standards inconsistently.  

So:
1. He reports the issue to the **steward or business agent**.  
2. The **union rep contacts the foreman or contractor rep** to resolve it within 48 hours.  
3. If they can’t, the BA files it formally for the **Labor-Management Committee** to review.  

Because the employer does have the contractual right to control staffing (under **Section 2.02, Management Rights**), the grievance would likely center on **whether the denial was arbitrary, discriminatory, or inconsistent with safety policy** — not just that the worker “wanted to come in.”
