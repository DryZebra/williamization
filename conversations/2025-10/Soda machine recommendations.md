## 🧪 Does glass actually make soda fizzier?

**Scientifically speaking:**  
The *material* of the bottle itself doesn’t directly cause “more carbonation,” but it affects how well that carbonation **stays** in the liquid. The biggest factors are:

| Factor | Plastic (PET) | Glass | Why it matters |
|--------|---------------|-------|----------------|
| **CO₂ permeability** | Slightly porous — CO₂ slowly diffuses out over time | Impermeable — CO₂ stays trapped | Over days, glass keeps fizz longer. Over minutes, it’s identical. |
| **Surface smoothness** | Slightly rough inside — encourages nucleation (bubble formation) | Very smooth — fewer nucleation sites | Glass “burps” fewer bubbles while pouring = perceived smoother fizz |
| **Temperature stability** | Insulates slightly; warms faster in hand | Conducts cold better | Cold liquid = higher CO₂ retention; so cold glass *feels* fizzier |
| **Pressure tolerance** | Designed for safety — will flex outward under pressure | Rigid; holds pressure better | In practice, both limited by machine’s regulator, not bottle strength |

👉 **Conclusion:** Glass *feels* crisper because it stays colder and releases CO₂ more slowly *while drinking*, not because the carbonation level is higher.  
If you chill PET water to the same temperature and pour into a cold glass, the fizz level is virtually indistinguishable.  

So:  
- For short-term drinking = **no real difference.**  
- For long-term storage (hours–days) = **glass wins.**
