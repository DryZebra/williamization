## What a “continuing resolution” typically does

- A continuing resolution (CR) is a way to extend federal funding at existing levels when Congress hasn’t finished passing all the regular appropriation bills.  
- A “clean” CR means it doesn’t include additional policy changes or “riders” (i.e. unrelated policy mandates or cuts).  
- In this case, Republicans claim their CR is “clean” and merely keeps the government running. citeturn0search1turn0search3turn0search7

So the reason Democrats voted “no” isn’t just obstruction — it’s that they viewed the proposal as failing to protect or restore healthcare provisions they consider essential.

## Did Republicans *add* something to the CR?

By the public-facing description, Republicans claim it is “no-policy” or “no riders” — i.e. a clean CR. citeturn0search1turn0search7turn0search5  
However:

- Even if a CR is formally “clean,” the baseline funding levels or omissions can implicitly accept prior cuts or policy changes that Democrats view as unacceptable.  
- The disagreement is whether the CR leaves in place harmful policy shifts (especially in healthcare) or implicitly signals acceptance of those cuts. Democrats want an affirmative restoration or extension.  
- Because Republicans control both the House, Senate (in majority), and the Presidency, Democrats view the CR as enabling GOP policy priorities if Democrats simply concede.

So, to answer your question:

- Democrats opposed it not because they were *against* keeping the government open in principle, but because they viewed the Republicans’ version as insufficient or harmful in its policy implications.
