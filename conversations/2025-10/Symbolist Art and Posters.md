It sounds like you’re describing **symbolist** or **Art Nouveau**–style posters — works that *represent* ideas or natural cycles (like the seasons, sun, or moon) through **abstract, allegorical imagery** rather than literal depiction.

Here are a few major styles and movements that fit what you’re describing:

---

### 🜂 **Art Nouveau (1890s–1910s)**
- **Artists:** Alphonse Mucha, Gustav Klimt  
- **Features:** Flowing organic lines, stylized women representing abstract concepts (e.g. “Spring,” “The Moon”), ornate borders, and symbolic color use.  
- **Goal:** Capture *essence* or *mood* — not realism.

---

### 🜁 **Symbolism (late 19th century)**
- **Artists:** Odilon Redon, Jean Delville, Fernand Khnopff  
- **Features:** Dreamlike scenes, mythological or spiritual allegories, surreal colors.  
- **Goal:** Express emotional or spiritual truth through symbols, not physical likeness.

---

### 🜃 **Modernist or Bauhaus Abstraction (1920s–40s)**
- **Artists:** Wassily Kandinsky, Paul Klee, Joan Miró  
- **Features:** Geometric shapes, color harmonies, and abstract forms used to evoke universal forces (sun, moon, balance, movement, time).  
- **Goal:** Represent invisible structures — energy, rhythm, or feeling.

---

### 🜄 **Mid-Century Modern & Cosmic Posters (1950s–70s)**
- **Designers:** Charley Harper, Saarinen-era illustrators, early NASA graphic artists  
- **Features:** Simplified, stylized shapes representing nature and cosmic order (seasons, stars, weather).  
- **Goal:** Abstract nature and emotion into clean, iconic forms.

---

If you’re thinking of posters that show, say, *“Winter” as a woman with white flowing hair, snowflakes, and pale blue tones*, that’s **Art Nouveau / Symbolist**.  
If you’re thinking of something more *geometric and abstract* — like circles and triangles suggesting *sun and moon*, or colors shifting through *seasons* — that’s closer to **Modernist abstraction**.

### 🌕 3. **Why They Feel the Same**

- The fairy = the spirit of a season  
- The sleeping child = nature’s rest  
- The woman framed in flowers = life’s cyclical beauty
