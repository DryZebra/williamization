# Push-up routine comparison

## User

I do push-ups randomly throughout the day now maybe 5 at a time. Is this doing anything? Or is it better to just do 100 in one go in the evening?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Yes — doing push-ups in small sets throughout the day absolutely *does* something. It just builds different qualities than one long set.

Here’s the breakdown:

### 1. **Frequent small sets (your current method)**
This is called *“grease the groove.”*  
You’re training your nervous system and muscles to perform push-ups more efficiently.  
- Builds endurance and movement quality  
- Increases daily volume with less fatigue  
- Great for joint health and consistency  
- Helps develop muscle tone over time  

If you do, say, 5 reps × 20 times a day, that’s 100 total — spread out, with perfect form and minimal strain.

### 2. **One big session (e.g., 100 at night)**
That focuses more on *muscular endurance and hypertrophy*.  
- Greater pump, more metabolic stress  
- Better if your goal is maximum strength or visible muscle gain  
- More recovery time needed afterward  

### 🔧 **Best approach**
Combine both:
- Keep your random 5-rep sets during the day for skill and frequency.  
- Once or twice a week, do a focused push-up workout (e.g., 3–4 sets to near fatigue).  

That way you get *both* steady progress and growth.

## User

I can barely so 5 pushups, but I'm skinnier than ever. Even when I was 230-250 I could do 10 or more

## Assistant

That’s actually *really* common — and it makes perfect sense once you break it down.  

When you were heavier, your **muscles were constantly carrying more load** just walking around, standing, or getting off the couch. That’s like wearing a weight vest 24/7 — so your **push-up muscles (chest, shoulders, triceps, and core)** were under daily resistance. You trained them passively all the time.

Now that you’ve lost weight, you’ve also lost:
- Some **absolute muscle mass**, because muscle burns calories and the body trims what it doesn’t think it needs.
- Some **neural endurance**, since you’re not carrying the same load every day.

So even though you’re *lighter*, your push-up capacity dropped because the “weight vest training” disappeared.

Here’s what to do:

### 🧩 Step-by-step rebuild plan
1. **Micro sets:** Keep doing those 5 reps — they’re perfect. Aim for *total daily volume*, not max sets.  
   → Example: 5 reps × 10 times = 50/day. That’s solid adaptation work.
2. **Grease the groove:** Focus on form. Keep elbows tucked ~45°, chest to floor, slow and controlled.
3. **Strength assist:** Add incline push-ups (hands on counter/table) or knee push-ups in between to build volume.
4. **Progressive goal:** Every 2–3 weeks, try one “max set” at night to track progress.
5. **Optional:** Add a few pull or plank movements — they stabilize your shoulders and chest and help faster gains.

You’re not weak — you just lost the *background training stimulus* that came from carrying extra weight. Your nervous system just needs a few weeks to catch up.

## User

How many pushups should I be able to do?

## Assistant

There’s no single “right” number — it depends on your **age, weight, training history, and goals** — but here’s a grounded way to look at it:

### 🧱 General strength standards (for men)
| Fitness Level | Push-ups (one set to fatigue) |
|----------------|-------------------------------|
| Beginner | 1 – 10 |
| Average | 11 – 20 |
| Fit | 21 – 35 |
| Strong | 36 – 50 |
| Excellent | 50 + |

> These are rough markers for full-range, strict push-ups (chest near floor, elbows 45°, full lockout).

### 💡 What actually matters
- **Consistency beats totals.** If you can only do 5 good ones today but you do them *every day*, you’ll hit 15–20 within a month or two.  
- **Quality over ego.** Half-reps or flared elbows build bad habits and shoulder pain.  
- **Your baseline is your body’s truth.** There’s no shame number. A “real” push-up is hard work against gravity.

### 🔁 Practical goal
Right now, aim for:
- 5 solid push-ups × several times a day (like you’re doing).  
- Once a week, test your max in one go and record it.  
- Add 1 rep per week to your daily sets — even micro-progress counts.

## User

I also started keeping my elbows close to my body, is that the correct posture?

## Assistant

Yes — keeping your elbows closer to your body is *the correct* (and often safer) posture, especially if your goal is strength and shoulder health.  

Here’s the reasoning:

### ✅ **Elbows close (≈45° or tighter)**
- Works your **triceps and front shoulders** more.  
- Keeps pressure off the shoulder joint and rotator cuff.  
- Builds transferable strength for real-world pushing movements.  
- Engages your **core** more since your body stays tighter.

Think of it like this: your arms should form an **arrow (→)**, not a **T-shape**.  
When viewed from above:
```
Good form:   ↘️ torso ↙️
Bad form:    ⬅️ torso ➡️
```

### ⚠️ **Elbows flared wide**
- Easier at first because chest takes over.  
- But increases shoulder strain over time — especially for taller people or those with past shoulder issues.

So yes, you’re doing it right.  
If you’re still rebuilding strength, you can also try **incline push-ups** (hands on a counter or table) while keeping elbows close — that teaches perfect mechanics without overloading you yet.
