---

### **1. “Kepha = Petros = Petra = Rock = Peter is the Rock”**
This whole linguistic chain is sleight of hand.

- **Kepha (Aramaic)** *does* mean “rock,” yes.  
- But Greek *distinguishes* between **Petros** (a small stone, typically masculine) and **Petra** (a large, foundational rock, feminine).

The Catholic apologist here waves that away by saying it’s just about gender—like calling a man "Pedro" instead of "Pedra." That would be fine **if Greek didn’t distinguish between types of rock**—but it does. That’s the **entire point** of using different words. Jesus says, *"You are Petros, and upon this Petra I will build my Church."* That parallel isn’t gendered—it’s **deliberate contrast**.

It’s like saying:  
> “You’re Rocky, but on this mountain I’ll build my house.”  
The distinction matters.

---

### **2. “Peter was made the Chief Shepherd in John 21!”**
This is just a convenient proof-text that ignores context and history.

- John 21:15–17 is about *restoring* Peter after his betrayal—not making him a monarch.  
- Jesus tells him to *feed His sheep*, yes—but He also says the same thing to **all** the disciples in other places (e.g. Matthew 28:19–20, John 20:21–23).

The idea that this moment installs a hierarchical Pope is **retroactive justification**, not honest reading.

---

### **3. “Eliakim in Isaiah 22 proves the Pope!”**
This is the wildest leap.

- Isaiah 22 refers to a steward of a **Davidic monarchy**—not the Church.  
- Even if Jesus is drawing a parallel in Matthew 16:19, it's a metaphor for **delegated authority**, not absolute power.  
- No early Christian would’ve read that and said, “Ah yes, a papacy with infallible declarations ex cathedra!”

This is **anachronistic logic**, treating Scripture like a coded contract rather than a living record of moral truth and struggle.

---

### **Final Point: “Submit to the Pope 😹🔥”**
This tone is the real giveaway. Not reasoned argument. Not shared investigation. Just **ideological bullying** disguised as clever rhetoric.
