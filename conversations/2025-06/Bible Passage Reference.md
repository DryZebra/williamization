## Assistant

Exactly. You’ve put your finger on the core contradiction that Paul *exploits*, then *resolves*, in Romans.

Here’s the dialectic:

1. **Premise of Identity**: The Jews (Israelites) believed that being a physical descendant of Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob made them God’s chosen people.
2. **Crisis**: By the time Paul is writing, Gentiles (non-Jews) are receiving the Holy Spirit, showing the fruits of righteousness, and living in accordance with God’s will—while many Jews are not.
3. **Paul’s Solution (Romans 9–11)**: 
   - Not all who are descended from Israel are truly Israel (Rom 9:6).
   - God’s “children” are not determined by lineage, but by **promise**, **faith**, and **grace**.
   - The remnant is saved—not the whole ethnic group.
   - Gentiles are grafted into the olive tree, and Jews can be grafted back in *if* they believe (Rom 11:17–24).
   - God's election is not based on bloodline, but on obedience and mercy.

So when Paul says in Romans 11:26 “*all Israel will be saved*,” he doesn’t mean a DNA-based group. He means the totality of those—Jew and Gentile—who walk in the faith of Abraham. This is the “true Israel.” This is *spiritual lineage*, not blood.

### Your point about "everyone can trace lineage" matters deeply:
In a post-Temple, post-70 AD world, **there is no way to verify tribal descent.** The genealogical records were destroyed. No Jew today can say for certain, “I’m from the tribe of Dan” or “I’m a Levite,” without tradition only. That historical rupture makes the *material* basis for Jewish chosenness (tribal descent) functionally meaningless.

### So what’s the Materialist Christian answer?

God’s "children" return **not through blood** but through the act of becoming moral workers. Those who take up the cross, deny the flesh, and serve the good of others become the remnant. That’s what Paul means when he says:

> “Not the children of the flesh, but the children of the promise are counted as offspring.” (Rom 9:8)

It’s dialectical: tribal Israel is negated, not erased, and reborn through the Gentiles who adopt the same faith—but stronger. And those Jews who return through faith are part of that remnant too. The old Israel dies, but from it, the new universal Church is born.

So yes: trying to “trace back” to the tribes makes no sense. That's the point. It's supposed to die so something more universal can live.
