# First Night Effect

## User

Is there some kind of away from home don't sleep right syndromes? I feel sick like a headache after visiting my mother in law

## Assistant



## Assistant

Yes, there **is** something like what you're describing—sometimes called the **"first night effect"** (FNE), and it’s a well-documented phenomenon in sleep research.

### Here's what's going on:
When you're sleeping in an unfamiliar environment (like a hotel, a guest room, or your mother-in-law's house), your brain often doesn't fully "let go." One hemisphere (typically the left) stays more alert than usual, almost like a night watch. This means your sleep is shallower and more easily disrupted, even if you’re not consciously aware of waking up during the night.

### Common symptoms:
- Headache or migraine
- Grogginess or foggy head
- Increased sensitivity to light and sound
- General fatigue or feeling slightly sick
- Irritability or mild anxiety

### Possible contributing factors:
- Different smells, lighting, or ambient noise
- Mattress or pillow that your body isn’t used to
- Emotional or social stress (even if subtle—like hosting or being hosted)
- Changes in temperature or humidity
- Disruption of your circadian rhythm or nighttime rituals

### What helps:
- Bringing a familiar item (like your pillow or blanket)
- Using a white noise machine or app to block out new noises
- Keeping your bedtime routine consistent (even brushing teeth and winding down the same way)
- Staying hydrated and avoiding caffeine/alcohol close to bedtime
- Melatonin for short-term use (if cleared by your doctor)

If it’s a **recurring pattern**, especially if you're someone who’s very sensitive to your environment or routine, it might help to preemptively prepare for sleep disruptions when you're traveling or staying somewhere unfamiliar.
