# Yaupon Caffeine Content Guide

## Assistant

Yaupon holly (*Ilex vomitoria*) is the only native North American plant known to contain caffeine. It's been used traditionally by many Native American tribes for its stimulant properties. Here's a breakdown to answer your questions clearly and practically:

---

### ⚡ **Caffeine Content Per Leaf**
- The **average caffeine content** of **1 yaupon holly leaf** (medium size, sun- or oven-dried) is roughly **0.5 to 1.0 mg of caffeine**.
- Caffeine concentration can vary based on:
  - Whether the leaves are **young (more caffeine)** or mature.
  - **Growing conditions** (more sun = more caffeine).
  - **Processing method** (light roast retains more caffeine than dark).

---

### 🫖 **How Many Leaves Per Cup of Tea?**
- A standard cup of yaupon tea (8 oz) uses **1–2 grams of dried leaves**, or roughly **15–30 medium-sized leaves**, depending on how tightly you pack or crush them.
  - This yields **20–60 mg of caffeine per cup** (comparable to a weak cup of green or black tea).

#### Drying Method Differences:
- **Sun-dried**: Tends to preserve more leaf integrity and possibly slightly more caffeine, depending on the heat.
- **Oven-dried**: Quicker, but high temps may reduce some caffeine if roasted too hot.
  - For caffeine retention, dry at **low heat (~150°F or 65°C)** for a few hours.

---

### 🧼 **Can You Chew the Leaves?**
- Yes, **you can chew the leaves**—they’re not toxic. However:
  - **Chewing raw leaves** may release **less caffeine** than brewing.
  - They’re **bitter** and **not very palatable** in raw form.
  - The caffeine release is slow and low through the mouth's mucosa—**less effective than tea**.

---

### 🔍 **How Can You Tell How Much Caffeine Is in a Batch?**
There’s no easy at-home way to precisely measure caffeine, but you can estimate by:

1. **Taste + effect**: If the tea is noticeably stimulating and slightly bitter, it likely has decent caffeine content.
2. **Leaf type**:
   - **Younger leaves = more caffeine**.
   - **Roasting = darker roast, less caffeine**.
3. **Compare to baseline**: Try brewing 1 gram (about a loose tablespoon) and see how your body responds.

---

### ⚠️ Notes
- **Do not consume yaupon berries**—only the **leaves** are safe.
- Yaupon is not as strong as coffee, but it’s **gentler and less acidic**.
- You can also **roast it lightly in a pan** like yerba maté to deepen the flavor and reduce bitterness.

## Assistant

Yaupon holly (*Ilex vomitoria*) is a **perennial evergreen shrub or small tree**—not seasonal or annual. Here's what that means for you:

---

### 🌿 **Key Facts About Yaupon Holly**
- **Evergreen**: Keeps its leaves year-round, so you can harvest whenever needed.
- **Perennial**: Lives for decades and doesn’t die back seasonally like an annual.
- **New growth** (spring and early summer) has the **highest caffeine content** and is most tender for tea.

---

### 🍃 Best Time to Harvest
- **Spring and early summer**: Tender new leaves, highest caffeine.
- **Fall and winter**: Leaves still usable, just slightly tougher and sometimes more bitter.

---

### 🌳 Growth Notes
- Can grow as a shrub or pruned into a small tree.
- Thrives in USDA zones **7–10** (which includes most of the South, including North Carolina where you are).
- Wild yaupon often grows in sandy soils and forest edges.
