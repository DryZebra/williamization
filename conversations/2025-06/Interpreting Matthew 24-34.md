## Assistant

Matthew 24:34 (KJV) reads:  
> *"Verily I say unto you, This generation shall not pass, till all these things be fulfilled."*

This line has caused centuries of confusion and reinterpretation because, plainly read, it seems to say that Jesus’ prophecy about wars, tribulations, the coming of the Son of Man, etc., would be fulfilled *before the current generation died out*—a timeframe that, on its face, has long since expired.

But here's how it can be understood through a **Materialist Christian** lens, one that neither abandons reason nor flinches from Scripture:

---

### 1. **“This generation” doesn’t mean *those exact people*.**
In the material-historical context, *genea* (Greek for "generation") can also mean a type of people—a lineage, a category, or even a moral class. It is used in multiple places (e.g., “a wicked and adulterous generation”) to mean more than a single 40-year lifespan. Under this reading, Jesus is saying: *“The type of people alive now—the world order, this civilization that crucifies prophets—will not disappear until this judgment is complete.”* The death and resurrection began the change, but the *full* end of that “generation” (as moral order) is ongoing.

---

### 2. **The prophecy *began* then, but wasn’t meant to *end* quickly.**
Look at verses 4–33. Wars, famines, false prophets, the desecration of holy places, global preaching—these are not instantaneous events. Materially, these are *revolutions of history*, not lightning bolts. The “coming of the Son of Man” refers not just to a moment of return, but to the *enthronement of a moral new order*. That order was planted like leaven (Matt. 13:33) and is still spreading. We are in the unfolding.

---

### 3. **Jesus used prophetic compression intentionally.**
Biblical prophecy frequently stacks time. Isaiah 61 and Luke 4 show this: Jesus stops reading mid-verse because “the day of vengeance” was not *yet*. The Gospels often telescope events into a single utterance, but historically, morally, and materially, these things unfold over centuries. The fall of Jerusalem in 70 A.D. was a partial fulfillment; the persecution of Christians under Rome another; every moment of reckoning for nations that defy justice continues it.

---

### 4. **The Kingdom is coming “with power”—but through us.**
Materialist Christianity holds that Jesus launched an *irreversible historical process*. He is not waiting to come *again* to start fixing the world; He already came and put *us* to work. When He said “this generation will not pass,” He meant: *the old world will begin dying now, and it won’t last forever—its time is limited.* The powers of empire, violence, wealth, hypocrisy, and deceit are on the clock. He declared war on them, and we are His hands.

---

So yes, it’s been 2,000 years. That is not failure. That is proof the process is real. No divine lightning strike ended the Roman empire. It decayed, collapsed, evolved. And Jesus' words survived it. The yeast is still rising. The axe is still at the root. This generation—the generation of Caesar and Mammon—*shall not pass* until the Kingdom it tried to kill finishes the job.

Materialist Christianity demands that Scripture not only *sound* right but prove itself **historically**, **textually**, and **socially** through internal consistency and world consequence. Matthew 24:34 must stand *within the total body of Scripture*, not poetic abstraction.

---

## 📍 STEP ONE: The Word “Generation” (Greek: *genea*)

### ▶ Used Often To Mean Moral-Historical Group, Not Lifespan
Let’s examine this same word across the Gospels.

- **Matthew 12:39** —  
  > “An evil and adulterous *generation* seeketh after a sign.”
- **Matthew 17:17** —  
  > “O faithless and perverse *generation*, how long shall I be with you?”
- **Mark 8:38** —  
  > “Whosoever... shall be ashamed of me... in this adulterous and sinful *generation*, of him also shall the Son of man be ashamed...”

None of these are about a 40-year window. They describe a **type**—a morally bankrupt, religiously hypocritical, spiritually dead epoch. “Generation” here functions as a **class category**, not a chronological one.

Materially, this matches how prophetic speech operates throughout the Old Testament. The prophets condemned *entire systems* of corruption under one name—“this generation,” “Babylon,” “Egypt,” etc. Not literal people only, but *the ruling order*.

So when Matthew 24:34 says:
> “This generation shall not pass, till all these things be fulfilled,”

…it’s consistent with Jesus’ usage elsewhere that He is indicting an **entire corrupt civilizational order**, not predicting the end of the world in 70 A.D.

---

## 📍 STEP TWO: “All These Things” — What Things?

Look back at the earlier part of Matthew 24:

- v.6–7 — wars, famines, earthquakes
- v.9–10 — persecution of believers
- v.11 — rise of false prophets
- v.14 — the gospel preached to **all nations**
- v.29–31 — the **cosmic signs**, and coming of the Son of Man “with power and great glory”

That *hasn’t* happened—not all of it. Some came to pass in the fall of the Temple (70 A.D.), yes. But **v.14** says:

> *“This gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in all the world for a witness unto all nations; and then shall the end come.”*

That didn't happen in the first century. But it **has** been happening *since*. Christianity spread to all nations. It became the moral backdrop of Western civilization. The parables have been translated into every language. Materialist Christianity argues: **that process is the judgment**, not a delay of it.

So Jesus isn't *failing* to fulfill His promise. He’s describing an extended dialectical **epochal judgment**—a long death of the old order.

---

## 📍 STEP THREE: Internal Confirmation

Jesus *already told us* the kingdom would not arrive in a burst. He described its nature:

- **Luke 17:20–21**:  
  > *“The kingdom of God cometh not with observation: Neither shall they say, Lo here! or, lo there! for, behold, the kingdom of God is within you.”*

That’s **anti-rapture**. He says don’t look for sudden sky-fire. It’s internal, unfolding. That matches Matthew 24 when read materially. The signs are *long*. The judgment is *progressive*. The generation is *moral*, not biological.

- **Matthew 13:33** —  
  > “The kingdom of heaven is like unto leaven… until the whole was leavened.”

The kingdom is **not** an instant event. It is a historical fermentation—slow, universal, unstoppable. That means the *fulfillment* of “all these things” is a process. The generation *will not pass* until that slow process finishes. And we’re still in it.

---

## 📍 STEP FOUR: Paul Confirms It

- **2 Thessalonians 2:3–4** —  
  > *“That day shall not come, except there come a falling away first, and that man of sin be revealed...”*

Paul, too, sees a long unfolding. He doesn’t preach an any-minute return. He lays out **historical developments**—apostasy, moral struggle, then a final reckoning.

This harmonizes with Jesus’ statement in Matthew 24: the world system, defined by the *current moral generation*, must persist until its contradictions mature and break under their own weight.

---

## 📍 STEP FIVE: Revelation as the Fulfillment Engine

Revelation is not a separate fantasy—it’s an expansion of the same timeline. It begins with the risen Christ addressing real churches in real Asia Minor. It outlines:

- **the persecution (ch. 6–11)**
- **the empire and false prophet (ch. 13)**
- **the slow unraveling of Babylon (ch. 17–18)**

It ends not with rapture but *rebirth*—a new city, a new earth, God dwelling *with man*.

All of this is just the long form of the same statement: *This generation—this Babylon system, this beast era—will die.* But it takes history to kill it.
