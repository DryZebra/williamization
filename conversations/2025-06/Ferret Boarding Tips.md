# Ferret Boarding Tips

I have to board Henry my ferret for a week. The place I bought him offers boarding for a minimal fee. But I'm worried that he's going to think we brought him back or that we abandoned him. A week is a long time. I don't want him to be traumatized. He runs around the house constantly, little dude doesn't even know what a cage is anymore!

## Assistant

It makes complete sense to be worried—Henry's not just a pet, he's your tiny, slinky roommate. And for a creature as curious and emotionally aware as a ferret, a sudden return to a cage (especially somewhere that *smells* like where he came from) can be confusing and even a little heartbreaking.

Here’s what you can do to make the boarding experience less traumatic:

---

### 🧳 **1. Bring “home” with him**
- **Blanket or hammock**: Anything that smells like your house or your scent—especially his usual bedding—can be hugely comforting.
- **Worn shirt**: A used T-shirt of yours (unwashed) in his sleeping area will help him feel like you’re still there.

---

### 🐾 **2. Leave a scent trail**
- If possible, rub a cloth on some areas he loves at home (his tunnel, your couch corner, his play area) and place that with him in the cage. Familiar scents can drastically reduce stress.

---

### 🧸 **3. Pack his favorite toys**
- Not just *any* toy—his weird little obsession toy. You know the one. Bring the comfort item.

---

### 🕰️ **4. Ask about playtime**
- Check if the boarding place allows supervised free-roam time, or if you can pay a little more to ensure he gets regular outside-of-cage time. Stress drops dramatically when they can move around.

---

### 📽️ **5. Create continuity**
- The day before you drop him off, try a “practice cage day.” Have him spend a few hours in a travel cage with the blanket, shirt, and toy you’re planning to send. It’ll build familiarity and reduce shock.

---

### ❤️ **6. When you return, go all out**
- Greet him like a hero returned from war. Give him all the love, cuddles, and playtime he can handle. He *will* remember you. If he sulks or acts aloof at first, don’t worry—ferrets have pride, but they don’t hold grudges.

---

And no, he won’t think you’ve abandoned him forever. But he *will* probably be confused for a day or two. If you treat the return like a celebration and make home feel like his kingdom again, he’ll bounce right back—and maybe cling to you even more.
