# Matthew 1:6 Infidelity

## User

In Matthew 1:6 is that infidelity?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Yes—Matthew 1:6 contains a subtle but powerful reference to **infidelity**, **betrayal**, and **moral failure**, especially when read through a Materialist Christian lens.

Here’s the verse:

> **"And Jesse begat David the king; and David the king begat Solomon of her that had been the wife of Urias;"** (KJV)

Instead of naming Bathsheba, the verse deliberately refers to her as **"her that had been the wife of Urias."**

This phrasing does *two* things at once:

1. **Preserves David’s royal lineage** (he is still the father of Solomon and ancestor of Christ),  
2. **Exposes the sin**: it reminds the reader that Solomon's mother was **married to another man**, Uriah the Hittite, before David arranged his death to cover up adultery (see 2 Samuel 11).

So yes—this is a **direct acknowledgment of infidelity** and murder, encoded in a genealogy. It tells us that even the bloodline of Christ includes deep **moral brokenness** and **human sin**. Materially, it says: *even kings sin,* and the Kingdom of God comes through **repentance, not purity**.

This sets the tone for the entire gospel—Christ will not come through perfection, but through *history*, *conflict*, and *grace through failure*.
