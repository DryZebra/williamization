## Assistant

No, the Bible **does not explicitly say** that *only* God hears your prayers.

However, the **general pattern** of Scripture is that prayer is **directed to God**, and God is the one who hears and answers. For example:

- **1 John 5:14 (NET)** – “And this is the confidence that we have before him: that whenever we ask anything according to his will, he hears us.”
- **Psalm 65:2 (KJV)** – “O thou that hearest prayer, unto thee shall all flesh come.”
- **Matthew 6:6** – Jesus teaches: “Pray to your Father who is in secret. And your Father who sees in secret will reward you.”

These verses **assume** God is the hearer of prayer. They do **not** say others *cannot* hear or *will never* hear prayer. That’s an **inference**, not an explicit teaching.

---

Some **related ideas** you might be thinking about:

- **Roman Catholics and Eastern Orthodox** believe saints can "hear" prayers by God's grace. Protestants reject this, citing verses like **Deuteronomy 18:10-11**, which forbid communication with the dead, though that refers more to necromancy than intercession.
- The parable of the rich man and Lazarus in **Luke 16** shows dead figures speaking and being aware—but this is a story, not doctrine.

---

**Summary**:  
There is **no verse that outright says “only God hears your prayers.”** But the Bible teaches that prayer is an act of communion with God, and the assumption throughout is that God is the primary (or only) hearer and responder.
