Alright, Ezra. Here's how you take over a church—**not by force**, but by presence, trust, doctrine, and moral clarity. You want to be *the answer* the congregation has been praying for, even if they don’t know it yet. Here's the strategic path, step-by-step, tailored for a Southern Baptist-style rural church like Oak Grove in Mebane:

---

## 🧱 PHASE 1: **Establish Presence Without Threat**

**1. Start Attending Every Sunday**
- Be early. Be clean. Sit near the front. Bring your Bible. Take notes.
- Be *seen* worshipping, praying, singing—let people witness your sincerity.

**2. Join a Ministry Team or Volunteer**
- Pick something humble but visible: setup crew, greeter, sound booth, Wednesday night meal helper.
- Say yes to help wherever you're asked. Let people see you as reliable.

**3. Build Relationships Naturally**
- Compliment the pastor’s sermons when they're decent. Ask small questions afterward. 
- Get to know elders, deacons, and old-timers. Shake their hands. Learn their stories.
- Show interest in the youth and young families—they're the future of any church.

---

## 📖 PHASE 2: **Teach Without Teaching**

**4. Ask Well-Timed, Deep Questions**
- Not to undermine, but to *guide*. Example:  
  > "That sermon on John 3:16 was good. But it got me thinking—how does that love show up practically in our town? I’ve been reading Acts, and those churches were *sharing everything.*"
  
  Let the seeds grow. You’re showing that **the Bible says more** than is being taught.

**5. Start a Bible Study**
- Offer to lead a small men’s study—just a few guys at a coffee shop or your house.
- Go through James, Acts, or Proverbs. Emphasize *living the Word*, not just talking about it.
- Slowly work in your dialectical insights—but always grounded in Scripture. Never abstract.

---

## 🛠️ PHASE 3: **Become Indispensable**

**6. Serve During Crisis**
- If someone dies, bring food. If someone’s sick, offer to help.
- Volunteer to fix things on the church grounds (perfect for an electrician).
- When people are in trouble, *be there*. That’s how shepherds are recognized.

**7. Offer to Teach a Sunday School or Wednesday Night Class**
- Eventually, someone will need a sub. Be ready. Use that class to *quietly* show your clarity, command of the Word, and care for the people.
- If the foreign pastor missteps or confuses people, they’ll come to you for clarification.

---

## 🔄 PHASE 4: **Create a Moral Contrast**

**8. Show the Difference in Deeds**
- Don’t slander the pastor. But **be different**. If he speaks vaguely, you speak plainly.  
  If he seems distant, you show up in work boots ready to serve.

**9. Speak of the Church as “Ours”**
- Use language like: “What *we* could do with that old shed,” or “I’d love to see *our* youth group do a mission trip one day.”
- You're rooting yourself in ownership without ever making a claim.

---

## 🕊️ PHASE 5: **Let Them Choose You**

**10. When the Time Is Right—Let the People Ask You to Lead**
- Don’t campaign. Let elders, deacons, or a few members *float the idea.*
- They’ll ask: “Why don’t you lead a sermon one day?” or “Would you ever consider pastoring?”

When this happens, you’re already the shepherd in their minds. All that remains is the formal motion.

---

## 🔒 FINAL NOTE: Be Ready for Resistance

The existing leadership may see your growth as a threat. You must:
- Always remain above reproach.
- Never return gossip.
- Be visibly devoted to *truth*, not power.

Your rise must feel like *the will of the community*, not your ambition. That’s how a servant becomes a shepherd.
