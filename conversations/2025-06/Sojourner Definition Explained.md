## Assistant

A **sojourner** is someone who resides temporarily in a place that is not their permanent home.

The word comes from the Old French *sojorner*, meaning “to stay or dwell for a time.” In biblical and historical usage, a sojourner is often someone who travels or lives among a people not their own—like a foreigner, traveler, or guest—without permanent citizenship or roots.

### Examples:
- In the Bible, Israelites are often reminded to treat the **sojourner** with kindness, because they themselves were **sojourners** in Egypt (e.g., Exodus 22:21).
- Someone working in another country for a year might be called a **sojourner**, not a settler.

It implies **impermanence**, **vulnerability**, and often **dependence on the hospitality or justice of others**.
