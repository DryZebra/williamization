You're not wrong to be skeptical, and you're right that Orthodox Christianity—especially in its Greek and Russian expressions—is traditionally very conservative on issues like abortion. But let’s walk through this **carefully and truthfully**, not just with knee-jerk reactions or assumptions.

### ✅ What the Orthodox Church Teaches Officially:
The vast majority of Eastern Orthodox jurisdictions—Greek Orthodox, Russian Orthodox, Antiochian, Serbian, etc.—**condemn abortion as a grave sin**, treating it as the taking of a human life. This stance has been consistent since the early Church and is rooted in patristic writings and canon law.

**Some examples:**
- The *Didache* (1st–2nd century) explicitly condemns abortion.
- The *Council in Trullo* (691 AD), Canon 91, equates abortion with murder.
- The Russian Orthodox Church’s official stance (2000): “Abortion is a grave sin equated with murder and repentance is required.”

### 🤔 So Why Would an Orthodox Post a “Prayer After Abortion”?
This doesn’t mean Orthodox Christianity offers *no path of repentance*. Quite the opposite:

> **Repentance is at the heart of Orthodoxy.**

In practice, many Orthodox confessors and spiritual fathers will walk deeply wounded people through a long and painful—but ultimately healing—process of repentance. This may include:
- A recognition of the sin (even using harsh language like “murder”).
- Mourning and prayer.
- Seeking forgiveness from God.
- Often, a **naming of the child** (this is real in Orthodox praxis).

So a prayer like **“I killed my son, name him, forgive me”** would not be uncommon in the context of **deep repentance**, not in the sense of justifying the act, but in **mourning it fully**. The Orthodox Church sees the mother not as an unredeemable monster, but as someone who—through repentance—can find healing in Christ.

### 📜 Is That TikTok Orthodox Doctrine?
The video in your screenshot was posted by an account called **One Baptism** and subtitled **"A Cry to the Mother of God."** That language suggests deep Marian devotion and possibly poetic repentance. But if it stops at **“I killed my son”** and doesn’t clearly affirm **the sinfulness of the act**, it could be leaning into emotionalism or unorthodox sentiment.

Let me say this clearly:

> If the prayer presents the abortion as spiritually tragic but materially fine—or fails to call it sin—it’s not Orthodox. But if it’s a prayer of honest repentance for a grave sin, that’s Orthodox to the bone.
