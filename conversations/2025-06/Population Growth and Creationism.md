### If you want a more detailed explanation:

#### 🔸 **1. Historical population data supports slow growth**
- Estimates place the human population around:
  - **10,000 BCE** (start of agriculture): ~1–10 million
  - **1 CE**: ~170–300 million
  - **1800 CE**: ~1 billion
  - **Today**: ~8 billion

That shows incredibly slow growth for 99.9% of human history, then a sudden explosion **only after modern sanitation and industrial agriculture.**

#### 🔸 **2. Population growth is not constant**
Young Earth Creationists often use a naive exponential formula like:

\[
P = P_0 \cdot r^t
\]

…and assume a high growth rate (like 2–3%) over 4,000 years to get ~8 billion people. But this:
- Ignores plague, war, infertility, and massive death tolls.
- Ignores that population *declines* happen all the time.
- Would require growth rates *unheard of* in any pre-modern society.

#### 🔸 **3. Population evidence *supports* ancient humanity**
The fact that humanity only recently exploded in numbers matches archaeological, historical, and biological evidence — not a young Earth. A sudden 4000-year surge would leave unmistakable records — and we have no such evidence.

#### 🔸 **4. It’s projection, not observation**
They’re taking *modern growth rates* and projecting them backward into ancient history, rather than observing actual archaeological and demographic data.
