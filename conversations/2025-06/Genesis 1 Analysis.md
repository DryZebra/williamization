**Justification of Translation:**  
- **בְּרֵאשִׁית (bere’shith)** lacks a definite article, which opens the door to “at the beginning of [something]” rather than “in the beginning” in an absolute, metaphysical sense. The construction aligns better with a temporal material process: *when things began to be ordered* rather than the beginning of *being* itself.  
- **בָּרָא (bara)**, while traditionally rendered “created,” consistently implies a formative act — not out of nothing (ex nihilo) but shaping, distinguishing, or separating within existing chaos. The same verb is used later in contexts of human and animal shaping.  
- **אֱלֹהִים (Elohim)** is plural in form but behaves grammatically singular. Within the manuscript, it takes on the social function of power, decision, or direction — the one who orders or acts with authority.  
- **אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ** pairs two spheres: *shamayim* (the upper regions — sky, weather, what moves above), and *aretz* (the ground, what is stood upon, cultivated, or lived on). The phrase parallels structure and division — above/below — not a totality of “universe” but of *conditions for life*.  

Hence: “set in motion” better captures the implied division, ordering, and motion—not sudden poof-creation, but initiating ongoing transformation.

---

**Dialectical/Moral Motion:**  
This verse introduces motion out of stasis — the transformation from undifferentiated existence (as the next line will make clearer) into ordered conditions that allow life and meaning. The moral dialectic begins with the separation of realms — *above* and *below* — implying that meaning and judgment itself require distinctions to exist. God’s first act is *to divide*, not to explain, command, or even reveal. The moral world requires difference before it can develop.
