Let’s break it down with clarity and dialectical force:

---

### 🔍 1. **Premise: We can’t be fully certain of anything.**
This is textbook **epistemological skepticism**. Fine. It’s been around since Pyrrho, Descartes, and every freshman philosophy class.

But **radical skepticism** undermines *everything*, not just atheism or science—it would also undermine Theism. Yet somehow, it’s only *selectively* used here.

> ⚠️ **Red flag**: If uncertainty undermines knowledge of the natural world, it also invalidates *any claim* about a supernatural one.

---

### ⚖️ 2. **False Dichotomy: Brute factism *vs.* Theism**
This framing is flawed. It assumes only two options:
- **Brute Factism**: The universe "just is"
- **Theism**: The universe was created by a willful, immaterial agent

But this skips dozens of options:  
- Dialectical materialism  
- Cyclic cosmologies  
- Emergent or self-causing systems  
- Models with unknown priors, like Hartle–Hawking or bouncing cosmologies  

> ❗ The "two options" frame is a trick. It excludes non-theistic explanations a priori by pretending interpretation must collapse into "faith" the moment science goes quiet.

---

### 🧠 3. **Category Error: Epistemic humility ≠ faith-based license**
They say:
> "Since knowledge is filtered and uncertain, you must pick your metaphysical poison by faith."

This is like saying:  
> “Because microscopes are imperfect, you might as well guess what cells look like with your imagination.”

That’s not humility—it’s **abandonment of the dialectical method**.

> 🛠 **Materialist response**: Yes, knowledge is filtered—but it *still emerges* dialectically from material conditions. We refine models over time through contradiction, experimentation, and practice—not surrender.

---

### 🙏 4. **The “faith in something” argument**
This is rhetorical sleaze dressed as philosophy.

> “Even brute factism requires *faith* that the universe just is.”

No. That’s not *faith*—that’s a **placeholder**. A refusal to invent an answer without evidence.

It’s like saying:
> “Since you don’t know who stole your wallet, it’s faith to *not* believe it was a ghost.”

---

### 🔥 Your insight:
> “You can only make that claim *after* being introduced to God.”

Exactly. The conclusion isn’t born from epistemology—it’s born from *narrative familiarity*. Theism is only even an option once the idea of “God” is already culturally planted. The “ineffable” isn’t just *there*—it’s **introduced** through specific stories, traditions, and authority figures.

That’s not metaphysics—it’s **historical materialism** at work.
