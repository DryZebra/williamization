Here’s how you become a **licensed preacher** in the Southern Baptist Convention (SBC):

---

## 1. Understand that licensing is local, not denominational  
- **Licenses and ordinations are handled by your local church**, not by state conventions or the SBC itself. The SBC has no centralized licensing system citeturn0search0turn0search7.  
- A **license** is typically seen as a preliminary recognition of your calling—a church's way of allowing you to preach or lead certain ministries while evaluating you further citeturn0search0.  
- **Ordination** usually follows a call to ministry or a pastoral role, often after a licensing period citeturn0search0turn0search7.

---

## 2. Steps to be licensed by your at SBC church  
1. **Sense your calling**: Be confident that God is leading you toward preaching or pastoral ministry.
2. **Talk to leadership**: Share your sense of calling with your pastor or deacons and seek their guidance.
3. **Request a license**: Formally ask your church to license you for ministry.
4. **Church vote**: The congregation usually votes in a business meeting to approve the license.
5. **Receive a certificate**: Upon approval, you'll typically be presented with a “Certificate of License”—sometimes purchased from a Christian bookstore citeturn0search0.

---

## 3. What licensing enables you to do  
- Allows you to **preach from the pulpit**, **administer baptism and the Lord’s Supper**, and possibly meet other ministerial responsibilities depending on your church’s guidelines citeturn0search3turn0search0.  
- It is **not** the full pastoral standing—you usually **can’t officiate weddings** or fill all ordained roles, which are reserved for ordained ministers citeturn0search3.

---

## 4. Requirements and evaluation process  
- **Vary by church**, but most expect:  
  - A credible **testimony of faith**, demonstrating godly character.  
  - Belief in the **Baptist Faith & Message** (2000).  
  - Some may ask you to complete a theological or scriptural vetting process.  
- **Background checks** are increasingly common and encouraged during vetting citeturn0search10.

---

## 5. After licensing: towards ordination  
- Many churches require you to serve with your license for a time before considering ordination.  
- **Ordination** often includes:  
  1. A **call** to a specific ministry role (e.g., as pastor).  
  2. Approval to form an **ordination council**—a group of pastors and deacons who interview and evaluate you.  
  3. A congregational **vote** to ordain citeturn0search0turn0search7.

### 🛠 Summary: Here’s a quick checklist  

| Step | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **1. Sensing your calling** | Pray, seek counsel, confirm God's call to preach |
| **2. Talk to leaders** | Begin a conversation with your pastor/deacons |
| **3. Request license** | Formally ask your church to license you |
| **4. Church vote** | Congregation approves in a meeting |
| **5. Receive certificate** | You’re officially licensed for ministry |
| **6. Serve & prove** | Preach/share ministry, undergo vetting |
| **7. Move to ordination** | If called, go through council and ordination ceremony |

---

### 🌟 Final Thought  
There’s no “SBC license” you apply for through the convention—it’s about your **home church’s discernment and affirmation**. If you’re sensing a call, **start with conversation and involvement in your local church**. From there, they can guide you through licensing and ordination.
