### 🔨 The Knights of Labor: A Brief History

#### 🛡️ Origins and Vision
- **Founded**: 1869 in Philadelphia by **Uriah Stephens**, originally a secret society.
- **Peak**: 1880s, with around **700,000 members** by 1886.
- **Philosophy**: Aimed to unite all “producers,” regardless of trade, race, or gender. Wanted to create a cooperative commonwealth through:
  - 8-hour workday
  - Abolition of child and convict labor
  - Equal pay for equal work
  - Public ownership of railroads and telegraphs
  - Worker-run cooperatives

#### ⚔️ Structure
- Unlike trade unions, the Knights organized workers **by industry** rather than by craft.
- Included skilled and unskilled workers, women, and Black workers—**very rare for the time**.

---

### 📜 Industrial Involvement

#### 🏭 General Industry Influence
- The Knights tried to **organize entire industries**, including:
  - **Railroads** (most famous)
  - **Coal mining**
  - **Textiles**
  - **Agriculture**
  - **Printing trades**
- Their broad inclusion meant they often tried to act like a **shadow government of labor**, issuing boycotts, supporting strikes, and lobbying for federal reform.

#### 🧾 If You Meant the “Note” Industry (Possible Interpretations):

##### 1. **Financial Notes / Banking / Treasury**
- The Knights **opposed monopolistic banking** and wanted the federal government to control currency issuance.
- Supported the **Greenback movement**, which wanted paper money not backed by gold, to help debtors and workers.
- Wanted **cooperative financial institutions**, like worker-run banks and credit systems.

##### 2. **Printing / Publishing / Paper**
- The Knights did organize **printers and typesetters**, but faced competition from the **International Typographical Union (ITU)**.
- Workers in this area often preferred craft unions, which had stronger control over wages and job standards than the Knights' broad-based approach.
- Still, the Knights made gains among low-paid press workers and paper mill laborers.

##### 3. **Stationery / Notepad Manufacturing**

- However, as part of their general sweep into **light manufacturing**, it’s possible they briefly organized such shops in cities like Philadelphia, Chicago, or New York.

---

### 📉 Decline
- **Haymarket Affair (1886)**: Though not responsible, the Knights were **blamed** for the bombing at a labor rally in Chicago, sparking an anti-labor backlash.
- Leadership under **Terence Powderly** was weak and slow to respond to capitalist resistance.
- By the late 1880s, more disciplined trade unions like the **AFL (American Federation of Labor)** took over their base.

---

### 📌 Legacy
- Pioneered inclusive organizing across trades, races, and genders.
- Helped normalize the 8-hour workday demand.
- Laid groundwork for later labor organizing and inspired movements like **Populism** and the **Cooperative Commonwealth Federation**.

The **Greensboro Massacre** took place on **November 3, 1979**, in **Greensboro, North Carolina**.

### What happened:
- Members of the **Communist Workers Party (CWP)** were holding a **"Death to the Klan"** rally in a Black neighborhood known as Morningside Homes.
- A caravan of **Ku Klux Klan members and neo-Nazis** drove to the event, pulled out firearms, and opened fire.
- **Five anti-racist organizers** were killed, most of whom were CWP members.
- The police were notably **absent** during the shooting, despite being informed of potential violence.

### Aftermath:
- Multiple trials resulted in **acquittals** by all-white juries, despite video evidence of the shooters.
- The massacre highlighted the connections between **white supremacists, state actors, and anti-communist repression** in the South.

It's one of the clearest cases of **political violence against Communists** in U.S. history and remains a symbol of the risks faced by Southern leftists organizing against racial and economic injustice.
