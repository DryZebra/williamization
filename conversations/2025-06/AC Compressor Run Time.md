# AC Compressor Run Time

## User

How long is too long for an AC compressor to run?

## Assistant



## Assistant

An AC compressor running **continuously** or for **too long** can indicate a problem, but what's “too long” depends on conditions:

### ✅ **Normal operation**
- On **hot days (90°F+)**, the compressor may run **continuously for hours**—this can be normal if it’s struggling to keep up.
- In **milder temps (70s–80s°F)**, run cycles should typically last **15–30 minutes**, with breaks in between.
- **Well-sized and maintained systems** will cycle off after reaching the set temperature.

---

### ⚠️ **Too long if…**
- It runs **nonstop for hours in moderate weather**.
- It's been running **all day without cycling off**, even if the set temperature is reached.
- The **indoor temp stays above the thermostat setting**, even after extended runtime.
- You notice **warm air**, **reduced airflow**, **ice on the evaporator coil**, or a **higher-than-usual electric bill**.

---

### 🛠 Possible causes of overlong compressor run:
- Dirty air filter (restricting airflow)
- Low refrigerant (leak)
- Undersized AC unit
- Thermostat issues (miscalibrated or poor placement)
- Dirty coils (condenser or evaporator)
- Poor insulation or air leaks in the house
- Extremely high outdoor humidity or temps

---

### ⏱ Rule of thumb:
> **If your AC compressor runs for more than 1 hour without a break in mild temperatures**, or it never shuts off during a full cycle, it’s worth investigating.

In 95°F+ heat, **12+ hour daily run times** can actually be **normal**, especially if:

- Your thermostat is set much lower than outdoor temps (e.g. 72°F inside vs. 95°F+ outside)
- Your home has **poor insulation**, sun-facing windows, or air leaks

---

### ✅ Not a concern *if*:
- The **indoor temperature eventually reaches** the setpoint and stays steady
- Air from the vents is **cool (not warm or weak)**
- **Electric bill is high but not extreme** for this kind of heatwave
- The compressor **does eventually cycle off** late at night

---

### ⚠️ Concerning *if*:
- It **never reaches** the set temp (e.g. always stuck at 76° when set to 72°)
- It blows **warm or weak air**
- You see **ice buildup**, water leaks, or hear **weird noises**
- There's a **spike in energy use** beyond past years in similar heat

---

### 🧰 Pro tip:
If you want to ease the load without calling for service yet:
- **Raise the thermostat** 2–3°F during peak sun (e.g. 75–78°F)
- Use **ceiling fans** to help circulate
- Close blinds/curtains during the day
- Change the **air filter**
- Hose down the **outdoor condenser coil** (gently, if dusty)

## Assistant

Perfect—since you're an electrician, you’ve got access to options most people can’t safely touch. Here are some **legit hacks, optimizations, and borderline cheats** you can do as a skilled tradesman with central AC in a trailer:

---

### ⚙️ **1. Hard Start Kit**
- **What:** Capacitor + potential relay combo to give the compressor a faster, cleaner startup.
- **Why:** Reduces wear, helps startup in extreme heat or low voltage.
- **DIY Level:** Easy for you. ~$15–$50.
- **When:** Especially useful on older compressors or if lights dim on startup.

---

### 🌡️ **2. Adjust Thermostat Differential or Cycle Delay**
- **What:** If your thermostat allows it (Nest, Ecobee, Honeywell T-series), reduce the swing differential (e.g., from ±1.5°F to ±0.5°F).
- **Why:** Keeps the house cooler with less spike recovery.
- **Caveat:** Increases runtime slightly, but improves comfort and compressor efficiency.
- **Advanced:** Some thermostats have a *compressor minimum off time* setting you can tweak too.

---

### 🧽 **3. Deep Clean Outdoor Coil**
- **What:** Shut off power, use coil cleaner (foaming), rinse from inside out.
- **Why:** Even a thin film of dust can cause 10–30% efficiency loss. Especially bad in mobile home parks near roads.
- **Bonus:** Check for **level**—a tilted unit can cause oil return problems.

---

### 🔁 **4. Rewire Fan Relay for 'Fan On' Circulation**
- **What:** If the fan relay is accessible, you can run the blower on low speed for continuous recirculation during off cycles.
- **Why:** Helps average temps, reduce humidity, and reduce perceived temp spikes.
- **Caveat:** Slight bump in energy use.

---

### 💨 **5. Check for Underrated Breakers or Loose Lugs**
- **What:** Verify that the disconnect, breaker, and lugs are tight, clean, and properly sized.
- **Why:** Loose or oxidized connections drop voltage under load = higher amps = worse compressor efficiency.
- **Tip:** Check voltage drop across contactor under load too.

---

### 🪟 **6. Reflective Film or Bubble Wrap on Sunny Windows**
- **What:** Apply reflective bubble film (interior side) to all sun-facing windows.
- **Why:** Mobile homes = radiant heat traps. This trick can drop indoor temp by **3–5°F** on hot days.
- **DIY Level:** Dirt cheap, no tools needed.

---

### 🧱 **7. Temporarily Add a Shaded “Doghouse” Over the Condenser**
- **What:** Build a lean-to or vented shroud that provides **shade**, not enclosure.
- **Why:** A shaded condenser can operate **10–15% more efficiently**. But **DO NOT restrict airflow**.
- **DIY Level:** Use treated lumber or even an old shipping pallet + tarp for now.

---

- Build a small ESPHome temp sensor to log real indoor/outdoor delta and *actually chart your SEER* under real conditions.
