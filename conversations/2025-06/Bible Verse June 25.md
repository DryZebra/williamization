Psalm 73:26 — *“My flesh and my heart faileth: but God is the strength of my heart, and my portion for ever”* — finds deep resonance throughout the New Testament, especially in passages where the frailty of the human body is contrasted with enduring spiritual strength through Christ. Here are the most direct echoes:

---

### **1. 2 Corinthians 4:16–18 (KJV)**
> *"For which cause we faint not; but though our outward man perish, yet the inward man is renewed day by day.  
For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory;  
While we look not at the things which are seen, but at the things which are not seen: for the things which are seen are temporal; but the things which are not seen are eternal."*

Paul is almost directly paraphrasing the Psalmist’s realization: the perishing flesh is not the measure of man’s strength, for our hope and renewal come from an unseen, eternal truth—what Materialist Christianity would call the moral inheritance and historical direction embodied by Christ.

---

### **2. Philippians 3:8–10 (KJV)**
> *"Yea doubtless, and I count all things but loss for the excellency of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord: for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and do count them but dung, that I may win Christ... That I may know him, and the power of his resurrection, and the fellowship of his sufferings, being made conformable unto his death;"*

Paul speaks of losing everything, including the confidence of the flesh (v. 3–4), and finding in Christ the true portion — the “power of his resurrection,” the strength that persists even when heart and body fail.

---

### **3. Hebrews 13:5–6 (KJV)**
> *"...be content with such things as ye have: for he hath said, I will never leave thee, nor forsake thee.  
So that we may boldly say, The Lord is my helper, and I will not fear what man shall do unto me."*

This parallels the *portion forever* language. In Psalm 73, “portion” refers to inheritance and sustenance. Hebrews reframes this as *confidence in the Lord's presence* — a legacy not of land or flesh, but of enduring moral companionship through Christ.

---

### **4. Romans 8:18 (KJV)**
> *"For I reckon that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us."*

Again: the flesh fails, but the future — the portion — is not bodily comfort, but participation in the revealed goodness of God, echoed through the life of Christ and continuing in His Church.

---

These verses function as New Testament expansions on the idea in Psalm 73:26. Materially, they teach that our strength and portion are not found in worldly reward or bodily resilience, but in the shared suffering, moral inheritance, and redemptive motion that comes from aligning with Christ. That is the portion forever.
