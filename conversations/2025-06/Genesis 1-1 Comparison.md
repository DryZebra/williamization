### 📘 Justification:

- **בְּרֵאשִׁית** (*bereshit*): Ambiguous whether absolute (“in the beginning”) or construct (“in the beginning of…”). Materially, it functions as a temporal **initial framing**—not necessarily the beginning of time, but of **intentional ordering**. I rendered it “at the start of the ordering” to reflect this.

- **בָּרָא** (*bara’*): Always used with God as subject, but never explicitly means “create from nothing.” Instead, it implies **divine designation or setting apart**—a **functional** separation or formation. This is confirmed by repeated patterns later in Genesis 1 (e.g. light/dark, land/sea). Hence, "set apart" is more faithful than "created" in material terms.

- **אֱלֹהִים** (*Elohim*): Grammatical plural but semantically singular. Materially, this implies a **figure of central authority**, not a metaphysical doctrine of the Trinity or plurality. The term socially marks God as **ultimate sovereign**—the one whose command organizes chaos.

- **אֵת הַשָּׁמַיִם וְאֵת הָאָרֶץ** (*et hashamayim ve'et ha'aretz*): A merism meaning **everything above and below**—culturally understood as the complete spatial domain. This aligns with NET’s “ordered universe.”

---

There is no moral judgment yet, but a fundamental shift from unordered being to ordered possibility. It’s a **dividing line** between “was” and “will be.”
