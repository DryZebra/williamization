What's with the controversy on this in "He" being translated as "God" in the KJV?

## Assistant

**Short version up front:**  
The controversy is whether 1 Timothy 3:16 says “He was revealed in the flesh” (**ὃς**, “who/He”) or “God was revealed in the flesh” (**θεός**, “God”). The KJV says “God,” but most modern translations (and earliest Greek manuscripts) say “He.” This isn’t a trivial difference—it reflects a real scribal change, not a mere translation error.

---

**Manuscript-internal evidence (from the Greek NT file):**

- **Earliest/majority Greek manuscripts** read **ὃς** (“who/He”).  
- **Later Byzantine/medieval manuscripts** show **θεός** (“God”)—which the KJV translators used.
- The KJV’s “God was manifest in the flesh” comes from a textual tradition that *changed* the original pronoun to “God,” most likely to make the Christological claim stronger or more explicit.

**Why did this happen?**
- In Greek uncials (capital script), the abbreviation for “God” (ΘΣ) and “who” (ΟΣ) are almost identical; a small line (a “titlo” or horizontal stroke) over ΘΣ makes the difference.
- Scribes sometimes mistook one for the other, especially as church doctrine around Christ’s divinity developed and made the verse a target for “clarification.”

---

**Material, cultural, and social function:**

- **Original function (ὃς/“He”):**  
  The verse as originally written is a poetic summary of Jesus’ life and movement, focusing on the *event* and *person*, not forcing a metaphysical formula.
- **Later function (θεός/“God”):**  
  When the word was changed to “God,” it made the passage an explicit theological proof-text—turning a lived reality (“He was revealed in the flesh”) into a doctrinal assertion (“God was revealed in the flesh”).

**Why does it matter?**  
- The original (“He”) leaves the identity as something witnessed, lived, and experienced, aligning with Paul’s habit of describing Christ in functional, historical terms.
- The altered (“God”) short-circuits the dialectic, making the “mystery” less mysterious—now it’s a direct claim about metaphysical status, not social motion.

---

**Dialectical analysis:**  
- **ὃς/“He”:** Preserves the tension and the process: the mystery is *seen* as it unfolds in the person and life of Christ.
- **θεός/“God”:** Collapses the tension, asserting the answer as dogma—reflecting the needs of a church in doctrinal conflict, rather than the original social dynamic.

---

**Summary Table:**  
| Reading  | Manuscripts         | Function                           | KJV/Tradition      | Materialist Motion              |
|----------|--------------------|------------------------------------|--------------------|-------------------------------|
| ὃς “He”  | Early, best Greek  | Personal, event-focused mystery    | Most modern Bibles | Preserves lived, dialectical tension |
| θεός “God” | Later, Byzantine   | Doctrinal, metaphysical assertion  | KJV                | Collapses process into dogma         |

---

**In conclusion:**  
The KJV’s reading (“God”) reflects a later scribal clarification, likely motivated by the desire to reinforce church doctrine. The earliest, most robust reading (“He”) preserves the social and dialectical motion of the text, making the verse about the lived, unfolding mystery, not a metaphysical statement forced onto the reader.
