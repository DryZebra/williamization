Many of the churches that received letters in the New Testament—particularly those mentioned in Revelation and the Pauline epistles—no longer exist in the form they did in the 1st century. However, here's what we know about their historical and present status:

### 1. **Churches in Revelation (The Seven Churches of Asia)**
- **Ephesus** – Once a major center of Christianity; the ancient city is in modern-day Turkey and in ruins. The church as a physical and organized body no longer exists.
- **Smyrna** – Modern-day İzmir, Turkey. There is still a small Christian presence, including Orthodox churches.
- **Pergamum** – Also in modern Turkey, near Bergama. The original church is gone; archaeological remains exist.
- **Thyatira** – Modern-day Akhisar, Turkey. No known continuation of the ancient church, but Christian minorities have passed through over time.
- **Sardis** – Near modern Sart, Turkey. The ancient city is ruins; no active church.
- **Philadelphia** – Now Alaşehir, Turkey. Some Christian communities existed into the Ottoman period; today, little remains.
- **Laodicea** – Near Denizli, Turkey. Completely ruins; no active church.

These churches were in what is now a predominantly Muslim region, and Christianity largely disappeared from this area due to conquest, migration, and conversion over centuries.

### 2. **Pauline Churches**
- **Corinth (1 & 2 Corinthians)** – Ancient Corinth is in Greece. Modern Corinth has churches, but the original community does not persist in unbroken succession.
- **Galatia (Galatians)** – A region in central Turkey. The churches are not traceable today.
- **Philippi (Philippians)** – In northern Greece. The ruins remain, and there is a modern Orthodox presence nearby.
- **Thessalonica (1 & 2 Thessalonians)** – Now Thessaloniki, Greece. A major Christian city today, mostly Greek Orthodox.
- **Rome (Romans)** – The church in Rome continued and evolved into the Roman Catholic Church, according to tradition.
- **Colossae (Colossians)** – Ruins only; no active Christian community.
- **Ephesus (also received a Pauline letter)** – As above, ruins remain.

### 3. **Other Letters**
- **Hebrews** – Not addressed to a specific church but to Jewish Christians broadly; the community as such has dispersed.
- **James, Peter, John, Jude** – These are general epistles to wide audiences.

### Summary
Most of the original church *locations* still exist as ruins or modern cities. A few, like Rome and Thessaloniki, still have vibrant Christian communities, though often doctrinally distinct and historically evolved. Most of the churches in Asia Minor (modern Turkey) have vanished due to centuries of political, religious, and cultural transformation.

If you're asking whether those **exact same church communities** still exist in unbroken succession—the answer is mostly no, with rare exceptions like Rome. But if you mean the **spiritual legacy**, many Christian denominations claim to be heirs of those early communities.

Of the original biblical churches that *still exist spiritually*—meaning Christian communities continuing in the same cities or claiming succession—the overwhelming majority belong to **Eastern Orthodoxy** or **Roman Catholicism**, depending on the region.

Here’s the breakdown by denomination:

---

### **1. Eastern Orthodox Churches**
These are dominant in regions like Greece and parts of the Balkans, where some original church cities still have active Christian life:

- **Thessalonica (Thessaloniki)** – Greek Orthodox
- **Philippi** – Greek Orthodox presence nearby
- **Smyrna (İzmir, Turkey)** – Small Greek Orthodox remnant exists
- **Antioch (not one of the seven churches, but major)** – Historically Greek Orthodox; now in decline due to conflict

Orthodoxy maintains strong liturgical and historical continuity, often claiming to *be* the same church (e.g., the Church of Thessaloniki sees itself as the church founded by Paul).

---

### **2. Roman Catholic Church**
- **Rome** – Claims direct spiritual and institutional succession from the church Paul wrote to. The **Roman Catholic Church** traces its papal lineage to Peter.
- The **Latin Patriarchates** of Jerusalem and Antioch (historical centers of early Christianity) are also now part of Catholic structure, even if not continuous communities.

---

### **3. Armenian and Syriac Churches**
In Asia Minor and the Levant, Christian communities like the **Armenian Apostolic Church** and the **Syriac Orthodox Church** maintained spiritual traditions linked to early Christian congregations, though not usually the ones Paul directly wrote to.

---

### **4. Protestantism**
While **Protestants** often claim theological descent from the New Testament church, they do **not** claim physical or apostolic succession in the same way. Some Protestant denominations (especially evangelical and charismatic) will invoke names like “the church of Ephesus” symbolically or doctrinally, but they are *new creations*, not continuous.

---

### Summary
The churches that still **exist in continuity**—even if scattered or small—are mostly:

- **Greek Orthodox** (esp. in Greece, parts of Turkey)
- **Roman Catholic** (esp. Rome itself)
- **Armenian/Syriac Orthodox** (for broader early Christian regions)

Of these, **Eastern Orthodoxy** holds the largest number of the specific *cities* Paul and John wrote to that still claim some form of spiritual lineage today.
