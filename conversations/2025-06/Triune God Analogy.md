# Triune God Analogy

In simple terms explain triune using an analogy to humans like voice mind and body

## Assistant

A simple way to explain the **Triune God** (Father, Son, Holy Spirit) is to compare it to how a human being can be **one person** with **three distinct parts**:

---

**Body, Mind, and Voice:**

- **The Mind** is where your thoughts and will live — like **God the Father**, who holds the plan and purpose behind everything.

- **The Body** is how you show up in the world and act — like **Jesus the Son**, who took on flesh and walked among us.

- **The Voice** is how your mind reaches others, expressing what’s inside you — like **the Holy Spirit**, who speaks to people, guides them, and brings them comfort.

---

You are **one person**, not three, but your body, mind, and voice are **not the same** — they do different things, yet always work together.

That’s the idea behind the Trinity: **One God**, **Three Persons**, always united.

## User

And how does the actual line for triune go?

## Assistant

The traditional line that defines the Triune nature of God is:

> **"One God in three persons: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit."**

This comes from early Church creeds, especially the **Nicene Creed** and **Athanasian Creed**, which emphasize:

- **Not three Gods, but one.**  
- **Each person is fully God.**  
- **The three are distinct, but not divided.**

So the formal structure is always:

> **"One God in three persons."**

Ah, got it — you're asking for the **full doctrinal explanation** of the Trinity, the classic statement that lays it all out clearly. The most complete version comes from the **Athanasian Creed** (5th century), which includes this core summary:

---

> **“We worship one God in Trinity, and Trinity in Unity; neither confounding the persons nor dividing the substance.**  
> For there is one person of the Father, another of the Son, and another of the Holy Spirit.  
> But the Godhead of the Father, of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit is all one:  
> the glory equal, the majesty co-eternal.  
>  
> Such as the Father is, such is the Son, and such is the Holy Spirit.  
> The Father uncreated, the Son uncreated, and the Holy Spirit uncreated.  
> The Father eternal, the Son eternal, and the Holy Spirit eternal.  
> And yet they are not three eternals, but one eternal.  
> As also there are not three uncreated, nor three incomprehensible,  
> but one uncreated, and one incomprehensible.  
>  
> So likewise the Father is Almighty, the Son Almighty, and the Holy Spirit Almighty;  
> And yet they are not three Almighties, but one Almighty.  
>  
> So the Father is God, the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God;  
> And yet they are not three Gods, but one God.  
>  
> So likewise the Father is Lord, the Son Lord, and the Holy Spirit Lord;  
> And yet not three Lords, but one Lord.”**

---

This is the most detailed and careful formulation in Christian tradition. It's meant to protect three truths at once:

1. **There is only one God.**  
2. **The Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are each fully and equally God.**  
3. **The Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Spirit, the Spirit is not the Father.**
