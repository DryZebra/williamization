### 🧠 What the science says:
Here’s a breakdown of what *actually* helps:

#### 1. **Dual N-Back Training**
- **What it is**: A memory task that requires you to remember sequences of audio and visual cues.
- **Evidence**: Repeated studies have shown **increased working memory**, **fluid intelligence**, and **transfer effects** (improvements that generalize to other skills).
- **Studies**: Jaeggi et al., 2008; recent meta-analyses confirm cognitive benefits if done consistently (15–30 min/day, 5x/week).
- **Best handheld app**: **BrainN-Back** or **Peak** (both have good mobile versions).

#### 2. **BrainHQ by Posit Science**
- Developed in collaboration with **Michael Merzenich**, a legit neuroscientist (not just an app dev).
- Tested in NIH-funded **ACTIVE trial** (10-year study).
- Showed **reduced dementia risk**, **improved memory**, and **slower decline**.
- **Best if** used regularly; it adapts to your skill level and focuses on **auditory processing**, a critical factor in aging cognition.

#### 3. **Learning a new language or instrument**
- **DuoLingo** or **Memrise** (language)
- **Yousician** or **SimplyPiano** (instrument)
- Evidence shows learning new structured skills creates **new neural pathways**, more than repeating the same crossword routine for 40 years.

---

### ❌ Crosswords and Sudoku?
- Crosswords: Good if you're already verbal and educated—**but limited in cognitive range**. They help with vocabulary recall but don’t stimulate **executive function**, **working memory**, or **processing speed** much.
- Sudoku: More math logic than deep cognitive flexibility. Good for pattern recognition but again, **limited transfer effects**.

**What to do**:

**Why it works**:  
This is **working memory boot camp**. Zero dopamine. Pure training.
