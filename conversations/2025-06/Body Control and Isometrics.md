What you're describing sounds like a form of **isometric exercise**—tensing a muscle without moving the joint—which is generally harmless and even beneficial in moderation.

Here’s a breakdown:

### ✅ Why It’s Probably Fine (or Even Good):
- **Isometric holds** like the one you're doing (flexing without moving the joint) are used in physical therapy, martial arts, and strength training.
- Doing them *intentionally* like you described increases **mind-muscle connection**, body awareness, and sometimes blood flow.
- If it **feels good**, and you **stop when you're fatigued**, you're listening to your body—usually a good sign.

### ⚠️ When It Could Be Bad:
- If you start feeling **tingling, numbness, joint pain**, or **sharp discomfort**, especially in joints like the wrist or elbow, it may indicate nerve strain or tendon stress.
- Doing it **compulsively** or excessively, especially if it interferes with other activities, could hint at a nervous habit or minor obsessive tendency (not necessarily bad, but worth noting).

### 👣 What You're Probably Tapping Into:
It sounds like you're instinctively exploring your body’s **range of micro-movements** and **neuromuscular control**. This is common in:
- People who enjoy precision sports (climbers, martial artists, musicians)
- People recovering from injury who became aware of small movements
- People with strong bodily intuition or curiosity

### Summary:
If it feels good, doesn’t cause pain or lingering fatigue, and you’re not doing it to the point of interfering with life—**go ahead**. Think of it as a way your body and brain stay connected.
