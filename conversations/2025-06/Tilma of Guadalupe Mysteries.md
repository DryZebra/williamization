---

### 🧵 1. **The Cloth (Tilma) Itself:**
- **Material:** The tilma is made of **ayate**, a rough fabric woven from agave (cactus) fibers.
- **Normal Lifespan:** This type of cloth usually disintegrates within **15–30 years**, especially in the humid, acidic air of central Mexico.
- **Miraculous Longevity:** The tilma has survived **nearly 500 years (since 1531)** without significant decay, despite being exposed to smoke, pollution, and handling—this is one of the core arguments for its miraculous preservation.

---

### 🎨 2. **The Image and Pigments:**
- **No Underpainting or Brushstrokes:** When studied under magnification, there are no detectable brush strokes, outlines, or layers like those found in painted images.
- **Not Painted on the Surface:** Parts of the image appear to be *suspended* slightly above the fibers—not soaked into them nor lying directly on the surface.
- **Unknown Pigments:** Chemical analysis in the 20th century revealed that many of the pigments do **not match any known animal, vegetable, or mineral-based dyes** from the 16th century—or even today. Some colors remain unidentified.
- **Inexplicable Color Stability:** The colors haven’t faded much at all, even after centuries of exposure to sunlight, candles, and incense.

---

### 👁️ 3. **The Eyes:**
- **Human Eye Reflection:** When the eyes of the Virgin on the tilma are examined under magnification, they appear to contain **reflections consistent with Purkinje-Sanson images**—the kind of optical reflections you’d find in a living human eye.
- **What’s Reflected?:** Researchers claim to see **a bearded man (Juan Diego?), a bishop, and several others** in the eye reflection, arranged with correct depth and size for corneal curvature.

---

### 🔥 4. **Survival of Disasters:**
- **Acid Spill (1790s):** A nitric acid spill didn’t destroy the image—only a small stain remained.
- **Bombing (1921):** A powerful bomb hidden in flowers was placed beneath the tilma. It exploded and **shattered windows and twisted metal**—but the tilma and its fragile glass covering were untouched.

---

### 🕊️ 5. **Symbolism and Cultural Impact:**
- **Aztec Symbols:** The image is filled with **indigenous Nahuatl symbolism**—the stars on her cloak, the black maternity sash, the flower placement—all of which spoke deeply to the native people, aiding conversion.
- **Massive Conversions:** After the apparition and the display of the tilma, **millions of indigenous people converted to Christianity** in just a few decades.
