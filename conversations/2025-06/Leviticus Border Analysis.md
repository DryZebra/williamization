## Assistant

Absolutely. Let’s crush this liberal misquote with a historically materialist, worker-centered analysis.

---

**The Liberal Misreading:**
Liberals love to quote Leviticus 19:33–34:

> *“When a stranger resides with you in your land, you shall not oppress him. The stranger who resides with you shall be to you as the native among you, and you shall love him as yourself…”*

They wave this around like it's a divine endorsement of unrestricted immigration, claiming it proves any resistance to open borders is inherently un-Christian. But this interpretation completely strips the verse from its historical, material, and moral context.

---

**The Materialist-Christian Response:**

This commandment wasn't written to global empires or capitalist states. It was a rule for a **communal, tribal, theocratic economy**—where land wasn't privately owned, where debt was forgiven, and where the whole society ran on kinship, proximity, and agricultural production. It was not written to justify the disintegration of **worker sovereignty** through artificially engineered mass migration. The “stranger” was not a weapon of empire. He was a real human, seeking to become part of the moral and economic *life* of the people.

The verse assumes that:
1. The stranger **resides among you**—they join your community, adopt your customs, and participate in your moral structure.
2. There is a **shared law**—“one law for you and for the stranger” (Lev 24:22).
3. The stranger does not dissolve or trample the workers’ capacity for **self-rule**.

Compare that to today: Open borders under capitalism aren’t about loving the stranger. They are about **capital finding new reservoirs of cheap labor**, suppressing wages, fracturing worker solidarity, and turning migration into **a managed pipeline of desperation**—controlled not by migrants or citizens, but by capitalists.

---

**Dialectical Clarification:**

You’re not opposing the stranger. You’re opposing the **liberal use of the stranger** as a bludgeon against the democratic will of the proletariat.

Under socialism, when borders are dissolved, it must be because **the working class on both sides has come into moral, democratic union**—not because the ruling class has decided to import labor and moralize about hospitality to anyone who resists.

Leviticus tells Israel to love the stranger *as themselves*—which assumes the stranger is willing to become like them, not to overturn them. It assumes moral assimilation, not cultural relativism. It assumes mutual care, not class fragmentation.

---

**Conclusion:**

So no, Leviticus 19 doesn’t support open borders. It supports **moral community**, **integration**, and **shared law**. And if your country is run by banks and corporations instead of righteous workers, then your open borders aren’t Levitical—they’re Babylonian.

Workers have the right to define their community, protect their solidarity, and welcome others on the basis of shared labor and love—not imposed humanitarianism from the mouths of exploiters.

If they want a Christian argument, tell them this: *“You don't get to quote the Torah while helping Pharaoh build his store-cities on the backs of strangers and locals alike.”*

Let the workers decide. Let them love who they *choose* to love. That’s what real Leviticus looks like.

## Assistant

Several passages across both the Old and New Testaments point to the idea that God’s creation is *meant* to be knowable—that the created order is not a random chaos, but structured and available to human understanding through reason, labor, and observation. Here are some of the strongest ones, especially from a dialectical, Materialist Christian perspective:

---

### **1. Romans 1:20 (NET):**
> *"For since the creation of the world his invisible attributes—his eternal power and divine nature—have been clearly seen, because they are understood through what has been made. So people are without excuse."*

This is the most explicit. Paul affirms that God’s nature is visible through *created things*. The world reveals divine structure—not mystically, but through rational reflection on reality. To the Materialist Christian, this is a justification of science, history, and reasoned dialectic. The truth of creation is not hidden behind a veil—it’s *understood through what has been made.*

---

### **2. Proverbs 25:2 (KJV):**
> *“It is the glory of God to conceal a thing: but the honour of kings is to search out a matter.”*

Here, the dialectic of hiddenness and discovery is sacred. God embeds mystery in creation, not to deny understanding but to *elevate* the act of pursuit. The search for truth, for cause, for structure—this is not rebellion but *honor*. It justifies science, history, and investigation as righteous acts.

---

### **3. Job 12:7–10 (NET):**
> *“But now, ask the animals and they will teach you, or the birds of the sky and they will tell you. Or speak to the earth and it will teach you, or let the fish of the sea declare to you. Which of all these does not know that the hand of the Lord has done this?”*

Even Job, the great book of suffering and uncertainty, affirms that the *creatures themselves* reveal the hand of God. To understand nature is to understand the pattern of the Lord—not through magic or revelation, but by **paying attention** to creation.

---

### **4. Psalm 19:1–2 (KJV):**
> *“The heavens declare the glory of God; and the firmament sheweth his handywork. Day unto day uttereth speech, and night unto night sheweth knowledge.”*

Nature is not silent. The heavens “declare,” the skies “sheweth,” the days and nights “utter speech” and “shew knowledge.” Material existence is not dumb—it teaches. It reveals. It’s intelligible.

---

### **5. Ecclesiastes 1:13 (KJV):**
> *“And I gave my heart to seek and search out by wisdom concerning all things that are done under heaven: this sore travail hath God given to the sons of man to be exercised therewith.”*

Here, Solomon says plainly that **God gave humans the task** of understanding everything done under heaven. It’s hard work—“travail”—but it is *assigned* by God. Knowledge of creation is not arrogance. It is a burden we were given.

---

### Summary for Memorization:

> **“His invisible attributes are understood through what has been made.”** — *Romans 1:20*

Use that as your anchor verse, with backup from Proverbs 25:2, Job 12, Psalm 19, and Ecclesiastes 1. All of them say the same thing: Creation is not opaque. It speaks. And it is the **honor** of man to listen, learn, and understand.
