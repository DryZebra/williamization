To answer your question about whether the pictured means of support is legal per the 2020 National Electrical Code (NEC):

Based on your photo, it looks like you’re asking whether it's code-compliant to support electrical conduit using **plastic zip ties** or **tie wire** to what appears to be a *ceiling grid*, *strut*, or possibly *suspended framing member*.

### Here’s the NEC-based answer (2020 NEC):

**No**, using *non-listed* plastic zip ties (or unapproved supports) for securing conduit is not compliant under most conditions. Here’s why:

---

### 🔧 NEC 2020 Reference:
**Article 358.30** (for EMT as an example):
> EMT shall be **securely fastened in place** and supported **in accordance with Table 344.30(B)(2)** (or similar, depending on conduit type). Supports must be **approved** and **secure**.

**Article 300.11(A):**
> Raceways and cables shall be **securely fastened in place** and **shall not be supported by ceiling grids**, unless specifically permitted, and **must use fittings listed for the purpose**.

**Also:**
> *"Support wires that are part of the suspended-ceiling framing system shall not be used to support raceways or cables."*

---

### Summary:
- **Plastic zip ties** (unless UL listed and rated for this use) are *not compliant* for permanent support of conduit.
- **Tie wire** might be okay **only if** it meets structural support requirements and is **securely fastened** to framing or strut—not ceiling grid wires.
- Supports must be **secure**, **permanent**, and attached to **building structure** or listed support systems—not temporary or makeshift.

---

### What *is* allowed?
- **Listed conduit hangers, clamps, or straps** attached to:
  - Studs
  - Concrete
  - Structural members
  - Properly installed strut systems
