Great photos — and you're absolutely on the right track.

This insect is **most likely a queen ant**, probably in the **alate (winged) stage**, which is when queens and males leave the colony for their nuptial flight. Here's how we can tell:

### ✅ Queen Ant Indicators:
- **Wing structure:** Two pairs of wings with the front pair noticeably larger than the back pair — classic for ants.
- **Elbowed antennae:** Clear bend in the antennae, unlike termites whose antennae are straight or slightly curved.
- **Narrow waist:** A distinct, pinched waist (petiole) between the thorax and abdomen.
- **Body segmentation:** You can clearly see the three-part ant structure (head, thorax, abdomen) with a large thorax typical of queens.

### ❌ Not a Termite:
- Termites have:
  - Straight antennae
  - No narrow waist (more uniformly cylindrical bodies)
  - Wings of equal size
- This insect does **not** match that profile.

So yes — you’ve found a **queen ant**, likely looking for a place to start a new colony. The little granules around her could be **sawdust, sand, or old bait particles**, but they aren't ant eggs or termite frass based on appearance.
