North Carolina’s **House Bill 442**, titled **“Flounder/Red Snapper Seasons & Shrimp Trawl”**, is a 2025–2026 legislative measure with two major components:

---

### 🎣 1. Recreation & Seafood Regulations
- **Flounder fishing**: Recreational season expanded from 4 days to a minimum of 6 weeks, with a one-fish daily limit per person.  
- **Red snapper fishing**: Establishes a year‑round season, allowing state‑waters anglers to harvest up to two fish daily per person, with a 20‑inch minimum size requirement. citeturn0search2turn0search7

---

### 🚢 2. Shrimp Trawl Amendment (Key Focus)
A significant Senate-introduced amendment would **ban commercial shrimp trawling** in inshore waters — specifically within half a mile of shore, including sounds and estuaries — effective December 1. The goal is to protect juvenile fish habitats and reduce bycatch. citeturn0search2turn0search4turn0search5

#### Support & Opposition:
- **Backed by**: Environmental groups like the NC Wildlife Resources Commission and Wildlife Federation emphasize the need to protect seafloor habitats and nursery grounds for fish, citing staggering bycatch ratios (~4 lb of juvenile fish for each pound of shrimp) citeturn0search4turn0search11.
- **Opposed by**: Coastal shrimpers and seafood businesses argue it jeopardizes livelihoods, supply chains, and market prices, noting a long tradition of sustainable shrimping and concern over forced deeper offshore operations citeturn0search2turn0search5.

---

### 🏛️ Legislative Status
- **Introduced**: March 18, 2025  
- **Passed House**: May 7, 2025  
- **Senate Amendment Introduced**: June 18, 2025  
- **Passed Senate (as amended)**: June 19, 2025 (39–2) citeturn0search0turn0search7  
- **Current**: Back in the House for concurrence before it goes to the Governor.

---

**In short:** HB 442 updates recreational fishing rules for flounder and red snapper *and* institutes a controversial ban on inshore commercial shrimp trawling — raising environmental protection vs. economic impact debates.
