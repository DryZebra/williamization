## Assistant

What you’re describing is not a standard literary category but a *structural mode of storytelling* that blends several narrative lenses. Let’s break it down piece by piece and then propose a name for it:

---

### 🧠 What You’re Actually Describing:

1. **Historical second-person plural**  
   → The implied subject is “you all,” as in: *“You were delivered from Egypt,”* or *“You hardened your hearts.”* This is **collective memory** being directly narrated to the descendants of a people, as if they themselves lived it.  
   > *Deuteronomy 5:3 – “The Lord did not make this covenant with our ancestors, but with us, all of us who are alive here today.”*

2. **Moral epic**  
   → Not just a record of events, but a retelling shaped by what *should have* happened or what was *righteously* revealed through consequence. This fits with what you said about stories being retained because they “worked”—morally, socially, economically.

3. **Reverse Destiny**  
   → Prophets are seen in hindsight, not foresight. The stories that survive are those whose outcomes proved them right, as measured by future survival, moral revolution, or divine vindication. That’s a historical dialectic of meaning.

---

### 🔥 Suggested Term for This Writing Mode:

#### **Dialectical Collective Epic**  
or  
#### **Retrospective Providentialism**  
or  
#### **Postdictive Moral Historiography**

Each of these attempts to capture:

- That the **narrator speaks to a people as if they lived it**  
- That **only the events with moral-consequential weight survive**  
- That **meaning is constructed in hindsight, not declared in advance**  
- That **the structure itself reflects dialectical motion**—a tension of actions, reactions, and outcomes, with truth rising from contradiction.

---

### Bonus: What it *isn't* (to clarify)

- **Not classic second person:** It’s not individual “you” as in Choose-Your-Own-Adventure
- **Not purely mythic narrative:** It’s grounded in *moral causality* and real social contradictions
- **Not omniscient third person:** It admits the narrator is within the story’s flow

---

If I had to coin a name faithful to your own system:

### 🏛️ **“Canonical Hindsight Epic”**  
→ A genre where stories are curated by *what proved true*, written as if the audience *lived it*, and framed to forge identity through *shared survival of contradiction.*
