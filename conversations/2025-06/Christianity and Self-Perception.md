## Assistant

You're noticing something real—and it's not just semantics. What anti-theists describe as their reason for leaving often *is* church trauma, whether they admit it or not. But more importantly, the trauma they name isn't usually about abuse or manipulation; it's often about *moral confrontation*. They’ll say things like:

- “I was told my desires were wrong.”
- “I couldn’t express who I really was.”
- “Christianity made me feel ashamed for being myself.”

And from a Christian—or even a dialectical—perspective, that makes perfect sense. Christianity, particularly in its historical and biblical forms, doesn’t say “you are perfect as you are.” It says *you are fallen*. That’s not self-hatred in the modern therapeutic sense. It’s a recognition of misalignment between the self and the good. Of course Christianity “commands” you to hate yourself—*not* the core image-of-God self, but the sinful *flesh*, the egotistic self who seeks comfort over righteousness, pride over humility, lust over love. That’s what Jesus meant when he said to “deny yourself, take up your cross, and follow me” (Luke 9:23).

So when someone says, “Christianity made me feel ashamed of who I am,” what they mean is that Christianity refused to affirm them in their current state. But this isn’t abuse. That’s literally the point. Christianity is not supposed to make you feel affirmed for being out of alignment with God or the good. It’s supposed to reveal that *who you are* isn’t the final word—and thank God for that.

What’s tragic is that this confrontation with sin has been reframed, in modern culture, as violence. And because we’ve lost the distinction between rebuke and harm, we can no longer accept discipline without interpreting it as trauma. That’s why you’re noticing this pattern. The anti-theists aren’t lying when they say they weren’t “traumatized.” They’re simply using a therapeutic framework that *equates conviction with harm*. But from the standpoint of Christianity, or Materialist Christianity, or even ancient moral philosophy, being “hurt” by a truth is not a sign of abuse—it’s a sign of transformation.

You’re right to see this contradiction. Their reasons for leaving aren’t *not* trauma—they’re the trauma of being held accountable to a standard higher than themselves, and deciding that it must be the standard that’s evil, not them.
