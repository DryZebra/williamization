# Honda CRV AC Issue

## User

My mother in laws Honda crv doesn't blow cold air. It's not hot air, even when it's hot outside, but it doesn't cool the car. She replaced her compressor already 

## Assistant



## Assistant

If your mother-in-law’s **Honda CR-V AC blows air that isn’t hot but also not cold**, even after **replacing the compressor**, here’s a list of the most likely causes in order of priority:

---

### 🔧 1. **Low or Overcharged Refrigerant**
- **Most common issue.**
- Replacing a compressor doesn't guarantee the refrigerant was refilled correctly.
- **Too little or too much** refrigerant will prevent proper cooling.
- **Fix**: Have the system evacuated and recharged **to the exact specified amount** (not just "topped off").

---

### 🔍 2. **AC Condenser Fan Not Running**
- If the **condenser fan** isn’t working, the refrigerant can’t cool properly and the air won’t get cold.
- You may notice this is **worse at idle** or in traffic.
- **Fix**: Check the condenser fan for operation. Replace fan motor or relay if needed.

---

### 🌡️ 3. **Blend Door Actuator Issue**
- The AC may be cooling, but the **blend door** could be stuck or malfunctioning, **mixing warm air** into the cold stream.
- Often causes **lukewarm airflow**, even when the AC itself is working.
- **Fix**: Diagnostic check on HVAC actuators. Replace faulty actuator.

---

### ⚠️ 4. **Clogged or Blocked AC Condenser**
- If the condenser is blocked (by debris or damage), it can't shed heat.
- AC won't be cold even though the system is charged.
- **Fix**: Inspect the front of the condenser. Clean debris or replace if bent/crushed.

---

### 🧪 5. **Receiver/Drier or Expansion Valve Problem**
- After a compressor replacement, the **receiver/drier** and **expansion valve/orifice tube** should also be replaced.
- If not, moisture or clogging could prevent proper cooling.
- **Fix**: Replace these components if not already done.

---

### 🧰 What to Do:
1. **Hook up AC gauges** – Measure high and low side pressures.
   - This will tell you if the system is correctly charged and if the compressor is doing its job.
2. **Verify condenser fan operation** – Watch it turn on when AC is engaged.
3. **Feel the lines** under the hood – Suction line should be **cold**, high-pressure line should be **hot**.
4. **Scan for HVAC codes** – Some models will throw body control module (BCM) or HVAC codes.

### 🔧 **Step-by-Step Diagnosis**
#### 1. **Start the car and turn the AC on full blast (Max A/C, recirculate).**
- Let it run for 5 minutes.
- Feel the air: is it warm, cool, or room temp?

---

#### 2. **Use the AC gauge on the low-pressure port**  
- Look for the **low side service port** (usually a blue or black cap).
- It’s on the larger of the two AC lines, near the passenger side firewall.

**Connect the gauge with the AC still running. Read the pressure.**

##### 🔎 Interpret the pressure:
| Gauge Reading | What It Means | What to Do |
|---------------|---------------|------------|
| **25–40 psi** | Likely normal | Could be blend door issue |
| **< 20 psi** | Undercharged | May have a leak |
| **> 45–60 psi** | Overcharged or condenser/fan issue | Don’t add more refrigerant! |
| **Fluctuating wildly** | Compressor or expansion valve problem | Mechanic time |

**DON’T recharge unless you’re <25 psi.** Overfilling can destroy the system.

---

#### 3. **Pop the hood and check the AC condenser fan**
- With the AC on, the **condenser fan (in front of the radiator)** should be spinning.
- If it’s not, the AC won’t get cold—this is a **common fix**.

---

#### 4. **Feel the AC lines**
- Suction line (larger): **Should be cold**
- High-pressure line (smaller): **Should be hot**
- If both feel same-temp: **Compressor isn’t doing its job**, or refrigerant isn’t flowing.

---
