### 🧭 Current Restrictions – HB 900 (Effective May 2025)

**House Bill 900**, which took effect in May after passing in 2024, imposes the following changes:

- **Flavor ban**: Most flavored vape cartridges and e-liquids not FDA‑regulated were banned statewide.
- **Retail compliance**: Vape shops were required to remove or sell off banned items by the end of June 2025, or face unannounced inspections and fines citeturn0search3.
- **Allowed products**: Regulated items from major manufacturers like Njoy, Vuse, and Juul remain legal citeturn0search3.

So currently, **flavored vapes are banned**, but standard tobacco/menthol vapes from FDA‑registered brands are still legal.

### Upcoming Legislation – Raising Sales Age, Licensing, and THC Regulation

Several bills are under consideration this session aiming to further expand vape and nicotine restrictions:

1. **“Solly’s Law” – HB 430 / Senate companion**
   - Raises the minimum purchase age from **18 to 21** for tobacco and vape products.
   - Introduces a **$400 annual retailer permit** and civil penalties/enforcement via Alcohol Law Enforcement citeturn0search5turn0search8.
   - Has bipartisan support and may proceed despite missing the crossover deadline, due to funding language citeturn0search5.

2. **Senate Bill 533**
   - Also raises purchase age to **21**, extends **licensing to all vapor-product sellers**, and enhances enforcement.
   - Scheduled to take effect **December 1, 2025** citeturn0search7.

3. **THC product regulation – HB 328**
   - Expands restrictions to **hemp-derived vapes/edibles** containing Delta‑8/9/CB D to adults 21+, bans them on school property, and imposes licensing and penalties citeturn0search2turn0search4.

- **HB 328** (covering hemp-THC vapes) just passed key committees and is moving toward a full Senate vote citeturn0search2turn0search4.
