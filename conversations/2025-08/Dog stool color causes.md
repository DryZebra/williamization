## Assistant

A very light tan, cow pie–textured stool in a 16-week-old dog who’s recently been adopted is worth keeping an eye on. Here's a breakdown of what it might mean and what to do:

---

### ⚠️ **Most Likely Causes**:
1. **Diet Transition**  
   Sudden changes in food (common after adoption) often cause light-colored, soft stools. The gut flora is adjusting.

2. **Low Bile Output**  
   Pale stool can mean reduced bile in the digestive tract. This might be temporary or indicate an issue with the liver, gallbladder, or pancreas.

3. **Parasites or Infections**  
   Especially in pups, internal parasites (like Giardia) or bacterial infections can cause pale, soft stool.

4. **Malabsorption / Poor Diet**  
   If the previous diet was poor quality (e.g., full of fillers), the gut may still be healing. Malabsorption can also cause light-colored, loose stool.

---

### ✅ **Signs Things Are Improving**:
- If the stool has darkened even slightly since adoption
- If the dog is eating, playing, and gaining weight
- If there’s no vomiting or diarrhea (true liquid stool)

---

### 🚩 **Call Your Vet If**:
- Stool is **white, chalky, or gray** consistently
- There’s **vomiting, lethargy, or loss of appetite**
- You see **mucus, blood**, or **worms**
- It hasn’t improved after 3–5 days on a consistent, high-quality diet

---

### 🛠️ **What You Can Do Now**:
- **Stick to one food brand/type** for at least 2 weeks
- **Add plain pumpkin puree** (1 tsp per meal) to firm up stool
- **Keep a poop log** – color, consistency, frequency
- **Bring a stool sample** to the vet for a fecal test if unsure

### 🐶 Your Next Steps:
1. **Stay Consistent with Food**

2. **Hydration & Slow Feeding**  
   Make sure he’s not gulping food. Use a slow feeder bowl if needed. Add a splash of water to kibble to help digestion.

3. **Pumpkin or Slippery Elm (optional)**  
   A little plain pumpkin (not pie mix) or slippery elm powder can help coat and soothe the gut.

4. **Fecal Test Follow-Up**  
   If not already done at the shelter, ask your vet for a fecal float and Giardia test just to be safe. Those often go undiagnosed and cause lingering digestive issues.

5. **Monitor for “True Brown”**  
   A transition from light tan to a normal medium-to-dark brown is a sign of full recovery.
