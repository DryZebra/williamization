That depends on how we define **“oldest”** (single living trunk vs. clonal colony), but here’s the clear picture:

---

## 🌍 **Oldest Known Individual Trees**
- **Great Basin bristlecone pine (*Pinus longaeva*)**, California, USA  
  - Oldest confirmed individual: **~4,850 years old** (“Methuselah” in the White Mountains).  
  - Another unnamed bristlecone may be **over 5,000 years old**, but its exact location is secret to protect it.  
- These are the **oldest non-clonal, individual living trees** known on Earth.  

---

## 🌍 **Oldest Known Clonal Colonies** (technically one organism with many stems)  
- **Pando, the trembling aspen colony (Populus tremuloides)** in Utah, USA  
  - Age: Estimated **80,000+ years**.  
  - It reproduces by cloning through an underground root system.  
- **King’s Lomatia (*Lomatia tasmanica*)**, Tasmania  
  - Age: ~43,000 years (clonal).  

---

## 🌍 **Oldest East Coast Trees (near you)**

  - Confirmed tree: **2,624 years old** (dated in 2019).  
  - That makes it the **oldest known living tree in Eastern North America**.
