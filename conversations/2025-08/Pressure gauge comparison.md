# Pressure gauge comparison

## User

Why does my bike pump say 60 psi but my pressure gauge says .0060 psiñ

## Assistant



## Assistant

That’s because the two readings are using **different units and scales**.  

- Your **bike pump’s built-in gauge** is almost certainly reading in **PSI (pounds per square inch)** — a *gauge pressure* scale that starts at zero for ambient air pressure. So when it says 60 psi, it means *60 pounds of pressure above atmospheric pressure*.  

- The other **pressure gauge** sounds like it’s reading in **absolute pressure** but in **different units** — likely **megapascals (MPa)** or **kilopascals (kPa)**, and it may be showing the value in a **decimal form**.  

If your gauge says **0.0060 MPa**, then:  
```
0.0060 MPa × 145.038 PSI/MPa ≈ 0.87 PSI absolute
```
That’s *way* off from 60 psi because one of two things is happening:  

1. **It’s not connected or sealed properly**, so it’s just reading close to atmospheric pressure.  
2. **The gauge is meant for tiny pressures** (like differential pressure for airflow in HVAC) and can’t register 60 psi — it’s not the right tool for a bike tire reading.  

Basically — 60 psi on your bike pump is correct for a tire. Your other gauge is probably a *low-pressure differential gauge*, not a high-pressure tire gauge, so it’s showing a meaningless tiny number in its own scale.  

If you tell me the *exact units* printed on that gauge face or display, I can work out the exact conversion and confirm. Do you see anything like “kPa,” “MPa,” “bar,” or “in H₂O” on it?
