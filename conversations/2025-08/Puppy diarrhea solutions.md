Thanks for the detailed info—here’s what I’m seeing, based on your description:

### 💩 Stool Analysis
- **Color/Texture:** Light tan cow pie = poorly digested food, fat malabsorption, or fast GI transit.
- **Mucus:** Mucus in stool often indicates **intestinal inflammation or irritation**, especially in the colon.
- **No worms, eating/drinking well, normal energy** = **not an emergency**, but something's still irritating his gut.

### ⚠️ Key Considerations
1. **Abrupt Diet Change**  
   Switching from Sportmix to Fromm without a slow transition is likely the **main trigger**. Puppies’ guts are sensitive, and even a higher-quality food can cause flare-ups if not eased in over 5–10 days.

2. **Previous Diet/Gut History**  
   If he came from a rescue or unstable situation, his gut may already be reactive from stress, parasites (even if cleared), or poor-quality food.

3. **Grass Eating**  
   This is common but could be related to mild nausea or a way to self-soothe GI discomfort.

### ✅ What You Can Do
- **Stick with one food now (Fromm)** for at least **2–3 weeks** to allow his gut to stabilize.
- **Add a probiotic** (like FortiFlora, Purina, or a vet-recommended one) to help re-establish healthy gut flora.
- **Keep watch on energy levels**, vomiting, worsening diarrhea, or blood in stool. If those appear, a vet trip is needed.

### Optional Supportive Steps
- A **bland diet (boiled chicken and rice)** for 2–3 days can sometimes reset the gut, then slowly reintroduce the kibble.
- Try **canned pure pumpkin** (1–2 tsp per meal) for fiber support—it helps regulate loose and mucousy stools.

Fromm has pro biotics in it. What's up with the pumpkin?

## Assistant

Good question—let’s break it down:

### 🟠 Why Pumpkin Helps
Canned **pure pumpkin** (not pie filling) is often recommended for both diarrhea *and* constipation because it contains:
- **Soluble fiber**: Absorbs excess water in the colon = firms up soft stools
- **Prebiotics**: Feeds the good bacteria (even the ones Fromm adds)
- **Gentle on the gut**: Doesn’t irritate inflamed intestines like some veggies or grains can

It works **not because it's a cure**, but because it:
- Helps regulate **gut motility** (slows things down if it’s too fast)
- Adds **bulk and softness** to the stool
- Supports the **microbiome** while the gut is healing

### ⚖️ When It Helps
- **Cow pie + mucus** = likely colon irritation or imbalance → pumpkin can help calm things down
- Especially useful **during or after a diet change**, antibiotic use, or rescue transition

### How Much?
Start small:
- **½ to 1 tsp per meal** for a 17-week-old puppy
- Mix with kibble, or serve plain if he likes it
