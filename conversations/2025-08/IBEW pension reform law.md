## Assistant

The law you’re thinking of is the **Multiemployer Pension Reform Act of 2014 (MPRA)**.  

It was slipped into a year-end spending bill (the “Cromnibus”) and originally **pushed by Democrats and supported by the AFL-CIO leadership** as a way to “save” troubled pension funds. What it really did was allow trustees of multiemployer plans — like those covering IBEW members — to **cut already-earned benefits for retirees** if the fund was in “critical and declining” status. That broke the long-standing rule that pensions, once earned, could never be cut.  

For IBEW and other building trades, this law was a knife at the throat of pensions. The only reason benefits weren’t slashed wide-scale was because members organized, fought back, and later won relief in the **American Rescue Plan of 2021**, which provided federal aid to stabilize multiemployer plans【15:0†2025-Policy-Brief-Full.3.21.25_copy.pdfL78-L81】【15:4†2025-Policy-Brief-Full.3.21.25_copy.pdfL39-L40】.

That came in **2018–2019**, when Congress set up the so-called **Joint Select Committee on the Solvency of Multiemployer Pension Plans**. It was a bipartisan panel backed by both Democrats and Republicans, and **the AFL-CIO leadership supported negotiations**. The draft bills coming out of that period (like the “Rehabilitation for Multiemployer Pensions Act” in the House vs. the Senate’s austerity-style counterproposals) would have either:

Neither approach fixed the problem, and both kept the door open for pension cuts. Retirees, including many IBEW members, mobilized hard against it.
