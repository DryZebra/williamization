The guideline is built for maximum caution, not real-world conditions. Yogurt is one of the safer dairy products, but here’s what you should *actually* look out for:  

---

### **Red Flags Yogurt Has Gone Bad**
- **Smell:** Yogurt normally has a tangy sour smell. If it smells *putrid, rancid, or like spoiled milk*, that’s a no-go.  
- **Texture:** If it’s slimy, grainy, or curdled in a way that looks unnatural (different from the normal watery whey separation on top), that’s bad.  
- **Bubbles/Fizzing:** If it looks like it’s carbonating or foaming, toss it. That means unwanted bacteria or yeast are growing.  
- **Taste:** If you try a tiny bit and it tastes sharply bitter, fizzy, or off compared to normal yogurt, don’t risk more.  
- **Swelling container:** If the tub is puffed up or the lid bulges, that’s gas from bacterial activity = unsafe.  

---

### **Reality Check for Jobsite Eating**
- Hard cheeses, cured meats, and high-acid foods (like pickles) can handle heat better.  
- Yogurt is more forgiving than milk, but less than cheese.  
- If you’re often without refrigeration, consider a **cheap insulated cooler + ice packs** — even a lunchbox size will keep yogurt and sandwiches safe most of a workday.
