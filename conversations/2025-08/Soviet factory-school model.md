## Assistant

You’re thinking of the **Soviet “factory-school” or “enterprise-school” model**, often described under the broader system of **“production-based education” (производственное обучение / proizvodstvennoye obucheniye)**.  

### Core Idea
- The USSR sought to directly tie **education, settlement, and production** together.  
- A factory (or other major enterprise) would not only employ most of the local population but also **sponsor schools, training institutes, housing, and cultural life**.  
- Students were trained with the explicit expectation of entering the factory’s workforce, sometimes doing practical shifts there while studying.  
- This was part of the broader **polytechnic education movement**, codified especially in the 1920s–30s, which aimed to unite theory with productive labor.

### Pedagogical Philosophy
- Rooted in Marxist–Leninist ideas that labor should not be separated from learning.  
- Education was seen as preparation for **collective life and socialist production**, not as an abstract pursuit.  
- Teachers and engineers collaborated to create a pipeline: **school → factory apprenticeship → lifelong worker education**.

### Urban & Regional Design
- Whole towns (often called *monogorods*, or “single-industry towns”) were built around this principle.  
- A mine, steel plant, or textile mill might have its own **vocational school, technical institute, hospitals, housing blocks, and cultural centers**.  
- These were not incidental — the **educational system itself was structurally subordinated to the factory**, reproducing skilled labor for it.

### Influence Abroad
- **China and North Korea** (and to a lesser extent Vietnam) adopted the Soviet model in the 1950s–60s.  
- In China, this developed into the slogan **“education must serve proletarian politics and be combined with productive labor”**, seen in Mao-era schools and university reforms.  
- In North Korea, “school-factory” integration is still very visible — whole model villages and towns are structured around this.

# 2) Institutional shell

1) **Production Floor** — a consortium shop (multiple employers) building real products (e.g., prefab electrical racks, switchgear panels, control skids, HVAC assemblies).
2) **Polytechnic High School (grades 9–12)** — a CTE magnet/charter that spends 2–3 days/week on the floor.
3) **Apprenticeship & Technical Institute (post-HS)** — registered programs (IBEW-style JATC, machinist, welding, process tech, instrumentation).
4) **Community Services Hub** — childcare, clinic, transit pass desk, and a union-run worker resource center.

- **Public land/Industrial Development Authority** to hold the site and prevent a private company-town dynamic.
