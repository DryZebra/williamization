At what size screen does the difference between 1080p and 4k kinda disappear?

## Assistant

Good question — the point where **1080p vs. 4K becomes indistinguishable** depends on two factors:  

1. **Screen size**  
2. **Viewing distance**  

Basically, the farther you sit and the smaller the screen, the less benefit you get from 4K. Once the pixels are too small for your eyes to resolve, the difference disappears.  

---

### The Math in Simple Terms
- Human visual acuity is about **1 arcminute** (1/60 of a degree). That’s the smallest detail the average eye can resolve.  
- If the pixels are smaller than that, you won’t see them — so 1080p and 4K will look the same.  

---

### Rough Guide
At **normal couch distances (7–10 ft / 2–3 m):**  
- **Under ~50 inches** → 1080p and 4K look almost identical.  
- **55–65 inches** → 4K starts to look sharper, but only if you’re closer than ~8 ft.  
- **75+ inches** → You’ll notice 4K more easily, even at 9–10 ft.  

At **desktop distances (2–3 ft / 0.5–1 m):**  
- Even a **27-inch monitor** clearly shows 4K vs 1080p.  
- **32+ inches** at close range makes 4K much more valuable.  

---

### Rule of Thumb
- If you sit **farther than 2–3 screen heights away**, you probably can’t tell 1080p from 4K.  
- If you sit **closer than 1.5 screen heights**, you’ll notice the difference.  

---

👉 So the difference “kinda disappears” when:  
- TV is **50 inches or smaller** and you’re **8+ feet away**, OR
