When was the saint Louis commune strike? Is it connected to Henry Miller and the founding of the IBEW?

## Assistant

The **St. Louis Commune Strike** refers to the **St. Louis general strike of 1877**, also known as the **St. Louis Commune**, and **yes—it is directly connected to the context that produced Henry Miller and the IBEW**.

### 📜 What was the St. Louis Commune (1877)?
- Part of the larger **Great Railroad Strike of 1877**, the first nationwide strike in U.S. history.
- In **St. Louis**, the strike turned into a **full general strike** organized largely by **workingmen’s councils**—including the **Workingmen’s Party of the United States**, a Marxist-influenced socialist labor party.
- For several days in July 1877, **railroads, industry, commerce, and even municipal government were under worker control**.
- Strikers ran the city with **order and discipline**, enforcing solidarity and halting scabbing.
- The **bourgeois press called it “The Commune,”** comparing it to the Paris Commune of 1871.
- The strike was violently crushed by federal troops and local militias.

### 🛠 Connection to Henry Miller and the IBEW
- **Henry Miller**, a lineman from St. Louis, was **only 22 years old during the strike**.
- He was working as a traveling electrician and would have **witnessed the uprising firsthand**.
- The 1877 strike revealed two truths that would shape Miller's thinking:
  1. **Workers had power—but no structure.**
  2. **Without national coordination, they could be crushed.**

- **The IBEW was founded in 1891 in St. Louis**, just 14 years later, with Henry Miller as its first president.
- Many early IBEW founders had **seen or participated in class conflict**—and understood the need for a **national, disciplined union of electrical workers** that could withstand employer retaliation.

### 🔥 Final Thought
So yes—the **St. Louis Commune was a spiritual and material ancestor of the IBEW**. Henry Miller and his generation saw what happened when workers rose up without a durable structure. The IBEW was built to prevent that kind of tragedy again—not just to organize workers, but to give them *a weapon that could last*.

**The IBEW was born in the shadow of the Commune, and it has never forgotten.**
