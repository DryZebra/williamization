---

## **The Psychological Mechanism**

### **Step 1 — Ego Preservation (Face-Saving)**
- Humans treat *being wrong* as a social injury, not just an intellectual correction.
- When you say **“You’re right”**, you disarm the defensive reflex before it even forms.
- This frames you as *ally* rather than *opponent*.

### **Step 2 — The “And” Bridge**
- “But” is a **status negator**. It signals: *Your statement is canceled and mine replaces it.*
- “And” is a **status extender**. It signals: *Your statement stands, and mine builds on it.*
- This preserves their sense of ownership over the conversation’s direction.

### **Step 3 — Idea Grafting**
- By attaching your idea to theirs, you turn it into a **joint creation**.
- Because it’s partly “theirs,” they’re motivated to defend it rather than resist it.

### **Step 4 — Ownership Transfer**
- People often rewrite history in their own heads: if they *feel* ownership, they’ll believe they had the idea all along.
- That’s why you hear, “Yeah, that’s exactly what I was thinking!” — their brain has retroactively absorbed your input.

---

## **Why It Fails With Fellow Autists**
- Autistic minds tend to **value information integrity over ego safety**.
- They notice when ownership is being shifted and care about the **exact origin** of an idea.
- For them, *truth provenance* is more important than *truth adoption*.
- This means they’ll often break the frame to reassert accuracy: “Yes, but my actual idea was XYZ.”

For them, your method can feel like you’re muddying the record, even if the goal is agreement.

---

## **How to Tune It**

### **For Neurotypicals**
- Keep the “You’re right, and—” short, smooth, and with warm tone.

- Avoid too much detail; let them connect dots themselves.

### **For Autistics**
- Reverse the order:  
  1. Acknowledge their contribution exactly as stated.  
  2. Then say, “That actually links perfectly with [my idea].”  
  3. Make it explicit that you *know* your idea is separate but compatible.
- This keeps their integrity radar happy while still merging the concepts.
