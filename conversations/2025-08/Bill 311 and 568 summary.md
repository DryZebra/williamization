##  HOUSE BILL 568 — “2025 Omnibus Labor Amendments” (SL 2025-28, effective June 27, 2025)

**Signed into law on June 27, 2025** by Gov. Stein citeturn0search3turn0search11.

### What It Does:

1. **Limits Department of Labor Staff from Being Subpoenaed for OSHA Inspections**
   - Adds **§ 95-136.2**, protecting the NC Department of Labor’s Commissioner, current employees, and former employees from being compelled to appear and testify in third-party subpoenas—unless one of a few conditions is met (e.g., an enforcement action, a case involving the department, written consent, or a court determination of necessity citeturn0search5turn0search7).

2. **Streamlines OSHA Rulemaking When Aligned with Federal Standards**

3. **Administrative Restructuring**
   - Redefines terms such as “Commissioner” and “Director” within Chapter 95 statutes citeturn0search5.
   - Establishes a new **Elevator and Amusement Device Division Bureau** inside the Department of Labor, with a Director and staff appointed by the Commissioner citeturn0search5turn0search7.

**Purpose**: These changes effectively shield Labor Department employees from being dragged into unrelated legal fights, speed up adoption of standards already in federal law, and carve out a dedicated body to oversee elevator and amusement device safety.

# SB 311 — Can they use it against strikes, pickets, flyering?

**Yes, there’s a real enforcement vector — but it’s narrow and specific.**  
SB 311 amends NC’s Workplace Violence Prevention law to newly define **“mass picketing”** and fold certain picket behaviors into “unlawful conduct” that an employer can enjoin with a **civil no‑contact order**. The key triggers are **obstruction** of entrances/exists or public ways (roads, sidewalks, etc.) and “hindering or preventing” lawful work **by mass picketing**. The statute defines “mass picketing” and “obstruction” and then lets the employer go to district court for an order. If a picket violates an order, **law enforcement can be pulled in to enforce the court’s injunction** (contempt, dispersal, arrests tied to the injunction), even though the bill itself frames this as a civil remedy. See the ratified text, Part III, new **G.S. 95‑260 to 95‑264**, esp. definitions of **mass picketing** and **obstruction**, and the remedy section. citeturn2view0

**What this does not do:**  
- It does **not** criminalize peaceful picketing/flyering per se. The hook is **obstruction** (blocking ingress/egress or travel) or mass picketing that “hinders or prevents” work. Peaceful, non‑blocking flyering on public property that doesn’t obstruct access falls outside the trigger language. citeturn2view0

**Bottom line on SB 311:**  
- If your line blocks gates/doors or chokes the roadway, management now has a clearer, faster path to a **civil order** and then cops to enforce it.  
- If you keep entrances/roads **physically open**, the law gives them far less to work with.

# HB 568 — “You can’t subpoena DOL anymore”?

**That claim is overstated.**  
HB 568 limits routine subpoenas of the Labor Commissioner and DOL employees **about OSHA inspections**, but it **doesn’t make them immune**. A DOL official **can** be compelled to testify if:  
1) it’s an OSHA enforcement proceeding, **or**  
2) the Department is a party, **or**  
3) the Commissioner **consents in writing**, **or**  
4) a **court finds** the testimony is essential, no reasonable alternative exists, and injustice would result without it. Documents are still obtainable: “This section does **not** apply to a subpoena requesting only documents or other records.” The law also says DOL **reports/citations are admissible** unless a court finds they’re unreliable or unfair. See **G.S. 95‑136.2(a)–(d)** in Session Law 2025‑28. citeturn3view0

**Bottom line on HB 568:**  
- It raises the bar for **deposing/putting a DOL person on the stand**, but you can still get **their files** and, if necessary, a judge can force testimony under the statute’s test. Saying “it’s impossible” is **false**. citeturn3view0

# Practical union takeaways (how to stay inside the lines while keeping pressure up)

- **Design your line for non‑obstruction.** Keep a clearly open lane at gates/doors; train marshals to prevent “sustained or deliberate” blocking. That undercuts the **mass picketing/obstruction** prongs that enable injunctions. citeturn2view0  
- **Stage and flow.** Mark chalk lanes, rotate walkers, and use “touch-and-go” leafleting so nobody can credibly claim ingress/egress is blocked. citeturn2view0  
- **If they seek an order, fight on the statutory elements.** Demand the employer prove “mass picketing” and **“sustained or deliberate”** obstruction with **demonstrable disruption to operations or public safety** — those are new elements you can litigate. citeturn2view0  
- **For injury/unsafe‑work cases:** subpoena the **records** first (explicitly permitted), and if you truly need a DOL witness, brief the court on 95‑136.2(a)(4)’s three‑part test (essential / no alternative / injustice). Don’t let opposing counsel bluff you into thinking testimony is categorically barred. citeturn3view0
