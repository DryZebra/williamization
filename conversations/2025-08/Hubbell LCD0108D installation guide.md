---

## 1. Why You’re Seeing 77 V in the “OFF” Position
That **phantom ~77 V** is almost certainly **induced or back-fed voltage**, not a real usable supply. Digital meters (high impedance) will happily display it.  

- **0–10 V dimmers** like the LCD0108D never fully “open” the circuit like a snap switch would. They use triac/transistor electronics, which **leak a tiny current**. That leakage, combined with induction from parallel conductors in conduit, often shows up as 50–90 V “ghost voltage” when measured with a standard digital multimeter.  
- If you put a **solenoid tester (Wiggy)** or a meter with a low-Z/LoZ function on it, that 77 V will collapse to 0 V.  

So — the 77 V is not powering anything. It’s just the electronics plus induced coupling.

---

## 2. Why the Red (Load) Wire Reads 277 V All the Time
That’s the bigger clue. On a properly wired dimmer:  

- **Black = Line (hot feed in)**  
- **Red = Load (switched leg out to fixture)**  
- **Green = Ground**  
- **Violet/Gray = 0–10 V control (optional, to dimmable ballast/driver)**  

If your red is always showing **constant 277 V**, then two possibilities:  

1. **They swapped the wires** – the line and load are reversed.  
   - In this case, the fixture side might be tied to line, so the dimmer electronics don’t actually control the load.  
   - You’ll see constant voltage on “red” because it’s actually being fed from line instead of going to the lamp.  

2. **The fixture/ballast neutral is missing or open** – so even though the dimmer is delivering switched power, there’s nowhere for current to return. In that situation, you’ll meter 277 V everywhere, but the lamp won’t light.  

---

## 3. How to Confirm
To sort which one is happening:  

- **Kill power, pull the dimmer out.**  
- Identify which conductor is **feed (line)** — trace it back to the panel feed or incoming hot bundle. That belongs on **black**.  
- Identify which conductor is the **load** going to the light. That belongs on **red**.  
- Verify **neutral is continuous** back to the fixture/ballast.  

If they’re swapped, the dimmer won’t actually interrupt the circuit properly — it’ll just sit there leaking and showing you that weird 77 V.  

---

## 4. Likely Scenario
Your theory is probably right: **line and load got reversed**. That explains:  

- Why the light never turns on.  
- Why the “load” (red) is always hot at 277 V.  
- Why the “line” (black) shows a weird half-voltage when the dimmer tries to “switch” an unloaded output.  

---

✅ **Next Step**: Swap the wires back to correct orientation:  
- **Black → Constant hot feed**  
- **Red → Fixture switched leg**  
- Double-check neutral integrity at the fixture  

That should bring the light back.
