Got it — thanks for clarifying.  

From your photos, that plant looks like **common lamb’s quarters (Chenopodium album)**, sometimes called wild spinach, goosefoot, or pigweed (though “pigweed” also refers to other unrelated weeds like amaranth).  

**How to recognize it:**  
- Leaves: Diamond or goosefoot-shaped, with irregular, shallow teeth.  
- Texture: Often has a whitish or dusty coating on the young leaves (especially on the undersides).  
- Growth: Upright, branching, can grow several feet tall if left alone.  
- Habitat: Loves disturbed soils (like construction sites, gardens, edges of pavement).  

**Interesting notes:**  
- It’s technically edible — young leaves can be cooked like spinach (it’s in the same family as quinoa and beets).  
- However, like spinach, it contains oxalates, so it’s best not eaten raw in large amounts.  
- It’s considered a weed in most places because it grows aggressively and self-seeds.
