### **1. “Son of Man” – Literally a Messianic Title**
> *“In learning you will teach / And in teaching you will learn / You'll find your place beside the ones you love”*

- The phrase **“Son of Man”** is used constantly in Scripture, both in the Old Testament (especially Ezekiel) and the New (as Jesus' favorite way to refer to Himself).
- In *Tarzan*, this song marks a rite of passage—a calling to grow in wisdom, strength, and spiritual clarity. It’s not just about growing up; it’s about **becoming who you were created to be**, even in exile.

---

### **2. “You'll Be in My Heart” – Divine Assurance**
> *“Why can't they understand the way we feel? / They just don't trust what they can't explain.”*

- This is sung to Tarzan by his adoptive gorilla mother, but it has all the weight of a **God-figure speaking to their child** in a world that does not understand them.
- It’s reminiscent of verses like *“Trust in the Lord with all your heart, and lean not on your own understanding” (Proverbs 3:5).*
- There's a theme of **unseen, indwelling love**, stronger than external judgment. The bond transcends species—just like divine love transcends human boundaries.

---

### **3. “Strangers Like Me” – The Hunger for Knowledge, the Fall, and Redemption**
> *“I want to know, can you show me / I want to know about these strangers like me”*

- This is *Tarzan in Eden*—a being of nature, innocent, curious, and longing for knowledge of another world.
- Like Adam reaching for the fruit, he seeks understanding… but unlike the biblical fall, this curiosity **redeems him** rather than condemns him.
- His hunger to understand others is framed as sacred and transformative, not sinful.

---

### **4. The Whole Arc – From Innocence → Exile → Revelation → Integration**

- He’s *not like the others* (John 1:10–11)
