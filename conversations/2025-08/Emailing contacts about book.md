# Emailing contacts about book

## User

How bad of an idea is it to email all 500 of my contacts about my book?

## Assistant



## Assistant

That depends on *how* you do it and *who those 500 contacts are*. Let’s break it down:  

---

### Potential Upsides
- **Immediate reach:** You’ll put your book in front of people who already know you in some way.  
- **Zero cost:** Aside from time, it’s free compared to ads.  
- **Personal network effect:** Friends, family, coworkers, and acquaintances are more likely to support you than strangers if the message feels genuine.  

---

### Risks
- **Perceived as spam:** If you just send a mass impersonal email, a large portion may see it as spammy or self-promotional, which can damage relationships.  
- **Unsubscribes / complaints:** If you use a bulk email tool without permission, some could report it, risking your account or domain reputation.  
- **Mixed audience:** 500 “contacts” probably include people who aren’t the right fit (old jobs, vendors, people you barely know), which reduces effectiveness.  

---

### Smarter Approaches
1. **Segment your contacts:** Don’t blast all 500. Start with those most likely to be interested—friends, family, church, union brothers/sisters, intellectual comrades, etc.  
2. **Make it personal:** Instead of a single mass email, write a heartfelt, short note that feels like you’re sharing something important, not selling.  
3. **Use BCC at minimum:** Protect privacy; don’t dump 500 emails in the “To” line.  
4. **Give value, not just a pitch:** Frame it like *“I’ve been working on this for years, it’s about [theme]. If it resonates, here’s the link. If not, I’d just be grateful for your prayers/good wishes/share.”*  
5. **Test in small batches:** See how the first 20–30 respond before sending more. That way you can tweak tone.  

---

### My Take
It’s **not automatically a bad idea**, but blasting all 500 at once *without thought* is. Done right—segmented, personal, respectful—it can actually be one of the best grassroots ways to get the book moving. Done wrong, it risks looking desperate or spammy.
