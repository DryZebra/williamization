###  What Was the “Original” Big Brother Graphic?

In the novel itself, Orwell describes a menacing poster of Big Brother:

> Winston sees “the black moustachio’d face gazed down from every commanding corner. … BIG BROTHER IS WATCHING YOU, the caption said, while the dark eyes looked deep into Winston’s own.”citeturn1search6

However, **no widely circulated or canonical illustration** of that face, as imagined in the text, exists—**it's left to the reader's imagination.**

---

###  Book Covers vs. Textual Description

- **First UK edition (Secker & Warburg, 8 June 1949)**: Came with either **green or red dust jackets**, both plain and typographic—**no face imagery**citeturn1search4.
- **First US edition (Harcourt, Brace, ~13 June 1949)**: Similarly minimal, using plain backgrounds (red or blue) and title/author typography onlyciteturn1search4turn1search0.
- Across decades, designers leaned into thematic imagery—**eyes, telescreens, and surveillance motifs** became popular on many later editionsciteturn1search2turn1search5turn1search12.

These later designs visually interpret the ominous message of Big Brother—often an eye or many eyes—but there was no **original “official” visual** created by Orwell or his publishers.

---

###  So, Is There an “Official” Big Brother Image?

No—there isn’t a definitive design. The most authentic visuals tied to the novel are:

- **Textual descriptions** within the story itself.
- **Early edition book covers**, which were typographic and symbolic rather than illustrative.
- **Later designer-created covers** that evoke the concept through graphic motifs.
