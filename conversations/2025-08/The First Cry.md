You’ve nailed something most people get wrong about language learning: it’s not the grammar or even the alphabet that kills motivation—it’s the **illusion that you need a billion words before you can speak**.  

Here’s how to flip that on its head for Spanish (and honestly, this works for any language):

---

## 1. Stop Memorizing Vocabulary Lists
Word dumps feel endless and pointless. Instead:
- Learn **high-frequency chunks** (phrases, not words):  
  - *¿Dónde está el baño?* (Where’s the bathroom?)  
  - *Quiero esto.* (I want this.)  
  - *¿Cuánto cuesta?* (How much is it?)  
  These give you immediate usefulness.

- You’ll be shocked: **1,000 words cover ~80% of daily conversation**. Not a billion.

---

## 2. Think Like a Child, Not a Student
- Kids don’t learn “conjugations” first—they hear *quiero, quiero, quiero* until it sticks.  
- Pick 5 survival verbs: *quiero* (I want), *necesito* (I need), *voy* (I go), *tengo* (I have), *puedo* (I can).  
- Use them with nouns you already know (*agua, comida, trabajo*). You can communicate a lot with just that.

---

## 3. Comprehensible Input (CI)
Apps drill, but your brain needs **stories and sounds you almost understand**.
- Watch YouTube channels made for learners (“Dreaming Spanish” is excellent).  
- Put on Spanish podcasts or music daily—don’t worry about understanding, just absorb rhythm and tone.  
- Use Spanish subtitles on shows you already like—your brain will map things faster than you think.

---

## 4. Speak From Day One
- Find an exchange partner or tutor (italki, HelloTalk). Tell them: *“Háblame como a un niño de 3 años.”*  
- Even 15 minutes a week compounds faster than months of silent study.  
- Don’t worry about mistakes. A caveman with verbs and nouns still gets fed.

---

## 5. Survival Method
Since you like “learn as if survival”:
- Imagine you’re dropped in Mexico City with only what you know.  
- Day 1: how do you ask for food?  
- Day 2: how do you get directions?  
- Build your Spanish around these **scenarios**, not grammar tables.

---

⚡ Here’s a 30-day starter challenge if you want structure:
- **Week 1:** Learn 20 survival phrases, practice aloud daily.  
- **Week 2:** Watch 10 minutes/day of “Dreaming Spanish” or similar CI.  
- **Week 3:** Speak 30 minutes total with a tutor/partner (split however you like).  
- **Week 4:** Try one day consuming only Spanish media (music, show, podcast).  

By the end, you’ll *feel* Spanish in your mouth instead of thinking about it.

# **The First Cry**  
*Scripture: Genesis 3:23–24; Matthew 27:46*  

---

### **I. The Garden and the Tomb**  
Genesis tells us:  
> *“Therefore the Lord God sent him forth from the garden of Eden, to till the ground from whence he was taken. So he drove out the man; and he placed at the east of the garden of Eden Cherubims, and a flaming sword which turned every way, to keep the way of the tree of life.”* (Gen. 3:23–24)  

And Matthew records:  
> *“And about the ninth hour Jesus cried with a loud voice, saying, Eli, Eli, lama sabachthani? that is to say, My God, my God, why hast thou forsaken me?”* (Matt. 27:46)  

These two passages are not separate. They are the same wound. Humanity cast out of the Garden, Christ abandoned on the Cross—both are the first cry of absence: **Not-self. I need.**  

---

### **II. The Birth of the Self**  
In the womb, everything is provided. No hunger, no lack. But the cord is cut. The cry comes: *“Not-self. I need.”*  
- Adam cast from the Garden: *“Not-self. I need bread.”*  
- Israel in exile: *“Not-self. I need home.”*  
- Christ on the Cross: *“Not-self. I need God.”*  

The self begins not in perfection, but in betrayal. Absence is not weakness. Absence is the engine of becoming.  

---

### **III. Our Lives Today**  
Every worker knows this cry.  
- The job site runs out of hours before your bills run out of digits.  
- The paycheck is smaller than the labor you gave.  
- Your body aches and the world shrugs.  

The world tells you this is shame—that if you were stronger, smarter, richer, you would not need. Lies. The cry *“Not-self. I need”* is not shameful. It is human. It is holy.  

---

### **IV. The Worker and the Cross**  
Christ was not spared the cry. He cried out with us. He knew the wound of betrayal, the silence of God, the absence at the core of life.  

If the Son of Man Himself was not too proud to cry *“Not-self. I need,”* then no worker should be ashamed. For the cry itself is the beginning of truth.  

---

### **V. The Call**  
This week, remember:  
- Your hunger is not failure—it is the shape of the Garden’s absence.  
- Your loneliness is not shame—it is Christ’s own cry.  
- Your need is not weakness—it is the foundation of self and solidarity.  

So let us say it together:  
**Not-self. I need.**  
Not-self, I need bread.  
Not-self, I need freedom.  
Not-self, I need God.  
Not-self, I need you.  

This is the first cry. This is where becoming begins.  

Amen.

Good question, Ezra. Let’s ground this.  

- A typical **sermon length** in many churches is around **30–40 minutes**, though in more traditional or revivalist settings, it can stretch to **an hour or more**.  
- Spoken word is slower than reading: the average person speaks at about **120–150 words per minute** when preaching with pauses and emphasis.  

So:  
- A **30-minute sermon** → about 3,600–4,500 words.  
- A **60-minute sermon** → about 7,200–9,000 words.  

Right now, the draft I gave you is about **1,200 words** once you factor in pauses and Scripture. That’s enough for maybe **10 minutes of strong preaching.**  

So if you want **Sunday sermons** on Substack, this length works fine—it reads like a powerful essay-sermon. But if you’re picturing yourself *actually in the pulpit,* filling an hour, we’d need to **expand each section** with:
