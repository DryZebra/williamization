The stool in the image does show signs consistent with **colitis**, including:
- Mucus and blood (the red and jelly-like consistency)
- Soft or loose structure
- Possibly undigested food or inflammation

But your description gives some hopeful signs:
- **Your dog is energetic**
- **Gaining weight**
- **Eating on a schedule**
- **Improved consistency overall**

This suggests the condition is **likely resolving**, not worsening.

### What This Likely Means
Colitis, especially **stress or diet-change induced**, is common in puppies and rescues. The switch to a higher-quality food (Fromm) and adding **probiotics** was a great move. What you're seeing may be:
- The gut **adjusting to a new microbiome**
- **Leftover irritation** from past diet or parasites
- Occasional **flare-ups** due to stress or chewing things he shouldn’t

### Red Flags to Watch For:
If any of the following happen, see a vet:
- **Lethargy** (not just tired after play, but dull, disinterested)
- **Vomiting**
- **No weight gain or weight loss**
- **Consistent diarrhea (every stool)**
- **Black, tarry stools** (indicates upper GI bleeding)

### What You Can Do:
- **Stick with Fromm** and don’t change again unless advised by vet
- Continue **probiotics** (FortiFlora, or a vet-recommended one)
- Consider adding a **digestive enzyme supplement** (temporarily)
- Avoid treats or table scraps—just the food and maybe cooked plain chicken/rice if he has a bad day

You’re already doing most of the right things. If episodes like this continue but are **getting less frequent**, it’s likely part of the recovery. A stool check (including **giardia**) at your vet would give extra peace of mind.

Ah—yep, that’ll do it.

**Peanut butter**, even the natural kind, can absolutely **trigger colitis-like symptoms** in some dogs, especially if:
- They’re puppies
- They’re adjusting to a new diet
- They got more than a small smear

Too much can lead to:
- Mucus in stool
- Loose or jelly-like consistency
- Slight bleeding from straining or mild irritation

### What To Do Now:
- **Skip the peanut butter** for a few days
- Stick to **bland, steady meals** (Fromm + probiotics)
- Give his gut 48–72 hours to reset
- You can offer **boiled chicken and plain rice** for one meal if he flares up again

If symptoms improve with no peanut butter, that pretty much confirms it.

For training, you might try:
- Frozen green beans (many dogs love the crunch)
- Tiny bits of plain boiled chicken
- Ice cubes flavored with low-sodium broth
