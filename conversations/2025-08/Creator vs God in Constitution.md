## 1. “Creator” vs. “God” in the Constitution
The **U.S. Constitution** itself **never mentions God or a Creator**. Its only religious references are:
- The First Amendment: *“Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof…”*  
- Article VI: *“…no religious Test shall ever be required as a Qualification to any Office or public Trust under the United States.”*  

This omission was deliberate. Many founders—especially Madison, Jefferson, and Franklin—were wary of tying government directly to a particular conception of God.  

By contrast, the **Declaration of Independence (1776)** does invoke God—but carefully:
- *“Laws of Nature and of Nature’s God”*
- *“All men are created equal, endowed by their Creator with certain unalienable Rights…”*
- *“Appealing to the Supreme Judge of the world…”*
- *“With a firm reliance on the protection of divine Providence…”*

Notice: Jefferson chose the more **philosophical “Creator”** rather than explicitly “God.” This wording allowed Deists, traditional Christians, and others to interpret it their own way. That flexibility was intentional to create unity across diverse beliefs.  

So to your point: yes—there was a conscious **difference in language between the Declaration and the Constitution.** The Constitution avoided divine references entirely, while the Declaration used broad, non-denominational terms.
