Yes, **regular tennis balls can be bad for a dog’s teeth**—especially over time. You're 100% right to be thinking about dental health early. Here's the key info:

---

### 🦷 Why Regular Tennis Balls Are Harmful
1. **Abrasive Felt**  
   The fuzzy coating on tennis balls is actually quite coarse—*like sandpaper*. It can wear down enamel, especially if your dog is an aggressive chewer or plays a lot of fetch.

2. **Traps Dirt and Grit**  
   Tennis balls easily pick up sand, dirt, and debris, turning them into a grinding surface that accelerates tooth wear.

3. **Can Lead to Dental Erosion**  
   Long-term, this can cause:
   - Enamel wear (flattened or yellowed teeth)
   - Exposed nerves or tooth pain
   - Premature tooth loss

---

### ✅ Safe Fetch Options
If he loves fetch (which is awesome exercise and bonding), here are **dentally safe alternatives**:

- **Chuckit! Ultra Balls** – Durable rubber, designed for dogs. Soft enough on teeth, easy to clean.
- **KONG Squeezz or Extreme Balls** – Softer rubber, can double as chew toys.
- **West Paw Zogoflex Balls** – Dishwasher safe, durable, and tooth-friendly.

Avoid anything that's **harder than a tooth** (e.g., bones, antlers, some hard plastics), and try to pick balls that are:
- Smooth (no felt)
- Soft but durable
- Easy to clean

---

### 🪥 Bonus Dental Tips for a Long, Healthy Life
You're doing great by planning to **brush his teeth regularly**—that’s the gold standard. Add in:
- **Dental chews** (e.g., VOHC-approved ones like Greenies)
- **Water additives** (some help with plaque)
- **Routine vet cleanings** when needed

---

### Bottom Line
