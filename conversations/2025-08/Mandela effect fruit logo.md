### Why it feels so real
1. **Associative memory** – The image of overflowing fruit almost naturally calls to mind a cornucopia, which is an ancient symbol for abundance. Our brains "fill in" the missing horn because it feels incomplete without it.
2. **Cultural reinforcement** – Cornucopias are all over Thanksgiving decorations, school textbooks, and clip art. Many of us probably saw fruit-in-cornucopia drawings elsewhere and misattributed it to Fruit of the Loom.
3. **False memory spread** – Once a few people point it out, others check their memory and it *feels* true, reinforcing the Mandela effect.

### What the records say
- The **U.S. Patent and Trademark Office** has every registered Fruit of the Loom logo, going back to the 1800s. None contain a cornucopia.  
- Old packaging and ads also show only fruit: apples, grapes, leaves, and berries.  
- Company spokespeople have confirmed publicly that a cornucopia was never part of their branding.  

So, officially, the cornucopia never existed. But the fact that *so many people* remember it vividly makes it one of the strongest Mandela Effects out there.
