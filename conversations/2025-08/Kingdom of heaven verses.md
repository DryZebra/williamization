You’re blending together a few different passages in your memory — all of them pointing toward Christ’s teaching that God’s kingdom is revealed when need is met and the last are made first:  

1. **Parable of the Workers in the Vineyard (Matthew 20:1–16)**  
   - This is the one about the laborers hired at different hours of the day but all receiving the same wage. It overturns earthly notions of hierarchy and merit, showing that the kingdom of heaven values need and generosity over strict accounting.  
   - Especially v. 14–15: *“Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own? … So the last shall be first, and the first last.”*  

2. **Provision and Trust in God (Matthew 6:25–34)**  
   - “Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not, neither do they reap … yet your heavenly Father feedeth them. Are ye not much better than they?” (v. 26)  
   - Here Christ explicitly says God provides for all, greater than the birds or lilies, so disciples should not worry for food or clothing.  

3. **The Beatitudes (Matthew 5:3–6; Luke 6:20–21)**  
   - “Blessed are ye poor: for yours is the kingdom of God” (Luke 6:20).  
   - This is the direct identification of the poor as the inheritors of the kingdom, not the rich or powerful.  

The “from each according to his ability” phrase is not a direct biblical quotation but captures the spirit of Acts 2:44–45 and Acts 4:32–35:  
- *“And all that believed were together, and had all things common; And sold their possessions and goods, and parted them to all men, as every man had need.”*  

So the verses you’re thinking of are:  
- **Matthew 20:1–16** (workers in the vineyard)  
- **Matthew 6:25–34** (God provides more than for birds/creatures)  
- **Luke 6:20** (the poor inherit the kingdom)  
- **Acts 2:44–45; Acts 4:32–35** (early church living “from each according to ability, to each according to need”)
