## 🔎 Romans 1:26
> **Διὰ τοῦτο παρέδωκεν αὐτοὺς ὁ θεὸς εἰς πάθη ἀτιμίας·**
>  
> **αἵ τε γὰρ θήλειαι αὐτῶν μετήλλαξαν τὴν φυσικὴν χρῆσιν εἰς τὴν παρὰ φύσιν·**

### Structural Translation, broken down:

#### 🔸 “Διὰ τοῦτο παρέδωκεν αὐτοὺς ὁ θεὸς”
- *Dia touto* = “Because of this”
- *paredōken autous ho theos* = “God gave them over / handed them over”

> ➤ **Because of this, God handed them over…**  
(This is not divine wrath from above, but abandonment—a *withdrawal* that allows consequences to unfold.)

---

#### 🔸 “εἰς πάθη ἀτιμίας”
- *eis pathē atimias* = “into passions of dishonor/disgrace”

The word *pathē* (plural of *pathos*) appears in Greek philosophy and NT contexts as **overpowering inner emotions**, not inherently sexual—grief, rage, and shame all count.

*Atimia* (ἀτιμία) = **loss of honor**, not “immorality” per se. It denotes being *degraded socially*, publicly shamed, stripped of standing. This is a deeply **material and cultural term**, not abstract sin.

> ➤ **…into passions that degrade them publicly.**

---

#### 🔸 “αἵ τε γὰρ θήλειαι αὐτῶν”
- *hai te gar thēleiai autōn* = “for even their females/women…”

> This is not the generic word for woman (*gynē*), but *thēleiai*, a rare biological/female-specific form. This likely highlights **biological sex over social role**.

---

#### 🔸 “μετήλλαξαν τὴν φυσικὴν χρῆσιν εἰς τὴν παρὰ φύσιν”

- *metēllaxan* = “they exchanged / replaced”
- *phusikēn chrēsin* = “natural use/function”
- *eis tēn para phusin* = “into the one alongside/natural order”

> ⚠️ *Para phusin* (παρὰ φύσιν) does not mean "unnatural" in the modern sense. It means **outside or beside the normal function**—against established norms, or outside typical purpose.

We must determine what this *exchange* is by following the dialectic into the next verse.

## 🧭 Dialectical Summary

Romans 1 does **not** make a metaphysical declaration about homosexuality. It is a material critique of:

- **Idolatry → disorder** (vv. 23–25)
- **Disorder → degrading passions** (vv. 26–27)
- **Degrading passions → self-inflicted consequence**

The acts described are not condemned based on gender or pairing—but on **exchanging the true for the false**, **misusing the body**, and becoming **enslaved to self-consuming desire**. Both heterosexual and homosexual persons can fall into this pattern.

## ✅ Answer to the Core Question

> **Does Romans 1:26–27 condemn homosexuality?**

Using only internal Greek context:

- It **condemns behavior** that abandons the function/purpose of sex and body, whether same-sex or not.
- It does **not condemn homosexual identity**.
- It portrays these acts as **symptoms** of a broader **moral and social disintegration** caused by idolatry, not the root cause.
- The shame arises **not from the pairing** but from the **self-consuming excess** and **loss of order**.

The Materialist Christian interpretation is clear: this is a **mirror** to expose when desire becomes detached from moral function—not a universal declaration of sin against same-sex relationships.
