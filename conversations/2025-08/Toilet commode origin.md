The word **“commode”** originally comes from the French *commode*, meaning “convenient” or “suitable.”  

- In the **17th–18th centuries**, a *commode* referred to a piece of furniture—a chest of drawers or a cabinet.  
- By the **18th century in England and France**, some of these cabinets were adapted to contain chamber pots. This “convenient” piece of furniture for relieving oneself kept the polite name *commode*.  
- Over time, especially in **American English**, the word shifted from meaning the furniture itself to meaning the toilet fixture.  

So when someone calls a toilet a **commode**, they’re using a word that started as a genteel euphemism for a cabinet that discreetly held a chamber pot. It stuck in certain regions (especially the American South) as the everyday term for “toilet.”
