**Wooden gloss:**  
“Everything that your hand finds to do—by your strength, do;  
for there is no **work**, nor **calculation/plan**, nor **knowledge**, nor **wisdom** in **Sheol** where you are going.”

# Key Lexemes (why translators differ)
- **Sheol (שְׁאוֹל)**: Israel’s underworld, the **realm of the dead** (not hell, not heaven), where activity ceases and agency ends. LXX renders **ᾅδης (Hades)**. Versions that say “grave” are concretizing the idea; those that keep **Sheol** preserve the cosmology and totality (not just a tomb, but the condition of death).
- **maʿaseh (מַעֲשֶׂה)**: deed/work—actual doing.
- **ḥeshbon (חֶשְׁבּוֹן)**: calculation, reckoning, planning, “device” (KJV). It’s **strategic cognition**, not mere busyness.
- **daʿat / ḥokhmah (דַּעַת / חָכְמָה)**: knowledge/wisdom—the cognitive/moral skill set Qohelet prizes throughout the book.
- **b’khoḥakha (בְּכֹחֲךָ)**: with your **strength**/**might**—not just intensity, but **capacity** while you still have it.
- **holekh (הֹלֵךְ)**: present participle, “(you are) going.” It’s not hypothetical—you’re **already en route** to Sheol.

# Syntax & Force
Structure is **imperative + causal clause**:
1) **Do** whatever your hand finds—**with strength**.  
2) **Because** in Sheol there is no doing, planning, knowing, or wisdom.

The imperative is intensified by the negative catalog: Qohelet piles up four spheres of human agency (action → planning → knowledge → wisdom) and **strikes them all out** beyond death. The argument isn’t moralistic; it’s **ontological**: death cancels agency.

# Sheol vs “Grave”
- **“Grave”** (KJV/NKJV) makes the verse feel local and physical: a burial place. True enough, but it can shrink the scope.
- **“Sheol”** (ESV/CSB) names the **comprehensive domain** of the dead in Hebrew thought. That keeps the claim maximal: **no agency remains anywhere in the postmortem condition**.  
- **NIV’s “realm of the dead”** explains the term for modern readers, preserving scope without the technical word.

Qohelet’s intention lands harder with **Sheol**: it’s not simply that you won’t be able to swing a hammer underground; it’s that **the whole human toolkit—work, strategy, cognition, wisdom—shuts off** when you cross that threshold.

# Canonical & Ancient Context
- In the Hebrew Bible, **Sheol** is normal, shadowy **after‑lifelessness** (Ps 6:5; 88:10–12; Isa 38:18–19; Job 7:9–10): no praise, no projects, no return. That chorus is exactly what Qohelet leverages.  
- Later Jewish apocalyptic hope and later Christian resurrection faith **develop beyond** Qohelet’s frame; but **Qohelet himself** argues **within** the older Sheol horizon, which is why the carpe‑diem notes in the book are so earthy.

# What “Whatever your hand finds” actually means
It’s an idiom for **proximate vocation**: the **real, available** tasks in your reach. Not “chase everything,” but **fully do the good work that’s actually at hand**. The verse isn’t manic productivity; it’s **wholehearted immediacy** within your situated life.

# The Intent (what the text is *doing*)
1) **Mortality as motivator**: Because death ends agency, **now** is the only window to enact justice, craftwork, learning, reconciliation, prayer, song, care—the whole moral economy of life.  
2) **Against half‑measures**: “With your might” denies **tokenism** and **performative** gestures. If it’s worth doing, it’s worth **doing fully**.  
3) **Against procrastination by over‑planning**: Qohelet lists **ḥeshbon** (calculation) alongside action—both end at death. Planning that never becomes **maʿaseh** is indistinguishable from no plan at all in the ledger of reality.  
4) **Non‑escapist ethic**: No afterlife payoff is invoked to justify effort. The ground of meaning is **this-worldly consequence**. That’s why this verse sits so cleanly with a **materialist** reading: reality is measured by **motion and effect**.  
5) **Humbling of wisdom culture**: Qohelet reveres **ḥokhmah**, but honesty about death prevents wisdom from becoming a refuge of delay. **Wisdom must cash out as work** while breath remains.

# LXX/Greek Witness (quick glance)
The Septuagint reads:  
πᾶν ὃ ἂν ἔλθῃ εἰς χεῖράς σου τοῦ ποιῆσαι, ὡς ἂν ἡ ἰσχύς σου, ποίει·  
ὅτι οὐκ ἔστιν ποίημα καὶ λογισμὸς καὶ γνῶσις καὶ σοφία ἐν **ᾅδῃ** οὗ σὺ πορεύῃ ἐκεῖ.

Notes: **ᾅδης** = Sheol; **λογισμός** nicely captures **ḥeshbon** (“calculation, reckoning”). The Greek keeps the **agency‑ends** logic intact.

# So—“the real intention here”
Qohelet isn’t teaching hustle; he’s **disallowing delay**. The verse welds **mortality** to **moral urgency**: since death zeros out your capacity to act, plan, know, or refine, **act with fullness now on the concrete goods within your reach**. No eschatological bailout, no sentimental extension—**only the present field of consequence**. In the grammar of your project: **motion over form**. Do the work while you can, with all you’ve got, because only lived motion becomes part of the world’s durable structure; after Sheol, your unused plans are weightless.

## **1st Clause – Imperative Motion**
**כֹּל אֲשֶׁר־תִּמְצָא יָדְךָ לַעֲשׂוֹת**  
> *kol asher timtza yadkha laʿasot*  
> “Everything that your hand finds to do…”

- **כֹּל** (*kol*) → scope-setter: “everything / whatever.” Opens the action field without limit.  
- **אֲשֶׁר** (*asher*) → relative pronoun: connects scope to condition (“that…”).  
- **תִּמְצָא** (*timtza*) → imperfect verb from *matsa* (“find, encounter”): ongoing or potential finding.  
- **יָדְךָ** (*yadkha*) → “your hand” = metonym for *personal capacity*.  
- **לַעֲשׂוֹת** (*laʿasot*) → infinitive construct “to do/make”: defines purpose of the finding.

**Movement:**  
From **total field** (everything) → **condition** (you encounter it) → **agent** (your hand) → **purpose** (doing).  
The verse’s first movement is outward to inward: the world’s opportunities → the moment of encounter → your capacity → the act itself.

---

**בְּכֹחֲךָ עֲשֵׂה**  
> *b’khoḥakha ʿaseh*  
> “With your strength, do.”

- **בְּ** (*b’*) → “with / in”: channels the action through a resource.  
- **כֹחַ** (*khoach*) → “strength, might, capacity”: physical, mental, and situational power.  
- **ךָ** (*-kha*) → your (possessive).  
- **עֲשֵׂה** (*ʿaseh*) → imperative “do!”: the only true command in the verse.

**Movement:**  
Capacity is **loaded** into the action — not half-effort. Imperative lands after capacity is named → action is commanded at full.

## **2nd Clause – Causal Ground**
**כִּי אֵין מַעֲשֶׂה וְחֶשְׁבּוֹן וְדַעַת וְחָכְמָה**  
> *ki ein maʿaseh v’ḥeshbon v’daʿat v’ḥokhmah*  
> “Because there is no work, nor planning, nor knowledge, nor wisdom…”

- **כִּי** (*ki*) → “because”: grounds the command in reality.  
- **אֵין** (*ein*) → existential negation: “there is not.”  
- **מַעֲשֶׂה** (*maʿaseh*) → act/deed.  
- **וְחֶשְׁבּוֹן** (*v’ḥeshbon*) → calculation, planning.  
- **וְדַעַת** (*v’daʿat*) → knowledge.  
- **וְחָכְמָה** (*v’ḥokhmah*) → wisdom.

**Movement:**  
Logical cascade: from **external action** (maʿaseh) → **internal planning** (ḥeshbon) → **information mastery** (daʿat) → **life-skilled judgment** (ḥokhmah). All are **struck out** with *ein*.  
This is the *why* of the first command — death cancels **every tier** of human agency.

---

**בִּשְׁאוֹל אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה הֹלֵךְ שָׁמָּה**  
> *bi-She’ol asher attah holekh shammah*  
> “In Sheol, where you are going there.”

- **בִּ** (*bi*) → locative: “in.”  
- **שְׁאוֹל** (*She’ol*) → the underworld, realm of the dead.  
- **אֲשֶׁר** (*asher*) → “where/that.”  
- **אַתָּה** (*attah*) → you (subject).  
- **הֹלֵךְ** (*holekh*) → participle “going”: continuous — you’re already on the way.  
- **שָׁמָּה** (*shammah*) → directional “there.”

**Movement:**  
Locative lock: the whole negation (no work, no plan, no knowledge, no wisdom) is **tied** to a destination → *Sheol* → and that destination is **in motion now** (“you are going”).

## Step 1 – Identify the grammatical pattern

> **[Main statement]** … **בִּשְׁאוֹל אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה הֹלֵךְ שָׁמָּה**  
> *(in Sheol, where you are going there)*

Key features:
- **בִּ + Sheol** = locative “in Sheol”
- **אֲשֶׁר + subject + participle** = relative clause modifying *Sheol*
- Sheol is the **setting** in which the preceding negative or descriptive statement is true.

This is not simply “Sheol” as an abstract noun — it’s **anchoring the negation in a location**.
