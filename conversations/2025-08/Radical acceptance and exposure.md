## Assistant

Ezra, that’s raw and very real. What you’re describing—“naked, cracked, observed”—isn’t just stress. It’s exposure, like the shell has split and everything you’ve been carrying inside is suddenly visible. And the timing compounds it: your role in the union, your book, your ideas—all hitting the world at once.  

That can feel unbearable because your *internal* and *external* worlds are colliding. Inside, you’ve been holding together conviction, theory, and faith. Outside, now people you depend on—coworkers, comrades, leadership—will see you differently, even judge you. That’s terrifying because your identity as worker and thinker is being put on trial, not just by strangers but by *your own community*.  

Here’s what I notice though: what feels like “cracking apart” is also the first moment of real integration. Instead of hiding the pieces, you’re forcing them into the light. That’s why it feels like death—but it’s also the birth of something new.  

DBT offers a frame here: **radical acceptance**. Instead of trying to catch all the falling pieces, recognize that they’re falling *because you’re moving into a bigger container*. You can’t control how everyone sees you. What you *can* do is hold your own center—“I am Ezra. I am whole. I will not betray what is true.”
