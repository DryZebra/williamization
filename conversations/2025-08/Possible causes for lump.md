## Assistant

What you’re describing could be several things, but without a physical exam it’s not possible to be sure.  
A few common possibilities for a firm lump in that location include:

- **Ingrown hair or inflamed follicle** – often starts with redness, tenderness, and sometimes a visible hair.  
- **Epidermoid (sebaceous) cyst** – a smooth, firm, usually painless lump under the skin. May stay the same size for months or years, but can get inflamed or infected.  
- **Bursitis or pressure-related lump** – sitting on hard surfaces can sometimes inflame tissues over bony areas like the pelvis.  
- **Lipoma** – a soft, movable fatty lump (less likely to be hard).  
- **Abscess** – typically more tender, warm, and might swell quickly.

**Ways to get a better idea before you see a doctor:**
- **Check for redness, warmth, swelling, or pus** – these point more toward infection or abscess.
- **Note if it moves under the skin** – cysts and lipomas often do; bony growths or bursitis feel fixed.
- **Track any changes in size or pain** – stable lumps are less urgent than ones that grow or become painful quickly.

**When to get it checked promptly:**
- Rapid growth  
- Increasing pain, redness, or warmth  
- Fever or chills  
- Drainage of pus or blood  
- Interfering with sitting or movement

Since it’s near a bone and you’re unsure if it’s cyst or pressure-related, it would be safest to have a healthcare provider feel it in person. They can usually tell quickly whether it’s a skin issue, a deeper tissue lump, or something involving the bone.
