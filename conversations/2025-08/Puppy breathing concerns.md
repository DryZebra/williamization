Got it — thanks for describing it so clearly. A few things to keep in mind:  

- **Puppy breathing rate:** Puppies normally breathe faster than adult dogs, especially when resting or sleeping. Up to **30–40 breaths per minute** can be normal, sometimes even higher when dreaming.  
- **Shallow, raspy, or snuffly breathing:** Many puppies sound noisy because their nasal passages are small, and some breeds (especially short-snouted ones like pugs, bulldogs, or mixes) naturally sound congested or “smoker-like.” Even long-snouted pups can snuffle a bit just from growing airways.  
- **Energy and behavior:** Since you said he runs, plays, and rests calmly without distress, that’s very reassuring. True breathing trouble usually comes with red flags like open-mouth breathing at rest, blue or pale gums, wheezing/coughing, collapsing after play, or refusing to eat.  
- **Possible causes of noisy but “fine” breathing:**  
  • Normal puppy fast breathing  
  • Mild nasal congestion (dust, recent kennel cough recovery, allergies)  
  • Breed-related airway shape  
  • Mild habit of panting/shallow breathing at rest  

📌 **What you can do:**  
1. Time his resting breaths per minute while he’s asleep — under 40/minute is usually normal for a pup.  
2. Listen for changes: if it gets louder, harsher, or he starts coughing, wheezing, or struggling, that’s a red flag.  
3. If you’re worried, a vet can listen with a stethoscope to rule out heart or lung issues.  

👉 Since you’re describing it as “he sounds like he smoked for 49 years,” it’s worth at least having the vet check his lungs and airway just once, especially if he’s a brachycephalic breed or if you notice anything worsening.

Ah, that extra detail helps a lot. What you’re describing — a constant “snorty,” flappy sound every breath, both awake and asleep — is usually not about how *fast* he’s breathing, but about the *structure of his airway.*  

A few things this could be:  

- **Elongated soft palate** – Common in many breeds, even some not considered flat-faced. The back of the soft palate flaps into the airway, making every breath sound like a little snore.  
- **Narrow nostrils or nasal passages** – Some pups are just born with tight airways, which causes constant snuffling.  
- **Laryngeal/airway floppiness** – Puppies can have “floppy” tissue in their throats that vibrates when they breathe, much like people who snore or need CPAP.  
- **Breed predisposition** – If he’s brachycephalic (bulldog, pug, boxer, shih tzu, etc.) this is extremely common. But even mixed breeds can have it.  

📌 **The key point:**  
If he’s playful, eating, growing, and not collapsing or gasping, it’s not immediately dangerous. Many dogs live their whole lives like this, just being noisy. But: over time, if it *is* an elongated palate or narrow nostrils, it can put strain on the airway and heart, and some dogs benefit from minor surgery when older.  

👉 **What you can do now:**  
- Record a short clip of the sound to show your vet (super helpful).  
- Watch for *red flags*: blue gums, fainting after play, constant open-mouth breathing at rest, or worsening noise as he grows.  
- At your next vet visit, ask them to check specifically for **brachycephalic airway syndrome** (even if he’s not flat-faced) or elongated soft palate.  

Some dogs just stay “snorty” forever and it’s only a quirk. Others may need help later.
