The wording comes from **Exodus 20:3** (the opening of the Ten Commandments). In Hebrew it reads:  

**לֹא־יִהְיֶה לְךָ אֱלֹהִים אֲחֵרִים עַל־פָּנָיַ**  
*Lo yiheyeh lekha elohim acherim al-panay.*  

A literal rendering is:  
**“There shall not be to you other gods upon my face/presence.”**

Key points in the language:  

- **אֱלֹהִים (elohim):** This doesn’t exclusively mean “gods” in the sense of Zeus or Baal. It’s a flexible term used for divine beings, spiritual powers, rulers, or authorities. It can mean “gods,” but also “powers.”  

- **אֲחֵרִים (acherim):** Not just “other” but “foreign, strange, alien.” The emphasis is not only numerically “other than me” but qualitatively “not of me, foreign to me.”  

- **עַל־פָּנָיַ (al panay):** Literally “upon my face” or “before my face.” In Hebrew idiom, this means “in my presence” or “over against me.” It implies rivalry, competition, or intrusion into the relationship with YHWH.  

So the commandment is not phrased as “don’t believe in other gods” in a metaphysical sense. It’s relational and covenantal:  
**“You shall not bring foreign powers into my presence, set before me, or treat them as rivals.”**

It’s more like a marriage vow: exclusivity, loyalty, fidelity. The emphasis is on YHWH as the sole God of Israel, not in the abstract denial that others exist but in the demand that none are to be placed before Him in worship, allegiance, or trust.
