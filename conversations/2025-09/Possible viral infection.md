## Assistant

What you’re describing—malaise, body aches, and pain at the back of the nose after attending a big conference—sounds most consistent with a **respiratory viral infection**.  

The top possibilities are:  

- **Common cold (rhinovirus, adenovirus, etc.)**  
  These often start with a sore nose/throat, body aches, and fatigue. Usually mild, clears in 5–7 days.  

- **Flu (influenza)**  
  Flu is more likely if the body aches are stronger, you develop fever/chills, and fatigue is sudden and overwhelming. Conferences can spread flu quickly if it’s circulating.  

- **COVID-19**  
  Still very possible at large gatherings. Symptoms often include sore throat, congestion, fatigue, body aches, and sometimes loss of taste/smell, though newer variants tend to present more like a bad cold.  

- **Other respiratory viruses (RSV, metapneumovirus, etc.)**  
  These can cause similar symptoms but are less common in adults compared to cold/flu/COVID.  

### What I’d suggest:
1. **Monitor your symptoms** – if fever spikes, breathing gets harder, or you feel much worse quickly, treat it as more serious.  
2. **Test for COVID and flu** if you have access, especially since you’ve just been at a large event.  
3. **Rest, hydrate, and treat symptomatically** with things like acetaminophen/ibuprofen for aches, saline spray or steam for nasal irritation, etc.  
4. **Limit contact** with vulnerable people until you know what it is.

## Assistant

That new detail shifts the picture a bit. A sudden wave of sneezing, watery eyes, and lots of clear snot—especially right after drinking water—sounds much more like **your upper airway/nasal passages being irritated** than a systemic illness kicking in. A couple possibilities:  

### Likely Explanations
- **Allergic or irritant reaction** – Sometimes conference halls (dust, mold, air filters, cleaning chemicals) leave you primed. Back home, even a small trigger like cold water or a shift in humidity can set off your nasal nerves, causing a sneezing fit with tearing.  
- **Viral infection in very early stage** – The burning at the back of the nose/throat and mild malaise could be the “prodrome.” Viruses often start with irritation and post-nasal drip before fever or body aches show up.  
- **Nonallergic rhinitis (“gustatory rhinitis”)** – For some people, cold drinks or spicy food cause the nose to suddenly run and trigger sneezing, unrelated to infection or allergy.  

### What to Watch
- If it stays as sneezing, runny nose, watery eyes without fever → could be allergies or irritation.  
- If sore throat worsens, body aches build, or fever appears → more likely a virus (cold/flu/COVID).  

### What You Can Try
- Rinse nose with saline spray or neti pot to clear irritants.  
- Take an antihistamine (like loratadine or cetirizine) if you suspect allergies.  
- Rest, hydrate, and monitor whether systemic symptoms (fever, fatigue spike) develop.  

⚠️ The sharp burn in the back of the nose/throat is often the very first sign of a viral infection, but the **explosive sneezing and tears right after drinking water** is unusual for an infection alone—it points to your nasal reflexes being triggered.
