# Soda hunger effects

## User

When I drink a diet soda early in the morning I get hungry later. Would a regular soda have the same effect?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Good question. The short version: **yes, both diet soda and regular soda can make you feel hungrier later, but they do it through slightly different mechanisms.**

---

### Diet Soda
- **Artificial sweeteners** (like aspartame, sucralose, or acesulfame K) are much sweeter than sugar but provide little to no calories.  
- When you drink one, your body anticipates calories because it detects sweetness. But since no calories arrive, it can trigger increased hunger later.  
- Some studies suggest artificial sweeteners may alter gut hormones and microbiota in ways that influence appetite, though results are mixed.

---

### Regular Soda
- **Sugar (high-fructose corn syrup or sucrose)** gives you calories up front.  
- These calories come as **simple carbohydrates** that cause a **spike in blood sugar**, followed by an **insulin response**.  
- The crash afterward can make you feel hungry, often within a few hours.  
- Regular soda also adds a lot more total calories, which can lead to weight gain if it becomes a habit.

---

### The Difference
- **Diet soda** → “phantom calories” effect: your body expects energy but doesn’t get it, which may make you hungrier.  
- **Regular soda** → “sugar crash” effect: you get quick energy that drops off, making you hungry again.  

Both can lead to mid-morning hunger, though the effect may feel stronger or faster depending on your personal response to sweeteners vs. sugar.

---

✅ If your goal is to avoid that rebound hunger, water, unsweetened tea/coffee, or even a small protein-containing breakfast (like a boiled egg or Greek yogurt) will be much better at keeping you steady.
