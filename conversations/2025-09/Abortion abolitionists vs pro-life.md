# Abortion abolitionists vs pro-life

## User

What's the big differences between abortion abolitionists and pro life?

## Assistant



## Assistant

The two overlap in some beliefs, but they’re not the same movement. Here’s the big picture:  

---

## **Pro-Life Movement**
- **Strategy:** Works mainly through gradual legal restrictions, political lobbying, crisis pregnancy centers, and cultural persuasion.  
- **Goal:** Reduce the number of abortions and ideally overturn Roe v. Wade (before Dobbs) or pass state/federal restrictions afterward.  
- **Approach to Law:** Supports incremental measures—like heartbeat bills, parental consent laws, late-term bans, or funding restrictions.  
- **Tone:** Often frames itself as “compassionate,” aiming to win public opinion and create exceptions (rape, incest, life of the mother).  
- **Identity:** Broad tent, includes Catholics, evangelicals, conservatives, and moderates who want fewer abortions but may not call it murder in the same sense as homicide.  

---

## **Abortion Abolitionists**
- **Strategy:** Reject incrementalism; push for immediate, total criminalization of abortion without exceptions.  
- **Goal:** Treat abortion as legally equal to homicide—both for doctors and, often, for mothers seeking abortion.  
- **Approach to Law:** “No compromise.” They oppose pro-life bills that regulate abortion rather than ban it outright, calling them unjust.  
- **Tone:** Confrontational—use language like “abortion is murder,” openly condemn churches and politicians who compromise.  
- **Identity:** Smaller but intense movement, often rooted in evangelical circles. They see themselves as parallel to slavery abolitionists—arguing you can’t regulate evil, you must abolish it.  

---

## **Key Differences in Practice**
- **Pro-life:** Might support a 20-week ban → “It saves some lives.”  
- **Abolitionist:** Opposes the 20-week ban → “It still allows murder before 20 weeks.”  
- **Pro-life:** Seeks political coalitions.  
- **Abolitionist:** Rejects coalitions if they involve compromise.
