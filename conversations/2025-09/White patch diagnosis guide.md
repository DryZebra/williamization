## Assistant

I’m not a doctor, but I can give you some context.  

A white patch or spot inside your lip that doesn’t hurt could be several things:  

- **Canker sore (aphthous ulcer):** Usually painful, so less likely if there’s no discomfort.  
- **Friction/trauma lesion:** Sometimes caused by biting your lip or irritation from teeth/braces. These often heal on their own in 1–2 weeks.  
- **Leukoplakia:** A white patch that doesn’t scrape off, usually painless. This needs evaluation because it can sometimes be precancerous.  
- **Oral thrush (yeast infection):** Usually wipes off, sometimes with a raw surface underneath. More common if you’ve been on antibiotics or have immune issues.  
- **Other causes:** Oral lichen planus, mucous cysts, or less common conditions.  

⚠️ **What to do:**  
- If it goes away in a week or two, it’s probably irritation or minor trauma.  
- If it’s been there for **more than 2 weeks**, or if it grows, changes shape, or feels firm, it’s important to have a dentist or doctor check it.  
- If you use tobacco or alcohol, it’s especially important to get oral white patches examined, since those raise risk for precancerous changes.  

👉 For now: keep an eye on it, avoid irritating foods (very spicy, acidic), and note if it changes. If it persists beyond 2 weeks, make a dental or medical appointment.

That makes sense. If you bit down hard enough to break the skin and even make your teeth meet, what you’re seeing now is most likely a **healing trauma lesion**. Those often appear as a whitish patch or ulcer as the tissue regenerates. They can look alarming but are usually harmless and fade in about **7–14 days**.  

A few things you can do while it heals:  
- **Keep it clean:** Rinse your mouth with warm salt water (½ tsp salt in a glass of water) once or twice daily.  
- **Avoid irritation:** Don’t chew on that side if possible, and avoid acidic or spicy foods.  
- **Moisturize:** Keeping your mouth hydrated helps tissue heal faster.  

⚠️ Watch out for:  
- If it doesn’t start improving after 1–2 weeks.  
- If it gets bigger, harder, or painful.  
- If new white patches appear elsewhere without clear trauma.  

If any of those happen, that’s the time to see a dentist or doctor.
