Short version: a UV “bug zapper” by itself won’t dent mosquito numbers—they’re not into UV light—so it mostly fries non-biting insects. To actually lower the population cheaply, pair your zapper with CO₂/lures **and** kill larvae where they hatch. citeturn1search1turn1search3

# A cheap, long-term setup that actually works

**1) Kill them before they fly (larvae control).**  
Once a week, dump/scrub anything that holds water. For water you can’t dump (birdbaths, rain barrels, ponds), toss in **Bti** (Mosquito Dunks/Bits). Bti targets mosquito larvae, is safe for people, pets, fish, and bees when used as directed. Re-dose about every 30 days. citeturn0search1turn0search6turn0search10turn0search15

**2) Run “gravid buckets” (baited ovitraps) with Bti.**  
Park a couple of dark 5-gal buckets with grass clippings/hay infusion + a Bti dunk. Females lay eggs there; larvae die. This is the same principle behind CDC’s AGO traps that cut Aedes populations by ~50–80% in field trials. Keep buckets where pets/kids can’t drink them; refresh monthly. citeturn0search3turn0search8turn0search12

**3) Make your big zapper actually target mosquitoes.**  
Add **octenol (1-octen-3-ol)** cartridges (e.g., Flowtron MA-1000, ~\$10–\$15, ~450 hrs per cartridge) and hang the unit **25–40 ft away** from where you sit, 6–8 ft high, and ideally **between** breeding spots and people (downwind). Octenol helps, but it works best when mosquitoes are already being drawn by CO₂. citeturn0search4turn0search9turn2search3turn2search6

**4) Add cheap CO₂ when you’re outside (biggest attractant).**  
Mosquitoes track carbon dioxide; even a **yeast + sugar bottle** makes useful CO₂ for 24–36 hours and boosts catches; CO₂+octenol > CO₂ > no bait > octenol alone in trials. Set the CO₂ source **upwind of the zapper** so the plume passes over the grid. (Don’t put lures near people—pulls biters to you.) citeturn0search2turn3search0turn3search5

## What not to rely on
