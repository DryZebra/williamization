The proverb distinguishes between two orientations toward labor. The diligent worker, who applies himself consciously and steadily, is not just performing tasks but shaping the conditions of life. That labor is voluntary in its essence, not coerced, because it is chosen as life’s prime want. The one who shirks labor, who avoids it or treats it as a burden, ends up enslaved by external force — “under tribute.”
