# Decaf vs Coffee

What's the actual difference between decaf and coffee?

## Assistant

The main difference between **regular coffee** and **decaf** is the **amount of caffeine**:

- **Regular coffee**: Contains caffeine naturally. A typical 8 oz (240 ml) cup has about **95 mg of caffeine**, though this varies depending on the bean, roast, and brewing method.
- **Decaf coffee**: Has most of the caffeine removed during processing. It’s not completely caffeine-free—an 8 oz cup usually has **2–7 mg of caffeine**.

### How decaf is made
Decaffeination is done before roasting, usually while the beans are still green. Common methods include:
- **Solvent-based**: Using chemicals like methylene chloride or ethyl acetate to dissolve caffeine.
- **Swiss Water Process**: Uses water and carbon filters to remove caffeine without chemicals.
- **CO₂ process**: Uses pressurized carbon dioxide to extract caffeine.

### Taste and nutrition
- **Taste**: Decaf can taste slightly different, often milder or flatter, since some flavor compounds are removed along with caffeine. High-quality methods (like Swiss Water) preserve flavor better.
- **Nutrition**: Both decaf and regular coffee have similar levels of antioxidants, minerals (like magnesium and potassium), and natural compounds that contribute to health benefits.

### Effects on the body
- **Regular coffee**: Gives you caffeine’s stimulant effects—alertness, improved concentration, possible jitters, and sleep disruption if consumed late.
- **Decaf**: Lets you enjoy the flavor and some health benefits of coffee with little to no stimulant effect, making it better for people sensitive to caffeine or drinking later in the day.
