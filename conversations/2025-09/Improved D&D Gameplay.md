That makes a lot of sense — early attempts at D&D with me (or my earlier versions) often had those problems: continuity slips, new names popping up, and "one-page-at-a-time" story generation without really respecting your choices.  

Now I can do a lot better in a few key ways:

### 1. **Continuity & Memory**
I can keep track of characters, stats, locations, and items over a whole campaign. If you pick up a sword in session 1, I’ll remember you have it in session 5 without suddenly renaming it.

### 2. **Structured Gameplay**
Instead of me just generating a wall of text after each of your decisions, I can:
- Present clear **choices** when appropriate (like a DM would).  
- Handle **dice rolls and modifiers** with transparency.  
- Keep a **log of your character sheet** (stats, spells, abilities, inventory) that updates as you go.  

### 3. **Tone Control**
I can narrate in **shorter beats** (like a DM at the table) instead of full pages, so you get space to decide what to do next without the story running away from you. If you want longer story-like passages, I can switch into "prose mode" when you want immersion.

### 4. **Consistency Tools**
When we play, I’ll keep:
- A **session recap** at the start.  
- A **character/party sheet** that updates as we go.  
- A **world notes log** (town names, NPCs, factions, etc.) so details don’t get lost.
