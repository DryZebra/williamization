## Assistant

Not exactly. **Ibuprofen isn’t a plant—it’s a synthetic drug.**

It was first made in the 1960s by chemists at Boots UK. The researchers were actually looking for a safer alternative to aspirin. While they started by studying compounds related to natural plant chemicals (like propionic acid derivatives, which are also found in some plants), ibuprofen itself does **not** come directly from any plant. It’s fully manufactured in a lab from petroleum-derived starting materials.  

That said, the *idea* behind ibuprofen was inspired by natural anti-inflammatory compounds found in plants, and some of its relatives (like salicylic acid, which gave us aspirin) do originally come from plants such as willow bark.  

👉 So short answer: ibuprofen is man-made, not a plant, but it’s part of a family of drugs originally inspired by plant chemistry.
