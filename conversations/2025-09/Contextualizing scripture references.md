## Assistant

That comment is pulling together three passages that have historically been debated about whether Jesus is a created being or eternal. Here’s the context behind each:  

---

### **Colossians 1:15**  
> “He is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation.”  

- **Surface reading:** Some groups (like Arians in the 4th century, and later Jehovah’s Witnesses) argued this means Jesus was literally the first created being.  
- **Mainstream Christian interpretation:** “Firstborn” in biblical usage often means *preeminent rank*, not literal birth order. Example: Israel is called God’s “firstborn” in Exodus 4:22, even though Israel wasn’t the first nation to exist. Paul is emphasizing Jesus’ supremacy over all creation, not that He is part of creation.  

---

### **Proverbs 8:22**  
> “The Lord possessed me at the beginning of his work, the first of his acts of old.”  

- In context, Proverbs 8 is about **Wisdom personified**, not explicitly Jesus.  
- But early Christians (like Justin Martyr) saw “Wisdom” as a foreshadowing of Christ. Arians used this verse to argue Christ was *created*.  
- Orthodox theologians responded that the Hebrew word can mean “possessed” or “brought forth,” and that the passage is metaphorical—wisdom is eternal with God, not something created in time.  

---

### **Revelation 3:14**  
> “The words of the Amen, the faithful and true witness, the beginning of God’s creation.”  

- **Surface reading:** Sounds like Jesus is the first created being.  
- **Mainstream Christian interpretation:** The Greek word *archē* (“beginning”) can mean *source* or *ruler*. The idea is not that Jesus was created, but that He is the origin or ruler of creation (similar to John 1:3, “without him nothing was made that has been made”).  

---

### **Big Picture**  
The person quoting those verses is probably leaning on an **Arian-style argument**: that Jesus is subordinate to God the Father and not eternal. Historically, this was condemned as heresy at the Council of Nicaea (325 AD), which affirmed Christ as “begotten, not made, being of one substance with the Father.”
