# Marx Engels Saint-Simon Influence

## User

Where is Marx and Engels work most closely influenced by saint Simon?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Marx and Engels were most closely influenced by **Saint-Simon** in their earliest intellectual development, especially before they broke decisively with what they later called “utopian socialism.”  

Here’s where the influence shows up most clearly:

---

### 1. Historical Role of Industry and Science
Saint-Simon emphasized that **industrial development and scientific progress** were the driving forces of history. He saw industrial workers, scientists, and engineers as the true leading class of modern society.  
Marx and Engels absorbed this idea and carried it further: in *The Communist Manifesto* they highlight how the development of productive forces (industry, technology) drives historical change.

---

### 2. Class Leadership of the Proletariat
Saint-Simon anticipated the idea that a new class would lead humanity into the future. He often emphasized **industrial workers as central** to reorganizing society.  
Marx and Engels radicalized this into the theory that the **proletariat, through class struggle, would abolish class society altogether.** The seed is Saint-Simon’s idea of workers as society’s essential force.

---

### 3. Reorganization of Society
Saint-Simon argued for the replacement of chaotic, exploitative competition with a **planned, cooperative society** led by producers.  
This resonates directly with Marx and Engels’ vision of communism as the rational, conscious organization of production for human need rather than profit.

---

### 4. Critique of Feudal and Idle Classes
Saint-Simon sharply criticized the parasitic aristocracy and idle classes, elevating productive labor as morally and socially superior.  
Marx and Engels inherit this language of **productive vs. unproductive classes**, which becomes central to their critique of capitalism.

---

### Where Marx and Engels Depart
- Saint-Simon remained **utopian and technocratic**, imagining elites of scientists and industrialists guiding society.  
- Marx and Engels insisted instead on **class struggle**, the self-emancipation of the working class, and a dialectical materialist method.  

---

📌 **In short:** Marx and Engels’ work is most closely influenced by Saint-Simon in their emphasis on industrial development, productive labor, and the need for a rationally organized society. The *Communist Manifesto* especially carries echoes of Saint-Simon’s industrial and worker-centered vision, though reshaped into a theory of revolutionary class struggle.

## Assistant

Yes — there are a few very clear spots where Marx and Engels either **draw from** or **explicitly address** Saint-Simon:

---

## 1. *The Communist Manifesto* (1848)
- **Chapter III: Socialist and Communist Literature, Section 1: Reactionary Socialism → Critical-Utopian Socialism**  
  Here, Marx and Engels explicitly discuss **Saint-Simon, Fourier, and Owen**. They credit them as the first to articulate the misery of the working class and the need for a new social order, but criticize them as “utopian” because they did not grasp the centrality of class struggle.  
  > “The founders of these systems … perceive the class antagonisms as well as the action of the decomposing elements in the prevailing form of society. But the proletariat … offers to them the spectacle of a class without any historical initiative or any independent political movement. … Hence, they search after a new social science …”  

  This is the most **direct passage** where they situate themselves in continuity with (and against) Saint-Simon.

---

## 2. *The Holy Family* (1845)
- Marx and Engels polemicize against the “True Socialists” in Germany, and in doing so they discuss **Saint-Simonian doctrines** as they had filtered into German philosophy and literature. They point out the **scientific and progressive kernel** of Saint-Simon while also mocking the idealist or religious dressing that German writers gave to his ideas.

---

## 3. *Socialism: Utopian and Scientific* (Engels, 1880)
- This is Engels’ clearest retrospective statement. He divides socialism into “utopian” and “scientific,” and he devotes whole sections to **Saint-Simon, Fourier, and Owen**.  
  - Engels praises Saint-Simon for recognizing the **importance of industry and productive classes**, and for seeing that **political power must belong to the producers**.  
  - He also emphasizes that Saint-Simon was the first to announce the transition from political to social rule — an anticipation of the idea of socialism.  
  - But he criticizes Saint-Simon for expecting enlightened benefactors (scientists, industrialists) rather than the **working class itself** to reorganize society.

---

## 4. *Anti-Dühring* (1878)
- Much of the material later excerpted into *Socialism: Utopian and Scientific* comes from here. Engels again treats **Saint-Simon as one of the intellectual forerunners** of scientific socialism, contrasting his utopian visions with Marx’s historical materialism.

---

### 🔑 If you want the **single clearest pointer:**
Engels’ **“Socialism: Utopian and Scientific”** is the most straightforward text where Marxism directly situates itself in relation to Saint-Simon.  
If you want to show it in Marx himself, the **“Critical-Utopian Socialism” section of the *Communist Manifesto*** is the most concise and famous place.

Can you give me an excerpt from the holy family where they talk about saint Simon explicitly?

## Assistant

Yes. In *The Holy Family* (1845), Marx and Engels mention Saint-Simon by name several times when discussing French socialism. Here’s one of the most explicit passages (chapter VI, “Critical Battle against French Materialism”):

> “The whole socialist and communist literature which had its theoretical origin with Saint-Simon, Fourier, Owen, etc., and its practical origin in the French Revolution, which in its development necessarily had to combine with the earlier French materialism, was taken notice of by the ‘Critical Criticism’ only in so far as it appeared in the form of speculative constructions, in so far as it was possible to regard it as a new revelation and to oppose it to French materialism as something higher.”  

(*The Holy Family*, MECW Vol. 4, p. 131)

---

Elsewhere in the same work, they bring up “Saint-Simonianism” as an intellectual current in Germany, mocking the way German writers spiritualized it instead of seeing its real, material content:

> “Saint-Simonianism, like Fourierism, like English Owenism, presupposes modern large-scale industry and the class struggle created by it. … In Germany it could therefore be received only speculatively, as a doctrine of universal love, of the reconciliation of all contradictions.”  

(*The Holy Family*, MECW Vol. 4, p. 36–37)

---

📌 So if you want a **direct excerpt** where Marx and Engels explicitly name Saint-Simon and show both recognition and criticism, those two are the best:  
1. p. 131 — situating Saint-Simon as a founder of socialist/communist literature.  
2. p. 36–37 — noting how Saint-Simonianism was distorted when imported into Germany.
