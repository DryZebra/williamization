## What to think about first

Because you eat only once a day, you’re more likely to have **nutrient gaps** (vitamins, minerals) than someone eating more frequently. So your multivitamin should aim to fill those gaps without going over safe limits.

Here are key criteria:

| Feature | Why It Matters | Rough Target(s) |
|---|---|---|
| **Close to 100% of the Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA)** | Avoid huge megadoses unless you have a medical reason | Don’t exceed the Tolerable Upper Intake Levels (ULs) |
| **Bioavailable forms** | Some forms (e.g. methylated B12, chelated minerals) are better absorbed | E.g. methyl-B12, magnesium citrate/glycinate, etc. |
| **Third-party testing / certification** | To ensure label accuracy and absence of contamination | USP, NSF, ConsumerLab, etc. |
| **Low levels of additives / fillers / artificial dyes** | Especially helpful if you have sensitivities | Clean ingredient lists |
| **Appropriate levels for men (especially avoiding excess iron)** | Men usually don’t need high iron unless in special cases | Iron typically at a lower level or omitted in men’s formulas |

Also keep in mind:

- Multivitamins can **help fill gaps**, but they are *not* a substitute for a balanced diet.  
- Some research suggests that for generally healthy people, multivitamins do *not* dramatically reduce risks of major diseases (cancer, cardiovascular disease) when diet is good. citeturn0search12  
- Because you eat once a day, timing your vitamin intake matters (with your meal to help absorption, especially for fat-soluble vitamins)  
- Watch for interactions if you take medications (especially vitamins K, calcium, magnesium, etc.)

## What nutrients are especially at risk when eating once a day

Because your window for absorption is narrow, you’re more likely to miss:

- **Vitamin D** — many Americans are low  
- **Magnesium**, **potassium**, **iodine**  
- **B vitamins** if your diet is light on whole grains, legumes  
- **Trace minerals** (zinc, selenium, etc.)  
- Possibly **vitamin K** depending on greens intake  

Your multivitamin should help cover those in safe amounts.
