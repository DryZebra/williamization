## Assistant

That’s a good observation, and it’s not uncommon. What you’re describing is most likely **postural hypotension** (also called *orthostatic hypotension*). Here’s what’s happening:  

### What’s Going On
- When you squat with your heels on the ground for a long time, your hips and knees are bent sharply. This position **compresses blood vessels** in your legs.  
- Blood pools in your lower body instead of circulating as freely.  
- When you suddenly stand, your body has to push that pooled blood back up to your heart and brain.  
- There’s a brief lag before your blood pressure catches up — that’s when you feel dizzy or like you might pass out.  

### Why It Feels Intense  
- The longer you stay squatting, the more blood pools in your legs.  
- Standing quickly exaggerates the drop.  
- Your brain doesn’t get enough oxygenated blood for a few seconds → dizziness, tunnel vision, “about to faint” feeling.  

### Is It Dangerous?  
- In a healthy person like you, it’s usually harmless — your body adjusts fast.  
- The real risk is if you actually black out and fall, which is rare but possible if you stand up too abruptly.  
- If it happens often outside of squatting (like just standing up from a chair or bed), that could signal an underlying blood pressure or circulation issue worth checking with a doctor.  

### What You Can Do  
- **Stand up slowly** after long squats. Even pausing half-way (knees bent, back upright) can help.  
- **Tense your leg muscles** before standing — squeezing your calves and thighs helps push blood back upward.  
- **Stay hydrated** — dehydration makes postural drops worse.  
- If it ever comes with chest pain, palpitations, or repeated fainting, that’s when you’d definitely want medical evaluation.  

---

So in your case: nothing “wrong” with you. It’s a side effect of squatting physiology, not a lung issue. You’re alright — just be mindful about popping up fast after a long squat.
