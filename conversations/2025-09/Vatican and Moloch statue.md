Short answer: no.

A statue of “Moloch” was placed at the entrance of the Roman **Colosseum** in October 2019 as part of a state-run exhibition, **“Carthago: The Immortal Myth.”** The Colosseum is managed by Italy’s Parco archeologico del Colosseo (Italian Ministry of Culture), not the Vatican. The figure itself was a prop/replica inspired by the 1914 Italian film *Cabiria*, used to introduce the Carthage exhibit, which ran Sept 27, 2019–Mar 29, 2020. citeturn1search2turn1search3turn1search8turn1search5

Some posts blended this with a separate 2019 controversy over **Pachamama** statues present during the Amazon Synod in churches near the Vatican—those were indigenous cultural figures, not “Moloch,” and that episode was unrelated to the Colosseum exhibit. citeturn0search8turn0search11

So: the Vatican did **not** “put up a Moloch statue.” A temporary, secular museum display at the **Colosseum** did. citeturn1search2
