Materialist Christianity would answer Christian universalism by shifting the frame away from metaphysical destinations and back into history and motion. In universalism, every person is eventually redeemed and taken into heaven because of Christ. That logic treats salvation as an abstract guarantee, applied the same to all, regardless of their real motion. Materialist Christianity cannot accept that, because in this view salvation is not a metaphysical ticket but the continuity of what survives in history.

In the documents, judgment is explained as entropy, collapse, and motion: when something ceases to move in a way that sustains others, it fractures, disappears, or is consumed. A kingdom falls because its motion disordered, not because God arbitrarily condemned it . The same applies to people and their ideas: when Christ curses the fig tree, the point is that it bore no fruit—its motion ended, so it withers. When he speaks of wheat and chaff, the chaff is burned because it has no further use. In material terms, that is annihilation: you are forgotten, erased from continuity, remembered only in condemnation if your deeds were destructive.

So against universalism, Materialist Christianity would say: not everyone continues. The resurrection of Christ shows how motion can pass through collapse and re-emerge, but not all things resurrect. Some fall into the “dustbin of history.” To live in Christ is to join the motion that survives, that gets repeated, remembered, and built upon. To fall outside it is to end, not with eternal torment, but with silence—your work ceases, your name forgotten, or your memory hated forever. That is the “gnashing” the gospels describe, not a supernatural punishment, but the reality of being ground down into nothing, or condemned to live on only as a warning.

In other words: Christian universalism says everyone is saved, even if hell is temporary. Materialist Christianity says salvation is continuity, not guarantee. You are saved insofar as your life joins the motion of Christ—the labor, memory, and structure that survives collapse. If you betray that, you are cut off, like the barren fig tree.
