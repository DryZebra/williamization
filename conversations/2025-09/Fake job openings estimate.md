## Assistant

A lot more than most people realize.  

Estimates vary depending on country, industry, and whether you’re looking at big job boards like Indeed/LinkedIn or company career pages. Studies and recruiter surveys suggest that **anywhere from 30% to 50% of job postings may be “fake” or misleading** in some way. Here’s what usually falls into that category:

### 1. **Ghost Jobs**
Companies leave listings up even though they don’t plan to hire soon. This keeps a “pipeline” of résumés flowing or makes the company look like it’s growing.

### 2. **Already-Filled Positions**
Sometimes the posting stays live after a candidate has already been chosen (internal hire, referral, etc.). The listing is just compliance paperwork.

### 3. **Fishing for Talent**
Recruiters may post roles to see who’s out there, benchmark salary expectations, or collect résumés for future use—not because the job exists right now.

### 4. **Misleading or Low-Quality Jobs**
Roles that don’t match the description, bait-and-switch commission sales jobs, or contract “gigs” disguised as careers.

---

### The Takeaway
If you’re scrolling through job boards, it’s fair to assume **about 1 in 3 listings aren’t truly available** at that moment. The safest bet is to focus on direct company websites, check recent updates, and prioritize postings where you can confirm a real recruiter or HR contact.
