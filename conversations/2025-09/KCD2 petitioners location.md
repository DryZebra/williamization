Short answer: **acidum chloridum = hydrochloric acid (HCl)** — commonly sold as **muriatic acid** for pools/cleaning and as **laboratory-grade hydrochloric acid** from chemical suppliers. Below are practical buying options, what concentrations to expect, and essential safety & legal notes.

# Where you can buy it
- **Hardware / home-improvement stores** — sold as *muriatic acid* (used for masonry, cleaning, pools). Easy to buy off the shelf.  
- **Pool supply stores** — muriatic acid for pH adjustment (typical product for homeowners).  
- **Online retailers (general)** — muriatic acid appears on Amazon, Home Depot, etc., but shipments are subject to hazardous-goods rules.  
- **Laboratory / chemical suppliers** (Fisher Scientific, VWR, Sigma-Aldrich, etc.) — sell reagent-grade HCl in a range of concentrations (more expensive; often requires a business or institutional account and has hazardous-material shipping rules).  
- **Local chemical distributors** — many cities have chemical suppliers that sell to the public or trade customers.  
- **Industrial suppliers** — for bulk quantities (business accounts required).

# Typical concentrations you’ll find
- **Muriatic acid (pool/brick cleaning): ~31–33% w/w HCl.** Strong and corrosive — this is what most people mean when they buy acid for heavy cleaning.  
- **Lab-grade HCl:** ranges from dilute (0.1 M solutions) up to concentrated reagent (around 35–37% HCl). Choose based on your intended use.

# Shipping & purchase restrictions
- Concentrated acids are **hazardous materials** for transport — sellers may limit quantities, require age verification, or refuse consumer shipping.  
- Lab/industrial suppliers often require **business or institutional accounts**.  
- Some retailers will only sell to those who can accept hazardous-material deliveries (not PO box).

# Safety (read this before you buy or use it)
- **Corrosive:** will burn skin, eyes, and respiratory tract.  
- **Never** mix HCl with **bleach (sodium hypochlorite)** or oxidizers — this creates **toxic chlorine gas**.  
- Always use **chemical-resistant gloves (nitrile/neoprene), splash goggles or face shield, and work in good ventilation** (fume hood or outdoors).  
- Use **compatible containers** (HDPE plastic or glass rated for acids) and store upright, tightly closed, labeled, in a cool ventilated area away from bases and oxidizers.  
- Keep **neutralizing agent** (sodium bicarbonate) and lots of water on hand for spills. For small spills, neutralize then absorb; for large spills call emergency services/HazMat.  
- Read the **Safety Data Sheet (SDS/MSDS)** before purchase or use.

# Disposal
- Follow local hazardous-waste regulations. For small, legal-to-dispose amounts, many jurisdictions allow careful neutralization (slow addition of sodium bicarbonate while monitoring pH) to near neutral and dilution — **but check local rules first**. Never pour concentrated acid down drains without confirming local guidance.

# Safer alternatives (depending on your intended use)
- **Cleaning mortar/efflorescence**: muriatic acid is common, but often **oxalic acid** or specialized masonry cleaners are safer.  
- **pH adjustment in small hobby pools**: use pool chemicals per label.  
- **Laboratory work**: buy laboratory-grade dilute solutions (0.1 M, 1 M) rather than concentrated acid.
