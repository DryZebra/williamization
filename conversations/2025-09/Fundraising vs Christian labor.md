Paul made tents, the community pooled its bread, and when wealth was shared it wasn’t philanthropy—it was the material reproduction of the church itself, born from work.  

Where the TikTok leans liberal is in assuming people will “naturally” give if asked nicely. That reflects a consumerist view of charity—treating goodness as a resource to be tapped. But in Scripture, generosity wasn’t abstract sentiment; it was structured discipline. The Acts church sold property, yes, but also organized distribution. The Epistles call believers to work with their hands so they “may lack nothing.” Even Christ’s feeding of the five thousand shows this: the bread is multiplied *through the hands of the disciples*, not dropped out of the sky.  

So the real difference is this:  
- **Fundraising (liberal model):** Begging either from the world or the congregation, appealing to sentiment or guilt, waiting on a benefactor.  
- **Christian labor (biblical model):** Meeting needs through shared work, production, and distribution.  

This doesn’t mean rejecting gifts—but it does mean refusing to treat the church like a nonprofit with donor drives. The truth is the opposite: the church itself should be a generator of material life, a house built on labor.
