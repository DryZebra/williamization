---

## 1. Starting a Church in Your House
- **Informal start:** In the U.S., you don’t need anyone’s permission to gather people for worship in your home. A “church” in the broad sense exists as soon as people consistently meet for religious practice.  
- **Formal recognition:** If you want legal recognition (e.g. tax-exempt status), you’d usually incorporate as a **nonprofit religious organization** in your state, then apply to the IRS for **501(c)(3)** status.  

---

## 2. Using Your Website (materialistChristianity.org)
- This can be the church’s **public face**—a place for sermons, study guides, statements of belief, donation links, etc.  
- You could register the church as “The Church of Materialist Christianity” with that domain as its official site.  
- That also helps if you later want to apply for grants—funders look for an official online presence.  

---

## 3. Tax Standing
- **Tax-exempt status:** If you’re granted 501(c)(3), donations are tax-deductible, and the church doesn’t pay federal income tax.  
- **Housing allowance (parsonage exemption):** Ministers of recognized churches can designate part of their income as a tax-free housing allowance—even if they live in their own home. This is one of the biggest financial advantages.  
- **Property taxes:** Some states/counties exempt church property from property tax, but rules vary and usually require a formal building used exclusively for worship. A house-church sometimes qualifies if you can show a dedicated worship space.  

---

## 4. Income Streams
- **Donations/tithes:** The main one. You’d need a transparent system for this if you want to be credible.  
- **Publishing/teaching:** Sermons, devotional materials, study guides—these can be sold or given free with optional donations.  
- **Grants:** Some religious nonprofits get grants, but usually only if they also provide community services (food pantry, counseling, addiction recovery, tutoring, etc.). You’d need to structure programs that align with grantmaker goals.  
- **Subsidies:** Churches don’t typically get subsidies directly. But they can receive support indirectly (free food distribution from local nonprofits, donated goods, etc.).  

---

## 5. Practical Steps
1. **Draft bylaws and statement of belief** (Materialist Christianity doctrines).  
2. **Incorporate** as a nonprofit religious corporation in NC.  
3. **Apply for EIN** (free through IRS website).  
4. **Apply for 501(c)(3)** religious organization status. Churches sometimes qualify automatically, but formal application makes it easier to access grants and open bank accounts.  
5. **Set up financial accountability** (separate bank account, board members, documented decisions).  

---

## 6. Warnings
- The IRS has cracked down on “sham” churches created only for tax avoidance. You’ll need to show real activities: regular meetings, ministry, outreach, etc.  
- You personally can’t just live tax-free. But if you’re legitimately running a church, the **minister’s housing allowance** can be significant.  
- Grants won’t be handed to you for existing. They usually require service projects with measurable results.

### **Do you need the physical SSN card?**
- **No, the IRS doesn’t require the actual card.** They require your **SSN itself** (the number).  
- **Yes, banks usually want to see the physical card** when you open a nonprofit checking account. Some banks accept just your driver’s license + the SSN written down, but many insist on the physical card.  

---

### **For the EIN (first IRS step)**
- You can apply online at the IRS site in about 10 minutes.  
- The form asks for a **“responsible party”** (you, the founder). It will ask for your **name and SSN**, but it doesn’t make you upload or show the card.  
- Once that’s approved, you’ll immediately have an EIN to start filing and preparing 501(c)(3).  

---

### **For the 501(c)(3) application (Form 1023)**
- Again, the IRS only asks for your SSN and personal details typed in. They don’t ask for a copy of your card or certificate.  
- What they do require is: Articles of Incorporation, Bylaws, Mission, Budget, etc. (the paperwork we listed earlier).  

---

### **Where the physical SSN card/birth certificate matters**
1. **Bank account** — Most banks want to see your card or at least a second piece of ID tied to your SSN. Without a church bank account, you can’t properly receive tax-deductible donations or grants.  
2. **State registrations** — If North Carolina (your state) asks for proof of identity for nonprofit incorporation, they may accept just driver’s license + SSN, but some situations can trigger extra ID checks.  
3. **Grants** — Some grant funders (especially federal) will ask for multiple forms of ID from the responsible officer.  

---

👉 So the *short truth*:  
- You **can** get the EIN and file 501(c)(3) online with just your SSN number (not card).
