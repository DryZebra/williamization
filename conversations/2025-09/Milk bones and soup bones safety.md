Good question, Ezra — let’s break this down into **two parts: Milk-Bones (dog biscuits)** and **soup bones (for chewing/eating).**

---

### 🦴 Milk-Bones
- **Generally safe:** Milk-Bones are one of the most common commercial dog treats. They’ve been around for decades and are considered safe in moderation for most healthy dogs.  
- **Ingredients:** They’re made mostly of wheat, meat/meat by-products, and added vitamins/minerals. Some varieties have food coloring or preservatives, but not typically plastics.  
- **Microplastics concern:** There’s no evidence that Milk-Bones are “full of microplastics.” Like most processed foods (for humans or pets), trace environmental microplastics can be present, but they’re not deliberately added.  
- **Caution:** If your ferret (not a dog) or other non-canine animal gets into them — avoid. They’re made for dogs specifically.

---

### 🥩 Soup Bones
- **Cooked bones = unsafe:** If you mean leftover soup bones from cooking, those are dangerous. Cooking makes bones brittle, so they can splinter and cause choking, internal injury, or blockages.  
- **Raw bones = sometimes safe:** Large raw beef or bison marrow bones (marketed as “soup bones” at butchers) can be safe under supervision. They should be too big to swallow whole. Dogs gnaw the outside but usually can’t crush them.  
- **Risks:** Even raw bones can crack teeth, or in rare cases splinter. Supervision is key, and limit chewing sessions.  
- **Safer alternatives:** Raw beef knuckle bones or manufactured long-lasting chews (nylon, rubber, etc.) are safer if you’re worried about splinters.  

---

✅ **Summary:**  
- Milk-Bones: fine as occasional treats, not full of plastics.  
- Soup bones: **never cooked**, raw only and under supervision, with some risk of tooth damage.
