# Wrestling with God

Who wrestled God?

## Assistant

In the Bible, it was **Jacob** who wrestled with God.  

The story is in **Genesis 32:22–32**. On the night before meeting his estranged brother Esau, Jacob was left alone and wrestled with a mysterious “man” until daybreak. The text later reveals that this was no ordinary man but a divine being—often understood as God Himself, or an angel representing Him.  

- During the struggle, Jacob’s hip was dislocated, but he refused to let go until he received a blessing.  
- The figure blessed him and changed his name from **Jacob** (“supplanter”) to **Israel**, which means *“he struggles with God”* or *“God prevails.”*  
- This moment marks a turning point: Jacob becomes Israel, the father of the twelve tribes.  

So, the one who wrestled with God was **Jacob, renamed Israel**.

## User

Does this parable exist in the Quran?

## Assistant

The **Qur’an** does not contain the story of Jacob wrestling with God.  

- Jacob (*Yaʿqūb* in Arabic) is mentioned many times in the Qur’an, especially as a prophet and patriarch, but his life is portrayed differently than in Genesis.  
- The Qur’an emphasizes Jacob’s devotion to God, his role as a prophet, his counsel to his children, and his continuity with Abraham and Isaac (see *Surah al-Baqarah 2:132–133* and *Surah Yusuf 12*).  
- Nowhere in the Qur’an does Jacob physically wrestle with God or an angel. That story is unique to the **Torah/Bible** tradition.  

However, Islamic tradition (tafsir and hadith) sometimes discusses stories of the prophets outside of the Qur’an, borrowing from Jewish and Christian traditions (known as **Isra’iliyyat**). In some of those later writings, echoes of the wrestling story appear, but they are not Qur’anic and are not central to Islamic teaching.  

👉 So the short answer: **No, the parable of Jacob wrestling with God is not in the Qur’an.** Jacob is a prophet in Islam, but his story is told differently, without that episode.
