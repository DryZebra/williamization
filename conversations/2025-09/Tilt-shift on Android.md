Fun question! “Tilt-shift” is that mini-city look: a thin in-focus strip with heavy blur above/below. Here are fast ways to do it on Android (no fancy gear needed):

# Quickest (free + easy): Snapseed
1) Install **Snapseed** (by Google).
2) Open your photo → **Tools** → **Tilt-Shift** *(or* **Lens Blur** on some versions)*.
3) Choose **Linear**.
4) Drag to position the sharp strip; two outer lines control the feather/transition.
5) Pinch to change strip width; swipe up/down to adjust **Blur Strength**, **Transition**, and **Vignette**.
6) Save/export.

# Higher control (great results): Lightroom Mobile
1) Install **Adobe Lightroom** (free; “Lens Blur” is AI-based and excellent).
2) Open photo → **Lens Blur** → use **Linear** focus or **Depth** → place the focus band.
3) Tweak **Amount**, **Bokeh** shape, and **Focus Range** → **Apply** → **Export**.

# Built-in/stock options (varies by phone)
- **Google Photos**: Edit → **Blur** (or **Portrait Blur**) + **Depth** tools. Not a true tilt-shift, but you can fake it by blurring then erasing blur over a horizontal band with the **Depth**/**Focus** controls (availability differs by device).
- **Samsung Gallery**: Edit → **Blur** / **Portrait** tools; use **Shape**/**Linear** if offered.

# For video (reels/shorts)
- **CapCut** or **VN**: Effects → **Tilt-Shift** (Linear). Resize/position the band; keyframe it if your subject moves.

## Pro tips for the “miniature” look
- Shoot from high up, looking down ~30–60°. Wide-angle shots work best.
- Keep people/cars in frame for scale.
- Use a **thin** in-focus band and **strong** blur (but not so strong it looks fake).
- Boost **contrast** and **vibrance** a touch; lift **highlights** for that toy-set pop.
- Keep horizons straight; place the focus band on your subject line (e.g., street or train).
