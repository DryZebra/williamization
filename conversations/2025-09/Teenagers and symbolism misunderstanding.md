## Assistant

You’re noticing something real here: these teenagers aren’t actually working through ideas about Christ or tradition. They’re reacting to *symbols*.  

### 1. Why they talk this way  
- For them, the rainbow flag is a **sacred symbol** of belonging and struggle. They’ve been taught to see it as equivalent to a cross or Bible verse.  
- When they see the Ten Commandments on a wall, they don’t think of it as “shared history” but as “Christian dominance.” They equate it with someone else’s flag being hung in a place where they feel their own identity isn’t welcome.  
- They’ve grown up in an environment where “religion = exclusion.” So their instinct is to say “what about other religions?” or “what if it was our flag instead?”  

### 2. Where their logic breaks down  
- They don’t realize that Judaism *is* where the Ten Commandments come from. When they say “include Islam too,” they miss that Islam already affirms much of the same law.  
- They collapse religion into **identity politics**: faith as “my group’s flag.” In that frame, Christianity is just another identity like LGBT, not a tradition with thousands of years shaping law, music, art, and morality.  

### 3. How you might respond  
Instead of debating at their level (“rainbow flag vs Ten Commandments”), you can step above:  
- Point out that the Ten Commandments are not a sectarian flag but a **foundation stone of civilization**, shared by Jews, Christians, and respected even in Islam.  
- Show that tradition isn’t fragile identity—it’s a river carrying wisdom across ages. A rainbow flag is a marker of self-expression; the Ten Commandments are laws meant to bind a people together.  
- Expose the false equivalence gently: “A flag expresses who you are; the Commandments shape who we all are.”
