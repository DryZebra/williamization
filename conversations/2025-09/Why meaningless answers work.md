## Assistant

What you’re noticing is a really common dynamic in how people process vague or unanswerable questions. A few things are happening at once:  

---

### 1. **The Brain Hates a Void**
When someone asks a question like *“When will people collectively wake up?”* it doesn’t have a concrete answer. Our minds dislike leaving questions unresolved, so people fill the gap with *something*—anything that feels like closure. Even if the answer is meaningless, it relieves the discomfort of the open question.

---

### 2. **Satisfaction Over Accuracy**
For many, the *feeling* of being answered is more important than the *content* of the answer. If the response matches their vibe—spiritual, conspiratorial, poetic, rebellious—it lands as “truthy.” That’s why “stop being addicted to WiFi signals” feels like an answer: it resonates with a suspicion they already carry, even if it’s logically unrelated.

---

### 3. **The Role of Pattern-Matching**
The brain is wired to connect patterns, not necessarily to test them. So when someone hears “wake up” and “addiction to WiFi,” their mind says, “Yes, that fits my worldview where modern tech numbs people.” That internal confirmation is what makes it “satisfactory.” The brain rewards resonance more than rigor.

---

### 4. **Social & Identity Signaling**
Sometimes the point of both the question and the answer isn’t information but *performance.* The asker shows they’re part of a questioning, outsider crowd. The responder shows they’re attuned to spiritual/anti-tech/alternative vibes. Both leave satisfied because they’ve reinforced belonging, not discovered truth.

---

### 5. **Why It Feels Like an Answer**
- It provides closure (no dangling question).  
- It aligns with pre-existing suspicion or desire.  
- It uses metaphor or authority-sounding language that feels profound.  
- It signals shared identity more than actual knowledge.  

In short: the brain confuses **emotional resonance** with **logical adequacy.**
