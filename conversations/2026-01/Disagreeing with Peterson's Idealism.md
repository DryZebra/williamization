## The core disagreement (one sentence)

> Peterson treats meaning and morality as emerging primarily from **ideas and symbols**, while a materialist sees meaning and morality as emerging from **real conditions, labor, and consequences**—ideas come *after*, not first.

---

## The real example: **Workplace safety vs. “personal responsibility”**

### Peterson’s idealist framing (simplified)
Peterson often argues that:
- Social problems are best solved by **individual moral alignment**
- If people take responsibility, act truthfully, and order themselves, society improves
- Structural critiques risk encouraging resentment or avoidance of responsibility

This *sounds* Christian. It emphasizes virtue. But here’s where it breaks.

---

### The materialist counterexample (very concrete)

Imagine a factory where:
- Guards are removed from machines to increase output  
- Overtime is mandatory  
- Reporting injuries gets you quietly fired  
- Management preaches “personal responsibility” and “professional pride”

Now look at outcomes:
- Injuries rise  
- Stress rises  
- Families suffer  
- Workers know it’s wrong but cannot safely resist  

**Question:**  
Did the suffering come from bad ideas?  
Or from **material conditions imposed by power and profit**?

The answer is obvious to anyone who has worked a job like this.

---

### Why Peterson’s approach fails *here*

If you tell an injured worker:
- “Stand up straight”
- “Take responsibility”
- “Tell the truth”
- “Fix yourself before blaming the system”

You are asking them to **solve a structural problem with a personal virtue**.

That’s idealism.

No amount of moral alignment:
- Reinstalls safety guards  
- Reduces production quotas  
- Protects whistleblowers  
- Pays medical bills  

Those require **material intervention**.

---

## Why this matters *Christianly*

Christianity does **not** say:
> “Blessed are those with correct internal attitudes.”

It says:
- Feed the hungry  
- Heal the sick  
- Free the captive  
- Remove burdens from people  

Jesus does not tell the injured man:
> “You should have walked more carefully.”

He asks:
> “Who placed him on the road to be beaten?”

That’s a **material question**.

---

## The clean takeaway you can say to your friend

> “I don’t reject personal responsibility. I reject the idea that responsibility explains suffering when material structures are doing the harm. Peterson starts with the soul and hopes the world fixes itself. I start with the world, because that’s where people actually get crushed.”

---

## One-line contrast (easy to remember)

- **Peterson:** Fix the person → society improves  
- **Materialism:** Fix the conditions → people can finally be good  

Christianity sides with the second far more often than people admit.
