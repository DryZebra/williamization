## The Core Principle (this keeps you sane)

A **rehoming fee is not payment**.  
It is a **screening tool**.

People who abuse, flip, or neglect animals overwhelmingly avoid even modest friction. Good owners tolerate it easily.

Your goal is:
- deter bad actors
- slow down impulsive people
- give yourself leverage to ask real questions

---

## What to Say (Public Listing Text)

You want to sound **calm, firm, and boring**. Not emotional. Not apologetic.

**Use something very close to this:**

> We are responsibly rehoming three puppies from an unexpected litter.  
>  
> Parents are rescued dogs (no papers). Puppies are healthy, well-socialized, and raised in a home environment.  
>  
> We are looking for committed, long-term homes only.  
>  
> A **rehoming fee of $___ applies** to ensure serious inquiries.  
>  
> We will ask a few questions before meeting.  
>  
> Please message with a short description of your household, experience with dogs, and why you’re interested.

That’s it.  
No justification. No story. No guilt.

---

## What **NOT** to Say (This Attracts the Wrong People)

Do **not** say:
- “Free to good home”
- “We just want them loved”
- “Price negotiable”
- “We don’t want money”
- “We’re flexible”
- “We had an accident / mistake” (this invites manipulation)

You don’t owe anyone your conscience.

---

## Rehoming Fee: Numbers That Actually Work

You do **not** need to match breeder prices.

You need a number that:
- is annoying to bad actors
- irrelevant to good owners

### Recommended range (for your situation):
**$150–$300 per puppy**

Here’s how to choose:
- $150 = minimum friction, still effective
- $200–$250 = sweet spot
- $300 = strong filter in high-demand areas

Given doodle demand, **$200–$250** is very reasonable and will not scare off good homes.

You are not profiteering at these numbers.

---

## The “Reverse Gacha” (Yes — but do it correctly)

Your instinct here is actually solid.

**Correct way to do it:**
- List the fee normally
- Screen seriously
- Meet in person
- Observe behavior
- THEN, if you feel right, you can say:

> We’re comfortable reducing the rehoming fee since we feel good about this placement.

Or even:
> We’d like to waive part/all of the fee and put it toward their first vet visit.

**Why this works:**
- Bad actors never reach this point
- Good people feel respected, not baited
- You retain control the entire time

**Do NOT advertise this possibility. Ever.**

---

## Screening Questions (Ask These, Exactly)

Before meeting, ask **3–5 short questions**. Don’t overdo it.

1. **Have you owned dogs before? If so, what breeds?**  
   (Experience matters more than enthusiasm.)

2. **Who lives in your household (adults, kids, other pets)?**  
   (You’re listening for honesty, not perfection.)

3. **Where will the dog sleep and stay during the day?**  
   (This filters neglect cases immediately.)

4. **Are you renting or owning? If renting, are dogs allowed?**  
   (Avoids future surrender.)

5. **Are you prepared for grooming, training, and vet care long-term?**  
   (Tone matters more than the answer.)

If someone gets defensive here, that’s your answer.

---

## Red Flags (Immediate No)

Say no without debate if you see:
- refusal to answer questions
- rushing (“I can come now with cash”)
- bargaining hard on the fee early
- fixation on resale value, breeding, or looks
- unwillingness to meet at a neutral or home location
- “gift for someone else” without details

You do **not** need to justify a no.

---

## Papers & Breed Questions (How to Answer Cleanly)

That’s it.  
No defensiveness.

If someone presses:
- They’re not your people.

## The Non-Negotiable Rule (for your peace of mind)

**No in-person meetings before 2 weeks.**  
**No handling by outsiders before 3–4 weeks.**

This isn’t about people.  
It’s about **neurology, immunity, and stress** — for mom *and* puppies.

---

## Why 48 Hours Is a Hard No (and you’re right to feel it)

At 0–14 days:
- Puppies are **neurologically immature**
- Immune systems are **entirely maternal**
- Stress on mom can reduce milk supply
- Exposure risk is real even with “clean” people
- Puppies cannot thermoregulate properly

Even well-meaning visitors increase risk.

A responsible breeder or rescue would say the same thing.

---

## Ideal Timeline for Ethical, Calm Meetings

### **0–14 days**
- No visitors
- Only household handling
- Focus: nursing, warmth, weight gain

### **14–21 days**
- Eyes opening
- Early movement
- Still **no public meetings**
- You can begin:
  - photos
  - short videos
  - temperament observations

### **3–4 weeks (BEST first meeting window)**
✔ This is your sweet spot

At this stage:
- Puppies can tolerate brief handling
- Mom is more relaxed
- Temperaments start to show
- Visitors can sit quietly and interact gently
- Disease risk is much lower

### **5–6 weeks**
- Ideal for serious bonding visits
- Puppy personalities are clear
- Still early enough for proper transition

---

## TOMORROW (Day 3–4): First Vet Visit — What This Visit Is *For*

This visit is **NOT** for shots.  
It is a **baseline health check**.

### What the vet should do
- Confirm **overall litter health**
- Check for:
  - cleft palate
  - hernias (especially umbilical)
  - limb or tail abnormalities
  - suckling ability
- Weigh each puppy and record weights
- Check mom:
  - milk production
  - nipples / mastitis risk
  - postpartum recovery
- Give you a **monitoring plan**, not treatments

### What should *not* happen
- ❌ No vaccines
- ❌ No dewormer unless the vet specifically recommends early intervention
- ❌ No flea/tick products
- ❌ No separation from mom

If a vet suggests early vaccines or routine meds at this age, ask *why*.

---

## 0–2 WEEKS (Now → Day 14)

### At home
- Daily weight checks (same time each day)
- Expect:
  - steady gain
  - sleeping almost constantly
- Watch for:
  - constant crying
  - failure to latch
  - cold skin or cool mouth
  - milk leakage from nose (possible cleft)

### Vet involvement
- Only if something looks wrong
- Otherwise: observe and log

---

## 2 WEEKS (Day ~14)

### What changes
- Eyes begin opening
- Ears start responding
