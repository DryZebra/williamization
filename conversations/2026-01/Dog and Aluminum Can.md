## What’s actually risky about an aluminum can 🥫

### 1. **Sharp edges form fast**
Even if he’s *not* chewing hard:
- Dents turn into **thin, razor-sharp creases**
- The **rim and pull-tab area** are especially dangerous  
One wrong bite or slip and you can get:
- Tongue cuts  
- Gum lacerations  
- Lip tears  

These often **bleed a lot** and look scarier than they are—but they *do* get infected easily.

---

### 2. **Metal fatigue → sudden failure**
Aluminum is sneaky:
- It feels sturdy
- Then it **suddenly folds or cracks**
That’s when:
- A previously “safe” dent becomes a sharp flap
- Dogs don’t react fast enough to avoid it

---

### 3. **Ingestion risk (lower, but real)**
You said he’s not chewing aggressively, which is good—but:
- A **small torn shard** can break off
- Swallowed metal = **GI irritation or obstruction**
Older dogs are *less* likely to pass sharp foreign objects cleanly.

---

### 4. **Noise obsession ≠ enrichment**
Dogs love cans because:
- They’re loud
- They move unpredictably
- They feel “alive”

That doesn’t mean it’s healthy stimulation. It’s closer to:
- A **prey-drive loop**
- Or a self-reinforcing fixation

Neither is ideal long-term.
