## Assistant

That sounds like what’s called a **mucosal ulcer with a secondary abscess or mucocele** — basically, you bit your lip so hard that you damaged one of the small salivary glands or ducts inside it. When that happens, saliva (and sometimes a bit of inflammation or infection) gets trapped under the skin, forming a little pocket that swells up and leaks periodically.

Here’s what’s likely happening and what to expect:

---

### 🧠 What’s Going On
- **Initial trauma:** The bite crushed tissue and probably the duct of a minor salivary gland.  
- **Swelling and “puss”:** What you’re seeing and tasting is usually *saliva mixed with inflammatory fluid*, not true pus (so it doesn’t have the strong foul taste or smell).  
- **Recurrent cycle:** Because the damaged duct keeps trying to heal but gets blocked, it fills up again, then leaks — like a blister that keeps refilling.  

---

### ⏱️ How Long It Lasts
- **Minor cases:** 1–2 weeks to resolve on their own if you stop re-biting or irritating it.  
- **Recurrent or large mucoceles:** They can persist for months or even become chronic unless the damaged gland is removed or the duct heals cleanly.  

---

### ⚕️ What You Can Do
- **Avoid irritation:** Don’t bite, poke, or suck on it, even if it’s tempting.  
- **Warm salt-water rinses:** 3–4 times per day (½ tsp salt in a cup of warm water) helps keep bacteria down and reduces inflammation.  
- **Keep your mouth clean:** Brush gently and stay hydrated.  
- **No antiseptic mouthwash with alcohol** — it burns and delays healing.  

---

### 🚩 When to See a Dentist or Doctor
If you notice any of the following, it’s worth getting checked (usually by a dentist or oral surgeon):
- It hasn’t gone away after **2–3 weeks**
- It’s **painful, hard, or keeps coming back**
- The fluid smells bad or is thick yellow/green
- There’s **visible white tissue, ulcer, or lump** that doesn’t change

They can evaluate whether it’s a **mucocele**, **aphthous ulcer**, or small **abscess**, and if needed, a dentist can **drain it or remove the damaged gland** to stop it from recurring.
