## 2) Why +4 Strength is such a big deal (the calculation)

OSRS max hit uses a formula where your **Strength bonus** multiplies with your **effective Strength level**. One common form shown on the wiki is:

**Base damage = 1.3 + (Effective_Str/10) + (StrBonus/80) + (Effective_Str × StrBonus / 640)**, then floored for max hit. citeturn2search18

### What +4 Strength bonus does, in plain math
The *increase* to base damage from +4 Str bonus is approximately:

- **(+4 / 80) + (Effective_Str × +4 / 640)**
- = **0.05 + (Effective_Str / 160)**

So if your effective Strength is, say:
- **60** → +0.05 + 0.375 = **+0.425 base damage**
- **90** → +0.05 + 0.5625 = **+0.6125 base damage**
- **100** → +0.05 + 0.625 = **+0.675 base damage**

Because max hit is *floored*, that often turns into **+1 max hit** at common breakpoints.

### Concrete example (breakpoints are why your “it doesn’t add up” feeling happens)
Holding everything constant except torso’s **+4 Strength**:

- At Effective_Str **60**, going from StrBonus **50 → 54** changes max hit **12 → 13** (that’s +1).  
- At Effective_Str **70**, it can change **14 → 14** (no change).  
- At Effective_Str **90**, it can change **17 → 18** (+1).  

That “sometimes it does nothing, sometimes it’s +1” is exactly the breakpoint behavior. citeturn2search18

---

## 3) Turning +1 max hit into DPS (why people still chase torso)

If torso gives you **+1 max hit**, your **average hit** increases by about **+0.5** (because average hit is roughly max/2 when accuracy is high).

With a **4-tick weapon** (scimitar), you swing **150 / 4 = 37.5 hits per minute**.

So +0.5 average damage per hit is about:
- **0.5 × 37.5 ≈ 18.75 extra damage per minute**

That’s not theory-crafting fluff — that’s a real, felt difference over long grinds.

And if you’re fighting low-defence stuff (crabs, many slayer mobs), your hit chance is already high, so that extra damage actually lands.

## The math that explains why 30–61 Range/early melee feels “broken”
If two weapons have similar accuracy and max hit, **the faster one wins** because it simply rolls damage more often.

A clean way to compare is:

**DPS ∝ (Hit chance) × (Average hit) ÷ (Seconds per attack)**

Where:
- 4-tick = 2.4s per attack  
- 5-tick = 3.0s per attack citeturn5search22

That means a 4-tick weapon gets:

**3.0 / 2.4 = 1.25×** as many swings as a 5-tick weapon  
→ **~25% more swings** over time.

So a slower weapon has to compensate with **a lot** more damage/accuracy to catch up.

### “How much more max hit would a 5-tick need?”
If accuracy were identical, a 5-tick weapon needs about:

**+25% average hit** to match a 4-tick weapon.

Example: if your 4-tick setup averages ~10 damage per swing, the 5-tick setup needs ~12.5 average per swing just to tie.

That’s why longswords feel bad for training unless they *also* solve an accuracy problem (like hitting a stab-weak monster with a much better stab bonus).
