# Democracy Is Power Overview

## User

Are you familiar with the book Democracy Is Power?

## Assistant

Yes, *Democracy Is Power: Rebuilding Unions from the Bottom Up* is a 1999 book by Mike Parker and Martha Gruelle. It’s a cornerstone of rank-and-file unionism and a practical guide for building democratic, member-led unions. The authors argue that real power for workers comes not from better contracts alone, but from transforming unions into participatory, democratic institutions where members actively shape direction and strategy.

Key themes include:

- **Bottom-up structure** over top-down leadership.
- **Internal democracy** as the core of lasting strength.
- **Education and transparency** to build class consciousness.
- **Member mobilization** beyond just voting or dues-paying.
- A heavy critique of business unionism and passive service models.

It’s not utopian—it acknowledges the risks, contradictions, and slow grind of internal reform—but it’s deeply hopeful in a material way: it believes workers can and must control their own organizations.

## Assistant

That’s great—you’re going to get a lot out of it, especially with your current union fight. *Democracy Is Power* isn’t just theory, it’s a toolkit written by people who’ve been in the trenches. Here’s what to be prepared for as you read:

**1. It will challenge "common sense" unionism.**  
If you're used to thinking of unions as service organizations—where leaders handle grievances, lawyers handle bargaining, and members just show up when needed—it'll flip that on its head. Parker and Gruelle argue that this model keeps workers passive, and passivity kills power.

**2. It emphasizes structure.**  
This isn't feel-good democracy. It's about building *systems* that empower members—transparent elections, steward networks, grievance procedures that inform the whole membership, not just solve problems behind closed doors.

**3. It will affirm instincts you probably already have.**  
If you’ve ever thought “Why don’t they let the apprentices talk?” or “Why are these meetings just rubber stamps?”—you’ll see those frustrations laid out, explained, and shown to be structural, not personal.

**4. It avoids saviorism.**  
You’ll never see the authors say “we just need the right leader.” They’re hammering home that the base—*the members themselves*—must be the center of power. Even when leadership is “on your side,” that’s not enough.

**5. It’s written in plain English.**  
This isn’t an academic tome. It’s direct, clear, and meant to be handed to any worker, not just intellectuals. You could read a chapter, bring it to a union meeting, and start applying it immediately.

**Final tip: read it like a builder.**  
As you go, ask yourself: *What from this chapter could I apply to my local?* Whether it's meeting structures, bulletin boards, grievance handling, or member outreach—everything in that book is actionable.
