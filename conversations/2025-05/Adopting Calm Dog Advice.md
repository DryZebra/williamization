# Adopting Calm Dog Advice

## User

I want to adopt another dog. I want one with a calm demeanor, but that won't play into my girlfriend's dogs anxiety or attachment. 

## Assistant



## Assistant

That’s a great goal, and with the right dog, you can bring a lot of peace and stability to your home. Here’s how to approach it:

### 1. **Look for a Dog with a Stable, Confident Temperament**
You're looking for a “neutral” dog—calm, confident, not overly playful, and not overly submissive or dominant. Shelter workers sometimes call these dogs “bombproof” or “easygoing.” Key traits:
- Low reactivity to barking or sudden movement
- Doesn’t fixate on other dogs
- Calm but not fearful
- Can be left alone without panicking

### 2. **Avoid Puppies and High-Energy Breeds**
Puppies often overwhelm anxious dogs. Also avoid breeds like Border Collies, Malinois, or Jack Russells unless you’re committed to major energy management. Instead, consider:
- **Greyhounds** – couch potatoes, very little interest in other dogs
- **Basset Hounds** – slow-moving, relaxed
- **Older Golden Retrievers/Labs** – friendly but less intense with age
- **Shih Tzus or Lhasa Apsos** – aloof but affectionate
- **Senior dogs** – calmer and more predictable behavior

### 3. **Assess Interaction Potential**
When visiting a shelter or rescue:
- Introduce the new dog to your girlfriend’s dog *on neutral ground*
- Look for signs of respectful disinterest rather than excitement or fear
- Avoid dogs that stare, lunge, or immediately try to play too rough

### 4. **Get Input from a Shelter Behaviorist**
Most shelters and rescues will happily recommend a dog based on your needs if you’re clear about your girlfriend’s dog’s anxiety. Ask: “Do you have a dog that doesn’t escalate when other dogs are nervous or reactive?”

## Assistant

Yes—**retired racing Greyhounds** in particular are famous for being couch potatoes. Despite their speed, they are surprisingly low-energy in daily life. Here's the breakdown:

### Why Greyhounds Are So Calm:
- **Sprinters, not endurance runners** – They burn energy in short bursts and then rest.
- **Adapt well to indoors** – They love soft couches, quiet routines, and regular naps.
- **Generally indifferent to other dogs** – They often ignore chaos rather than escalate it.
- **Low grooming needs** – Their short coat makes them easy to maintain.

### Things to Consider:
- **They can be aloof** – Some are not overly cuddly, more reserved and gentle.
- **Not all are cat-safe** – Their prey drive varies; some may chase small animals.
- **Cold-sensitive** – Thin fur and body fat means they need sweaters in winter.
