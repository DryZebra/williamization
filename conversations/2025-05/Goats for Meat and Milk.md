# Goats for Meat and Milk

## User

We're getting goats. Can material goats also be used for meat and milk?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Yes, **most goats are dual-purpose**, meaning *material goats* (likely referring to goats raised primarily for brush clearing, land management, or labor) **can also be used for meat and milk**, but with tradeoffs:

### 1. **Meat**
- Most goats can be eaten, but **meat breeds** like Boer or Kiko grow faster and have more muscle.
- Material-use goats (like brush goats or Spanish crosses) tend to be **leaner and hardier**, often producing **tougher meat** if butchered late but **flavorful** if managed well.

### 2. **Milk**
- Goats not bred for dairy (like Nubians or Saanens) **produce less milk** and lower butterfat.
- However, **any lactating female goat** will produce milk after kidding.
- It may be **less volume and lower cream content**, but still usable for **home use, cheese, or yogurt**.

### 3. **Recommendation**
If you're doing land management or "material" use, **consider Spanish/Nubian crosses**—they're hardy, good foragers, and can give passable milk and decent meat.

For a hobby farm in central NC focused on **meat, milk, and land use**, the **minimum number of goats** you should start with is:

---

### **Minimum Sustainable Goat Herd**
| Role        | Count | Purpose                                   |
|-------------|-------|-------------------------------------------|
| **Does**    | 2     | Minimum for milk and breeding potential   |
| **Buck**    | 1     | Needed for breeding (or rent one seasonally) |
| **Wether(s)** | 1–2 | Cheap, low-maintenance land clearers & meat |

Total: **4–5 goats**

---

### **Why This Number?**
- **Goats are social animals**—never keep just one. They get stressed and unhealthy without companions.
- Two **does** let you alternate pregnancies and test milk production.
- One **buck** is ideal if you're committed, but you can also *rent or borrow a stud* to save on feed and fencing.
- A **wether** (castrated male) is cheap, friendly, and perfect for brush control or meat without breeding stress.

---

### Optional Minimal Setup (No Buck)
- **3 Does** (or 2 does + 1 wether)
- **Use a local breeder for stud service during breeding season**

This lets you focus on milk, babies, and fun—without the hassle of managing a buck year-round.
