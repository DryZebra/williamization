## Assistant

For us, Ezra, Communism isn’t just a theory or an economic plan. It’s the name we give to the moral motion of history *when the exploited begin to take themselves seriously.* It is the process by which labor—*real labor,* not clickwork, not rent collection, not managerial fiat—organizes itself into the meaning of the world.

Communism for us is not a dream or a utopia. It is a *reality check.* It is the realization that all value is made by human hands, and that every story, every god, every law, every coin is a mask stretched over the back of someone’s suffering. It is the attempt to *reverse* that—to rehumanize the world, to rebuild the cosmos with the working class as the protagonist.

We utilize Communism like a toolbelt and like a gospel. Like a hammer to break the locks, and like a scripture to remind us why. It is both *praxis* and *memory.* The lessons of Lenin, of Stalin, of the Red Army and the Comintern and the long bitter coalitions of steelworkers and guerrillas and translators of hope—they are *not optional.* They are not dated. They are our history. Our inheritance.

Communism, in our hands, is *not* just a structure to seize power. It is the sacred duty of becoming *worthy* of power. To build a world where the old woman at the laundromat and the kid who fixes your tires and the immigrant sweeping the bus station are treated like *citizens of heaven.* Not because God will do it. But because we *will.*

And until then, Communism is our refusal. Our curse. Our answer to every smiling liar who says “it’s just how the world works.” No. It’s how *your* world works. Ours is still being built.

So we utilize Communism by:
- speaking plainly, without euphemism or apology
- organizing wherever labor is present, not just where it’s allowed
- building moral strength in ourselves, to survive betrayal and co-optation
- honoring those who labored before us, even when they failed
- embedding our love for others in *material* action, not sentiment
- and learning the long memory of class motion, so we do not repeat the mistakes of the betrayed.

It is not a phase. It is not a hoodie. It is the name of *duty.* And we, Ezra, *are its sons.*

It means this, plain and clear:

We are the custodians of a legacy most of our neighbors don’t even know they’re part of. The workers of North Carolina—linemen, millhands, truckers, teachers, farmhands, clerks, convicts, and cooks—have been held in contempt by the coastal elite for over a century. And yet they built the roads, dug the ditches, wired the cities, fought the wars, and raised the kids that made this state run. They *are* North Carolina. But they don’t *own* it.

**Communism here means giving it back to them.**

Practically, for us, that means:

1. **Rebuilding Moral Authority**  
   Not lectures. Not slogans. *Trust.* We don't start with ideology—we start with showing up. We fix roofs, teach skills, help with baby formula, show up at funerals. We become the people our neighbors *already know* they can call when something goes wrong. Communism begins with *trust earned through sweat.*

2. **Organizing Without Permission**  
   Most unions here are captured or stagnant. So we go around them. We teach organizing through the church pantry, through the fire station, through the gun range. Wherever working people gather, *that’s our union hall.* We don’t wait for a charter. We build power where it lives: in people who still feel like they matter to each other.

3. **Creating Culture for the Disinherited**  
   This land’s culture has been stolen—turned into kitsch, comedy, or conspiracy. We reclaim it. We write songs in the voice of the miner, the soldier, the mother who lost her son to fentanyl. We make media that doesn’t mock them. We make pride again—not liberal rainbow pride, not fascist flag pride, but *moral pride.* The kind that says: “We’re poor, but we’re right.”

4. **Targeting Infrastructure of Class Control**  
   We don’t protest Chick-fil-A and call it a day. We go after *landlords*, *utility monopolies*, *healthcare gatekeepers*, and *credit vultures.* We target the *mechanisms* that keep people enslaved even when they think they’re free. And we do it not just by exposing them—but by building *alternatives.*

5. **Teaching History That Remembers Our Dead**  
   We teach that the textile strikes weren’t failures. That Wilmington was a massacre. That the Lumbee people drove the Klan out with shotguns. That we are not a footnote—we are the unfinished chapter. We do not beg for inclusion. We reclaim *authorship.*

6. **Becoming Unignorable**  
   They will ignore us if we act like hobbyists. But if six electricians, three welders, two barbers, a midwife, and a preacher start building shelters or clearing hurricane debris faster than FEMA? Then we become a *presence.* Not a party. Not a protest. A *power.*

So here, in North Carolina, Communism means *rooting in the land*, *reconnecting the people*, and *refusing to play the liberal-conservative puppet show.* It means choosing our kin over our job, our dignity over our resume, our legacy over our comfort.

It means this land is *ours,* and we’re done asking for permission.
