### ✴ *Songs That Carry the Weight of the Real* ✴

1. **“Jesus Remember Me” – Taizé**
   - Already your anchor. Repeat it. Alone. In the woods. Under stars. It’s not music—it’s a ritual.

2. **“Anima Christi” – Traditional Latin, chanted**
   - This one doesn’t perform. It prays. The Latin isn’t a barrier—it’s *pressure*. Let it bruise the air.

3. **“Come Thou Fount of Every Blessing” – Sung by a trembling voice**
   - Choose a sparse recording. Look for one where the singer sounds *almost afraid*. That’s the one.

4. **“Dona Nobis Pacem” – from the Mass**
   - Sung in rounds. The phrase means “grant us peace,” but that’s not what it *does*. It begs for peace, because there isn’t any.

5. **“The Parting Glass” – Traditional Irish**
   - Not mournful. Resigned. It knows it will be forgotten, and still says goodbye with grace.

6. **“Wayfaring Stranger” – preferably an Appalachian folk version**
   - Don’t pick a pretty version. Pick one recorded on a porch with a cracked voice and a tired banjo.

7. **“Calling My Children Home” – The Cox Family / Emmylou Harris version**
   - Gospel harmonies. No flair. Just longing. It doesn’t need you to believe in God—it needs you to *remember* Him.

8. **“The Sound of Rain” – Field recording**
   - Not a song. Just the earth trying to speak.

9. **“Psalm 51 (Miserere Mei)” – Allegri**
   - Sung in a cathedral. Let it echo until you feel ashamed of every beautiful thing you’ve ever tried to own.

10. **“I See Fire” – Ed Sheeran (Live acoustic only)**
   - Hear beyond the pop context. Listen to it as a love song to doomed comrades. Dialectical ruin.
