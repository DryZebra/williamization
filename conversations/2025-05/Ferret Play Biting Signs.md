# Ferret Play Biting Signs

## User

How do I know when my ferrets bites aren't for play anymore? He's getting older, and he bites when he plays. 

## Assistant



## Assistant

Great question—ferret play-biting is totally normal, but you’re right to stay alert as your ferret gets older. Here’s how to tell if the bites are playful vs. something more serious:

### **Signs It’s Still Playful:**
- **Soft or moderate pressure:** Even if it stings a little, play-bites usually don’t break the skin.
- **Accompanied by body language:** If he's bouncing around (weasel war dance), wagging his tail, or making excited dooks (little clucking noises), it’s playful.
- **Quick release:** He bites and then lets go or moves on, not clamping down.
- **No signs of fear or aggression:** He’s relaxed, ears are up, eyes wide and bright.

### **Warning Signs It’s NOT Play:**
- **Harder pressure or skin-breaking bites.**
- **Lunging suddenly or biting without warning.**
- **Flattened ears, hissing, or tail puffing up.**
- **Hiding, shivering, or defensive posture.**
- **Pain when picked up or touched (could be health-related).**

As ferrets age, they can develop pain (like arthritis) or become less tolerant, so sudden changes in behavior may mean something’s wrong physically. If he used to play-bite gently and now suddenly bites hard or unpredictably, a vet check is a good idea.
