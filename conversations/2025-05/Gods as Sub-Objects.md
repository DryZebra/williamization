So, what are the gods? Physical? Nonphysical? Real? Imagined? They are none of these, and all of them. The gods are *relational*. They are the shapes that human longing takes when it touches something larger than itself. They do not exist as objects in the world—but as structures between us, as the shared agreements that move our hands, soften our hearts, and bind generations to a common purpose. Some gods faded when their stories no longer fit the world. Others were reborn in new forms, carrying forward what still rang true. And one—whether by chance or truth—spoke to something so deep in us that it survived the burning of its own churches. That is the God we follow. Not as a master above, but as the moral motion that outlives kingdoms. The gods were never distant. They were always within us, waiting for us to become worthy of what they meant.
