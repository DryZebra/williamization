# What is Nestorianism

## Assistant

A **Nestorian** is someone who follows the teachings associated with **Nestorius**, a 5th-century Christian bishop who was declared a heretic by the Council of Ephesus in 431 AD.

### The core idea of Nestorianism:
Nestorius taught that **Jesus had two distinct natures**—one divine and one human—and that these were not fully united in one person. He rejected the popular term for Mary at the time, **Theotokos** (“God-bearer” or “Mother of God”), and preferred **Christotokos** (“Christ-bearer”), arguing that Mary gave birth to the *human* Jesus, not to God.

This position was seen as dividing Christ into *two persons*—a human and a divine—rather than affirming that he was one person with two natures, fully united.

### What happened to Nestorians?
- The mainstream Church (especially in the Roman Empire) condemned Nestorius’s view as heresy and affirmed the **hypostatic union** (Christ as one person with both divine and human natures fully united).
- Nestorius's followers, however, continued as a separate church known as the **Church of the East**, often called “Nestorian” by outsiders (though modern scholars debate whether they truly followed Nestorius’s exact theology).
- This Church spread eastward—into **Persia, India, Central Asia, and China**—and became one of the most widespread branches of Christianity in the Middle Ages.

So, a Nestorian is either:
1. Historically, a follower of Nestorius's theology (emphasizing the distinction between Jesus's divine and human natures),
2. Or more broadly, a member of the Church of the East, even if their theology evolved over time.
