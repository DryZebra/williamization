## Assistant

This kombucha appears to have a layer of sediment and floating particles, which can be normal—especially if it’s homemade or unfiltered. However, there are a few things to look out for that could indicate spoilage:

**Normal signs (not bad):**
- **Brown stringy bits**: These are often yeast strands, a natural byproduct of fermentation.
- **Sediment on the bottom**: Normal for unfiltered or long-fermenting kombucha.
- **Floating culture or SCOBY**: A sign of active fermentation.

**Warning signs (possibly bad):**
- **Mold**: If you see fuzzy growth (white, green, black, or blue) floating on the surface (not just in the liquid), that's mold—toss it.
- **Strong rotten or cheesy smell**: Kombucha should smell vinegary or slightly sweet. A rancid or “off” smell is a bad sign.
- **Excessive cloudiness or strange texture** not consistent with yeast strands.
- **Slimy strings**: Some sliminess is fine, but if it looks like mucus, it may be contaminated.

From your photo, this looks like normal yeast sediment and SCOBY formation—not mold. But to be sure, **smell it**. If it smells cleanly vinegary or slightly fruity, it’s probably fine. If it smells off or cheesy, best to discard.
