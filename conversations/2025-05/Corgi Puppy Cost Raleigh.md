- **Prone to weight gain** – If you’re not walking them or playing daily, they can get chubby and develop joint issues.

**Bottom line:** If you want a clownish companion and can commit to at least moderate engagement (play, basic obedience, mental toys), Corgis are great. But they *will* misbehave if understimulated.

---

### **Retired Greyhounds**
**Pros:**
- **Surprisingly lazy** – They're called “45-mph couch potatoes” for a reason.
- **Low maintenance indoors** – Perfect for apartment living or relaxed households.
- **Quiet and gentle** – Usually calm, sweet, and non-destructive.

- **Can be sensitive** – Emotionally and physically. Some don’t do well with stairs, small pets, or hard floors.

- **Can have quirks** – Coming from racetracks, they might not understand toys, stairs, or windows at first.

**Bottom line:** If you want a calm, low-energy dog who’s happy just to chill with you and doesn’t need a ton of stimulation, **retired Greyhounds are excellent**.

$400 for *purebred Corgi puppies* is **unusually cheap**—and while it’s not automatically a scam, it *does* raise a few flags worth considering:

---

### **Why So Cheap? Possible Reasons**

**1. Unregistered breeder / no papers:**  
If they don’t come with AKC or UKC registration, they’re not “officially” purebred—just purebred by claim. That alone can cut the price in half.

**2. Backyard breeder or accidental litter:**  
People who breed without long-term plans, health testing, or standards often sell at lower prices just to offload the puppies.

**3. Urgency to move:**  
They state they’re “moving” and “need to find homes quickly.” That urgency often lowers prices just to avoid taking the pups to a shelter.

**4. No vet checks or vaccines included:**  
They don’t mention vaccinations, deworming, or vet visits. Those costs can add $100–$300+ per pup. If you're responsible for all that, it’s part of why the price is low.

**5. Craigslist risk zone:**  
Sadly, Craigslist is full of puppy mills, flip sellers, and scams. While the photos here look real and happy, it’s still buyer-beware unless you visit in person.

---

### **Should You Be Worried?**

Not necessarily—but you should **do your due diligence**:

- **Ask for vet records** (shots, worming, etc.)
- **Meet the puppies and parents if possible**
- Check for signs of illness (runny nose, lethargy, worms in stool)
- See where they’re being kept (clean? socialized?)
- Ask what food they’ve been eating (sudden changes can mess them up)

---

If they check out and you're okay with taking on the risk (and potential extra vet costs), **$400 might be a lucky deal**. But go into it knowing that you're not buying from a health-tested, breed-standard breeder—you're rescuing someone's last-minute situation.
