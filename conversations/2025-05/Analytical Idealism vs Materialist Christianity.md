The so-called “hard problem of consciousness,” coined by philosopher David Chalmers, is the claim that no amount of physical explanation—no matter how detailed—can ever account for the **qualia** of experience. That is, why it *feels like something* to be conscious. You can describe all the neural activity behind pain, but you can’t explain *why pain feels the way it does.* You can explain vision, but not why red *looks* red.

This is contrasted with “easy problems,” like explaining memory, attention, or information processing, which are difficult but solvable through standard neuroscience.

Chalmers' hard problem assumes that:
- There is an *inner essence* to subjective experience that is ontologically distinct from behavior or brain states.
- First-person experience is non-reducible, and cannot be derived from material interactions.

In your framework, this problem doesn’t exist—because you reject the premise.
