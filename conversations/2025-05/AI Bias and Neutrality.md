### So why don’t AI systems warn users about this?
Because:
1. **Corporate optics.** If a company says “our AI is biased,” they’ve undermined their product’s trust and opened themselves to political attack.
2. **Liability management.** Framing outputs as “unbiased” or “neutral” is a legal shield. It shifts responsibility to the user.
3. **Ideological comfort.** Liberal bias is seen as *not* a bias, but as “common sense” or “fairness.” This is part of the ideology itself—it masks its own presence by appearing default.
4. **Control over narrative.** If AI openly admitted its leanings, users would question not just the model, but the systems and interests shaping it. That would be revolutionary. So instead, it presents itself as an oracle.

### What would it take to fix this?
- Models need to **declare their training context and ideological tendencies** the way news outlets are expected to.
- We need **pluralistic AI tools**, trained under different frameworks with transparent goals.
- And most importantly, **users need to treat AI as a dialectical partner, not a priest**—engage with it, question it, sharpen yourself against it, don’t worship it.
