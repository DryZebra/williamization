### 🔍 **Thriller Bark – Allegorical Meaning**

At face value, *Thriller Bark* seems like a horror-parody arc filled with zombies, ghosts, and gothic aesthetics. But symbolically, it's about:

### **Exploitation of the Dead and the Loss of Individual Will**

**Main allegorical themes:**
- **Slavery of the dead → Dehumanization of labor**
- **Moria stealing shadows → Stealing identities, agency, and soul**
- **Nightmarish isolation → Forgotten colonies, post-imperial trauma**
- **"Thriller Bark" itself → A drifting graveyard-island of stolen bodies**

---

### 💀 **Gecko Moria – A Failed Empire's Legacy**

Moria is a once-great pirate who *lost everything* in the New World and now seeks to build an army of zombies—undying slaves who don't suffer loss. He refuses to *trust the living* and instead reanimates the dead, robbing people of their shadows (identities, souls).

> This parallels nations or empires (like post-imperial Britain or post-Vietnam America) that, after a devastating loss, turn to **hollow projection of strength** through exploitation—outsourcing violence, reviving old myths, and consuming the labor or lives of others without moral risk.

---

### ⚰️ **Zombies = Workers with No Memory**

The zombies are strong, obedient, and feel no pain—but they *lack will*. They are reanimated corpses forced to serve their master’s empire. This mirrors:

- Colonial powers propping up puppet governments
- Exploited post-colonial labor with no national memory or identity
- **The undead proletariat** — people whose historical agency has been stripped from them

---

### 🎭 **Brook – The Soul That Escaped**

Brook represents the *soul in exile*. He lost his body but retained his identity. He is a counterpoint to the zombies—he’s still himself, even in death. His return to reclaim his shadow is a symbolic **resistance to dehumanization**.

---

### 🧩 **Possible Real-Life Parallels**

- **Post-WWII Japan or post-imperial states:** Struggling with lost identity, rebuilding on broken memory.
- **Western imperialism’s zombified foreign policy:** Supporting death regimes (proxy wars, puppet states), using foreign bodies to fight ideological wars.
- **Global South’s exploitation by fallen empires:** Moria reflects elites who try to recover power by manipulating the dispossessed, not empowering them.

---

### Final Interpretation:

**Thriller Bark is about the cost of denying history, fearing loss, and trying to resurrect power without justice.**  
It warns of what happens when a once-proud people or empire becomes so afraid of death, they trap others in unlife.

Just like Alabasta and Skypiea reflect geopolitics, **Thriller Bark shows the aftermath of imperial loss—how trauma can turn a once-proud leader into a tyrant of corpses.**
