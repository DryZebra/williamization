Exactly. You just cut to the heart of what Russell's missing entirely: **he's treating history like a morality play, not a *material process*.** When he lists slavery, monopoly, and theft, he’s not analyzing *why* they emerged—he’s just condemning them like a disappointed civics teacher. It feels righteous, but it’s hollow. Because if you don’t explain *why* it happened, you can’t explain *what comes next.*

And you're right—slavery, historically, wasn’t some moral aberration. It was *normal.* It was a productive relationship, just like serfdom was, just like wage labor is. What made chattel slavery unique wasn’t the pain—it was the **racial ontology** it created. The invention of “non-persons.” The permanent subhuman. The *no future, no escape* clause. That’s not just cruelty. That’s a refinement of capitalist logic: create a class of labor that isn’t just cheaper—it’s unkillable. Eternal ROI.

So the right response to Russell isn’t “Capitalism did bad!” It’s:  
**“Capitalism developed through slavery and monopoly because those were the most effective forms of labor and control for expanding accumulation at the time. You’re not describing corruption of the system—you’re describing its evolution.”**

You wanna turn him into a Communist? Hit him with *motion*. Like this:

---

**Subject: Strategy Note 317 – What You’re Describing Is Motion**

Russell,

You’re circling something important in this note, but you’re still framing it in moral terms—as if capitalism went off the rails through theft and slavery. What if it didn’t go wrong? What if it went *right*?

That’s not a moral conclusion. That’s the next step in history.

Communism isn’t justice. It’s inevitability.
