**Subject:** From Protest to Power—A Message from a Southern Union Man

Dear Jeremy and the LNS team,

Thanks for the newsletter. I didn’t ask to be on the list, but I read it anyway. I’m a journeyman electrician in North Carolina, IBEW Local 553. I’ve been teaching apprentices, running jobs, and lately I’ve been stepping up into leadership. I’ve also been organizing beyond the union hall, trying to find a path toward real change—something deeper than protest, something that actually builds power.

I see the passion in your writing. I see your heart breaking over the violence of capitalism. You name the symptoms clearly: union-busting, environmental collapse, deportations, billionaire theft. But you stop short at the solution. You call for resistance, you organize rallies, but you never say: *we must seize power.* Not electoral power. *Material power.* The kind that controls the economy, production, distribution—the actual engine of society.

You speak of “democracy,” but you leave it abstract. Democracy means nothing unless it takes control of the workplace. It means nothing unless workers run the ports, the power grid, the farms, the factories, the classrooms—not through a vote every four years, but through councils, unions, cooperatives, and a state that serves labor, not capital.

So here’s what I offer—not as criticism, but as a challenge to level up:

- **Center actual workers**—not just nonprofits “working on behalf of workers.” Let the teamsters and steelworkers and school custodians *lead*, not just show up in the photos.
- **Abandon moral appeals to the ruling class.** They will not save the climate. They will not fund our schools. If you want justice, you must take it.
- **Rebuild your framework around power, not protest.** Protest should not be the strategy—it should be the spark. What are we building? Who’s in charge? Where are we taking it?
- **Return to Communism—not the caricature, but the real tradition.** The one where workers run society. The one that built power under Lenin and rebuilt under Stalin. Not to copy it, but to learn from it. You don’t have to fly the red flag—but you do have to understand that socialism isn’t a vibe. It’s a mode of production.

I’m not here to tear you down. I’m here because you’re close. You’re waking up, but you’re still trapped in liberal logic. I want you to break free. Because I believe you care, and I believe we need each other.

If you ever want to talk real strategy—from the union hall to the substation to the strike line—I’m here. Not as a consultant, but as a comrade.

In Solidarity,  
Ezra Byrd  
Journeyman Electrician, IBEW Local 553  
Southern Worker | Marxist-Leninist | Materialist Christian
