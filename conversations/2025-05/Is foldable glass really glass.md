# Is foldable glass really glass

## Assistant

Great question — **foldable glass**, like what's used on the Motorola Razr and other foldable phones (like Samsung’s Galaxy Z Flip), **is technically glass**, but with a twist:

### Here's what it really is:
Foldable glass is **ultra-thin glass (UTG)**, usually **less than 100 microns thick** — about the thickness of a human hair. Because it's so thin, it becomes **flexible to a degree**, but:

- It still **is glass**, usually made with **aluminosilicate** or similar compounds.
- It’s manufactured using special processes like **ion exchange** to increase durability.
- It’s often **coated with plastic polymers** or has a **plastic layer on top** to improve flexibility and prevent shattering.

### So is it *really* glass?
- **Yes**, the core material is glass.
- **But**, it behaves differently than traditional glass due to its ultra-thin structure and protective layers.
- It’s **not as hard or scratch-resistant** as Gorilla Glass — the plastic layer makes it more vulnerable.

In the case of the **Motorola Razr**, earlier versions used plastic screens only, but later models (like the **Razr+**) use **ultra-thin glass with a protective layer**, similar to Samsung's approach.

**In short:** Yes, it's glass — just *barely*. It's engineered to fold, but it's a hybrid of materials to make that possible.
