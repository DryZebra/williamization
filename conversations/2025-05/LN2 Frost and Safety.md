## Assistant

Yes, what you're seeing is normal for liquid nitrogen (LN₂) cylinders in use. Here's a breakdown:

### Frost and Ice Buildup
The frost and heavy condensation around the valves and top of the dewar (cylinder) happen because:
- **Liquid nitrogen boils at -196°C (-321°F)**, and as it vents or is dispensed, it rapidly cools the surrounding metal.
- Moisture in the air **condenses and freezes** on the cold surfaces, creating the thick frost or snow-like buildup you're seeing.

### Vents and Pressure Builders
The components labeled "VENT" and "PRESSURE BUILDER" are expected on such dewars:
- **Pressure builder**: Helps maintain the internal pressure to allow dispensing.
- **Vent**: Allows safe release of excess nitrogen gas.

### Safety Notes
- Ensure **adequate ventilation** in the area—liquid nitrogen displaces oxygen and can cause asphyxiation.
- Keep **PPE** like cryo gloves and face protection available.
- Monitor pressure—don't let frost cover the pressure gauge completely.
- Keep clear of frost-covered valves—they can **stick or fail** if not handled properly.

In summary: Yes, it's normal—but always respect it. LN₂ is extremely cold and can cause injury or dangerous oxygen displacement if mishandled.
