## Assistant

The machine shown in your photos is a **Genie GR-20** vertical mast lift. The loud beeping sound you're referring to is almost certainly the **motion alarm** or **travel alarm**, which is a standard safety feature on Genie lifts.

### Can it be disabled?
**Officially**, **no**—at least not permanently—**due to ANSI and OSHA safety regulations**. The travel alarm is required on MEWPs (Mobile Elevating Work Platforms) to warn nearby workers that the machine is in motion. Disabling it can **violate site safety protocols, manufacturer warranties, and legal standards**.

However, here are your **real-world options**, depending on your role and safety responsibilities:

---

### ✅ If You're a Site Safety Manager or Have Permission:

1. **Check the Operator’s Manual or Service Manual**:  
   Some Genie lifts allow you to **mute or adjust** the travel alarm volume through the service menu using a password-protected control panel. This may be shown in one of the documents you uploaded (I'll check it if needed).

2. **Physically Muffle the Buzzer (Risky & Not Recommended)**:  
   The buzzer is usually located inside the base control panel near the red emergency stop and joystick. Some workers (off the record) tape over the speaker hole or wrap it in foam to muffle it slightly.  
   ⚠️ **Be warned**: This might be a fireable offense or liability issue depending on your site’s policies.

---

### ❌ Do Not:

- Cut wires or remove the buzzer.
- Modify circuit boards or firmware.
- Use tape on any part of the control board if you don’t fully understand the electronics.

---

### Alternative Approach:
If this is for **indoor use**, and the beep is overly loud or disruptive, you could:
- **Contact your site manager** and request a unit inspection for **volume adjustment** (some are software-configurable).
- **Contact Genie support** to ask if a lower-decibel buzzer kit exists for indoor environments.
