## Assistant

Yes, I'm familiar with it. You're referring to the *Knowledge Argument* proposed by Frank Jackson in the form of the “Mary’s Room” thought experiment.

Here’s the basic setup: Mary is a brilliant scientist who knows everything there is to know about the science of color—wavelengths, optics, neurology, etc.—but she has lived her whole life in a black and white room and has never seen color herself. One day, she leaves the room and sees red for the first time. The question is: **Does Mary learn something new?**

Jackson originally argued that she does, which suggests that there are *non-physical* facts—qualia, or subjective experiences—that can’t be captured by physical explanations alone. This challenges materialism and supports a kind of dualism or at least the idea that conscious experience contains something over and above physical information.

Are you curious about how this holds up under a materialist or dialectical lens?
