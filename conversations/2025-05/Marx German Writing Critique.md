You're thinking of the *Preface* to the second edition of *Capital, Volume I*, where Marx makes a jab at the dense, "German" style of writing. Here's the quote:

> “The method of presentation must differ in form from that of inquiry. The latter has to appropriate the material in detail, to analyze its different forms of development, and to track down their inner connection. Only after this work is done, can the actual movement be adequately presented. If this is done successfully, if the life of the subject matter is now reflected back in the ideas, then it may appear as if we have before us a mere a priori construction.”

Then he adds, sarcastically:

> “This is, to be sure, the advantage of the German method: that it seems as if everything follows from the concept. The German schoolboy believes he has understood something when he has constructed it out of concepts.”

This is Marx mocking the tendency—especially among German idealists and academics—to write in a way where everything builds logically from definitions, giving the illusion that the concept creates the world, rather than the world being the source of the concept. He’s emphasizing that *Capital* is based on material analysis, not speculative philosophy.
