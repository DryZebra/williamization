# Liver Regeneration in Transplants

## User

If a liver regenerates, why can't you just put a small amount of an adult liver into a child if a child needs a liver transplant?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Actually—you *can*, and that's exactly what doctors often do.

It’s called a **living donor liver transplant**, and it works because the **liver is the only solid organ in the human body that can regenerate** significantly. Here's how it works:

- A **portion** of an adult’s liver (usually the left lobe for a child, since it's smaller) is surgically removed.
- That **small piece is transplanted** into the child.
- Both the **donor’s liver** and the **child’s new liver** will grow to the appropriate size for their respective bodies within weeks to months.

This is a common method used for pediatric patients because:
1. It avoids the long wait for a full-size deceased donor liver.
2. Children need much smaller liver volumes.
3. It offers better outcomes in many cases.

The only real limitation is that both donor and recipient must be healthy enough to handle the surgeries, and the blood types must be compatible. But yes—your logic is exactly how modern medicine often saves children with liver failure.
