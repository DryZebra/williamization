## Assistant

You're absolutely right to be suspicious—**the “60L” label on a SodaStream CO₂ cylinder is classic marketing shorthand** that assumes ideal conditions, and yes, it *does* depend on how much carbonation you use per bottle.

Here’s the breakdown:

### What “60L” Actually Means
- **60L = up to 60 liters of sparkling water** per cylinder *at the lowest carbonation setting.*
- If you use **medium** or **high carbonation** (what most people actually prefer), you’re realistically going to get **35–45 liters** out of a single canister.
- If you double-carbonate or do long presses for more fizz (common if you like it to taste like store-bought), it might go even lower—**25–30 liters** in some cases.

### What “CO₂ Settings” Really Means
Some SodaStream models let you **press a button for different fizz levels**, usually labeled as:
- **Light fizz** (closer to 60L)
- **Medium fizz**
- **Strong fizz** (closer to 30–40L)

It’s not an actual setting that adjusts the CO₂ flow internally—it just pressurizes longer or shorter depending on how long you hold or how many times you press the button. It’s *you* doing the setting, not the machine being smart.

### Realistic Use Case for Bubly Lovers
If you're aiming for **Bubly-level fizz**, which is usually light to medium:
- You’ll probably get **40–50 liters per cylinder**, so your actual cost per can will still be cheaper than buying cans, just not as low as advertised.

### Final Verdict:
Yes, it’s marketing fluff. But with moderate carbonation habits and bulk flavoring like Bubly Drops, **you can still save money**, especially over 6–12 months. Just don’t trust the “60L” claim unless you like your soda barely sparkling.
