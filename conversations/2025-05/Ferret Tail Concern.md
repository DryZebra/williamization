The obsession with creamy or oily textures is super common, but good on you for stopping him. Many human foods (like garlic, onions in ranch, or oils in hummus) are dangerous to ferrets, even in small amounts.

Here’s a quick tip to help manage that curiosity:

- **Redirect with safe treats**: Freeze-dried raw meats (like chicken, duck, or salmon) can be a good substitute when you're eating.
- **Create a “plate distraction” routine**: Give him a toy or his own “feast” right as you sit down to eat—he might get distracted by his own excitement.
- **Avoid leaving plates out**: Even just a lick can lead to tummy trouble or worse over time.

## Assistant

That’s such classic ferret behavior—obsessed with stealing food, but utterly uninterested in anything *made* for them. It’s like they want forbidden snacks *because* they’re forbidden.

A few things that might help:

### 1. **Tiny bits of the real deal (safe version)**
- Try *pure* peanut butter—no sugar, xylitol, or additives. A tiny dab on a spoon could be a treat if he’s crazy about it.
- Same with plain cooked chicken or scrambled egg (no seasoning). Just a tiny taste might break the “treat barrier.”

### 2. **Warm up the smell**
- Ferrets hunt by smell, not taste. Try warming a treat just slightly (not hot, just warm) to release scent oils.
- Rubbing it on your plate before offering might trick him into thinking it’s “real” food.

### 3. **Use the food he *does* like**
- If he likes his ferret food and dog food, you can “trick treat” by shaping tiny balls from soaked kibble and offering them as rewards.
- Some people mix ferret kibble powder with egg yolk and bake tiny treats from it.
