## Assistant

That’s really interesting — and actually makes sense biomechanically. Hanging from a towel changes a few key things compared to a bar:  

1. **Grip and joint angle** – Your wrists are neutral (palms facing each other), which is a much stronger and more natural position for the forearm and biceps. On a straight bar, your wrists are forced into pronation or supination, which adds strain.  

2. **Dynamic engagement** – The towel moves a bit, so your smaller stabilizer muscles engage, distributing effort differently. Paradoxically, that can *feel easier* because you’re not fighting rigid leverage points.  

3. **Range of motion** – With a towel, your elbows tend to flare slightly, and you might not be hanging at full dead weight since your grip shortens the distance a little. That reduces the total pull distance and load on the lats.  

4. **Elastic tension** – The towel gives a tiny bit, absorbing some of the downward force, which can make the movement feel smoother and less jarring.  

So while you’re still lifting most of your bodyweight, you’re doing it with a **stronger pulling angle**, **less wrist strain**, and **slightly shorter leverage**. It’s similar to a *neutral-grip pullup* (like between two parallel bars), which most people can do more easily.  

If you want to test this, try hanging two rings, or grab two ropes or handles in a neutral orientation — you’ll likely get similar results.

### 🪵 1. “Flag Lift” (Human Flag) Progression – Tree-Based Version
**Purpose:** joint torque, lateral chain, grip, scapular stability.

**a. Hanging Side Holds**  
Use the same towel setup, but grab one towel with each hand vertically aligned on the tree limb.  
- Pull slightly sideways so your body begins to tilt.  
- Hold for 3–5 seconds each side.  
- Think “push with lower hand, pull with upper.”  

**b. Side Knee Raises**  
Hang from one towel, brace your other hand on the trunk, and raise your knees toward your upper-hand side.  
- Builds obliques and lats together—the engine of the flag.  

**c. Diagonal Hangs**  
Instead of being fully horizontal, hang at a 45° angle.  
- When you can hold that for 10 seconds, you’re ready for near-horizontal work.  

**d. Assisted Flag**  
Wedge your lower leg against the trunk for partial support.  
- Use that leg just enough to stay stable; gradually rely on your arms more.  

---

### 🪨 2. The Rock-Lift Goal
**Purpose:** raw grip, posterior chain, tendon and bracing strength.

**a. Rock Rolls**  
Instead of trying to deadlift it yet, roll it over end-to-end.  
- This teaches you to *read the stone*: find edges, use your hips, keep back straight.  

**b. One-Edge Lifts**  
Try lifting just one edge or corner a few inches.  
- Focus on hip drive and keeping elbows tight.  
- You can use a stick or small log as a lever to help at first.  

**c. Partial Deadlifts**  
When it moves freely, get your hands underneath and lift it onto a slightly higher stone or stump.  
- Every time it’s higher, the leverage improves.  

**d. Clean to Lap → Stand**  
Eventually, you’ll be able to pull it into your lap (squat position), then stand by driving hips forward.  
That’s the farmer-stone method, old as the hills.
