# Reading hard hat date code

## User


What is this expiration ring on my hard hat? How do I read it?

## Assistant



## Assistant

That circular marking is a **manufacturing date code**—not an expiration date—commonly found on **hard hats and other molded plastic PPE**. It tells you **when the helmet shell was made**, which determines when it should be replaced.

Here’s how to read it:

### 🔍 The outer ring (numbers 1–12)
These represent the **month of manufacture**.  
A small arrow points to the month number.

> In your photo, the arrow points to **“1”**, which means **January**.

### 🔢 The inner circle (two-digit number)
This indicates the **year of manufacture**.  
In your image, the inner number is **“18”**, meaning **2018**.

### 🧠 How to interpret it
- The hard hat was **manufactured in January 2018**.
- Most hard hat shells are rated for **5 years from the date of manufacture**, and the **suspension** should be replaced every **1 year**.
- So this helmet would typically **expire around January 2023**, unless your manufacturer specifies otherwise.

You can usually find the brand’s official lifespan in their safety datasheet or molded into the brim (e.g., “Replace 5 years after manufacture”).

If you tell me the brand name (e.g., MSA, Bullard, 3M, etc.), I can pull up their specific lifespan policy for you.
