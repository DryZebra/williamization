When you train hard, you create **micro–damage** in the muscle fibers. Not injury, just tiny tears that trigger rebuilding. That soreness doesn’t show up immediately. It shows up **12–48 hours later**.

That delayed pain is called **DOMS**:
**Delayed Onset Muscle Soreness**

So even though yesterday was a “zero workout” day, your shoulders were still in the **inflammation + repair phase** from the day before.

Why it feels worse the day after rest:
• Blood flow drops during passive recovery  
• Inflammation becomes more noticeable  
• You’re no longer “warmed up” keeping things loose  

You weren’t hurting *yesterday* because your system was still in “active” mode. Today your body is switching into **rebuild mode**, and that hurts.

Why shoulders specifically:
• Shoulders are small, complex, high-use joints  
• Lots of stabilizer muscles in rotator cuff  
• Even indirect work (pushups, planks, posture, sleep position) hits them  

This is a **good sign**, by the way. It means your training load is enough to trigger real adaptation.
