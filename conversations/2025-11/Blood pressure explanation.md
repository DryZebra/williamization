## Assistant

That reading **isn’t “fine,” but it’s also not automatically an emergency** — both your coworkers and your doctor are half-right.

Let’s break it down clearly:

### What 184/65 actually means
- **184 (systolic)** → This is **very high**. Normal is under 120.
- **65 (diastolic)** → This is **normal/low-normal**.
- The **gap between them** (called pulse pressure) is **119**, which is unusually wide.

A systolic of **180+** is generally considered **hypertensive crisis territory** *if it stays that high*.

---

### Why your doctor may be saying it’s fine
A single high reading **at the dentist** can be caused by:
- Pain
- Anxiety (“white coat syndrome”)
- Caffeine
- Nicotine
- Dehydration

Doctors usually **don’t diagnose hypertension from one reading** in a stressful situation.

---

### Why your coworkers aren’t wrong either
Even if it was stress-related, **184 systolic is still a big spike**. That wide gap (184/65) can indicate:
- Vascular stiffness
- Stress response
- Adrenaline dump

It’s not something to **ignore**, but it’s something to **verify**, not panic about.

---

### What actually matters now
You need **home readings in calm conditions**.

If you can, get a cheap arm cuff from CVS/Walmart and check:
- After sitting quietly 5 minutes
- No caffeine first
- No nicotine first
- Take **3 readings**, 1 minute apart

**Normal targets:**
- Under 130/80 is good
- 130–140 systolic = borderline
- 140–180 = high
- Over 180 = concerning

---

### When to worry immediately
Go to urgent care or ER if you have:
- Chest pain
- Shortness of breath
- Vision changes
- Severe headache
- Dizziness or fainting
- Numbness in face/arm

---

### Honest answer
Your doctor is right that **one isolated reading doesn’t equal diagnosis**.
Your coworkers are right that **184 systolic should not be casually ignored**.

It’s a **yellow light**, not a red one.
