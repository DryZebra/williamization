# CO2 cartridge fridge safety

## User

If I put my CO2 cartridges in the fridge, will it make my soda cold when I make it, or will it just explode in my fridge?

## Assistant



## Assistant

You’re safe to chill them — within reason.  

Here’s how it works:

- **CO₂ cartridges don’t explode in a fridge.** They’re sealed metal cylinders designed to handle much higher pressures than what a refrigerator (around 35–40 °F / 2–4 °C) will cause. In fact, cooling **reduces** internal pressure slightly, making them even safer.  

- **Cold cartridges = colder soda** when carbonating. The CO₂ transfers better into cold liquid, so you’ll actually get *more fizz* and a slightly colder finished drink. The gas expands less on release, so carbonation efficiency improves.  

- **Don’t freeze them.** A freezer (below 0 °F / –18 °C) can make the rubber or metal seal brittle and risk leaks or bursts when you pierce it. Stick to fridge-cold, not freezer-cold.  

✅ **Best practice:**  
Keep both the water and cartridges refrigerated. Cold water absorbs CO₂ more efficiently, giving you a crisp, fizzy soda without wasting gas.  

So: fridge = good, freezer = bad, explosion = no.
