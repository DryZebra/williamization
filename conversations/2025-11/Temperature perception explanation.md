---

### ⚙️ **How it’s determined scientifically**

The “feels like” number comes from combining **three main factors**:

1. **Wind Chill (for cold days):**
   - When wind moves across your skin, it carries away the thin layer of warm air your body naturally creates.
   - The National Weather Service formula uses air temp and wind speed to calculate how much faster your body loses heat.  
   - Example:  
     > 40 °F air + 15 mph wind → feels like 31 °F  
     > 40 °F air + calm wind → feels like 40 °F  

2. **Heat Index (for hot days):**
   - Combines **temperature and humidity** to estimate how hard your body works to cool itself by sweating.  
   - More humidity = sweat doesn’t evaporate = feels hotter.

3. **Solar Radiation and Ground Reflection:**
   - Not always included in official “feels like” readings, but sunlight *really does* add warmth.
   - Your clothes, skin tone, and surroundings (asphalt vs grass) can change how much heat is absorbed.

---

### 🧠 Why it feels so different to *you* specifically
- **Sunlight** warms your skin and clothing directly (yesterday maybe cloudy, today full sun).  
- **Wind** yesterday stripped that warmth away faster.  
- **Adaptation** — your body adjusts day to day. After a very cold morning, 40 °F can feel warm the next day.  
- **Microclimate** — even standing near a wall, in sun vs shade, can create 10 °F+ differences in perceived warmth.

---

> **Today:** 39 °F → feels like 50 °F+ once the sun hits you
