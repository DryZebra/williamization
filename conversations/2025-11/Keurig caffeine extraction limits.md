# Keurig caffeine extraction limits

## User

In a standard Keurig k cup what Oz of water stops meaningfully add caffeine?

## Assistant



## Assistant

For a **standard Keurig K-Cup**, caffeine extraction follows the same curve as any other drip/brew method:

### **Short answer (practical):**  
**Anything past ~6–8 oz of water adds almost *no* meaningful caffeine.**  
Flavor keeps getting weaker, but caffeine extraction is basically done.

---

## **Why?**  
A K-Cup contains **about 9–12 grams of coffee**, which—no matter what—you can only extract **so much caffeine from**:

- A typical K-Cup yields **80–120 mg** caffeine total.  
- **~90–95% of that caffeine comes out in the first 4–6 oz of water.**  
- By **8 oz**, extraction is nearly complete.  
- From **10–12 oz**, you're just diluting the same amount of caffeine.

Think of it like rinsing a tea bag: the first steep extracts almost everything, the rest is weak.

---

## **Practical Keurig caffeine advice**
If your goal is *maximum caffeine per minute*:

- Brew **two 6-oz cups** back-to-back using *two* K-Cups.  
- Or brew **one 6-oz cup** and run it through a second pod with the “strong” setting if available.

If your goal is *strongest single cup*:  
Use **6 oz** on “strong.”

If your goal is *most caffeine with the least bitterness*:  
Use **8 oz**—you’ll get almost the same caffeine as 6 oz, but smoother.

---

## **Bottom line**  
**6–8 oz is the extraction ceiling.  
Past that, caffeine doesn’t meaningfully increase.**
