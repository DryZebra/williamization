---

## 🚗 1. You still count as a “Household Goods Mover” in NC
North Carolina *does* regulate in-state (intrastate) moving companies, but lightly compared to interstate.

According to the **North Carolina Utilities Commission (NCUC)**:
- Anyone who moves **household goods for compensation within North Carolina** must have a state-issued **Certificate of Exemption or License (a “C-number”)**.
- You’ll need to:
  - Register your business (LLC or sole proprietorship)
  - Get **liability insurance**
  - Show proof of **cargo insurance**
  - File a **tariff** (a rate sheet showing what you charge)
  - Have **a simple background check** and vehicle inspection if using trucks  

This isn’t nearly as complex as federal (interstate) registration — it’s a single-state process, and once done, you’re legal statewide.

👉 The exact page: [NC Utilities Commission — Transportation: Household Goods Movers](https://www.ncuc.gov/Industries/docinfo/household.html)

---

## ⚙️ 2. Your options depend on scale

### **A. Small “Driver-for-Hire” or Courier-Style Service**
If you:
- Use your own pickup truck, van, or trailer,
- Limit to *smaller* loads (dorm rooms, small apartments),
- Focus on *local or same-day* moves,
- And let customers handle their own packing/loading,

You might be able to operate as a **courier/personal driver** rather than a full mover — essentially “transporting goods at owner’s request.”  
→ This often falls below the “household goods mover” threshold if you don’t *advertise as a mover* or use large trucks.  
Still, it’s smart to call the NCUC to confirm your model.

---

### **B. Registered In-State Mover**
If you want to:
- Advertise as a moving company
- Use a box truck or trailer for household goods
- Handle multiple clients’ items or do loading/unloading

Then you’ll apply for an **NC household goods mover license**:
- Fill out **Form T-431A**
- Pay a small filing fee (around $200)
- Provide insurance documentation
- Wait for approval (~30–60 days)

Once approved, you can legally move goods anywhere within NC.

- A **clear written contract** spelling out what you are and aren’t responsible for
