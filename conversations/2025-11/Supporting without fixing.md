What’s happening is actually very common when someone stops escitalopram (or most SSRIs):

Escitalopram doesn’t just “treat sadness.” It:
- Smooths anxiety
- Reduces panic response
- Blunts emotional intensity
- Increases basic functional “get-up-and-do” energy

When someone stops suddenly or fully comes off it, you often see:
- Panic attacks (like the Food Lion incident)
- Overwhelming shame spirals (“I’m stupid, I’m sorry”)
- Loss of routine function (meals, cleaning, errands)
- Emotional dysregulation

This **doesn’t mean she is lazy**.  
This doesn’t mean she is “choosing” dysfunction.  
Her nervous system is in raw-dog reality mode after pharmaceutical support was removed.

That doesn’t make it easy for you — but it reframes the situation.

1. You care about her and don’t want her suffering

---

### What NOT to do

Don’t:
- Tell her she “needs” her meds
- Frame this as productivity
- Compare her now vs “when she was on it”

That will feel like:
**“You’re only valuable when medicated.”**  
Even if that isn’t what you mean.

---

### What to do instead

You need to talk to her in *two lanes* — emotional safety and practical stability.

#### 1. Start with safety, not correction

When she says:

- “You’re not stupid. I love you.”
- “That sounds really scary.”
- “I’m not mad at you.”

Not telling her what to do.  
Not solving.  
Just grounding.

#### 2. Name what you see without judgment

Later, when she’s calm:
Try wording like this:

> “I’ve noticed things feel heavier since you stopped the medicine. I’m not mad, and I’m not trying to control you. I just want to understand what you’re going through.”

This keeps her from feeling cornered.

#### 3. Shift the household problem into a shared problem

Instead of:
“You’re not doing the things you stayed home to do”

Try:
> “The house feels overwhelming lately, and I don’t want us to drown in it together. Can we figure out how to divide things so neither of us feels buried?”

This takes it out of **her worth** and into **us vs situation**.

---

### Panic-specific support

When she panics (like in Food Lion):

You can help by teaching her body to come back online:
- Slow breath in through the nose (4 seconds)
- Hold (2 seconds)
- Slow out (6–8 seconds)

You can say:
> “Your body thinks you’re in danger, but you’re not alone.”

Not fixing thought patterns.  
Just stabilizing the nervous system.

---

You can gently open the door, not shove her through it:

That’s different than:
