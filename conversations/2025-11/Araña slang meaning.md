Yeah — **“araña” does mean “spider” in Spanish**, but in this case it’s almost certainly *not* referring to an actual spider.  

On construction sites, especially in concrete or block work, **“Araña” is a slang nickname** used by some Spanish-speaking tradesmen for a **junction box, conduit spider box, or multi-arm box** — basically any electrical box or conduit hub that branches out in several directions like a spider’s legs.  

Here’s what the markings suggest:  
- The **red line** is a level or elevation line (8 ft 5 in high).  
- “Araña” written on the block indicates **where that spider-type box or fitting will be installed**.  
- The **dimensions** (like 2 ¾ in, 8 ft 5 in) mark either the mounting height or offset from the finished wall surface.

So you’re looking at layout notes for electrical rough-in work — someone’s marking where a “spider” junction will go.
