## Assistant

Yeah, that **is** unsafe – you’re right to be concerned.

What you’re smelling and tasting is likely **concrete/grout dust**, and that almost always contains **crystalline silica**, which is genuinely dangerous.

Here’s why this matters:

**Silica dust is not just “annoying dust.”**
When concrete is ground, it releases microscopic particles of **respirable crystalline silica**. You can’t see the worst part of it. It is small enough to get deep into your lungs and stay there permanently.

Long-term exposure can cause:
• **Silicosis** (incurable scarring of the lungs)  
• Chronic bronchitis  
• Reduced lung capacity  
• Increased lung cancer risk  
• Higher TB risk  

If you can **taste it**, you’re almost definitely inhaling a respirable amount.

This work should *never* be done dry in shared air.

Proper controls should include:
• **Wet grinding** (water suppression)  
• **HEPA vacuum shrouds** attached to grinders  
• **Negative air machines** or sealed work area  
• Workers wearing **respirators (P100 or N95 minimum, P100 preferred)**

If they’re dry grinding without containment, that is a serious safety failure.

**What you should do immediately:**
• Get out of that area if possible  
• Tell your supervisor or GC that you’re being exposed to airborne silica  
• Ask what dust controls are in place  
• Ask if air monitoring has been done  

You’re not being dramatic. This is an OSHA-level hazard.

If you want, tell me:
– Are they dry grinding or wet?  
– Is the area sealed off?  
– Are the workers wearing respirators?  

I can help you decide the safest next move.
