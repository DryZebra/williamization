## Assistant

Yes. The simplest way is to create or convert to a PayPal *Business* account (or a separate business profile) under a “brand name” or your daughter’s art name. When people send money, they’ll see that business name instead of your personal details. Below is an overview of the main approaches:

---

### 1. Add a Separate Email to Your Existing PayPal Account
- **How it works**: PayPal lets you add multiple email addresses to a single account. You could add a new email address (something like `[email protected]`) that your daughter shares with her clients.  
- **What payers see**: Typically, they’ll see whatever “account name” you have set in PayPal, *unless* you configure a business profile. If your personal name is still on the account, some of your personal details might appear in the receipt or transaction details.

**Recommendation**: If you’re trying to keep your personal name and email hidden, you generally want to ensure you’re using a Business Account or at least have a “business name” set in your profile that masks your personal info.

---

### 2. Create (or Convert) to a PayPal Business Account
- **How it works**: PayPal Business Accounts allow you to operate under a business/brand name. You can still manage everything under your own login, but you’ll have a “Business Name” field, which is shown to customers.
- **What payers see**: When someone sends money or looks at the transaction details, they see the business name (e.g., “Daughter’s Art Commissions”) instead of your personal name. Your personal email doesn’t show up either—only the business email or brand.

**Steps**:  
1. **Log in** to your PayPal account.  
2. **Go to Settings** (the gear icon).  
3. Look for an option like **“Upgrade to a Business account”** or **“Switch to a Business profile”**.  
4. **Enter your desired business name**—this is what will appear to your daughter’s clients.  
5. **Add a new “business email”** (which could be an address you set up specifically for her art).

---

### 3. Use a PayPal.Me Link (With a Business Name)
- **How it works**: PayPal.Me is a personalized URL (e.g., paypal.me/YourDaughtersArt) you can share with clients so they can pay you quickly.
- **What payers see**: By default, a personal PayPal.Me link can show your personal name. If you set up a business PayPal.Me link in a Business Account, it’ll display the business name.
- **Notes**: If your PayPal account is strictly personal, PayPal.Me might still reveal parts of your personal profile. If you want a PayPal.Me link that shows only your daughter’s art name, you’ll need the Business Account approach.

---

## Points to Consider

1. **Account Ownership**: If your daughter is a minor, PayPal often requires the account holder to be 18 or older. You, as the parent, might have to remain the primary account holder.  
2. **Business vs. Personal**: Business Accounts usually require filling out a bit more information (like a business category, address, etc.). But for art commissions, it can just be a “Sole Proprietorship” with a creative name.  
3. **Fees**: PayPal fees for receiving money (especially from commissions) apply whether it’s a personal or business account.  
4. **Privacy Settings**: Double-check your PayPal profile settings so that your personal phone number, address, or other personal details are not shared or displayed on invoices.

---

### Summary

To keep your personal information private, the best route is **upgrading to (or creating) a PayPal Business account** under your daughter’s art name (or a brand name). Then you can add a dedicated email for her commissions. When clients pay, **they’ll only see the business/brand name and that new email address**, not your personal one. 

That way, you can still control the funds and supervise as necessary, while ensuring your own privacy.

## Assistant

If your goal is to avoid sharing *any* personal name or email with clients, your best bet is to set up a **separate, business-style PayPal account** (or similar platform) that uses a **brand name** or alias instead of your real name. That way, customers see only that brand/alias in transactions. Here are a few practical tips to make it as anonymous as possible:

---

## 1. Use a Dedicated “Business Name” (or Artist Name)
- **Why it helps**: A Business PayPal account can display the name of your choosing (e.g., “StarSketch Studio”) instead of your personal info.  
- **What you do**:  
  1. Create a new email address that doesn’t include your real name (e.g., `[email protected]`).  
  2. Sign up for PayPal as a Business Account (or upgrade an existing personal account) using that brand name.  
  3. Double-check that “StarSketch Studio” (the brand name) is set as the primary/visible name in all PayPal settings.  

**Result**: People sending payment will only see “StarSketch Studio,” not your personal name/email. Keep in mind PayPal itself still requires *your* real info behind the scenes for verification—just your customers won’t see it.

---

## 2. Use a PayPal.Me Link (with a Business Name)
- **How it helps**: A PayPal.Me link can be customized to show your business/brand name, giving you a simple URL (e.g., `paypal.me/StarSketch`).  
- **What you do**:  
  1. Create or switch to a PayPal Business account.  
  2. Generate a PayPal.Me link for that account under your brand name.  
  3. Share only the PayPal.Me link with clients.  

**Result**: Clients click the link and see your brand name—no personal details are revealed.

---

## 3. Use an Online “Tip Jar” Platform
If you’re open to using a different service (still powered by PayPal on the backend), you can sometimes shield your personal details further. For instance, platforms like **Ko-fi** or **Buy Me A Coffee** let you accept PayPal payments behind a public profile.  
- **Why it helps**: The platform itself acts as a layer between you and the payer, so it shows your page’s display name (e.g., “StarSketch Studio”), not your personal name/email.  
- **What to check**: Some platforms still show the “merchant” name from your PayPal (if not configured as a business), so you should confirm that your PayPal business name is set up correctly.

---

## 4. Open a Separate Bank Account (Optional Extra Layer)
If you’re concerned about financial footprints or record-keeping, consider associating a dedicated bank account (rather than your everyday personal account) with your new PayPal. 
- **Why it helps**: Keeps finances separate, easier for tax/reporting, and if your daughter is the one actually doing the art, you’ll have a clearer overview of “art” vs. personal expenses.

---

## Caveats / Things to Keep in Mind

1. **Verification Requirements**: Even if you use a pseudonym or brand name publicly, PayPal will still want your real info (address, possibly ID) for identity verification. This info is private to PayPal, not shown to customers.  
2. **Minors**: If your daughter is under 18, you’ll have to be the official account holder—PayPal’s policy generally requires the user be at least 18.  
3. **Transaction Receipts**: Make sure to send or request money in a way that references only your brand name. Double-check any automated receipts or invoices to ensure they don’t include your personal name or address.  
4. **Business vs. “Anonymous”**: True anonymity isn’t entirely possible if you’re collecting real money—there must be an actual person or entity behind the account. However, from the *customer’s perspective*, you can conceal your real name and contact info by using a dedicated business/brand identity.

---

### Bottom Line
**Setting up (or converting to) a new PayPal Business account under a brand name** is the most “anonymous” approach while staying within PayPal’s rules. Customers will only see the brand or alias you choose, and you can keep personal details out of sight.

If you want a **semi-anonymous way** to accept commissions without PayPal, there are other platforms and payment methods that provide some level of anonymity and can still be linked to your daughter’s art commissions. Here are a few options:

---

### 1. **Cryptocurrency (e.g., Bitcoin, Ethereum)**
- **Why it works**: Cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin or Ethereum allow for relatively anonymous transactions, where the payer only sees a wallet address, not personal information. 
- **What you do**:  
  1. Set up a **cryptocurrency wallet** (such as **Coinbase**, **Exodus**, or **Electrum**).  
  2. Share your **wallet address** with clients when they want to pay you.  
  3. Use a **non-custodial wallet** (one where you control the private keys) for more privacy.
- **Pros**: Transactions are fast and private; no personal bank info or PayPal account needed.  
- **Cons**: Cryptocurrency values can fluctuate, and some customers may not be familiar or comfortable with using it.

---

### 2. **Venmo (With a Business Account)**
- **Why it works**: Venmo, owned by PayPal, allows you to set up a business profile with a **separate name** and email.
- **What you do**:  
  1. Set up a **Venmo Business Account** with your daughter’s art name or brand.  
  2. Use a **business email** that doesn’t reveal your personal details.  
  3. Share your Venmo handle or payment link for clients to pay.  
- **Pros**: Quick and easy; clients don’t see personal details if the business profile is set up.  
- **Cons**: Venmo is still tied to your real identity behind the scenes, but the public-facing account can use a pseudonym.

---

### 3. **Ko-fi**
- **Why it works**: Ko-fi is a platform for creators where clients can support you with one-time payments or subscriptions. You can set up an anonymous profile and link it to a **PayPal** or **Stripe** account, but Ko-fi shields most of your personal details from clients.
- **What you do**:  
  1. Set up a **Ko-fi account** with your daughter’s art name.  
  2. Link it to a **PayPal** or **Stripe** account with business settings.  
  3. Share your Ko-fi page with clients to receive donations or commissions.  
- **Pros**: Clients see your Ko-fi page, not your personal info. Ko-fi also allows direct one-time payments.  
- **Cons**: You still need a payment processor like PayPal or Stripe for payouts, which can involve sharing some of your personal details.

---

### 4. **Buy Me A Coffee**
- **Why it works**: Similar to Ko-fi, **Buy Me A Coffee** is a platform that allows creators to receive support and commissions via **donations** or **custom commissions**. You can use it to accept payments without revealing much personal info.
- **What you do**:  
  1. Create a **Buy Me A Coffee** account with your daughter’s art name.  
  2. Link it to a **PayPal** or **Stripe** account with business settings, ensuring personal info is hidden.  
  3. Share the Buy Me A Coffee link for clients to send commissions.  
- **Pros**: Very user-friendly for artists, and clients don’t see personal details unless you choose to share them.  
- **Cons**: Still requires PayPal or Stripe for processing, meaning some personal info may be involved.

---

### 5. **Stripe (via Third-Party Platform)**
- **Why it works**: Stripe allows payments without disclosing personal information. While you will need to verify your identity, clients will only see the name and email linked to your Stripe account, which can be a business name.
- **What you do**:  
  1. Set up a **Stripe account** under your daughter’s art name.  
  2. Link it to a **third-party platform** (such as **Shopify**, **BigCartel**, or **Gumroad**) that accepts Stripe payments.  
  3. Share the platform link for clients to place orders.  
- **Pros**: You have more control over the setup and branding, and transactions can be anonymous from the client’s perspective.  
- **Cons**: The platform fees, and some setup may be needed.

---

### 6. **Etsy (With a Business Name)**
- **Why it works**: Etsy lets you sell art and accept payments without directly revealing your personal details. You can set up a **shop name** (like "StarSketch") that clients will see, rather than your own name.
- **What you do**:  
  1. Create an **Etsy shop** under a **business name** or art name.  
  2. Accept payments via **Etsy Payments**, which handles everything through a secure system without revealing personal info.
- **Pros**: Trusted platform for creative works; clients see only the shop name.  
- **Cons**: Etsy takes a cut of each sale, and you’ll need to follow platform rules for setup.

---

### Summary

For a **semi-anonymous** setup, the **best options** are:
1. **Cryptocurrency** for true anonymity.
2. **Ko-fi** or **Buy Me A Coffee** for a more user-friendly, creator-focused platform with a layer of privacy.
3. **Venmo** or **Stripe** linked to a business profile for simple, masked transactions.
  
All of these allow you to maintain a level of separation between personal information and your daughter's commission business. However, keep in mind that **full anonymity** is difficult when dealing with real money since payment platforms generally require some personal verification behind the scenes.

## Assistant

Managing commissions and money independently can be tricky for a 14-year-old because most payment platforms require users to be at least 18. However, there *are* a few teen-friendly banking and payment options—often referred to as “teen banking apps” or “family finance apps”—that provide a debit card, allow deposits, and let minors manage their funds (usually with some parental oversight). Below are a few ideas:

---

## 1. Cash App for Teens (U.S.)

- **Why it works**: Cash App introduced teen accounts (13+) in the U.S. a couple of years ago. A parent/guardian has to approve the teen account, but afterward, the teen can receive money from others via their unique “\$Cashtag.” They can also request a Cash App debit card (Cash Card) to spend their balance.

- **How to set up**:
  1. A parent or guardian with their own Cash App must invite or approve the teen’s account.
  2. The teen then gets their own Cash App login and \$Cashtag.
  3. Once set up, your daughter can share her \$Cashtag with art clients for payments.
  4. She can use (and customize) a physical or virtual Cash Card to spend her earnings.

- **Pros**:
  - Straightforward for clients who already use Cash App.
  - Teen can see the balance and manage funds directly.
  - Debit card can be used in stores or online.

- **Cons**:
  - Available primarily in the U.S.
  - Parent oversight is required for sign-up and certain parental controls might apply.

---

## 2. Teen/Family Banking Apps with a Debit Card

Several banking apps offer **prepaid or debit cards** designed for minors. Although they are often marketed as allowance/chores cards, some can receive direct transfers from third parties (though you might need to share routing/account info or a deposit link). Popular ones include:

1. **Greenlight**  
2. **GoHenry**  
3. **FamZoo**  
4. **Step**  
5. **Current Teen Account**  

### How they typically work
- A parent opens a family or teen account, then creates a sub-account for the child.  
- The teen gets their own debit card and can see their balance in the app.  
- Depending on the service, you can enable direct deposits or bank transfers into the teen’s account. 

### Pros
- Teens learn money management.  
- Parents can supervise and set spending limits.  
- Teens get their own physical or virtual debit card.

### Cons
- Often there’s a monthly subscription fee (e.g., Greenlight starts around \$4.99/mo).  
- **Not all** these apps make it easy for “random customers on the internet” to pay your child directly. You might need to share an “account and routing number” or a special link.  
- The parent is still the legal account holder.

---

## 3. Ko-fi or Buy Me A Coffee + a Family Bank Setup

If you want to avoid typical “under 18” restrictions on PayPal or Venmo, one workaround could be:

1. **Parent** sets up a Ko-fi (or Buy Me A Coffee) account under the daughter’s art name.  
2. **Link** it to a **family bank account** or a teen debit card account (like Step or Current), if that platform allows receiving payouts to the bank.  
3. **Your daughter** manages the Ko-fi page content, posts art, and shares the link.  
4. Clients pay via Ko-fi/Buy Me A Coffee, and the money eventually lands in the bank account that she can access with her teen debit card.

- **Pros**:
  - Ko-fi/Buy Me A Coffee is easy for buyers.  
  - The daughter can customize her page and manage commissions.

- **Cons**:
  - Legally, the parent still needs to be the official owner of the Ko-fi/Buy Me A Coffee account if the child is under 18.  
  - Any connected payment processor (Stripe/PayPal) will likely be in the parent’s name.

---

## 4. Cryptocurrency (with a Crypto-Linked Card)

While not everyone’s first choice, **crypto** could be an option if your daughter’s audience is comfortable using it:

1. Create a **crypto wallet** that a minor can manage (some custodial wallets allow this if a parent sets up the account).  
2. People pay in crypto to her wallet address.  
3. She uses a **crypto debit card** (offered by services like Crypto.com or Binance Card in certain regions) that converts crypto to USD (or local currency) when she spends.

- **Pros**:  
  - Potentially more international anonymity for receiving payments.  
  - A debit card that can spend the crypto balance.

- **Cons**:  
  - Volatility of cryptocurrency value.  
  - Most crypto platforms also have age restrictions (often 18+), so the parent might again have to sign up.

---

## Important Notes

1. **Age Restrictions**:  
   - Major payment processors (PayPal, Stripe, Venmo) generally require you to be 18+.  
   - Teen banking apps require parental setup or joint ownership.  

2. **Parental Oversight**:  
   - Even if the 14-year-old is “managing” the money, the account itself will typically be in the parent/guardian’s name or under a family plan. Full financial independence at 14 isn’t legally recognized by most institutions.  

3. **Legality and Taxes**:  
   - Earning money from commissions, even if under 18, can trigger tax obligations depending on where you live and how much is earned.  
   - Parents might need to keep records of the child’s income.  

4. **Privacy vs. Anonymity**:  
   - Completely anonymous financial tools for minors basically don’t exist in the mainstream banking system.  
   - “Semi-anonymous” or privacy-protective setups generally mean the public-facing name can be your daughter’s art alias, but behind the scenes, you (the parent) remain the legal entity on file.

---

### Which Is Best?

- If you’re in the **U.S.**, **Cash App for Teens** is probably the most straightforward way for a 14-year-old to directly receive and manage money, then spend it on a debit card—all with minimal hassle. It’s easy for buyers who are used to Cash App.  
- If you prefer a **more robust “family banking” approach**, something like **Greenlight** or **Step** will give her a card and let her see her funds. However, accepting random payments from art clients may be trickier, as you’ll need to share deposit details or set up some bridging platform.  
- For a **creator-focused** presence, a parent-managed **Ko-fi** or **Buy Me A Coffee** page + a **teen banking card** for payouts can give a nice front-end for commissions, with the money eventually transferring into the teen’s account.

In all scenarios, you as the parent will need to be involved at the setup stage, but the ongoing management can often be handled by your daughter.
