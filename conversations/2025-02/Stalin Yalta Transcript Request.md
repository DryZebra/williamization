## 1. Stalin on Poland

### Sixth Plenary Meeting (February 6, 1945, 4 p.m., Livadia Palace)

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “The main question is the question of the Polish Government. The Soviet Government recognized the Lublin Government because we saw no other authority in Poland which was defending the interests of the Polish people... We consider that the Lublin Government should be the basis for the future Polish Government of National Unity... That is our stand.”

He also insisted that the eastern frontier of Poland should follow the **Curzon Line**, saying:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “We stand on the Curzon Line. That we have said long ago. We have stated that since the Tehran Conference... The question of Poland’s western frontiers is for the Poles themselves to decide at a later stage, in consultation with the Allies, but we adhere to the Curzon Line in the East.”

Stalin repeatedly emphasized security concerns regarding Poland as a neighbor:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “We want a Poland that is strong, independent, and democratic... But we also want to be certain that Poland will not become a corridor again through which an enemy can march on Russia, as so often in the past.”

---

## 2. Stalin on Germany and Reparations

### Fourth Plenary Meeting (February 5, 1945, 4 p.m., Livadia Palace)

During the discussions on how much Germany should pay in reparations after the war, Stalin was very firm about a large sum (the Soviet side initially proposed 20 billion dollars total, with 50%—i.e., 10 billion—going to the USSR). According to the notes:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “Twenty billions is the minimum. We agreed on that sum as the total reparation to be taken from Germany. For the Soviet Union, we must insist on the figure of ten billions. We have suffered enormous destruction in our territory... We have lost more than any other country, both in lives and property.”

He also wanted to ensure dismantling of key German industries:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “Moreover, in order to prevent Germany from renewing attack on her neighbors in the future, we must take away the industrial equipment which Germany uses for war production... We want to make sure Germany will never again be able to attack Russia or her western neighbors.”

---

## 3. Stalin on Soviet Entry into the War Against Japan

### Eighth Plenary Meeting (February 8, 1945, 4 p.m., Livadia Palace)

A major topic was the Soviet promise to join the war against Japan, in exchange for certain concessions in the Far East—such as the return of southern Sakhalin, the Kurile Islands, and maintenance of Soviet interests in Manchuria’s railways and Port Arthur. Stalin explicitly stated:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “As soon as Germany is defeated, we shall enter the war against Japan. But we must have security in the Far East. We must have back what was taken from Russia by Japan in 1905... These are Sakhalin and the Kuriles. And we want guarantees in Manchuria that will assure the Soviet Union’s security against any future aggression.”

At the same meeting, he added:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “I repeat, the Soviet Union will declare war against Japan two or three months after the capitulation of Germany and the end of the war in Europe... We want to free mankind from the menace of Japanese aggression. But we must have the strategic frontiers that belong to us historically and that are necessary for our security.”

---

## 4. Stalin on the United Nations and “Soviet Republics”

### Sixth Plenary Meeting (February 6, 1945, continued)

When the question arose about membership in the new world organization (the United Nations), Stalin raised the possibility of having separate seats not only for the USSR but also for Soviet republics like the Ukraine and Byelorussia (Belarus). He argued:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “The Soviet Union is not a unitary state. It is a union of Republics, each one having its own government... Why should the British Dominions enjoy membership in the new organization, and the Soviet Republics not? We only ask for two or three of our Republics, the most important ones, because they have the greatest interest in security—they have suffered heavily from the war.”

He also said:

> **Marshal Stalin**:  
> “We do not demand separate membership for all the sixteen Republics. That would be impractical. But the Ukraine and Byelorussia especially should have separate votes in the United Nations. They have been front-line Republics that bore the brunt of invasion.”

Ultimately, the Western Allies accepted membership for Ukraine and Belarus (Byelorussia), but not for all the other Soviet republics.
