# Methodist vs Southern Baptist

## User

What's the theological difference between Methodist and Southern Baptist?

## Assistant



## Assistant

The theological differences between Methodists and Southern Baptists primarily revolve around **salvation, sacraments, church governance, and social issues**. Here are the key distinctions:

### **1. Salvation & Free Will**
- **Methodists (Arminianism)**: Believe in **free will**—that people can accept or reject God's grace. They follow **Wesleyan theology**, emphasizing sanctification and the idea that a person can lose their salvation if they turn away from faith.
- **Southern Baptists (Calvinist Influence)**: While not all are strict Calvinists, many believe in **eternal security** ("once saved, always saved"). They emphasize individual decision-making for salvation but often hold that true believers cannot lose their salvation.

### **2. Baptism & Communion**
- **Methodists**: Practice **infant baptism** and see it as a sign of God's grace, though they also baptize adults. They believe baptism is symbolic and not required for salvation. They practice **open communion**, allowing all believers to take the Lord’s Supper.
- **Southern Baptists**: Practice **believer’s baptism by immersion only**, rejecting infant baptism. They see baptism as an outward sign of an inward faith. Communion is typically **closed** or **restricted**, meaning only baptized believers can participate.

### **3. Church Governance**
- **Methodists**: Use an **Episcopal system**, meaning churches are overseen by bishops and governed by the larger Methodist denomination (e.g., United Methodist Church).
- **Southern Baptists**: Follow a **congregationalist** model, meaning each church is autonomous, making its own decisions without oversight from a governing body.

### **4. Role of Women in Ministry**
- **Methodists**: Generally allow **women pastors and bishops** (especially in the United Methodist Church).
- **Southern Baptists**: Do not allow women to be pastors, following a **complementarian** view of gender roles in church leadership.

### **5. Social & Cultural Issues**
- **Methodists**: Tend to be more **socially progressive** on some issues like LGBTQ+ inclusion and social justice (though it varies by denomination and region).
- **Southern Baptists**: Tend to be more **socially conservative**, opposing same-sex marriage and often aligning with right-wing politics.

### **6. Worship Style**
- **Methodists**: More structured and liturgical in worship, often following a set **order of worship**.
- **Southern Baptists**: More **informal**, emphasizing expository preaching and congregational singing.
