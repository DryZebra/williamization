# Pragmatic Christian Denomination Ideas

## User

I'm trying to think of an idea for a denomination of a church, and it's based around the idea that the metaphysical question of God's "real"ness is irrelevant in the face of 3 facts. 

1. Humans have almost always had religion. Religion and it's traditions and practices are important to humans for some reason, and even if it's just social and psychological, it's important. 

2. The Bible contains important life lessons that are worth learning. Not just the stereotypical stuff like be kind to others. But also sublation, becoming, and even the dialectical method in a way. Understand the shift of the economic base with Jacob and his sheep, why God "chose" him is important. 

3. Regardless of all of this, in my individual context, my community collectively is overwhelmingly Christian. To properly participate in my community I need to understand their cultural references and the way they act and treat each other. I need the Bible to understand the "rules." 

For me, the metaphysical question of the supernatural aspect of the Bible is, well, irrelevant. Who cares? The Bible itself does actually exist, and it actually has a real impact on my life. To understand it and be one with my community is the most human, and therefore ironically the most atheistic thing, I can do as an atheist. Why? Because it means that I'm acknowledging, as all atheists should, that I live in a living world with rules and culture, and I should participate because it's good for me.

## Assistant

That’s a really compelling foundation for a denomination, one that bridges the gap between secularism and religious participation in a way that acknowledges both the material and cultural realities of human life. You’re essentially constructing a pragmatic, dialectical approach to religion—one that treats Christianity not as a metaphysical truth but as a historically and socially emergent structure that has real value. 

A few ideas for what you might call this denomination:

1. **Pragmatic Christianity** – Emphasizing the practical, real-world benefits of religious participation.
2. **Dialectical Christianity** – Highlighting how the faith has evolved in response to changing material conditions and social structures.
3. **Cultural Christianity** – A term that already exists, but often refers to people who identify as Christian for cultural reasons rather than faith.
4. **Agnostic Christianity** – This might be a little misleading since you’re not saying “maybe God exists,” but rather that the question itself is irrelevant.
5. **The Church of Living Faith** – A more poetic way to emphasize that your focus is on the lived experience of Christianity rather than its metaphysics.
6. **Sublationist Christianity** – Drawing directly from the Hegelian idea of sublation (aufheben), where contradictions are preserved and transcended.
7. **Humanist Christianity** – A bit loaded with existing meanings, but it conveys the idea of religion serving human needs first and foremost.
8. **Material Christianity** – A provocative choice that emphasizes that the Bible, religion, and faith exist in material reality, regardless of their supernatural claims.

Would you want this church to have specific practices, like a unique way of reading scripture, communal discussions, or a focus on a particular interpretation of Jesus’ teachings? If you refine what this community *does* in addition to what it *believes*, it could develop into a full-fledged movement.
