# LLC Before Electrical License

## User

When pursuing my electrical license in NC, should I have my LLC before or after?

## Assistant

In North Carolina, it's generally advisable to establish your Limited Liability Company (LLC) **before** applying for your electrical contractor's license. Here's why:

1. **License Association:** When you apply for an electrical contractor's license, the license is tied to the entity performing the work. If you intend to operate under an LLC, having it established beforehand ensures the license is correctly associated with your business entity.

2. **Qualifying Individual:** The North Carolina State Board of Examiners of Electrical Contractors requires that a business entity (like an LLC) designate a "qualifying individual" who has met the necessary examination and experience requirements. By forming the LLC first, you can designate yourself (or another qualified person) in this role during the license application process.

3. **Administrative Efficiency:** Establishing the LLC first can streamline paperwork and reduce potential complications. If you were to obtain the license as an individual and then form an LLC later, you might need to go through additional steps to transfer or reassign the license to your new business entity.

**Steps to Follow:**

1. **Form Your LLC:** Register your LLC with the North Carolina Secretary of State. This process includes choosing a unique business name, filing the Articles of Organization, and paying the requisite fees.

2. **Apply for the License:** Once your LLC is established, apply for the electrical contractor's license under the LLC's name. Ensure you or another designated individual meets the qualification requirements set by the licensing board.

3. **Compliance:** After obtaining the license, ensure your LLC adheres to all state regulations, including maintaining necessary insurance, renewals, and continuing education requirements.

By following this sequence, you position your business for a smoother operational start and ensure compliance with North Carolina's regulatory framework.
