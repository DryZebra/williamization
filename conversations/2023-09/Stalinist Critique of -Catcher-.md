The idea of tolerating opposing viewpoints or allowing a diversity of ideas is often seen as a potential weakness that can undermine the stability and unity of the state.

2. Threat to Ideological Purity: Stalinist ideology places a premium on maintaining ideological purity within the party and society. The paradox of tolerance, which suggests that a truly tolerant society must tolerate even intolerant views, can be seen as a threat to this purity. Stalinists argue that tolerating intolerant ideologies can lead to their propagation and dilution of the "correct" ideology.

3. Prioritizing State Interests: From a Stalinist perspective, the state's interests and the goals of the ruling party are paramount. The paradox of tolerance, with its emphasis on individual freedoms and pluralism, may be viewed as potentially undermining the state's authority and control over society.

4. Suppressing Counterrevolutionary Elements: Stalinism historically involved the suppression of perceived counterrevolutionary elements and opposition, often through force and authoritarian measures. The paradox of tolerance, which suggests a willingness to tolerate dissenting opinions, can be seen as incompatible with the Stalinist approach of maintaining tight control and eliminating potential threats.

5. Concerns about Manipulation: Stalinists might argue that tolerating intolerant views opens the door to manipulation by counterrevolutionary forces seeking to subvert the state. They would contend that vigilance and suppression of such views are necessary to protect the socialist order.

In summary, from a Stalinist perspective, the paradox of tolerance may be criticized for potentially weakening the state, diluting ideological purity, prioritizing individual freedoms over collective interests, and allowing the propagation of ideas deemed counterrevolutionary. Stalinism traditionally favored a strong, controlled state with limited tolerance for dissenting voices in the interest of preserving the socialist order.
