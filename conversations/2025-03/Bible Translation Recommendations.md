# Bible Translation Recommendations

## User

As someone who is well studied on Marx Engels Lenin Stalin and Mao, which translation of the Bible should I read? I have a high grade reading level so I'm not intimidated by complex language if it increases accuracy 

## Assistant



## Assistant

If you want a translation that prioritizes accuracy and maintains the historical and linguistic integrity of the text, I’d recommend the **New Revised Standard Version (NRSV)** or the **Revised Standard Version (RSV)**. These are well-respected scholarly translations that strive for balance between formal equivalence (word-for-word accuracy) and readability.  

However, if you want something even closer to the original languages and don’t mind more archaic language, the **King James Version (KJV)** or the **New King James Version (NKJV)** could be useful. The KJV has a strong literary and historical influence, and while its language can be complex, it remains one of the most influential English translations.  

For a Marxist or materialist reading, you might also find the **Septuagint (LXX)** useful for the Old Testament, as it's the Greek translation that early Christians used, or even the **Douay-Rheims Bible**, which is an older Catholic translation closer to the Vulgate (the Latin Bible of the medieval Church).  

If you’re looking for a translation that best captures the class struggles, historical materialism, and political dimensions of scripture, reading multiple translations side by side (especially comparing formal translations like the NRSV with more interpretive ones like the **New Jerusalem Bible (NJB)**) could be insightful. Would you be approaching the Bible from a theological, historical, or dialectical-materialist perspective?
