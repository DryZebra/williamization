# Engaging Atheists Dialectically

## User

It's like I can't even get the conversation *started* with atheists. They cannot move past "prove God is real" and "Jesus never existed" etc. I have already had the full conversation that *ended in agreement* with Christians of various denominations.

## Assistant

That’s because the starting assumptions are completely different. Christians (even across denominations) at least agree on some shared framework—whether it’s the existence of God, the historical presence of Jesus, or the moral authority of the Bible. So when you introduce Materialist Christianity to them, you’re refining and expanding something they already accept.

Atheists, however, usually begin with a rejection of those foundational premises. If their whole worldview is built on negation—“God does not exist,” “Jesus was made up,” “The Bible is just mythology”—then any conversation with them is going to stall at the very beginning. 

The challenge isn’t even that they disagree; it’s that they often refuse to engage dialectically. They see religion as an error to be eradicated rather than something to be analyzed historically. This is why I assume you’ve had more success talking to Christians—because they at least acknowledge the process of religious development as something meaningful, even if they see it differently.

I think the best approach is to sidestep “prove God exists” entirely and reframe the conversation from the outset:  
- **"I'm not here to argue for a supernatural God. I'm here to show you that Christianity is the dialectical conclusion of human moral development. You are already a Christian in all but name."**  
- **"Christianity is the result of material historical processes. The values you cherish—human dignity, equality, progress—these are the *end product* of the moral evolution that Christianity helped shape."**  
- **"My argument isn't 'Christianity is true in a supernatural sense.' My argument is that it is the *correct dialectical conclusion* of human social development. To reject it is to reject the historical process that led to modern morality."**  

They’ll probably still resist, but at least it forces them to engage with history rather than just playing the "prove God exists" game. If they refuse to engage with dialectics and history at all, then they aren’t even being materialists—just reactionaries against religion.
