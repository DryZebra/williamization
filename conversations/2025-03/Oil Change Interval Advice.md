# Oil Change Interval Advice

## User

Why do oil change places want me to return after 3000 miles if I'm getting 0w20 full synthetic?

## Assistant



## Assistant

Great question — and you're right to be suspicious!

**If you're using 0W-20 full synthetic oil**, the **typical oil change interval** is **7,500 to 10,000 miles**, depending on your vehicle and driving conditions. So why do those quick lube places still push the old **3,000-mile** rule?

### The Real Reasons:
1. **Money.**
   - More oil changes = more revenue. Recommending 3,000 miles keeps you coming back unnecessarily often.

2. **Outdated habits.**
   - The 3,000-mile rule dates back to older vehicles and conventional oils, which broke down much faster. Synthetic oil is far more durable and resistant to breakdown.

3. **Generic reminders.**
   - Some shops just set a generic 3,000-mile timer for everyone, regardless of the oil type or vehicle needs.

4. **Covering themselves.**
   - If they recommend 10,000 miles and something goes wrong, they fear liability. So they play it safe.

### What You Should Do:
- **Check your owner’s manual.** Most modern cars with 0W-20 full synthetic recommend **7,500 to 10,000 miles** between changes.
- **Rely on your car’s oil life monitor** (if it has one).
- **Don’t let the sticker on your windshield fool you.** They’re often set by the shop, not your car manufacturer.

Since we're on the subject what about fuel cleaners

## Assistant

Great follow-up — fuel system cleaners are another upsell that gets tossed around a lot, so let’s break it down:

### **What Fuel Cleaners Are Supposed to Do:**
- Clean fuel injectors
- Remove carbon deposits
- Improve combustion efficiency
- Restore lost power or MPG

### **Do You *Need* Fuel Cleaners Regularly?**
**Usually, no.** Most modern fuels already contain detergents that **keep your fuel system clean** under normal driving conditions.

But there are exceptions:

### **When It *Might* Help:**
- **High mileage cars** (100k+ miles) that have never had a cleaning
- **Poor fuel quality or cheap gas** (stations with little detergent)
- **Rough idle, hesitation, or MPG drop**
- **Direct injection engines**, which are more prone to carbon buildup (though these often need a more thorough cleaning than what a bottle can do)

### **When It’s a Waste:**
- Newer cars with no symptoms
- If you already use **top-tier gas** (like Shell, Chevron, Exxon — these have detergents built in)
- If you’re getting the cleaner added during an oil change for an upcharge — they often just dump a $5 bottle in and charge $30+

### **Best Practice:**
- **Occasional use is fine** (e.g., every 10,000 miles or so if you want), especially with high-mileage engines.
- Use trusted brands like **Techron**, **Sea Foam**, or **Royal Purple Max-Clean**.
- Avoid paying big money at the shop for something you can do for **$6–12 yourself**.

To reset the oil life light on your **2018 Honda Accord**, here’s how to do it — and it only takes a minute:

### **Method 1: Using the Steering Wheel Controls (for most trims)**

1. **Turn the car on** (ignition ON, engine OFF — press the start button twice *without* holding the brake).
2. Use the **left-side steering wheel buttons** to scroll to **“Maintenance”** or **“Oil Life”** on the digital screen.
3. Press the **Enter** button (center of the control pad).
4. Hold **Enter** for about **10 seconds** until the **reset menu** appears.
5. Use the arrows to select **“Engine Oil”** (or “All Due Items” if you want to reset everything).
6. Press **Enter** again to confirm.

### **Method 2: For models without the digital display (rare)**

1. Turn the ignition to **ON** (engine OFF).
2. Press the **Trip** knob (on the dash) repeatedly until you see **“Oil Life”**.
3. Hold the **Trip** knob for about **10 seconds** until it blinks.
4. Release, then hold it again for **5 seconds** until it resets to **100%**.
