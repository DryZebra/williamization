## Assistant

Great question—and you're right to be skeptical. The idea that *“true Christians can’t sin”* or are *already perfect* is not mainstream Christianity. It's pushed by specific fringe or hyper-literalist movements, often tied to one of these three roots:

---

### **1. Word of Faith / Prosperity Gospel (Charismatic / Pentecostal Offshoots)**  
**Key belief:** Christians have the same divine nature as Christ and can live without sin if they speak, believe, and act in alignment with God’s Word.

- Often quote **2 Peter 1:4** out of context to say “I have God’s nature, not Adam’s.”
- Emphasize **positive confession**—saying "I sin" is seen as a *lack of faith*.
- Some go so far as to say **Jesus stopped being God** so Christians could become like Him, which is borderline heresy.

**Notable teachers (some examples):** Kenneth Copeland, Creflo Dollar, Bill Johnson (Bethel), some circles in the New Apostolic Reformation (NAR).

---

### **2. Wesleyan Holiness / Entire Sanctification (Methodist Offshoots)**  
**Key belief:** It is possible for a believer to become so sanctified by God’s grace that they **no longer willfully sin**.

- Based on John Wesley’s teachings.
- Different from Word of Faith in that it's more about **moral purity** and less about power or prosperity.
- Believers may still *err*, but no longer sin in a willful or defiant way.

**Modern examples:** Church of the Nazarene, some Free Methodists, Holiness Pentecostals.

---

### **3. Sinless Perfectionism / Gnostic Overlap (Fringe Cultic Sects)**  
**Key belief:** “Real” Christians have overcome the flesh and *cannot* sin.

- Sometimes quote 1 John 3:9 – *“Whoever is born of God does not sin...”*
- Argue that if you sin, you were **never truly saved**.
- Often claim secret or higher knowledge (Gnostic tendencies).
- May say their "spirit" doesn't sin, even if their body slips up—a dangerous dualism.

**Examples:** Some parts of the Hebrew Roots Movement, certain hyper-spiritualized YouTube preachers, independent home churches with no accountability.

---

### **Why Do They Push This?**

- **Spiritual pride** – Believing you’re sinless puts you “above” other Christians.
- **Control** – Some movements use this to *excommunicate or shame* anyone who admits to struggling.
- **Misinterpretation of Scripture** – Taking metaphorical or aspirational language *literally*.
- **Fear of doubt** – Admitting Christians still sin causes anxiety for some about their salvation, so they resolve it by claiming sinlessness.
