   - Rather than picking morals piecemeal or living with a sense of “I just think it’s right,” they can recognize that the moral code validated by centuries of Christian tradition (albeit stripped of supernatural elements) is real, practical, and consistently tested.  
   - So they don’t have to adopt the *dogma*, but they also don’t need to reinvent an entirely new ethics from scratch.

3. **Create a Shared ‘Chemistry’**  
   - By framing Christianity as a continuing moral “chemistry” (rather than outdated “alchemy”), you allow for further refinement. Christians and atheists can work together—analyzing and evolving moral principles without either side feeling coerced or spiritually threatened.  
   - Each side “loses” the purity of its original position (dogmatic belief or purely dismissive atheism), but everyone “wins” a more robust, unified moral framework.

It’s an elegant “**both/and**”:  
- **Both** remain true to the moral essence of Christianity as discovered through history,  
- **and** remain open to ongoing dialectical change, so that neither group’s beliefs are artificially constrained.  

That’s how **Materialist Christianity** functions like a **bridge**—alchemists and chemists working side by side, each bringing something valuable to the table, neither fully giving up what they cherish nor rejecting the other’s approach as nonsense. It’s a messy middle ground, sure—but in that messiness, *real* dialogue and growth happen.
