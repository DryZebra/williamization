## Assistant

In Marxist-Leninist terms, the superstructure is like the “wrapper” that covers the basic economic “core” of society. It includes all the laws, politics, art, and culture that help maintain and justify the economic system. Essentially, while the economy provides the material base—what people produce and how—they shape the ideas, beliefs, and institutions that keep the system working.

One example is the legal and political system—like the courts, laws, and government—which helps protect property rights and maintain the economic system. Another example is the cultural institutions, such as schools, media, and art, which shape our ideas and values to support the way society is organized.

And how does it do that?

## Assistant

The superstructure maintains the economic system by shaping how people think and act. Laws protect private property and punish those who challenge the system, ensuring that wealth and power stay with those who own the means of production. Schools teach history and economics in a way that makes capitalism seem natural and inevitable, discouraging people from questioning it. Media, like movies and news, often promote individual success stories while ignoring systemic exploitation, making it seem like hard work alone determines success. All of this helps keep the current system stable by influencing how people understand the world.
