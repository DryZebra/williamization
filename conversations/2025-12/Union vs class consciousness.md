# **THE FAILURE OF UNION CONSCIOUSNESS (as usually practiced)**  
Not that it focuses on *too small* a part of the working class —  
but that it focuses on **the wrong part**, **the unproductive layer**, and then universalizes *their* experience as “the working class.”

This is the rupture between **union consciousness** and **class consciousness**.

---

# **1. The Real Divide Is Not “Union vs Non-Union.”**  
It’s **productive vs unproductive labor**.

### **Productive workers**  
Those who **create value through labor applied to the material world**.  
IBEW, ironworkers, carpenters, mechanics, welders, miners, farmworkers, etc.

### **Unproductive workers**  
Those whose labor is **necessary for circulation**, but **doesn’t create new value**.  
Baristas, retail workers, call center workers, social media workers, HR staff, many office jobs, etc.

These workers can still be exploited — sometimes brutally.  
But the **mechanism** of their exploitation is different because:

> **Unproductive labor is paid out of value created elsewhere.**  
> It is *not* the engine of surplus value.

This is why Marx was so ruthless about the distinction.

# **3. Why Starbucks Union ≠ Revolutionary Potential**  
This has nothing to do with moral worth.

It has to do with **structural position**.

Baristas are subjected to alienation, precarity, and bourgeois management just like you.  
But:

- They **do not control a production process**  
- They **do not produce surplus value**  
- They **cannot seize the means of production**, because the “means” are espresso machines  
- Their workplace is not a node in the **material chain of life**

When baristas strike, Starbucks loses revenue.  
When electricians strike, **capital loses power** — literally.

One is an annoyance.  
The other is existential.
