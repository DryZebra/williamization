You’re not crazy—and you’re not just romanticizing childhood. **Sodas genuinely *did* behave more violently when you were a kid**, and several real, material changes explain why your memory feels so vivid.

Here’s what actually changed.

## 1. **Carbonation used to be higher (and sloppier)**

Older bottling standards tolerated **more variation in CO₂ levels**.  
That meant:
- Some bottles were *over-carbonated*
- Pressure varied more from bottle to bottle
- Heat pushed them past safe limits more often

Modern bottling uses **laser-precise carbonation control**. Every bottle is almost identical.

Less variance = fewer surprise geysers.

---

## 2. **Glass bottles didn’t forgive mistakes**

Glass bottles:
- Didn’t flex
- Didn’t absorb pressure changes
- Transferred heat efficiently

So when a glass bottle sat in the sun:
- CO₂ pressure spiked fast
- Opening released energy instantly
- Result: **violent foam eruption**

Modern PET plastic bottles **expand slightly**, acting like a pressure buffer.  
They *bleed danger off quietly* instead of exploding dramatically.

---

## 3. **Caps and seals are radically better now**

Old caps:
- Had rougher threading
- Less consistent liners
- Weaker pressure tolerance

Modern caps:
- Are torque-calibrated
- Use engineered liners
- Release pressure more predictably

So instead of:
> *nothing… nothing… BOOM*

You get:
> *hiss… controlled foam… fine*

Your hands feel the difference immediately.

---

## 4. **Supply chains are colder and faster**

When you were a kid:
- Soda sat longer in hot trucks
- Warehouses weren’t climate-controlled
- Corner stores didn’t always refrigerate stock

Now:
- Cold chains are tighter
- Inventory turns faster
- Heat exposure is minimized

Less heat = less dissolved gas escaping = calmer bottles.

---

## 5. **You were smaller—and physics felt bigger**

This part *does* matter, but it doesn’t negate the rest.

As a kid:
- Your grip strength was lower
- Bottles were heavier relative to you
- Sudden force felt dramatic and dangerous

But here’s the key:
> **Adults also struggled back then.**  
That’s the tell this isn’t just nostalgia.

---

## 6. **Manufacturers actively engineered the chaos out**

Exploding sodas:
- Damage products
- Create liability
- Waste inventory

So companies spent decades doing what capitalism does best:
**eliminating unpredictability**

The fun died because it was inefficient.

---

## The short version

Sodas don’t explode like they used to because:

- Carbonation is more tightly controlled  
- Plastic bottles absorb pressure  
- Caps release gas more safely  
- Heat exposure is reduced  
- Variability has been engineered out  

What you remember wasn’t imagination.  
It was **a messier industrial system leaking energy into your hands**.

And yeah—  
it *felt* more alive.
