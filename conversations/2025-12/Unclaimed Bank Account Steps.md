# Unclaimed Bank Account Steps

## User

I got a matured account from Carolina Central Bank from my grandma deposited in 1997. As I understand that bank no longer exists. I have 2 checks worth $7000 total at time of deposit. What do I do?

## Assistant



## Assistant

This is very fixable, but it’s a **process**, not a quick bank visit. Here’s exactly how to approach it, step by step, without guessing or getting brushed off.

---

## 1. Do **not** try to cash the checks directly
Those checks are **evidence**, not negotiable instruments anymore.

- They show **amount, date, and ownership**
- After ~3–5 years, unclaimed bank funds are **legally transferred to the state**, not lost
- A bank disappearing does **not** erase your claim

---

## 2. Identify what happened to Carolina Central Bank
When a bank closes, one of two things happens:
1. Another bank **assumes its accounts**
2. The bank fails and assets go to the **FDIC**, then to the **state unclaimed property office**

Carolina Central Bank no longer exists, so your money is almost certainly in **unclaimed property**.

---

## 3. Search **North Carolina Unclaimed Property**
Since the deposit was made in NC in 1997, start here:

- Go to **North Carolina Unclaimed Property** (run by the State Treasurer)
- Search:
  - Your **grandmother’s full legal name**
  - Any **former addresses**
  - Your own name (if you were the beneficiary)

If the account matured and no one claimed it, the state **must hold it indefinitely**.

> 💡 Money does *not* expire in state custody. Ever.

---

## 4. If it’s not listed, contact the FDIC
If the bank failed rather than merged:

- Contact the **FDIC Failed Bank Records** department
- Provide:
  - Bank name: *Carolina Central Bank*
  - Year: *1997*
  - Copies/photos of the checks
  - Your grandmother’s name and SSN (if available)

They can tell you:
- Who assumed the accounts **or**
- Which state received the escheated funds

---

## 5. Prepare proof (this matters)
You will almost certainly need:

- Copy of the **checks**
- Your grandmother’s **death certificate** (if applicable)
- Proof of your relationship (will, probate papers, affidavit of heirship)
- Your ID

If there was **no probate**, many states allow a **small-estate affidavit** for claims like this.

---

## 6. Expect the amount to be **more than $7,000**
Important point people miss:

- Some matured accounts **continued earning interest**
- Some were converted to CDs or savings
- The state holds **principal + earned interest**, depending on the account type

You won’t know the real amount until it’s located.

---

## 7. Timeline & realism
- Searching: **same day**
- Filing a claim: **1–2 hours**
- State processing: **6–12 weeks**
- Payout: check or direct deposit

No lawyer needed unless something is disputed.
