# **THE REAL STORY OF *MORTAL SHELL***  
*(Explained clearly, without vague lore-speak)*

## **1. The World Is Already Dead**  
The land you awaken into—Fallgrim, the catacombs, the temples—is the *afterbirth* of a broken religion.

The people worshipped the **Higher Gods**, beings who offered immortality through “the Sacred Nectar.”  
Over time that religion corrupted into:

- obsessive self-mummification  
- ritual starvation  
- extracting “tar” (memories/souls) from the dying  
- building giant obsidian monuments to divine silence  

Humanity essentially spiritually **starved itself to death** in pursuit of transcendence.

The world is a graveyard of failed believers.

---

## **2. You Are Not Human — You Are the Leftover Spark**
Your character is **the Foundling**  
— a pale, wormlike, faceless being.

You are **not a ghost, not an undead knight, not a man.**  
You are a remnant of *will* or *consciousness* split off from the original worshippers.

Your purpose is simple but horrifying:

### You parasitize dead bodies
The “shells” are the corpses of legendary warriors who died chasing truth.  
Their names (Harros, Solomon, Tiel, Eredrim) are titles recorded only in memory.

You *enter* them, *wear* them, and *inherit* their unfinished stories.

This is why:

- they have no heads  
- they cannot speak  
- everything you learn of them is glimpsed only through “tar” and flashbacks  

They are armor made of regret.

---

## **3. The Goal of the Game: Bring Back the Sacred Nectar**
The old faith shattered, and the temples no longer flow with the divine nectar that once gave immortality.

Sester Genessa guides you because she wants the same thing:

### “Bring me the sacred glands.”

The three major temples hold **glands** that drip a concentrated form of the divine substance.

Each gland is:

- part ritual object  
- part biological organ  
- part memory core  
- and part conduit to the Higher Gods  

Bringing them back is essentially **restoring the metaphysical bloodstream** of the dying religion.

---

## **4. The Truth: The Nectar Was Poison**
The “nectar” that the world worshipped was **addiction**, not enlightenment.

It slowly turned the population into:

- mindless husks  
- cannibalistic cult fanatics  
- gelatinous mutants  
- impossible horrors that cannot die  

The glands you return don’t restore salvation—they restore the *disease.*

And the characters who want them back (Genessa, the old prisoners, the remnants of the order) are:

- addicts  
- worshippers of silence  
- barely-sentient memories of a dead civilization  

You’re not saving anyone.  
You’re completing the collapse.

---

## **5. What the Foundling Learns in the End**
After returning the glands, the final temple reveals what happened:

**The world did achieve immortality—  
but at the cost of humanity.**

The “Higher God” behind all of this is basically **infinite hunger for consciousness**, and the nectar was simply a mechanism to dissolve identity and feed that god.

You return the glands.  
You open the way.  
You meet the Last God.  
You kill him.

Why?

Because you finally understand the truth:

### Immortality is parasitism.
### Religion became a machine for extracting souls.
### The world must be allowed to rot so something new can grow.

You, the Foundling, are the final remaining spark of free will.  
Killing the Last God ends the cycle.

---

# **6. So What Is *Mortal Shell* About?**  
A simple sentence:

> **It’s the story of a dying religion that destroyed humanity in its pursuit of immortality, and of a leftover fragment of consciousness wearing dead warriors to end the last god that caused the collapse.**

Or even simpler:

> **A parasite kills a parasite-god to free a world that already died.**
