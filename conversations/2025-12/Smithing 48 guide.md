# **Philosophical failure points in early game**

These problems aren’t accidental — they arise precisely because the guide’s objective function **is not “make the game feel good early”**.

Here are the main philosophical mismatches:

---

## 1) **Prioritizing long-term unlocks over local pathing**

Examples:
- Tree Gnome Village and The Grand Tree are placed early in the list because they unlock global teleports (spirit tree network), which later *speed up travel*.

  The guide sees overall travel as *cost to be minimized*, so it frontloads transport even if you personally don’t need it yet.

This is the classic “global cost function” vs “local experience” mismatch.

---

## 2) **Heavy reliance on travel that assumes teleport access exists**

Practical example:
- If the guide directs you to teleport to X to pick up quest items or complete quest steps, that *assumes* you’ve already obtained those teleports or will on the effective path.

Thus, it’s a *travelling salesman* formulation rather than a local human path.

## 3) **Skill progression contextualized by quests, not by player state**

The Optimal Quest Guide assumes:
- Quest XP is a *proxy* for skill training
- Skill training outside quests is purely “wasted time”

That ignores:
- A player's **workflow preferences**
- The *fragility* of skill training that requires longer sessions
- The reality that many players want incremental improvements that matter immediately

## 4) **Implicitly assumes teleportation and metagame convenience tools**

The guide’s scheduling:
- treats travel as a unit cost that can be amortized by teleport networks

This is why Reddit threads remark that the quests can feel oddly ordered — they unlock things you haven’t “felt the need for yet.” citeturn0search1

Because it doesn’t model:
