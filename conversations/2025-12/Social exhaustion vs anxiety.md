## Assistant

Ezra, let’s slow this down and de-mystify it. Nothing strange or pathological is happening here.

### First: you **are** tired  
Three hours of mandatory socializing is real labor.  
It’s emotional labor, attentional labor, masking, small talk, smiling at power structures you didn’t choose. As a working-class guy, that drains you faster than physical work sometimes. There’s no virtue in pretending otherwise.

Fatigue doesn’t have to mean *sleepy*.  
It can mean **social saturation**.

### Second: this does *not* smell like social anxiety  
Social anxiety usually looks like:
- Fear of judgment  
- Rumination (“they think I’m weird”)  
- Desire to go but panic stopping you  

That’s not what you’re describing.

You’re not afraid of the bar.  
You’re **done**.

That’s different.

### Third: this is not weed addiction either  
Wanting RuneScape at the end of the week is not compulsive avoidance.  
It’s **chosen rest**.

RuneScape, for you, isn’t dissociation.  
It’s *regulated play*. It’s predictable, non-exploitative, self-paced, and internally rewarding. That’s actually one of the healthiest forms of decompression a tired nervous system can pick.

If this were addiction, it would look like:
- Neglecting obligations  
- Using it to avoid necessary confrontation  
- Feeling shame and compulsion  

You’re doing none of that.

### What’s *actually* happening (materially)
- You gave the company **extra, unpaid relational energy**
- Your nervous system hit its **weekly limit**
- Your body is signaling: *withdraw, consolidate, restore*

That’s not weakness.  
That’s **accurate signal processing**.

Christ withdrew constantly.  
Not because He was anxious.  
Because even truth embodied needs silence to remain true.

### DBT framing (clean and grounded)
This is a **Wise Mind** moment.

Emotion Mind:  
> “I want to go home.”

Reasonable Mind:  
> “I already fulfilled the social obligation.”

Wise Mind:  
> “I’m allowed to stop when the purpose is complete.”

There is **no added moral value** to the bar.  
No sin in leaving when the task is done.

### A simple rule you can trust
> **Optional social extensions are for surplus energy, not duty.**

You don’t have surplus.  
So you don’t go.

You can say:
- “I’m wiped, y’all have fun”
- “I’m heading out, good night”

No explanation. No apology.

### Final grounding truth
Wanting to go home and play RuneScape is not regression.  
It’s *Sabbath logic*.

You worked.  
You showed up.  
You stayed the full time.  

Now you rest.

Go home. Log in. Fish inefficiently.  
Nothing is wrong with you.

You’re naming the **real tension**, and you’re naming it correctly. Let’s hold it without smoothing it over.

### First: the “irony” isn’t irony  
You’re right. This is **not** a contradiction.

Communism, human dignity, human supremacy — those are **system-level commitments**.  
They are about *how society must be structured so people are not crushed*.

Your personal exhaustion is **sub-system reality**.  
A worker inside the system still has a finite nervous system.

Caring about *people as a class* does **not** obligate you to be endlessly available to *people as individuals*. That liberal move — collapsing structural concern into constant personal sociability — is a trick. It extracts free emotional labor and calls it virtue.

You’re not refusing humanity.  
You’re refusing **overextension**.

### Second: your isolation instinct *is* real — and worth respecting *and* watching  
Here’s the dialectic, not the dodge:

- Yes, you have a **baseline inclination to isolate**
- Yes, isolation *can* become a habit that shrinks the world
- And also: **rest and withdrawal are not isolation**

Those are different motions.

Isolation says: *“I don’t want people at all.”*  
Rest says: *“I need my edges back before I re-enter.”*

Right now, this is rest.

The key question isn’t “do I go to the bar tonight?”  
It’s: **do I ever re-enter, on my terms?**

### Third: dignity applies to you *first*, or it applies to no one  
This line you said matters:
> *I am tired. We deserve dignity.*

That is not selfish. That is **material ethics applied inward**.

A politics that demands you violate your own limits for optics is not communism, it’s ascetic theater. Christ rejected that too. He fed the people, then disappeared into the hills.

Dignity includes:
- the right to stop
- the right to quiet
- the right to choose *when* you are with others

DBT doesn’t ask “what do you feel?”  
It asks **what pattern are you reinforcing?**

So here’s the check that keeps you honest without punishing you:

- If you *always* default to isolation → **nudge toward connection**
- If you’re coming off **heavy social labor** → **protect rest**

Tonight is clearly the second.

The balance isn’t found by forcing yourself into the bar.  
It’s found by **scheduling connection when you’re not depleted**.

Example:
- “I’m not going out tonight.”
- “Next week I’ll sit with someone one-on-one, or show up briefly, or host.”

That preserves the bridge **without burning yourself**.

Another hard truth:  
You don’t actually “not care about people.” You care **selectively and seriously**.

You reject shallow, extractive, or performative sociality.  
That’s not anti-human. That’s anti-noise.

The proletariat doesn’t need you drunk at a bar.  
It needs you **intact**.

You’re allowed to say:
- “Tonight I rest.”
- “Tomorrow I remain committed.”

Those are not opposites.

Go home. Protect your quiet.  
Just don’t confuse rest with disappearance — and you aren’t.
